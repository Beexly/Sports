## THE HIGHEST-RISK ARTIFACT (aggression meets real law here)
The "Caught Mispricing — GSE caught [book] misprice at 11:47Z" feed is the most
attention-demanding artifact AND the highest legal-risk one. Publicly stating a
named, billion-dollar company "mispriced" or "was wrong" is potential trade libel
/ Lanham Act (false-advertising) exposure if the framing is even slightly off or
misleading. Grok's "no defamation per truthful comparison" is OVER-CONFIDENT and
counsel-gated. The framing that stays litigation-proof AND still lands:
- SAY: "GSE model output: X. Observed [book] closing number: Y. Divergence: Z,
  sealed at [hash]." Pure, true, data-linked, no adjective.
- NEVER SAY: "wrong," "mispriced," "cheating," "dumb money." The facts do the
  accusing; we never editorialize. Truth is the defense, and only literal,
  provable, non-misleading facts are safe. This framing is a hard requirement,
  not a style note.

## FIVE GOLDS Grok missed

### GOLD 1 — Adversarial self-disclosure is the most expensive-to-fake signal in existence
Spence says the believed signal is the one too costly for a faker to send. The
MOST costly signal in this market is PUBLISHING YOUR LOSSES, because every
competitor's entire incentive is to hide them — a fraud literally cannot afford
to show a losing month. So GSE bragging about its losses (a public,
receipted loss ledger + honest calibration curve) is simultaneously the most
credible, the most newsworthy, and the LEAST copyable move available. Grok
touched "publish own failures"; it did not see that this is THE lead artifact.
The headline writes itself: "The sports-prediction site that publishes its
losses on purpose — and can prove it."

### GOLD 2 — "The book followed us" must be a PERMANENT LIVE LEADERBOARD, not a chart
A one-off convergence chart is a tweet. A standing, always-on, self-updating
public scoreboard — "GSE's pre-close number vs the closing line, N games, the
market moved toward us X% of the time, every row receipted" — is a MONUMENT. It
is the artifact that makes a sportsbook's head of trading personally open the
site every morning. Build it as a permanent surface, receipt-anchored, not a
marketing one-off.

### GOLD 3 — the reflexivity confound is actually a STRONGER, safer claim
Grok worried about separating "we moved the line" from "we agreed with the sharps
who moved it" (matched-volume control). But GSE does not NEED the causal claim.
The CORRELATIONAL claim — "GSE's pre-close number agreed with the DIRECTION the
sharp market subsequently moved, X% of the time" — is easier to prove, harder to
attack, and already devastating: it proves GSE is on the side of the money that
moves markets. Downgrade the causal "we moved it" to a footnote; lead with the
bulletproof correlational claim. The confound becomes a feature.

### GOLD 4 — empty citations are the PROPRIETARY OPPORTUNITY, not a weakness (Division 4 unlock)
Grok kept hitting "[SPECULATION] — no primary franchise cite" on injury-
designation gaming and treating it as failure. Same meta-lesson as the ladder-
log: the per-team "Questionable → play-rate" table does not exist publicly
BECAUSE NOBODY BUILT IT. The empty citation record IS the moat signal. We
manufacture that table from public historical injury reports + actives (public
data), and we own a dataset ESPN doesn't have. Every "[SPECULATION] no cite" in
Division 4 should be re-read as "proprietary dataset to build," not "dead end."

### GOLD 5 — "FBI-level" inverts into a real, buildable position: AUDIT-READY BY DESIGN
The thing that makes regulators and institutional press notice a sports-data
company POSITIVELY is being the first to volunteer radical provability before
anyone asks — a public integrity dossier (methodology, calibration, receipt
chain, a data-provenance ledger) that a regulator or journalist could audit on
sight. In a field full of touts hiding records, the one company whose books are
OPEN is the one that earns institutional attention. "FBI-level" = audit-ready,
publicly, by design. That is a buildable positioning and it fuses perfectly with
the honesty doctrine.
