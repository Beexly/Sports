### A1. The Seam-Shifted Wake — @PitchingNinja (1181590629698199552) — LEARN-ONLY ★

- **What it is:** The canonical public explanation of the seam-shifted wake (SSW): a pitch's seams, held in a fixed orientation, trip the boundary layer asymmetrically and produce a wake that pushes the ball sideways — movement **not** explained by the Magnus effect, which is why some changeups and sinkers break "the wrong way" relative to their spin.
- **Citation/links:** baseballaero.com baseball aerodynamics main page (the Barton Smith / @NotRealCertain team's research hub), linked in-post.
- **How to use it / system mapping:** This is the physics layer underneath all ball-flight work: any trajectory model that only integrates gravity + drag + Magnus is misspecified for seam-active pitches. Maps to the fleet's physics lane (trajectory simulation) as the correction term to test first.
- **Transfer beyond sports:** Boundary-layer tripping by surface texture is general aerodynamics — the same physics governs any spinning or non-spinning projectile with surface features (industrial sprays, sports projectiles, drone rotors at low Reynolds number).
- **Autonomy + structure:** A flight-model ingest schema needs seam orientation as a state variable alongside spin axis/rate; without that field, SSW movement is unexplainable residual forever.
- **Second-order:** Pairs with A3/A4 (the notes and the fitted model) into a complete stack: phenomenon → rigid-body explanation → fitted detector.
- **Evidence:** explainer post; the underlying site is a research group's page. As-posted, UNVERIFIED at the paper level.
