## W2 — @run_the_sims — The Nash correction: "55% was NOT a Nash equilibrium" (FOOTBALL — full dossier; 2 posts; primary source fully read this run)

- Posts:
  - Correction TL;DR + full analysis link — https://x.com/run_the_sims/status/2117639201764131312 (Tue Nov 3, 15:25 GMT), linking https://thesidequest.com/newsletter/2026-10-30-i-called-it-a-nash-equilibrium/
  - "Hand up" self-correction post — https://x.com/run_the_sims/status/2117639188401058194 (15:25 GMT): optimal early-down run-pass mix now put at 56–75%.
- WHAT IT IS: A public, worked retraction and rebuild of the pass-rate equilibrium claim this corpus first filed on 2026-10-29 ("Equilibrium: about 55% pass. The NFL: 54%"). VERIFIED at the primary source this run — the full Side Quest article was read end to end. The corrected method, in the author's own construction: (1) The defense's "call" is redefined from raw box count (8+ vs 6 or fewer, which skipped the 7-man box — about a third of early downs) to box advantage (Tej Seth's definition: blockers in the box, from SumerSports charting, minus defenders in the box, from nflverse; Defense+ 34% of early downs, Even 48%, Offense+ 18%; 87,000 first/second-down plays, 2022–2025). (2) With payoffs measured per box advantage, the pass beats the run against every box (e.g., Even: pass +0.06 vs run −0.06 points per play against a situation-adjusted baseline, Kovash–Levitt style), so passing is a dominant strategy and the original line-crossing was never a Nash equilibrium — it only made the defense indifferent. (3) The fix that produces an interior optimum: pass value decays as the defense's certainty of a pass rises (measured with Ben Baldwin's expected-pass model as the certainty proxy): pass payoff falls 0.027 EPA per 10 points of certainty against Defense+, 0.019 against Even, 0.014 against Offense+, while run payoff holds roughly flat. The mix-value curve therefore bends (a negative quadratic term) and peaks before 100%: by box, 58% (Defense+), 63% (Even), 77% (Offense+); against the defense's best response the best mix is about 71% pass, with a resampling range of roughly 57–75% and the author's stated overall range 56–75% — all above today's ~52%. The author correctly renames the object: a Stackelberg equilibrium (the offense commits to a season tendency; the defense responds), not a Nash equilibrium.
- WHERE IT COMES FROM: @run_the_sims (Nick, The Side Quest), data SumerSports + nflverse, framework after Brian Burke (2008) and Kovash & Levitt (2009); tendency-response checked across 128 team-seasons (defenses shift to light boxes at 1.15× and two-high at 1.02× the situational rate). The article names its own limits plainly (below).
- HOW TO USE IT: GSE can reproduce the skeleton from nflverse alone (box counts, EPA, xPass are all nflverse-native); only the blocker count needs charting data (SumerSports-class) — without it, box count is the degraded proxy the article just finished criticizing, and any in-house version must say so. The decay slopes are the portable part: "each 10 points of defensive pass-certainty costs the pass ~0.02 EPA" is a number the engine's matchup reasoning can cite as a published estimate.
- WHICH LAYER IT PLUGS INTO: Matchups layer (early-down tendency analysis for any Week 9 preview) and the research program's game-theory shelf — this replaces the 55% figure everywhere it was filed. Any GSE content or reasoning that still cites "55% Nash equilibrium" is now citing a retracted claim; the correction is the canonical version.
- CREATIVE ANGLES: (a) The tendency-response multipliers (1.15× light box, 1.02× two-high, 0.6× extra DBs) are a ready-made defensive-adjustment model: given a team's pass-rate-over-expectation, predict how its next opponent's structure shifts. (b) The author's honesty format — "Hand up," itemized feedback, rebuilt method, renamed equilibrium — is the exact self-correction pattern the Hermes doctrine demands; as content, a GSE "we were wrong about 55%, here is the rebuild" piece would be on-doctrine and differentiating. (c) The QB-cost caveat (hits rise 9%→13% of dropbacks as pass likelihood goes under-40% to over-80%) connects the mix question to the health layer: the optimal mix is not free, and the price is paid by the QB.
- RISKS: The author's own stated limits, which the dossier adopts: payoffs are league averages (an elite-QB team and a backup-QB team get the same curve); the curve is estimated on snaps where score/clock drive expectations, not on teams choosing to pass more; no team has passed past ~67% on early downs in the data (the 2025 Cardinals' 64% is the max since 2001), so defensive response beyond that is unobserved; third/fourth downs are excluded, and short-yardage run value (71% conversion on 3rd-and-1 vs 59% for passes) feeds back into early-down calling; the Stackelberg range is wide (56–75%) — a range, not a point.
- FIRST TEST AND VERDICT: Reproduce the Even-box decay slope from nflverse (xPass vs pass EPA) on 2022–2025 early downs; if the slope lands near −0.019 per 10 points, the framework is adoptable as a cited method. Verdict: TEST — and the corpus's 55% Nash file is superseded by this dossier as of this run.
