# Sunday Slate: Week 5

Thirteen games. Two with 54.5 totals. Everybody sees the same two games. That's the whole puzzle.

## The slate

Two games tower over everything: Detroit at Arizona and Buffalo at LA. Both 54.5. Detroit carries a 30.0 team total, the best number on the board, indoors. The Rams sit at 29.0 with Buffalo answering at 25.5.

The next tier: Chicago at Green Bay (46.5) and Seattle hosting San Francisco (45.5), both with close spreads and both teams throwing. Everything else is 43.5 or lower. Cincinnati at Miami has a 43.5 total but a 7-point spread against a Dolphins team missing its weapons. Blowout risk is real there.

London already kicked off. Not on this slate. Forget it exists.

## Stack 1: Rams and Bills

Stafford with Nacua and Adams. Allen coming back the other way.

The 54.5 total with a 3.5-point spread. That's the combination you want. Close enough that nobody stops throwing. No blowout script. No clock killing. Just four quarters of both quarterbacks working.

The Rams at home are implied for 29.0. Buffalo answers with 25.5. Somebody scores last in this game. You want pieces on both sides when they do.

## Stack 2: Lions and Cardinals

Goff with St. Brown and Jameson Williams. Michael Wilson coming back.

The other 54.5, indoors. Detroit's 30.0 team total leads the slate. The worry is the 5.5-point spread. Detroit builds a lead and goes conservative. That's the risk you accept.

Here's the thing. Marvin Harrison Jr. is inactive and getting traded. His targets don't vanish. They go to Wilson and McBride. Wilson has 30 targets in two weeks. Somebody has to catch the ball in Arizona, and it's been him.

## Stack 3: Seahawks and 49ers

Seattle's passing game with Jaxon Smith-Njigba. McCaffrey or Kittle coming back.

45.5 total. Three-point spread. Competitive start to finish, which is exactly what a game stack needs. JSN has the highest ceiling of any receiver on this slate at 50.2. San Francisco stays in it the whole way. Nobody salts this one away.

## Value plays

Cheap pieces that let you afford the expensive ones. That's the entire job description.

Michael Wilson: he inherits the Arizona passing game today. No Harrison. Thirty targets in two weeks didn't happen by accident. He's the runback that makes the Detroit stack affordable.

Raiders defense at $2.5K: the cheapest defense worth rostering. It frees up real salary for the skill positions, and it pairs with the Bowers side of that game.

The punt tight end construction: in a dome game like Minnesota-New Orleans, a minimum-salary tight end lets you pay up at every other position. That's the classic shape of a tournament winner. One caution from the overnight sheet: verify the name is actually on a roster before lock. Ours wasn't.

## Contrarian angles

Fade the chalk game. The field will flock to Detroit-Arizona and its 54.5 total. Totals that big disappoint all the time. If it does, and you weren't there, you're alive while everyone else is drawing dead.

Seattle over Detroit. The field builds around the two 54.5s. The Seattle game has nearly as much scoring with a fraction of the ownership. Leverage is a number too.

Raiders defense plus Bowers. Game-script leverage in one pairing. If Las Vegas keeps it close, both pieces hit. If they don't, the defense only cost you $2.5K.

## Weather and injuries

Rain in Washington all day for Giants-Commanders. Rain in Pittsburgh for Colts-Steelers. Wet and windy is a tax on every passing game. Adjust expectations, not just lineups.

Marvin Harrison Jr.: inactive, being traded. He's not walking through that door.

Stefon Diggs: out with a hamstring. Washington's receivers are McLaurin (a true game-time call, likely limited if he goes) and rookies.

Ashton Jeanty: didn't practice Friday. Game-time decision. If he sits, Mike Washington steps into the lead role. Check before lock.

Ladd McConkey: didn't practice all week. Don't build around him.

Jayden Daniels: he's starting. The elbow is in a brace. Hope is not a medical plan, but he's playing.
