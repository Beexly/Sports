### 3a. NGS digest was staged but never committed
The delegate's linestar/ files went in with `4335f8a84`, but the whole `ngs/` set — including the
digest and all three raw payloads — sat **staged and uncommitted**. A subsequent session kept writing
(`raw_statboard_rushing.json` appeared at 19:42) without committing either. Fixed in `8683bfa30`.

### 3b. The "rushing is a route-specific gate" diagnosis was WRONG
The delegate hit `HTTP 401` on `statboard/rushing` and concluded in both the digest and the ops doc
that rushing was **blocked by a route-specific gate**, and marked it `BLOCKED` / "not captured".

Then a later session captured rushing as a **200 with 53 rows**. Contradiction — so I re-probed live
at 19:59:

```
GET /api/statboard/passing?season=2026&seasonType=REG    -> 401
GET /api/statboard/receiving?season=2026&seasonType=REG  -> 401
GET /api/statboard/rushing?season=2026&seasonType=REG    -> 401
```

**All three 401'd** — including passing and receiving, which had served 200s minutes earlier. So the
anonymous gate is **flaky / rate-based, not route-specific**, and the earlier "BLOCKED" call was a
misdiagnosis. Corrected in place in:
- `docs/data-sources/research/2026-10-13/ngs/ngs_statboard_2026-10-13.md` (header + gate note)
- `docs/data-sources/research/2026-10-13/ngs/ngs_statboard_2026-10-13.json` (endpoints, row counts)
- `docs/ops/LINESTAR-NGS-INGEST-2026-10-13.md` (§2.1 table + gate paragraph, §3 item 2
  `BLOCKED -> RESOLVED`, §4 next-steps)

**Practical upshot**: if you get a 401 from `nextgenstats.nfl.com`, just retry with pacing. Do not
treat it as a blocked route, and do not reach for the nflverse substitute — the payload is already
