# NFL Altitude Effects on Kickoff/Punt/FG Props: DEN vs Sea-Level vs Domes

## 1. PHYSICS OF ALTITUDE

### Air Density & Drag
- **Denver elevation**: 5,280 ft (1,609 m) — exactly 1 mile above sea level
- **Air density at Denver**: ~82% of sea level (0.0627 lb/ft³ vs 0.0764 lb/ft³ at 70°F sea level)
- **17% less oxygen per cubic foot** at elevation
- **Drag reduction**: ~18% less air drag at Denver's elevation
- **Gravity effect**: negligible — ~0.09% reduction at 5,280 ft (not a factor)
- **Primary mechanism**: F_drag ∝ ρ (air density). Reduced density → less drag → ball travels farther
- **Temperature counter**: Cold increases density, partially offsetting altitude. 30°F differential ≈ 5 yards kicking distance

### Stadium Elevations

## 2. FIELD GOAL EFFECTS

### Range Increase — Denver adds ~5 yards
- Consistent across HowStuffWorks, Yahoo Sports, ESPN, AFD, Reddit
- Theoretical: 66.7 yards range at Denver vs 62 yards at sea level (~4.7 yard gain)
- Denver has the **highest avg. FG attempt distance** in NFL: **39.48 yards**
- Denver ranks ~17th in overall FG% made despite longer attempts — altitude compensates

### Denver FG Success Rates by Distance (AFD Data)

|----------|-------|--------|--------------|
| 40-44 yds | 39 | 8 | 79% |
| 45-49 yds | 29 | 10 | 65% |

### Matt Prater (Broncos Era)
- **Denver**: 62-76 = 81.6%
- **Road**: 54-68 = 79.4%
- 3 of 5 longest FGs in NFL history (63, 63, 64 yards) were in Denver

### Indoor (Dome) FG Advantage (RPubs, n=5,503)
| Metric | Indoor | Outdoor | Diff |
|--------|--------|---------|------|
| Overall FG% | 85.4% | 84.2% | +1.2% |
| 50+ yds | ~75% | ~68% | +7% |
| 95% CI odds ratio | 11%-59% higher success | | |

- **Distance-dependent**: No diff <20 yds; significant from 20+ yds; constant boost beyond 35-40 yds
- Primary driver: **wind elimination**, NOT altitude (domes at sea level)
- Per-kicker data shows consistent indoor boost (McManus +4.1%, Boswell +6.7%, McLaughlin +10.4%, Carlson +8.0%)

### Wind Effects on FGs
| Wind Speed | Expected FG% | Actual FG% | Gap |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|-----|
| <10 mph | ~83.5% | ~84% | ~0% |
| 20+ mph | 88.8% | 77.0% | **-11.8%** |

- Wind >20 mph causes coaches to attempt FGs ~7 yards shorter
- Indoor = wind elimination = biggest practical kicking advantage

### Temperature Effects
- Below 39.2°F (4°C): FG accuracy drops **1.7%**
- Snow has massive effect (touchback rate >40% dry → <25% snow)
- Cold increases air density, partially offsetting altitude benefits

- Touchback rate: 66% (2024) → 21% (2025)
- Ball spot on touchbacks moved to 35-yard line

## 4. PUNT EFFECTS

### Altitude (Denver)
- **~5 yards extra distance** (Mike Priefer estimate)
- **~0.5 seconds extra hang time** (Priefer estimate)
- Johnny Hekker: hang time 4.3s (avg) → **4.69s in Denver** (+0.39s)
- Daniel Whelan (Packers): "Don't over kick" — ball floats longer and travels farther at altitude

### Weather Effects on Punts
- Distance decreases with precipitation and high wind
- Less consistent than FG data; trend exists with more variance

## 6. BETTING IMPLICATIONS FOR PROPS

### FG Made/Attempt Props
- **DEN home games**: Model ~5 extra yards of effective range. Kicker attempting 45-49 in Denver has similar success to 40-44 at sea level.
- **Indoor dome**: ~1.2% overall FG% boost, ~7% boost on 50+ yarders (wind elimination).
- **Wind >20 mph outdoors**: 11.8% actual-vs-expected FG shortfall; coaches attempt ~7 yards shorter.
- **Cold/snow**: FG accuracy drops ~1.7% per 39°F threshold; snow massively reduces touchback rates.

### Kickoff Props (2025 Dynamic Rules)
- **Altitude**: +7+ yard distance potential, 23% higher TB rate vs road, but 2025 rules have cut overall TB rates to 21%.
- **Dome vs Outdoor**: Weather (wind/snow) is primary factor; altitude doesn't help indoors.
- **Returns**: Expect 74.5% return rate in 2025 (dynamic kickoff) — not touchbacks.

### Punt Props
- **DEN altitude**: Expect +5 yards distance, +0.5s hang time. "Don't over kick."
- **Dome**: No altitude effect on punts (domes at sea level); wind elimination is the only indoor benefit.
