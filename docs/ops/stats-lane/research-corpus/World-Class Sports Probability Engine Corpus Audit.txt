Market Efficiency, Target Leakage, and Sequential E-Processes
A pervasive failure point in modern sports analytics is the conflation of market pricing models with pure performance forecasting2. Incorporating closing betting lines or near-game odds directly as input features into team strength models induces severe target leakage. The model absorbs the market's efficiency into its historical training set, destroying its ability to measure true Closing Line Value (CLV) because the algorithm simply learns to parrot the consensus bookmaker2.
To achieve true alpha, the pure performance prediction engine must be aggressively decoupled, deriving its signal entirely from play-by-play efficiency, spatial tracking, and box-score aggregates3. The evaluation of market dynamics is then relegated to a separate subsystem. The engine implements a Hawkes self-exciting process to detect clustered, informed betting pressure1. By fitting a per-side exponential-decay Hawkes model with online intensity updates, the system can identify sharp liquidity entering the market, effectively separating organic background volatility from momentum-driven line movement1.
Evaluating whether the decoupled performance model actually beats the market requires sophisticated sequential testing that transcends traditional p-values. The LiveOrchestrator utilizes a Forecast-Skill E-process—an anytime-valid sequential likelihood-ratio martingale based on Ville's inequality1. This E-process tests the null hypothesis that the model's probabilities are no better than the market consensus2. Because traditional p-values require fixed sample sizes and fail under continuous monitoring, the E-process allows the engine to continuously track compounded evidence of forecast skill. If the martingale multiplies the theoretical capital it risks by a significant factor, it provides mathematically sound proof of a predictive edge without falling victim to absorbing-zero failures or -hacking1.
