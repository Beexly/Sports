Galaxy Sports Edge (GSE) Conformal Uncertainty and Calibration Audit Report
Section A: KEEP
The foundational baseline for uncertainty quantification in sports betting engines relies on distribution-free, finite-sample predictive guarantees [1, 2]. Standard point forecasters and uncalibrated quantile regressions exhibit systematic under-coverage bias in finite samples whenever the feature dimension is non-trivial relative to sample size [3, 4, 5]. To maintain rigorous calibration, eliminate fake tightness, and enforce fail-closed selective publication on numeric lines (spreads, totals, player props) and binary sides (moneylines), GSE incorporates the primary conformal inference methodologies detailed below.
Opened Primary Literature and Formulas
Romano, Patterson, and Candes (2019) — Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR, arXiv:1905.03222)
Core Formula and Quote: "Suppose we are given n training samples {(Xi, Yi)}_{i=1}^n and we must now predict the unknown value of Yn+1 at a test point Xn+1... We fix the lower and upper quantiles to be equal to alpha_lo = alpha/2 and alpha_hi = 1 - alpha/2... The conformity score on the calibration set is E_i = max(q_alpha_lo(X_i) - Y_i, Y_i - q_alpha_hi(X_i))." [1, 6]
Quantile Inflation and Output: "Then, with Q_1-alpha(E; I_2) defined as the ceil((1 - alpha)(|I_2| + 1))-th largest element of {E_i}_{i in I_2}, the conformal prediction interval for Xn+1 is given by C(Xn+1) = [q_alpha_lo(Xn+1) - Q_1-alpha(E; I_2), q_alpha_hi(Xn+1) + Q_1-alpha(E; I_2)]. Under exchangeability, P{Yn+1 in C(Xn+1)} >= 1 - alpha." [1, 6]
Barber, Candes, Ramdas, and Tibshirani (2019) — Predictive Inference with the Jackknife+ (arXiv:1905.02928)
Core Formula and Quote: "Given leave-one-out fitted regression functions mu_-i and leave-one-out residuals R_i^LOO = |Y_i - mu_-i(X_i)|, the jackknife+ prediction interval at test point Xn+1 is defined as [q_alpha^-(mu_-i(Xn+1) - R_i^LOO), q_1-alpha^+(mu_-i(Xn+1) + R_i^LOO)]... Assuming exchangeable training samples, we prove that this modification permits rigorous coverage guarantees of at least 1 - 2*alpha regardless of the distribution of the data points, for any algorithm that treats training points symmetrically." [2, 7, 8]
Angelopoulos and Bates (2021) — A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction (arXiv:2107.07511)
Core Formula and Quote: "Compute q_hat as the ceil((n + 1)(1 - alpha)) / n empirical quantile of the calibration scores s_1, ..., s_n... Remarkably, this algorithm gives prediction sets that are guaranteed to satisfy P(Yn+1 in C(Xn+1)) >= 1 - alpha, no matter what model is used or what the distribution of the data is." [9, 10]
yromano/cqr Computation Code Logic
Computation Logic Quote: "Compute the conformity scores E_i = max(q_alpha_lo(X_i) - Y_i, Y_i - q_alpha_hi(X_i)) for each i in I_2; compute Q_1-alpha(E; I_2) as the ceil((1 - alpha)(|I_2| + 1))-th largest element of {E_i}; construct prediction interval [q_alpha_lo(Xn+1) - Q_1-alpha, q_alpha_hi(Xn+1) + Q_1-alpha]." [6, 9]
Mandatory Hand Computation: n = 5, alpha = 0.1 Evaluation
To evaluate conformal behavior under scarce calibration, consider a calibration set of size n = 5 evaluated at a target miscoverage rate alpha = 0.10 (nominal coverage 1 - alpha = 0.90 or 90%) [1, 9, 10].
Inflation factor evaluation: (1 - alpha) * (1 + 1/n) = (1 - 0.10) * (1 + 1/5) = 0.90 * 1.20 = 1.08 [1, 7, 9].
Rank index calculation: k = ceil((n + 1) * (1 - alpha)) = ceil((5 + 1) * 0.90) = ceil(5.4) = 6 [6, 9, 10].
Sample comparison: The calculated rank index k = 6 strictly exceeds the calibration sample size n = 5 [9, 10].
Theoretical Result (+infinity / Refusal vs. Clamping)
Behavior in Paper/Appendix: Evaluating an empirical quantile function at an inflated probability level exceeding 1.0 (1.08) causes the index k (6) to exceed sample size n (5) [1, 7, 9]. Mathematically, the empirical CDF cannot exceed 1.0, so the empirical quantile evaluates to +infinity [1, 9]. This forces an infinite prediction interval [-infinity, +infinity] or a total refusal to publish [9, 10]. This behavior is required because a distribution-free 90% marginal coverage guarantee is impossible when n < ceil(1/alpha) - 1 (which requires at least n = 9 for alpha = 0.1) [9, 10].
Clamping to Maximum Residual: If software clamps the index k to max(n) = 5, it selects the maximum observed calibration residual E_(5) [9, 11]. The true finite-sample coverage provided by E_(5) under exchangeability is n / (n + 1) = 5 / 6 approx 83.33% [9, 10]. Clamping falsely presents an interval as possessing 90% confidence while delivering an under-coverage defect of 6.67%, generating fake tightness [3, 5, 11]. GSE explicitly bans quantile clamping [11, 12].

Synthesis of Non-Exchangeable, Scarce, and Side Calibration Domains
Non-Exchangeable Sequential Models for Sports Seasons
Applying conformal prediction to a 272-game NFL season or an 82-game NBA season violates classical i.i.d. exchangeability due to weekly regime shifts, team momentum, roster changes, and weather variations [1, 12]. Methods such as Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI), Ensemble Batch Prediction Intervals (EnbPI), Sequential Predictive Conformal Inference (SPCI), and Rolling-Origin Conformal Prediction replace strict exchangeability with weaker assumptions such as local stationarity, mixing conditions, or bounded distribution drift [12].
ACI updates the nominal miscoverage rate dynamically after every observed outcome [12]. While ACI guarantees asymptotic coverage under arbitrary distribution shifts, it provides no finite-sample marginal guarantee over a short 18-week NFL schedule [12]. SPCI fits an autoregressive model to non-conformity scores, yielding valid finite-sample intervals provided the residual time series remains stationary [12]. Rolling-origin conformal prediction calibrates against the m most recent pseudo-out-of-sample forecast errors [12]. For a structured 272-game NFL season, rolling-origin CP with a sliding window of m = 32 to 64 games provides optimal adaptation to line movements, whereas EnbPI effectively aggregates bootstrap ensemble forecasts over rolling horizons [12].
Scarce Calibration and Fail-Closed Refusal Policies
When evaluating small sample sizes (e.g., n = 5), standard split conformal prediction cannot achieve 90% coverage without expanding intervals to infinity [9, 10]. Fail-closed operational policies require the system to emit infinite intervals or abstain entirely (No-Bet) when n < ceil(1/alpha) - 1 [9, 10, 12]. To avoid total non-availability in scarce settings, Jackknife+ utilizes leave-one-out data structures to guarantee 1 - 2*alpha finite-sample coverage without data splitting [2, 7, 8]. Synthetic-Powered Predictive Inference (SPI) augments scarce calibration sets with synthetic samples generated from domain priors, shrinking interval width while preserving coverage bounds [12]. Conversely, selective regression literature (e.g., Schröder et al.) argues against infinite intervals on user-experience grounds, advocating for truncated or clamped quantiles [14]. GSE explicitly rejects truncation as deceptive fake tightness, enforcing fail-closed refusal whenever empirical sample size is insufficient to certify the nominal quantile [11, 12].
Numeric CQR Intervals vs. Probability Calibration for Binary Sides
A fundamental structural distinction exists between numeric prediction intervals and binary probability calibration [1, 12, 15]. Numeric CQR constructs spatial intervals [q_lo(x) - Q_1-alpha, q_hi(x) + Q_1-alpha] for continuous responses Y in R using pinball loss [1, 6]. Applying CQR residual math to binary moneyline outcomes (Y in {0, 1}) is mathematically invalid; continuous residual expansion cannot produce a calibrated probability distribution p(X) = P(Y = 1 | X) [12, 15].
Moneyline and side markets require probability calibration methods, including Platt Scaling, Isotonic Regression (PAVA), and Venn-Abers calibration [12, 13, 16]. Isotonic regression non-parametrically fits monotonic step functions to model scores, but requires pre-coalescence of tied scores to prevent non-unique knot assignments [12, 16]. Venn-Abers calibration computes exact multiprobability bounds [p0, p1] by fitting dual isotonic regressions [12, 13]. The interval width p1 - p0 explicitly measures epistemic uncertainty stemming from data paucity [12, 13]. Continuous CQR intervals evaluate margin of victory or prop totals, whereas Venn-Abers bounds evaluate binary win probabilities for Expected Value (EV) calculation against market odds [1, 12, 15].
Non-Conformal Side-Finds
Ordinal Ranking vs. Win Probability Disparity: Team ranking models (e.g., power ratings) exhibit non-linear relationships with win probabilities [17]. Translating rank differences directly to win probabilities without accounting for spread variance introduces severe calibration drift in extreme underdog/favorite matchups [17].
Closing Line Value (CLV) Leakage: Incorporating market lines released near game time into training features leaks future information into historical evaluation [12]. Models must be strictly partitioned into release-time snapshots to measure true predictive information edge against closing lines [12].
Rights-Clean Play-by-Play Data Pipelines: Utilizing public, open-source play-by-play repositories (such as nflverse and mlbverse) provides audit-verifiable data pipelines free from proprietary vendor licensing restrictions [16].
Distributional Forecasting via Particle Filters and Copulas: Predicting full conditional joint distributions for game outcomes allows accurate pricing of correlated multi-leg prop parlays, outperforming point expectation models [18].
Section B: CONTRADICTIONS
Pair 1: Raw Quantile Regression Under-Coverage vs. Exact Conformal Finite-Sample Validity
Quote A (Romano, Patterson, & Candes, 2019, arXiv:1905.03222): "Conformalized quantile regression (CQR)... inherits both the finite sample, distribution-free validity of conformal prediction and the statistical efficiency of quantile regression. Under exchangeability, P{Yn+1 in C(Xn+1)} >= 1 - alpha in finite samples." [1]
Quote B (Lin, Trivedi, & Sun, 2021, arXiv:2106.05515): "We prove that linear quantile regression suffers from an inherent under-coverage bias... For alpha > 0.5 and small d/n, the alpha-quantile learned by quantile regression roughly achieves coverage alpha - (alpha - 1/2) * d/n regardless of the noise distribution." [3, 4, 5]
Auditor Analysis: Un-conformalized quantile regression systematically under-covers in finite samples due to parameter estimation error proportional to d/n [3, 5]. Raw quantile outputs cannot be published directly as numeric lines; they must be wrapped in a conformal calibration step to guarantee nominal coverage [1, 5].
Pair 2: Fail-Closed Infinite Intervals vs. Practical Quantile Truncation
Quote A (Angelopoulos & Bates, 2021, arXiv:2107.07511 / Dietterich & Hostetler, 2022, arXiv:2206.04860): "The value of delta must be >= 1/(n+1) to ensure that ceil((1-delta)(n+1)) <= n... If n is too small, distribution-free coverage cannot be guaranteed without emitting infinite intervals or abstaining." [9, 10]
Quote B (Schröder et al., 2024 / Selective Regression Literature, arXiv:2402.16300): "Emitting infinite intervals or total abstention renders prediction systems unusable in practice... truncating or clamping conformal quantiles to empirical maximums preserves practical utility without severe empirical miscalibration in well-specified models." [14]
Auditor Analysis: Practical engineering workflows frequently clamp quantiles to maintain system availability, directly violating finite-sample coverage proofs [11, 14]. GSE strictly enforces Quote A: clamping is treated as fake tightness, and scarce calibration settings trigger fail-closed No-Bet pass decisions [11, 12].
Pair 3: Distribution-Free Binary Probability Calibration Bounds vs. Point Estimates
Quote A (Barber, Candes, Ramdas, & Tibshirani, 2020, EJS): "In the binary regression setting, any algorithm providing distribution-free coverage of the conditional label probability pi(X) = P(Y=1|X) must necessarily also cover the binary label Y... meaningful distribution-free confidence intervals for pi(X) of bounded length are impossible without structural assumptions." [15, 19]
Quote B (van der Laan & Alaa, 2025, arXiv:2502.05676): "Generalized Venn-Abers calibration extends Vovk's approach... guaranteeing valid multiprobability bounds [p0, p1] that contain the true conditional probability in finite samples under exchangeability." [12, 13]
Auditor Analysis: Point probability estimates for binary sides cannot achieve distribution-free finite-sample guarantees without structural assumptions [15, 19]. Venn-Abers addresses this limit by outputting an interval of probabilities [p0, p1], explicitly communicating epistemic uncertainty [12, 13].
