Literature Clusters
Bayesian State-Space and Hierarchical Generative Models: This cluster represents the structural backbone for multi-sport probability modeling [1]. Its central premise is that latent team or player strength evolves temporally as a stochastic process, with sport-specific noise parameters (sigma_game) and likelihood functions that explicitly model the scoring rules and constraints of each sport [1]. Key literature includes paper 1701.05976 (a unified state-space model across Big Four North American leagues), paper 1911.01815 (a two-level hierarchical set and point model for volleyball), and paper 1607.00379 (a hierarchical Poisson rugby model) [1].
Sequential Inference, Win-Martingales, and Free Boundaries: This cluster treats live, market-implied win probabilities as continuous-time diffusions or martingales [1]. Its premise is to solve optimal stopping and free-boundary problems to decide when to lock picks or execute trades before a game reaches its fixed horizon [1]. A representative paper is 2608.12291 (Sequential Inference for win-martingales), which is evaluated strictly via abstract metadata and designated as UNREAD due to the absence of accessible full text [1].
Calibration Wrappers, Distributional Forecasting, and Conformal Prediction: This cluster focuses on post-processing point estimates or conditional quantiles using non-parametric wrappers to guarantee distribution-free coverage or quantify epistemic uncertainty on numeric lines and win probabilities [1, 2]. Essential references include paper 2208.08598 (Conformal Win Probability Calibration), Romano et al. (2019, arXiv:1905.03222 on Conformalized Quantile Regression), Barber et al. (2019, arXiv:1905.02928 on Jackknife+), and van der Laan & Alaa (2025, arXiv:2502.05676 on Generalized Venn-Abers bounds) [1, 2].
Ranking Models and Ordinal Pairwise Extensions: This cluster derives win probabilities from partial rankings or ordinal outcome data rather than continuous score margins [1, 2]. It extends Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL) and Elo frameworks using partial rankings and rank-clustered structures [1]. Key references include papers 2501.02505 and 2406.19563 (rank-clustered BTL models) and paper 2405.10247 (Bayesian Bradley-Terry-Davidson variants) [1].
Rare Event and Zero-Inflated Outcome Heads: This cluster addresses zero-inflated or heavy-tailed outcome distributions, such as shutout props, exact goal counts, or low-frequency player props, by separating zero-generating processes from primary probability engines [1]. A primary example is paper 2501.17711 (Zero-Inflated Compound Poisson framework) [1].
Feature Engineering Bloat: Spatio-Temporal Tracking and Topology: This cluster applies spatial tracking data, velocity vector fields, or persistent homology from Topological Data Analysis (TDA) to specific sports [1]. Representative examples include paper 2607.27838 (Vector field theory in soccer dynamics, marked UNREAD) and paper 2607.23509 (Topological Data Analysis for Tennis, marked UNREAD) [1].
Diagnostic Baselines and Sequence Null-Hypotheses: This cluster establishes simple stochastic baselines (such as independent Poisson processes or record-gap distributions) as diagnostic benchmarks before deploying complex predictive pipelines [1]. Core literature includes paper 2408.08331 (ML versus Poisson baselines), paper 1704.00197 (iWinRNFL diagnostics), and paper 2608.01882 (Gap distributions in cricket, marked UNREAD) [1].
Fights and Consensus in the Literature
A major structural disagreement in the literature centers on whether a global calibration layer can be applied across all sports versus fitting per-sport noise scales (sigma_game) [1]. The literature firmly establishes that global calibrators are mathematically flawed because signal-to-noise ratios vary wildly by domain [1]. In the NBA, talent dispersion and home advantage dominate game outcomes, whereas in MLB and NHL, inherent game-level randomness dominates, keeping outcomes close to coin-flips even between teams of mismatched skill [1].
A second methodological consensus involves the relationship between point-estimate calibration and conformal prediction intervals [1]. Conformal prediction guarantees interval coverage under sample exchangeability, but it cannot repair point-estimate bias [1]. If an underlying base model is severely miscalibrated, conformal wrappers yield prediction intervals that are unnecessarily wide or offset from the true conditional mean [1]. Consequently, base model specification and point calibration (such as optimizing directly under Brier loss) must precede conformal wrapping [1].
A third critical conflict concerns feature engineering and target leakage [2]. Incorporating closing betting lines directly as input features into team strength models leaks market efficiency into historical training sets, destroying the model's ability to measure true Closing Line Value (CLV) [2]. The literature establishes that pure performance forecasting models—trained on play-by-play, box scores, and efficiency metrics—must remain strictly decoupled from market pricing models designed to track odds movement and sharp liquidity [1, 2].

Detailed Evaluation of Triaged Claims
The spreadsheet was correct in elevating paper 1701.05976 as the architectural anchor [1]. By framing observation models around betting-market point spreads and totals-implied margins rather than raw points, the paper establishes a standardized framework across sports with different scoring mechanisms [1]. The sheet was also correct in prioritizing cheap diagnostic baselines—such as Poisson goal models (2408.08331) and iWinRNFL (1704.00197)—before building deep neural architectures [1].
However, relevance scoring suffered from severe keyword inflation [1]. Paper 2501.17711 was ranked "High" (Relevance Score 19) despite focusing on Olympic country medal counts using Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Networks (STGCN-LSTM), representing premature feature bloat for live sports betting [1]. Paper 1607.00379 received a top relevance score of 20, despite being a 2014 tutorial using frozen PyMC3 code (https://github.com/springcoil/TutorialPyMCRugby) on a Six Nations rugby dataset [1]. Furthermore, pushing topological data analysis (2607.23509) and soccer vector potential fields (2607.27838) when core probability engines struggle against simple mean absolute error (MAE) baselines reflects flawed engineering prioritization [1].
The sheet was thin regarding empirical verification [1]. Most papers in the dossier (such as 1911.01815) present log-score fits on niche leagues (e.g., Italian Superlega volleyball) but report zero Brier scores or Expected Calibration Error (ECE) metrics evaluated against liquid betting markets [1]. Additionally, while paper 1701.05976 covers NFL, NBA, MLB, and NHL, the triaged sheet failed to observe that Major League Soccer (MLS) parameters were completely omitted and require independent parameter estimation [1].

Core Methodological Disagreements and Mind-Shifts for Operators
Disagreement 1: Independent Per-Sport Models versus Shared Global Calibrators
Attempting to pass all sports through a single global calibration architecture is mathematically unsound [1]. In paper 1701.05976, the authors state:"Bayesian state-space model on betting-market point spreads / totals-implied margins, not raw scores, so the observation model is comparable across NFL/NBA/NHL/MLB. Team strength theta_{i,s,w} evolves: between seasons: AR(1) with variance sigma^2_between; within season: random walk with variance sigma^2_within." [1]
The empirical findings of 1701.05976 demonstrate that the NBA exhibits the largest talent dispersion and strongest home-court advantage, whereas MLB and NHL outcomes remain close to coin-flips given equal talent [1]. Because game-level noise (sigma_game) varies substantially between sports, each sport requires its own state-space parameterization and sport-specific calibration layer [1].
Disagreement 2: Decoupling Pure Performance Forecasting from Market Pricing Models
Fitting team strength models directly on market spreads or totals leaks market pricing efficiency into the performance feature space [1, 2]. To isolate Closing Line Value (CLV), operators must decouple the pure performance prediction engine (trained strictly on play-by-play, box scores, and drive efficiency) from the market pricing engine [1, 2]. Performance models evaluate underlying game dynamics, while market pricing models evaluate line movement and liquidity [1, 2].
Disagreement 3: Rules-Aware Generative Likelihoods versus Generic Machine Learning Heads
Fitting generic black-box classifiers (e.g., XGBoost, generic neural networks) directly to binary win/loss labels discards structural domain information [1]. In paper 1911.01815, the authors establish a rules-aware likelihood for volleyball:"Two-level hierarchical Bayesian model tailored to volleyball scoring rules. Level 1: logistic for set winner (team abilities + HA). Level 2, conditional on set winner: truncated negative binomial for loser's points; plus a Poisson inflation for extra points when the set goes beyond the 25/15..." [1]
Similarly, paper 1607.00379 models rugby scores via a log-linear Poisson likelihood capturing attack, defense, and home advantage parameters [1]. Incorporating sport-specific scoring rules directly into the likelihood function generates superior probability distributions compared to unconstrained classification heads [1].
Contradiction 1: Linear Quantile Regression Under-Coverage versus Conformal Validity
A fundamental contradiction exists between raw quantile regression outputs and conformalized bounds [2]. In Understanding the Under-Coverage Bias in Uncertainty Estimation (Lin, Trivedi, & Sun, 2021, arXiv:2106.05515), the authors prove:"We prove that linear quantile regression suffers from an inherent under-coverage bias... For alpha > 0.5 and small d/n, the alpha-quantile learned by quantile regression roughly achieves coverage alpha - (alpha - 0.5) * d/n regardless of the noise distribution." [2]
Un-conformalized quantile regression systematically under-covers in finite samples due to parameter estimation error proportional to d/n [2]. Conversely, Romano, Patterson, and Candes (2019, arXiv:1905.03222) demonstrate in Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR):"The conformity score on the calibration set is E_i = max(q_{alpha_lo}(X_i) - Y_i, Y_i - q_{alpha_hi}(X_i))... Conformalized quantile regression (CQR)... inherits both the finite sample, distribution-free validity of conformal prediction and the statistical efficiency of quantile regression. Under exchangeability, P(Y_{n+1} in C(X_{n+1})) >= 1 - alpha in finite samples." [2]
Operators must treat raw quantile outputs from neural networks or boosted trees as uncalibrated proposal distributions [2]. Every numeric line prediction must be processed through a conformal calibration step to guarantee valid finite-sample coverage [2].
Contradiction 2: Fail-Closed Refusal versus Quantile Clamping (Fake Tightness)
In small-sample calibration scenarios, theoretical guarantees conflict with heuristic production adjustments [2]. Angelopoulos and Bates (2021, arXiv:2107.07511) and Dietterich & Hostetler (2206.04860) state:"Compute q_hat as the ceil((n + 1)(1 - alpha)) / n empirical quantile of the calibration scores... The value of delta must be >= 1/(n+1) to ensure that ceil((1-delta)(n+1)) <= n... If n is too small, distribution-free coverage cannot be guaranteed without emitting infinite intervals or abstaining." [2]
In contrast, practical selective regression literature (e.g., Schröder et al., 2024, arXiv:2402.16300) suggests truncating or clamping quantiles to empirical maximums to keep systems operational [2]. Clamping quantiles under small sample sizes (n < ceil(1/alpha) - 1) violates distribution-free coverage proofs, generating under-coverage and "fake tightness" [2]. When calibration sample sizes are insufficient, automated systems must fail-closed by issuing a "No-Bet" or abstention decision rather than truncating intervals to force execution [2].
Contradiction 3: Binary Point Calibration Limits versus Venn-Abers Interval Bounds
In binary side markets (win probabilities), point-estimate calibration faces severe theoretical limits [2]. Barber, Candes, Ramdas, and Tibshirani (2020, Electronic Journal of Statistics) prove:"In the binary regression setting, any algorithm providing distribution-free coverage of the conditional label probability pi(X) = P(Y=1|X) must necessarily also cover the binary label Y... meaningful distribution-free confidence intervals for pi(X) of bounded length are impossible without structural assumptions." [2]
To address this limitation without introducing unverified assumptions, van der Laan and Alaa (2025, arXiv:2502.05676) propose Generalized Venn-Abers calibration:"Generalized Venn-Abers calibration extends Vovk's approach... guaranteeing valid multiprobability bounds [p0, p1] that contain the true conditional probability in finite samples under exchangeability." [2]
Operators should avoid presenting single binary point probabilities as guaranteed truths [2]. Instead, systems should utilize Venn-Abers multiprobability bounds [p0, p1], using the interval width (p1 - p0) to measure epistemic uncertainty for expected value (EV) calculations and selective gating [2].

Phase 1: Foundation and Diagnostic Baselines
Deploy independent per-sport state-space models using betting-market implied margins (1701.05976), explicitly estimating sport-specific noise parameters (sigma_game) [1]. Parameters for unmodeled leagues (such as MLS) must be estimated independently [1]. Establish mandatory diagnostic baselines—specifically Poisson models (2408.08331) and iWinRNFL (1704.00197)—to verify model skill prior to deploying complex architectures [1]. Implement state-transition validators (VERSA, 2601.21981) across data pipelines to filter out event-ordering errors before model ingestion [1].
Phase 2: Calibration and Point-Estimate Optimization
Transition model loss functions from binary cross-entropy (BCE) to Brier loss optimization (2508.02725) to fix base point-estimate calibration [1]. Decouple performance prediction models (trained on game play-by-play) from market pricing models to prevent target leakage and isolate Closing Line Value (CLV) [1, 2]. In sparse-data regimes (early season or minor leagues), integrate regularized adjusted plus-minus shrinkage (L-RAPM, 2601.15000) and rank-clustered BTL models (2501.02505) to pull noisy estimates toward player-level priors [1].
Phase 3: Conformal Prediction and Uncertainty Quantification
Wrap continuous numeric line models (player props, totals) in Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR, arXiv:1905.03222) to guarantee valid finite-sample coverage [2]. Enforce strict fail-closed refusal rules: when calibration sample sizes are inadequate (n < ceil(1/alpha) - 1), issue automated "No-Bet" decisions rather than applying arbitrary quantile clamping [2]. For binary win probabilities, adopt Generalized Venn-Abers calibration (arXiv:2502.05676) to output multiprobability bounds [p0, p1], utilizing bound width (p1 - p0) to measure epistemic uncertainty for execution gating [2].
Phase 4: Market Monitoring and Content Verification
Integrate state-space market dynamic monitoring (2605.30209) on live odds streams to detect abnormal line movements or integrity anomalies in real time [1]. Adopt claim-level verification architectures (Pitwall, 2607.06495) for automated content generation, decomposing text into factual assertions checked directly against live probabilistic engine states [1].
