## Correct core method

This is a **cross-book odds-discrepancy strategy**, not a new predictive model and not an opening-versus-closing CLV study. From 32 bookmakers the authors define consensus probability as **1 / mean(decimal odds)** for each home/draw/away outcome. This is not the mean of inverse odds and not standard proportional three-way de-vigging.

They compare that consensus with outcome frequency (80 bins of width 0.0125, minimum 100 observations/bin). An approximately additive commission correction motivates `p_real ≈ p_cons − alpha`. Outcome-specific fitted offsets are **0.034 home, 0.057 draw, 0.037 away**. The operational rule is **max(odds) > 1/(p_cons − 0.05)**, after examining alpha from 0.01 to 0.1; the 0.05 choice is empirically tuned, not an untouched out-of-sample theorem. Expected unit profit is `p_real * offered_decimal_odds − 1` (equations 1–7).

Closing-odds simulation is followed by minute-level odds simulation, paper trading and real-money execution with a human operator. The strategy fixes stake sizes, does not require more accurate forecasts than bookmakers, and is exposed to availability/latency and account limits. The random benchmark also chooses the highest available price; it is not simply a bookmaker-random-price straw man.

## What this does and does not say about CLV versus accuracy

It demonstrates a route to positive historical returns using market probabilities and favorable **offered prices**, without building a more accurate forecasting model. Its strongest simulation places bets **at closing odds**; this can have cross-book price advantage without proving that an earlier locked price subsequently improved relative to a defined closing benchmark.

It reports hit rates and ROI, **not per-pick Brier scores, CLV–Brier correlation, or a theorem that CLV predicts long-run profit**. Calibration, directional accuracy, temporal CLV, cross-book price shopping and execution-adjusted profit are different quantities. A strategy with sub-50% win rate can be profitable at sufficiently long prices; raw accuracy is not the objective here.
