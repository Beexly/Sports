## Exact core model

### Decision problem (§2, Eqs. 2.1–2.4)

A binary outcome `theta in {0,1}` is known at calendar horizon `S`. Given information filtration `G_s`, posterior belief is

`Pi_s=P(theta=1 | G_s)`.

It is a bounded martingale, not merely any bounded or empirically calibrated probability curve. A decision consists of stopping time `nu<=S` and declaration `d in D`, a compact subset of [0,1]. General Bayes risk:

```text
inf_(nu,d) E[ integral_0^nu f(s) ds + ell(theta,d) ]
h(x,d) = x ell(1,d) + (1-x) ell(0,d)
g(x) = inf_(d in D) h(x,d)
j(x) in argmin_(d in D) h(x,d)
=> inf_(nu<=S) E[ integral_0^nu f(s) ds + g(Pi_nu) ]
```

A quoted binary market price is only a motivating proxy, abstracting away frictions, strategic effects and risk premia. The paper **takes the win-martingale as given**; it does not estimate one from sports prices or prove prices are true posteriors.

Diffusion restriction:

`dPi_s = rho(s) sigma(Pi_s) dW_s`, `Pi_0=x in (0,1)`.

Volatility is separable into deterministic time and state factors. There is no drift term. The canonical models all use `rho(s)=1/sqrt(S-s)`:

| Model | State volatility sigma(x) |
|---|---|
| Aldous | `sin(pi*x)/pi` |
| Bass | `phi(Phi^-1(x))` |
| Binary sequential inference | `x(1-x)` |

Here `phi/Phi` are standard-normal density/CDF; constant mathematical pi is distinct from belief Pi. The Aldous process's variational “most exciting” status is motivation from earlier work, not an empirically fitted sports noise model introduced here.

### Time transformation (Eqs. 2.5–2.9)

```text
A(s) = integral_0^s rho(u)^2 du
Gamma = A^-1;  T = lim_(s->S) A(s)
X_t = Pi_Gamma(t)
dX_t = sigma(X_t) dB_t
c(t) = f(Gamma(t))*Gamma'(t) = f(Gamma(t))/rho(Gamma(t))^2
V_T(t,x) = inf_(tau<=T-t) E[ integral_0^tau c(t+u)du + g(X_tau^x) ]
```

For canonical `rho`: `A(s)=-log((S-s)/S)`, `Gamma(t)=S(1-exp(-t))`, so **finite calendar horizon maps to infinite operational time**. Consequently it is the **cost-to-noise ratio `f/rho^2`**, not the original running cost alone, that determines the direction of boundary changes.

Squared-error probability reporting gives `j(x)=x`, `g(x)=x(1-x)`; cross-entropy reporting gives `j(x)=x`, `g(x)=-x log(x)-(1-x)log(1-x)`. These are terminal **decision losses**, not sportsbook payoff functions.

### Exact assumptions and scope (§3.2)

Baseline A:

- `sigma` continuous on [0,1], locally Lipschitz in (0,1), positive inside and zero exactly at endpoints; locally integrable inverse square and the double-integral divergence in Eq. 3.4 ensure boundaries cannot be reached in finite operational time.
- Positive `c in C^1`, with `|c'/c|<=K`.
- Nonnegative `g`, continuous on [0,1], `C^2` inside, concave and zero exactly at endpoints.
- `L g`, with `L=(sigma^2/2) d^2/dx^2`, strictly decreases then increases around an interior minimum `x0`.
- `L g(x0)+c(t)<0`, giving nonempty continuation.

Extended B for the main time-inhomogeneous theorem: additionally `sigma in C^2`, `g in C^3` inside; strict derivative signs for `L g`; either bounded `g'` or a positive uniform lower bound for `c`; **symmetry about 1/2** of sigma, g and Lg; true-martingale stochastic-flow derivative on finite intervals.

The general stop/declaration reduction includes hard classification, but its kinked `min(x,1-x)` loss is not automatically covered by the main smooth-loss Assumption B results. Similarly, asymmetric decision costs require an extension of the symmetric-boundary analysis, not plugging into `1-b,b` unchanged. Early absorption at 0/1 in finite operational time (e.g. Wright–Fisher, absorbed Brownian motion) is **explicitly excluded and left for future work** (§9). Real in-play prices also jump, suspend and have transaction frictions; none is established as satisfying this diffusion setup.

## Exact reported results

### Structural results (§4)

- **Theorem 4.1:** continuation region `C_T={(t,x):1-b_T(t)<x<b_T(t)}`; first exit (capped by `T-t` for finite horizon) is optimal. `gamma(t)<b_T(t)<1`, where gamma delineates the negative `c+Lg` region.
- **Theorem 4.2:** value function is globally `C^1` on the preterminal domain and `C^(1,2)` off the free boundary; smooth fit holds.
- **Corollary 4.3:** second spatial derivative is **not continuous across the boundary**; inside continuation `V_xx=-2(c+V_t)/sigma^2`, in the interior of the stopping set `V_xx=g''`.
- **Corollary 4.4:** `b_T in C^1([0,T))`, not an unconditional claim of terminal-time convergence or smoothness.
- **Theorem 4.5:** boundary uniquely characterized by the nonlinear integral equation below, among continuous candidate boundaries with `gamma<beta<1`.

For `x=b_T(t)` or `1-b_T(t)`, Eq. 4.2 is

```text
g(x) = E[ g(X_(T-t)^x)
          + integral_0^(T-t) c(t+u) 1[X_u^x in (1-b_T(t+u),b_T(t+u))] du
          - integral_0^(T-t) Lg(X_u^x) 1[X_u^x outside that interval] du ]
```

For infinite operational horizon, terminal `g(X_infinity)=0` and integrals use the stated limiting interpretation. The homogeneous infinite-horizon case has a fixed continuation interval under its assumptions (§5), not an arbitrary fixed confidence threshold universal across models/costs.
