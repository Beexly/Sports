## Correct core method

### Linear margin model, not a generic probability calibration map

For home `u`, away `v`:

```text
Y_uv = mu + theta_u - theta_v + epsilon_uv       (Eq. 16)
Y_uvw = x_uvw' beta + epsilon_uvw                (Eq. 17)
```

`Y` is observed **margin of victory**; `mu` is home-court advantage, omitted for neutral venues. One team's strength is the zero baseline for identifiability. Team strengths are fit by least squares and **constant within a season** in the main analysis, then held fixed after the regular season. Index `w` indicates the observation period, not an estimated dynamic latent-strength process. Week-varying strengths/fused-lasso penalty in Eq. 23 are **future work**.

### Full augmented-sample conformal predictive distribution

For each candidate margin `y_c`, append `(x_new,y_c)` to the observed dataset and **refit the same regression**. Compute **signed** residuals for all `n+1` points, including the new point:

```text
R_i(y_c) = y_i - yhat_i(y_c)
pi(y_c,tau) = [sum_(i=1)^(n+1) { 1[R_i(y_c)<R_(n+1)(y_c)]
                              + tau*1[R_i(y_c)=R_(n+1)(y_c)] }]/(n+1)
```

This is Eq. 11 (Eq. 18 is its sports-period version). The abstract smoothed construction has `tau~Uniform(0,1)`; **all applied CPDs in this paper set tau=1/2**, a mid-p convention. Home win probability is **`1-pi(0,1/2)`**, not `pi(0,1/2)`. A full CPD also estimates threshold probabilities for spreads/margins. The paper says moving its near-continuous support to nonzero integers does not alter the reported win probability; the reassignment details are omitted.

**Critical dossier correction:** D35 prints a sum only through `n` and leaves the tie term malformed/free-indexed. The sum must include **all `n+1` points and all ties**; the new candidate is always tied with itself. The missing self-tie alone loses `tau/(n+1)`. A Python illustration with augmented residuals `[-2,0,2,0]` gives correct CPD **.500**, versus **.375** if the candidate is omitted. This is an illustrative formula check, not paper data.

This is **not split conformal with a frozen margin model and an empirical residual CDF**. Such a computationally cheaper variant can be studied, but must be labeled an adaptation and compared against augmented-refit CPD. GSE's absolute residual manager loses the residual sign and cannot directly serve as a CPD store.

### Baselines and guarantees

- Linear-normal comparator: same design, independent normal mean-zero errors, Student-t predictive tail including `sqrt(1+x_new'(X'X)^-1 x_new)` and estimated residual scale (Eqs. 14,19).
- Logistic comparator: binary home-win response and `logit(p)=x'beta` (Eqs. 15,20).
- Eq. 13 states marginal prediction-region coverage under exchangeability. Neither that statement nor empirical calibration improvement proves conditional per-game probability calibration, reduced Brier/ECE for an arbitrary GSE model, or future betting profitability. Fixing tau at one half should not be described as retaining every exact randomized-Uniform PIT property.
- Sports scheduling, repeated teams and evolving strengths make exchangeability questionable. Temporal splits prevent look-ahead; they do **not themselves establish exchangeability**. The article's main exchangeability assumption concerns the augmented observations, not merely a residual window deemed similar.

### Tournament mechanics (another distinct contribution)

For known matchup probabilities and fixed bracket, championship/round advancement is computed recursively:

`q_(u,J) = q_(u,J-1) * sum_(s in O_(u,J)) p_us q_(s,J-1)` (Eq. 3).

The paper attributes this to **Edwards (1991)**, not “David.” Field-selection probabilities combine conference champion/automatic bids with at-large selection by fixed team rank. Across independent conferences, the number of automatic bids won by teams below team `u` is **Poisson-binomial** (Eqs. 4–8); account for the intersection with `u` winning its own conference. This assumes a rank-based selection rule, not the real selection committee's full subjective process. Exact conditional bracket probabilities avoid tournament simulation error; they do not eliminate uncertainty about the missing 2020 bracket or the estimated game probabilities.

### Exact sampler/settings from the paper

**No MCMC, burn-in, chains, thinning or posterior sampler** in this method. Ordinary least-squares augmented refits and analytical bracket calculations are used. Applied `tau=1/2`; reliability bins have width **.025** (§4.4). The random third exemplar bracket is sampled using conference win probabilities, but the paper does not specify an RNG seed or Monte Carlo repetition count for it; no invented settings.

Separately, verified public `generate_cpd_ncaa.R` uses `lm(score~., data=aug_data)`, both comparisons summed over the augmented residual array, default candidate grid **201 points from -50 to 50**, `tau=1/2`, **8 worker threads**, and `set.seed(2020)`. These are **repository defaults, not additional settings claimed to appear in the paper**. The candidate grid is for plotting/full CPDs; evaluating win probability needs the zero threshold, not a large grid or stochastic sampling.
