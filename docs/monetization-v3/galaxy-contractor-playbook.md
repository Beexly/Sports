# Galaxy Contractor Playbook

**Audience:** Garrett. Internal-leaning, but shareable with any contractor in the engagement-onboarding phase.

**Purpose:** How Galaxy works with 1099 contractors — the people who scale Galaxy's editorial, design, legal, and BD capacity without becoming full-time staff. Templates + scope-fit filter + onboarding script + when-to-end-the-engagement protocol.

**When this matters:** Galaxy is single-operator V1. The 1099 contractor relationships ramp up in Q3 2026 (Almanac production) and Q4 2026 (Live BD). Most contractors are short-term (1099 project basis); a few become long-term (annual relationship).

## The contractor roles Galaxy will engage

Listed in order of when they activate during the v3 plan.

### Role 1 — Cover designer (Almanac)

**When activates:** August 2026 (Almanac production kickoff).
**Engagement type:** Project basis. Single annual relationship if quality holds.
**Budget:** $5,000–$8,000.
**Timeline:** ~6 weeks (brief → 3 concepts → revisions → final files).
**Output:** Print-ready PDF + Adobe Illustrator source files for hardcover + dust jacket.

**Why this hire:** the Almanac cover is the difference between a $99 book buyers feel ashamed to display and one they put on the shelf. Cannot be skipped.

### Role 2 — Copyeditor (Almanac + ongoing)

**When activates:** September 2026 (Almanac production mid-stage).
**Engagement type:** Project basis for Almanac ($1,000–$2,000); optional retainer for ongoing Galaxy editorial review.
**Budget:** $30–$50/hour. 40–60 hours for the Almanac.
**Output:** Copyedited chapters + style sheet documenting Galaxy-specific conventions.

**Why this hire:** Garrett writes well but should not be the final reader of his own work. A copyeditor catches consistency issues, voice drift, and accidental brand-safety violations.

### Role 3 — Layout designer (Almanac)

**When activates:** November 2026 (Almanac final production).
**Engagement type:** Project basis.
**Budget:** $3,000–$5,000.
**Timeline:** ~4 weeks.
**Output:** Print-ready layout file in Adobe InDesign (or Affinity Publisher); EPUB/PDF digital exports.

**Why this hire:** the Almanac is 300 pages. Self-designing in InDesign produces a recognizably amateur book. The layout designer makes the book read at the production tier the brand position requires.

### Role 4 — Lawyer (founding partner agreements + general)

**When activates:** late 2026 (Live partnership conversations) OR earlier if any regulatory question surfaces.
**Engagement type:** Project basis for partner agreement template ($2,000–$5,000); hourly retainer for ad-hoc legal questions.
**Budget:** $250–$500/hour.
**Output:** Convert `copy/live-founding-partner-agreement-template.md` into binding contract; review brand-safety constraints; advise on terms-of-service language for Vault.

**Why this hire:** Galaxy can't sign founding-partner contracts without a lawyer. Soft attempts cost more than the lawyer's fee.

### Role 5 — BD consultant (Live track, Sketch outreach)

**When activates:** if Sketch warm-intro audit fails after 2-week soft search.
**Engagement type:** Project basis, 90-day engagement.
**Budget:** $10,000–$25,000.
**Output:** Warm-intro path to Sketch within 90 days, OR equivalent tier-1 streamer relationship.

**Why this hire:** Sketch's brand-leverage value justifies the cost. Garrett's network may not have the right connections; a BD consultant brings the network.

### Role 6 — PR consultant (Vault launch press cycle, optional)

**When activates:** week of Vault public launch.
**Engagement type:** 30-day engagement.
**Budget:** $5,000–$10,000.
**Output:** Press outreach to Tier 1-2 outlets per `copy/vault-launch-press-pack.md`; placement coordination; embargoed-announcement orchestration.

**Why this hire:** optional. If Garrett has bandwidth to run press outreach himself, skip. If launch month is too busy, hiring a PR consultant pays for itself in 1-2 quality placements.

### Role 7 — Community manager (Vault scale)

**When activates:** Vault hits 1,000 paid members (target: month 12 of Vault operations).
**Engagement type:** Part-time 1099 → eventually possible part-time W-2.
**Budget:** $2,000–$5,000/month part-time.
**Output:** Vault Discord moderation + member support escalation + retention-email writing assistance.

**Why this hire:** at 1,000 members, Garrett's bandwidth is insufficient. Community manager scales support without breaking brand voice.

### Role 8 — Editorial assistant (Almanac + ongoing)

**When activates:** Year 2 if Almanac sells 5,000+ hardcover.
**Engagement type:** Part-time 1099.
**Budget:** $2,000–$4,000/month.
**Output:** Research support for Almanac essays + Vault digest follow-up + supporting essay drafts.

**Why this hire:** Year-2 editorial volume justifies the support. Year-1 doesn't.

## The scope-fit filter (before any engagement)

Before engaging any contractor, Garrett runs the brand-position filter:

### Step 1 — Send the operating values doc

Galaxy's operating values (`galaxy-operating-values.md`) get sent before the engagement conversation. The contractor reads it.

### Step 2 — The honest reaction question

In the kickoff call, Garrett asks:

> "Which of the five values would be hardest for you to operate under?"

**The right answer:** the contractor picks a specific value, explains why it would be hard, then explains how they'd operate under it anyway.

**The wrong answer:** "I love all five." That's the contractor who'll drift toward marketing-speak, founder-personality framing, or volume-maximizing output.

**The clearly-wrong answer:** "Some of these seem extreme — let's see how it goes." That contractor will eventually push back on the brand position. Don't hire.

### Step 3 — The specific examples question

Garrett asks: "Walk me through a past project where you worked on something brand-sensitive. What did you do when the client wanted you to drift?"

The right contractor has stories. The wrong contractor doesn't have a coherent answer.

### Step 4 — Decision

After the kickoff call:

- If brand-aligned + skills-aligned → engage.
- If skills-aligned but brand-misaligned → don't engage. Galaxy's brand position is the moat; misaligned contractors weaken it.
- If brand-aligned but skills-misaligned → consider engaging at lower scope as a trial; expand if the work holds.

## The engagement contract structure

Each contractor engagement has a standard contract shape. Lawyer converts into binding language per relationship.

### Standard 1099 contract template (outline)

```
GALAXY SPORTS EDGE — INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR AGREEMENT

Cover sheet:
- Contractor name + address
- Galaxy contact: Garrett Baxley, [Galaxy address]
- Effective date
- Engagement type: [project basis / hourly / retainer]
- Estimated duration

Section 1 — Scope of work
- Specific deliverables (list)
- Specific milestones (list with dates)
- Specific exclusions (what this engagement does NOT include)

Section 2 — Compensation
- Fee structure (flat fee / hourly / milestone-based)
- Payment schedule (50% upfront + 50% on delivery / monthly / on milestone)
- Galaxy's standard reimbursement policy (preapproved expenses only)

Section 3 — Brand alignment requirement
- Contractor agrees to operate under Galaxy's brand position
- Contractor agrees to brand-safety vocabulary rules (per attached `galaxy-brand-voice-canonical.md`)
- Contractor agrees not to publicly characterize Galaxy in any way the contractor would not also publicly characterize their other clients (parity test)

Section 4 — Intellectual property
- Work product is Galaxy's property upon payment
- Contractor retains the right to portfolio-display non-confidential work
- Contractor does not retain rights to Galaxy's methodology, factor weightings, or member data

Section 5 — Confidentiality
- Galaxy's internal data + member identities + factor weights confidential
- Confidentiality survives engagement for 2 years
- Permitted disclosures: legal advisors, family for support purposes only

Section 6 — Termination
- Either party may terminate with 14 days notice
- Termination for cause (material breach) is immediate
- On termination: contractor delivers all in-progress work + receives prorated payment

Section 7 — Independent contractor
- Standard 1099 language
- Contractor is not Galaxy employee
- Contractor handles own taxes, insurance, equipment

Section 8 — Brand-safety enforcement
- If contractor produces work that violates Galaxy brand-safety vocabulary, Galaxy may require correction at no additional cost
- Repeated brand-safety violations (3+ instances on the same engagement) = grounds for immediate termination

Signatures + Date
```

Each engagement adapts this shape. Lawyer's one-time review locks the boilerplate.

## Onboarding script (Day 1 of any engagement)

When a contractor signs the engagement contract, Garrett sends this onboarding email:

```
Subject: Welcome aboard — Galaxy [Engagement Name]

Hey [first name],

Glad to have you. Three things in this onboarding email:

1. Materials. Below are the documents you should read before our kickoff call. ~45 minutes of reading.

   - galaxy-operating-values.md — the five values (4 min read).
   - galaxy-brand-voice-canonical.md — voice rules + banned vocabulary (15 min).
   - [Engagement-specific brief — e.g., cover-design brief from almanac-production-pack.md, or partner agreement template, etc.]

   If anything in there is unclear or feels weird, flag it before the kickoff call. The brand position is restrictive on purpose; better to surface friction now than at delivery.

2. Communication. I'm at garrett@galaxysportsedge.com for everything. Default response time is 24 hours during business days. If something's urgent (genuine deadline issue), put "URGENT" in the subject and I'll come up to same-day.

3. Process. I work in 90-minute peak blocks Monday–Tuesday mornings and reserve Saturday off. Don't expect Saturday responses. Wednesday digest goes out Wednesday morning so that day is also peak. Plan to need me for review/feedback on Thursday or Friday afternoons.

For the kickoff call: [proposed Calendly link or 2-3 specific time slots].

Welcome.

— Garrett
```

The onboarding email is intentionally informal but specific. Contractors don't want a 5-page handbook on Day 1; they want to know how the relationship works.

## Working relationship — operating norms

### Communication cadence

- **Email is the primary channel.** Slack / Discord / WhatsApp for ongoing contractors only after 30 days of relationship.
- **Response time:** 24 business hours from Garrett's side. Contractor expected to match.
- **Weekly check-in (project-based contractors):** 30-minute video call every Friday morning during active engagement.
- **Quarterly check-in (ongoing contractors):** 60-minute call to review the relationship, raise concerns, plan next quarter.

### Deliverable handoffs

- **Drafts → review cycle.** Contractor delivers a draft. Garrett reviews within 24-48 hours. Comments inline. Contractor revises. Maximum 3 review rounds before scope creep becomes a separate conversation.
- **Final files in agreed format.** Cover designer delivers print-ready PDF + source files. Layout designer delivers InDesign / Publisher source + exports. Copyeditor delivers tracked-changes Word doc.
- **No deliverables go to Galaxy public surfaces without Garrett's explicit approval.** Even if the contractor is publishing on Galaxy's behalf.

### Payment

- **Net 14 days from invoice.** Galaxy pays promptly.
- **No work without contract.** If a contractor wants to start before paperwork is signed, that's a brand-position-misaligned signal.
- **Milestone-based vs hourly:** project-basis engagements prefer milestones (cleaner accountability). Ongoing engagements prefer monthly hourly.
- **Tax compliance:** Galaxy issues 1099-NEC for any contractor receiving more than $600/calendar year.

## When the engagement isn't working

### Soft signals (one each is fine; two together is concerning)

- Contractor missed first deadline without proactive communication.
- Contractor pushed back on a brand-safety constraint as "too restrictive."
- Contractor's first draft contained banned vocabulary.
- Contractor characterized the engagement publicly in a way Garrett wouldn't approve.
- Contractor introduced Galaxy to others as "their client" in a way that overstates the relationship.

### Hard signals (any one is grounds for termination)

- Contractor published Galaxy-confidential information.
- Contractor used Galaxy's brand or work in a context Galaxy doesn't endorse.
- Contractor refused to apply a brand-safety correction Galaxy requested.
- Contractor produced work that misrepresents Galaxy's product or position.
- Contractor billed for work that wasn't delivered or wasn't delivered to spec.

### Termination conversation script

```
Subject: Ending our engagement

Hey [first name],

This is a hard email to write. I'm ending our engagement effective [date].

Specific reason: [one or two sentences, factual, not blame-y].

What happens next:
- I'll pay for work completed through [date].
- Please deliver any in-progress materials by [date].
- The confidentiality terms of our contract continue per the agreement.

If there's a way to wrap this professionally, I'd like that. If there's feedback I should hear about how Galaxy could have engaged better, I'm open.

Best,
Garrett
```

The script is direct without being hostile. Galaxy's brand position requires the same restraint in difficult conversations as in publishing.

### When to consider re-engaging a past contractor

Some engagements end because of timing or scope, not fit. Those contractors can be re-engaged for future projects.

**Re-engage if:**
- Previous work was high quality.
- Termination was timing/budget, not brand-alignment.
- 6+ months have passed.
- Galaxy's needs match the contractor's skills again.

**Don't re-engage if:**
- Previous work required ≥3 brand-safety corrections.
- Termination was for misrepresenting Galaxy publicly.
- Contractor characterized the past engagement negatively on social or in interviews.

```
GALAXY SPORTS EDGE — COPYEDITOR ENGAGEMENT BRIEF

What we need:
- Copyediting of the 2026 Galaxy Almanac (~300 pages, ~70,000 words).

- Headline conventions.

Engagement scope:
- Almanac copyediting: October 1 – November 30, 2026. 40-60 hours total.
- Ongoing retainer (optional): 5-10 hours/month.

Deliverables:
- Copyedited Word doc with tracked changes.
- Galaxy style sheet (Google Doc, owned by Galaxy).

Brand-safety requirements:
- All edits respect Galaxy brand-safety vocabulary rules.
- Suggested rewrites do not introduce banned vocabulary.
- Style sheet documents (does not invent) Galaxy conventions.

Not in scope:
- Substantive rewriting (that's Garrett's work).
- Strategic voice changes (those need decision-log entries).
- Content suggestion (this is line-edit work, not editorial direction).
```

## What this playbook deliberately doesn't include

1. **No formal interview process.** Galaxy is single-operator. Contractor engagement is conversation-based, not panel-interview.
2. **No long-term staffing plan.** Galaxy doesn't plan staff growth Year 1.
3. **No equity grants for contractors.** Galaxy is bootstrapped; contractors get 1099 payment only.
4. **No exclusivity clauses by default.** Contractors can work for competitors unless their specific Galaxy work is competitive-position-sensitive (BD consultant, lawyer with privileged information).
5. **No NDAs for routine engagements.** Standard contract confidentiality covers most cases. NDAs only for engagements where the contractor would see member-identifying data.
6. **No formal performance review process.** Project-basis contractors are reviewed at engagement end; ongoing contractors at quarterly check-ins.
