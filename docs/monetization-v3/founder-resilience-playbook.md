# Founder Resilience Playbook

**Audience:** Garrett. Private. Not for public Galaxy surfaces.

**Why this document exists:** Galaxy's biggest single-point-of-failure isn't competitor risk, isn't market risk, isn't engineering risk. It's the operator burning out, spiraling on critique, or losing the brand-position discipline under pressure. This playbook is the operator's protection against that failure mode.

**The plan was authored at:** 2026-05-22 — three days after Garrett was terminated from prior employment, monetizing Galaxy under time pressure. That context shapes the playbook.

---

## The honest framing

A single-founder company optimizing for brand position over scale is a hard mode of operating. The operator carries:

- All editorial output (digests, autopsies, methodology updates).
- All commercial decisions (pricing, partnerships, refunds).
- All community moderation (Discord, office hours).
- All press response.
- All product direction.
- All anxiety from KPIs that move slowly.

Galaxy's brand position adds difficulty: the operator can't take shortcuts that other founders take. No clickbait. No hype. No false urgency. No founder-personality TikTok arc. The discipline costs energy.

The playbook below acknowledges that cost and structures the work to protect against it.

---

## Sleep, food, exercise — the floor

Before any operational discipline, the floor:

- **Sleep:** 7+ hours nightly. Non-negotiable. Galaxy's quality of judgment is a derivative of Garrett's sleep.
- **Food:** Three meals. Don't skip the middle of the day to "save time." Skipped meals cost the afternoon's quality of work.
- **Exercise:** 3–4 sessions/week, even 30 minutes. Not for fitness — for brain clarity. Run, walk, lift, anything that moves the body away from the screen.
- **No screens within 60 minutes of sleep.** Brain stays in operating mode if it does. Tomorrow morning's quality of work depends on this hour tonight.

If any of the four slip for more than 3 consecutive days, Garrett resets the routine before resuming Galaxy work. The reset is the discipline. Pushing through is not.

## Calendar protection rules

The default founder calendar fills with reactive work. Galaxy's discipline depends on calendar protection.

### Protected blocks (non-negotiable, recurring)

| When | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mon–Fri 09:00–11:00 | Deep work (digest writing, autopsy authoring) | Best brain hours go to the work that compounds |
| Mon–Fri 11:00–12:00 | Email + Discord scan | Single window for reactive work |
| Mon–Fri 13:00–14:00 | Lunch + walk | Off-screen. Don't compress this. |
| Tuesday 19:30–21:00 (monthly OH weeks) | Office hours + buffer | Galaxy's biggest live commitment |
| Friday 16:00–18:00 | Weekly retrospective + KPI ritual (last Friday of month) | Closes the week with intention |
| Saturday 00:00–23:59 | Off | Garrett does not check Galaxy on Saturdays |

### Anti-patterns (the calendar mistakes to refuse)

- "Quick podcast tomorrow" — Garrett blocks podcast appearances to specific days (Wednesday afternoons), not "whenever asked." Reactive scheduling = reactive thinking.
- "Lunch Zoom with a partner contact" — partner meetings happen at 14:30–15:30 only. Lunch stays sacred.
- "Just one more email" at 23:00 — refused. Tomorrow morning's quality depends on tonight's sleep.
- Press inquiries getting same-day response — standard is 24 hours, not 5 minutes. Galaxy's brand is built on consistency, not reactivity.

## Anti-spiral protocol (for when critique lands hard)

Galaxy publishes losses publicly. Vault Discord allows direct member pushback. Press will sometimes write things Garrett dislikes. Critique is a feature of the operating model.

The spiral fails the operator. The protocol below prevents the spiral.

### When critique arrives — the 4-step protocol

**Step 1 (within 5 minutes of reading the critique): Read it once. Walk away.**

Don't reply. Don't reread. Don't process. Walk away from the screen for 20 minutes minimum. The first emotional response is never the brand-aligned response.

**Step 2 (after the walk-away): Categorize the critique.**

Sort into one of three buckets:

- **Substantive critique with a specific fix:** Member says "the digest format is too long." Real signal. Worth engaging.
- **Substantive critique without a specific fix:** Member says "Vault doesn't feel like what I expected." Real signal but needs Garrett's interpretation. Worth engaging carefully.
- **Non-substantive critique:** Trolls, brand-violating language, demands for things Galaxy explicitly doesn't do (more picks, win-rate boasts, hype). Worth acknowledging but not engaging.

**Step 3: Respond, or don't, by category.**

- Substantive with fix → respond same-day with brand-aligned acknowledgment + action plan.
- Substantive without fix → respond within 24 hours with curiosity question to surface the underlying read.
- Non-substantive → response template: "Appreciate you taking the time to write. Not the direction Galaxy is going." Done.

**Step 4: Log it.**

Every meaningful critique goes into `templates/critique-log.md` (private, owner-only once copied into the live review archive). Pattern recognition over months matters more than individual instances. Three independent critiques surfacing the same issue = pattern. One critique = anecdote.

### The harder critique: when the brand position itself gets challenged

Sometimes a critique attacks the brand position itself: "Galaxy is too restrained — you'd grow faster if you published more." Or: "The Loss Room is performative humility."

**These are the highest-stakes critiques.** They test whether the operator believes the brand position.

The protocol:

1. Don't defend reflexively. Sit with the critique for 24 hours.
2. Ask: would Galaxy's restraint actually be better off being abandoned?
3. If yes — write a decision-log entry documenting the pivot. (This has never happened. It probably never will. But the option is real.)
4. If no — write a 200-word response that demonstrates the brand position rather than asserts it. Then move on.

The brand position is not Galaxy's accident. It's the bet. Critiques that challenge it are part of the bet's stress test.

## Energy management across the week

Galaxy's work isn't homogenous. Different work requires different energy. Match the work to the energy band.

| Energy band | Work that fits |
|---|---|
| **Peak (Mon–Tue mornings)** | Digest authoring, autopsy synthesis, methodology page revisions. The work that requires Galaxy's highest brain. |
| **Medium (Mon–Wed afternoons)** | Email replies, Discord engagement, content review, light press. Reactive work that requires judgment but not creation. |
| **Lower (Thu–Fri)** | Operational work: admin cockpit, billing review, KPI dashboard reads, partnership outreach drafting. Repetitive work that has structure. |
| **Low (Fri afternoon)** | Friday retrospective, planning next week. Reflective work that doesn't require creation. |
| **Reserve (Sun evening)** | Soft planning. 30 minutes max. Look at the week ahead. Pre-load Monday's work. |

**Don't write a digest on Friday afternoon.** Don't reply to a hostile press email at 23:00. Don't do partnership outreach in the morning. Match the work to the band.

If a peak-band block gets eaten by reactive work (urgent email, partnership conversation that runs long), the digest gets pushed to next morning. Galaxy doesn't ship a Wednesday digest authored at Tuesday 18:00 — the quality won't hold.

## Weekly retrospective (every Friday 16:00–17:00)

A one-hour weekly retrospective. Closes the week.

### What goes in the retrospective doc

```
Week of: 2026-MM-DD

## What I shipped this week
- Wednesday digest: [topic]
- Discord posts: [count]
- Press outreach sent: [count]
- Methodology updates: [if any]
- Other: [autopsy, pass-list entries, etc.]

## What I learned this week
- About the model: [1-3 sentences]
- About Galaxy members: [1-3 sentences]
- About Galaxy as a business: [1-3 sentences]

## What didn't work this week
- [Specific thing that should have shipped and didn't]
- [Reaction that wasn't brand-aligned]
- [Decision I'd reconsider]

## Energy check
- 7-day sleep average: [hours/night]
- Stress level Mon-Fri: [low/med/high per day, single word]
- Anti-spiral protocol invoked: [yes/no, details]

## Next week's three priorities
1. [single most important deliverable]
2. [second most important]
3. [third]

Each priority gets specific calendar time blocked next week.

## Personal kill criteria check
- Vault paid members: [N] / target threshold for current scenario
- If within 10% of personal kill criterion for runway scenario, flag for explicit thought.
```

Save as `retrospectives/2026-MM-DD.md`. Reread the prior week's retrospective at the start of each session.

### What the retrospective protects against

- Forgetting what was learned by the time it's actionable.
- Drifting toward easier work each week.
- Missing the slow build of a personal-kill scenario.
- Suppressing the energy check (sleep is the floor — make it visible weekly).

## When to take a day off

A single operator can't take "every weekend" off without the work compounding behind. But the operator MUST take days off — burnout is the larger risk than throughput.

### The acceptable cadence

- **Saturdays:** OFF every week, no exceptions for the first 12 months. Saturday is the brain-rest day. Even when something feels urgent.
- **Sundays:** 30-minute pre-load for Monday acceptable. Otherwise off.
- **One full weekend off per month:** Saturday AND Sunday. Picked at the start of each month and protected.
- **One full week off per quarter:** Vacation. Email auto-responder set. Vault digest pre-drafted if it would land during the week. Discord has a pinned "Garrett is offline this week — replies resume [date]" note.

If Galaxy is at a personal-kill-criterion threshold during a planned week off → the week off still happens. Burnout doesn't make the kill criterion go away; it makes it harder to navigate.

### The exception (rarely invoked)

A genuine P0 emergency — Vault Discord experiences a major incident, a press cycle requires same-day response on a story that's actively being written, the engineering system experiences a payment-related outage — can override the Saturday-off discipline for that one day.

After: take the following Saturday off plus Sunday morning. Restore the routine.

## The "you're not your KPIs" rule

Galaxy's KPIs (Vault member count, retention, NPS) will be slow for months at a time. The operator's emotional state will mirror them unless Garrett intervenes.

The rule: **the KPI dashboard does not define how Garrett feels about Galaxy.**

When KPIs are mid-band and emotionally hard:
- Reread `audit/kpi-operator-ritual.md` § "What this document does NOT do — the KPI dashboard does not validate or invalidate the brand position."
- Look at member quotes from `templates/vault-feedback-themes.csv` — qualitative reminders the work matters.
- Reread the master plan v3 § "the bet, restated honestly" — Galaxy is a multi-year compounding bet.

When KPIs are above-band and emotionally good:
- Don't celebrate publicly. Brand-position violation.
- Don't relax the discipline. Galaxy's brand depth is built in slow good months and slow bad months equally.
- Note the win in the weekly retrospective. Then move on.

KPIs are inputs. They're not the operator's mirror.

## When to ask for help

Garrett is a single founder. Asking for help is part of the operating discipline, not a sign of weakness.

### Categories of help to ask for

**Tactical help (low cost, easy to get):**
- Copy editing on the Almanac essays — 1099 copyeditor at $30–50/hour.
- Discord moderation when volume exceeds capacity — first hire to consider after Vault hits 1,000.
- Press outreach drafting when cycle is heavy — 1099 PR contractor for 30–60 day engagement.
- Founder peer feedback — single trusted founder friend who reviews monthly retrospectives.

**Strategic help (higher cost, longer relationship):**
- Lawyer for partner agreements + tax planning + regulatory questions.
- Accountant for quarterly bookkeeping + annual tax.
- Therapist or coach for processing founder stress (Garrett-personal; not a Galaxy expense).

**Community help (free, brand-aligned):**
- Vault founding-50 cohort. Members opted in to "help shape" Vault. Garrett can ask for input on direction without it being seen as weakness.
- Vault advisory channel (#vault-feedback). Members regularly post unsolicited feedback; Garrett can solicit specific topics.

### The asking-for-help script

For 1099 contractors:

> "I'm looking for a [copyeditor / PR consultant / Discord moderator] for a [duration] engagement. Specifically: [scope]. Hourly rate [$X-Y/hour] or fixed-fee [$X for the project]. Brand-position alignment matters — Galaxy operates under restraint discipline (publishes losses, no hype language). Available for a 30-minute intro call?"

For founder-peer feedback:

> "Hey [name], would you read my weekly retrospective for the next 4 weeks and give me one specific piece of feedback per week? Not asking for general thoughts — looking for one specific thing each time. I'll do the same for you if helpful."

### What not to do

- Don't post in public Vault Discord asking for emotional support. That's not the channel's purpose.
- Don't ask press contacts for personal advice. They're not advisors; they're observers.
- Don't ask multiple competing 1099 contractors for the same scope without telling them. That's bad-faith outreach.
- Don't use Vault office hours to vent.

## The single-founder kill criteria

Per `01-runway-scenarios.md`, there are runway-scenario-specific kill criteria. There are also founder-specific kill criteria that override the business criteria:

| Founder-state trigger | Response |
|---|---|
| 14+ consecutive days of <6 hours of sleep | Take immediate 5-day break. Vault digest pre-drafted; Discord on auto-respond. |
| Spiral protocol invoked 3+ times in a single week | Take immediate Saturday + Sunday off. Reset the routine. |
| Garrett reads the brand voice canonical and thinks "I don't believe this anymore" | Halt all Galaxy operations for 7 days. Reassess from scratch. |
| Garrett can't articulate the current Galaxy roadmap in 3 sentences | Take a week off. The roadmap exists; Garrett's confidence in it is the broken thing. |
| Garrett feels Galaxy is "performing" rather than "operating" | Reread the master plan v3 § "the bet, restated honestly." If still feels performative, take a week off. |

These are founder-state criteria, not business-state criteria. They fire before the business kill criteria do.

## What to do when this playbook gets ignored

The honest reality: this playbook will get ignored sometimes. Founders under time pressure will skip the Saturday off, will reply to the hostile email at 23:00, will write the Wednesday digest at Tuesday 18:00.

When that happens:

1. Notice it. Log it in the weekly retrospective under "What didn't work this week."
2. Don't shame. Shame doesn't help. Acknowledgment does.
3. Reset the next week. Calendar-block protected blocks for the next week before Sunday.
4. If the pattern continues for 3+ weeks, escalate: take a full week off + invoke founder-state kill criteria.

The playbook is a north star, not a compliance document. Drift is expected. Recovery is the discipline.

## What this playbook deliberately does NOT do

1. **No specific medication / mental health prescriptions.** That's between Garrett and a licensed clinician. The playbook addresses operational resilience, not clinical care.
2. **No "balance vs grind" cliché framing.** Both are oversimplifications. The playbook addresses specific operational rhythms.
3. **No spiritual / religious framing.** Galaxy and Alter XIV are deliberately separated per memory. This playbook stays Galaxy-side.
4. **No social comparison.** "Most founders work 80 hours/week" type framing is rejected. Galaxy's operating discipline doesn't measure against other founders' work hours.
5. **No public-facing variant.** This is private. Not for the Galaxy site, not for Twitter, not for press.
