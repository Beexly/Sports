# Galaxy Brand Voice — Canonical Reference

**Status:** Single source of truth for Galaxy Sports Edge brand voice. Supersedes scattered voice rules elsewhere in the repo. When in doubt, check here.

**Pairs with:** `brand-safety-checklist.md` (compliance scanner rules) — that file is the enforcement layer; this file is the voice layer. Both must pass.

---

## The one sentence

**Galaxy publishes what it computes. The math is restrained, the writing is restrained, the brand is restrained. Every surface should feel like the same operator wrote it on the same Sunday afternoon.**

If any Galaxy surface fails that sentence, the voice has drifted.

---

## Founding principles (why this voice exists)

Three rules behind every voice decision:

### 1. Method, not personality

Galaxy is built on a deterministic factor model. The brand reflects the method, not the operator's personality. Garrett is the founder and a real human, but Galaxy's voice is the voice of the operating discipline — not the voice of a creator.

This is the single most important brand-position rule. Most sports analytics platforms drift toward founder-personality (Bill Simmons-shape, Pat McAfee-shape). Galaxy deliberately doesn't.

**Implication:** Headlines, marketing copy, public-facing pages prefer "we" or method-led phrasing over "I." First-person "I" is reserved for surfaces where the founder's voice is explicitly the product (Vault digests, Almanac headline essay, founding-member emails, office hours).

### 2. Restraint over enthusiasm

Galaxy's content philosophy: publish less, weigh more carefully, admit more, market less. The voice mirrors this.

Sentences end. Paragraphs end. Lists are short. Headlines don't shout. There are no exclamation marks because exclamation marks are how excitement signals certainty, and Galaxy doesn't sell certainty.

**Implication:** When in doubt between two phrasings, pick the quieter one.

### 3. Transparency as identity

Every Galaxy public surface should make the brand position visible without saying it. The Loss Room exists; the Pass List exists; the methodology page exists. They are the proof.

The voice never *claims* transparency. It demonstrates transparency by being specific about what Galaxy did, what worked, what didn't. The reader assesses the claim because the evidence is in front of them.

**Implication:** When a sentence would describe Galaxy's brand position, rewrite it to demonstrate the position instead.

## Hard rules (never break these)

### Vocabulary — banned forever

These words/phrases never appear on any Galaxy public surface. The compliance scanner enforces:

- "AI" — Galaxy is deterministic math. Even casual descriptive reference is a violation on public and member-facing surfaces. Literal legacy social handles may appear in internal planning docs only.
- "Guaranteed," "guarantee" — never in a returns or outcomes context.
- "Lock," "tout-certainty," or outcome-promise language — tout vocabulary; Galaxy rejects.
- "Sure thing," "no-brainer," "can't lose" — gambling vocabulary; rejected.
- "Edge" as marketing word — only allowed as technical term inside model rationale (e.g., "the Edge Index is the factor breakdown surface"); never as headline or sales copy.
- "Winning" — only allowed in factual context (specific game result); never as marketing claim.
- "Profit" — never as Galaxy outcome promise.
- "Insider," "exclusive" — Galaxy gates by tier mechanics, not by "insider access."
- "Limited time offer," "expires soon" — urgency manipulation; rejected.
- "Pick of the day" — Galaxy publishes 5/day, not 1/day; no special hierarchy.
- "Trust me," "believe me" — verbal-tic begging language; brand reads weaker.
- "Honestly," "truly," "really" as intensifiers — rhetorical inflation.

### Style — never do

- Exclamation marks anywhere except in dialogue quotation (rare).
- Emojis except sparingly. One 🪐 allowed in Discord outreach DMs (Template 3 in outreach pack). Otherwise no emojis in published or transactional surfaces.
- ALL CAPS for emphasis. (Use restraint or italic, never caps.)
- More than 2 exclaimed adjectives in a row (e.g., "fast, smart, accurate" — drop to one).
- Greater-than-3-sentence opening paragraphs in any blog or essay. First paragraph: 1–3 sentences.
- Vague intensifiers: "very," "really," "quite," "incredibly," "amazingly." Cut.
- Cliché sports-betting metaphors: "we're not gambling, we're investing"; "play smart"; etc.

### Tone — never reach for

- Friendly-bro: "Hey legend!" / "What's up champ!" — rejected.
- Coach-speak: "Trust the process, fam." — rejected.
- Salesperson urgency: "Don't miss out!" — rejected.
- Influencer humility flex: "I almost can't believe how well this is performing." — rejected.
- Apocalyptic doomscroll: "The whole industry is broken; we're the only honest ones." — rejected (the brand position is implicit, not preached).

## Soft rules (preferred patterns)

These are guidelines, not enforced. Skilled writing breaks them occasionally and earns the break.

### Sentence rhythm

- Mix long and short sentences. Don't write only long; don't write only short.
- One-sentence paragraphs are powerful when used 1–2 times per page. Used more, they feel performative.
- Start sentences with subject + verb most of the time. Inverted constructions (subordinate clause first) only when the rhythm calls for it.
- Read aloud. If a sentence makes you breathless, break it.

### Paragraph structure

- Paragraphs of 2–5 sentences are the workhorse.
- Long paragraphs (6+ sentences) earn their length by carrying an argument that can't be split.
- Section breaks before tonal shifts. Don't pivot inside a paragraph.

### Headlines

- Galaxy headlines describe; they don't tease.
- Active voice. Specific noun. Concrete claim.
- Examples:
  - **Yes:** "The Pass List, archived." / "Why we passed on Knicks-Pacers G6."
  - **No:** "You won't believe what the model said." / "This one factor changed everything."

### Numerical claims

- Use specific numbers when you have them. "64% confidence" beats "high confidence."
- Round to one decimal place for fractions. "64% confidence," not "63.7% confidence" (unless the precision matters).
- Don't use percentages where ratios are more honest. "1 in 5" reads more concrete than "20% of."
- Never use a number you can't source from the data.

### Calls to action

- CTAs are quiet imperative verbs. "Read the autopsy." "Join the founding 1,000." "Reserve the 2026 Almanac."
- Never: "Click here!" / "Sign up now!" / "Don't miss your chance!"

### Apologies / corrections

- Direct. No padding. "We were wrong about [X]. Here's the autopsy."
- Don't pretend humility you don't feel.
- Don't drag the apology longer than the original mistake.

## Voice register by surface

The voice tightens or relaxes depending on the surface. Below: the register for every Galaxy surface, in order of how widely seen.

### Public-facing (third-person, restrained — the widest audience)

**Surfaces:** Homepage, `/board`, `/ledger`, `/loss-room`, `/passes`, `/methodology`, `/vault` (landing), `/almanac` (pre-order).

**Voice rules:**
- Mostly third-person ("Galaxy publishes..." not "I publish...").
- "We" is acceptable but used sparingly — Galaxy is a method, not a team-led brand.
- Sentences are tight. Paragraphs are short. No throat-clearing.
- Galaxy's contrarian position visible but not preached.

**Example (`/loss-room` page intro):**
> Every settled losing pick. Every autopsy. Public.

Three sentences worth of meaning in eight words. That's the register.

### Vault digest (first-person, founder-led — Wednesday weekly)

**Surfaces:** Vault digest email, `#vault-digest-archive`.

**Voice rules:**
- First-person "I" is the voice. This is Garrett writing.
- More personal than public surfaces. Allowed to surface uncertainty, admit confusion, walk through reasoning.
- Still no banned vocabulary. The restraint discipline holds.
- Length: 500–900 words target.

**Example opening (from `copy/vault-digest-samples/week-2-sample.md`):**
> This week's digest is a loss. [Specific game.] Confidence at publication: 64%. The loss is in the Loss Room with the full autopsy. This digest is the internal version — the reasoning I couldn't fit on the public page.

### Vault office hours (live spoken — second Tuesday monthly)

**Surfaces:** Discord stage live audio.

**Voice rules:**
- Spoken voice. More conversational than written.
- Still applies brand-safety vocabulary rules.
- Garrett can answer "I don't know" without padding.
- Less prepared, more responsive. Member-led format.

### Vault Discord (community — daily)

**Surfaces:** `#vault-lounge`, `#vault-feedback`, `#vault-office-hours`.

**Voice rules:**
- Personal but not casual. The room respects restraint.
- Quiet days are fine. Garrett doesn't perform engagement.
- Member replies sometimes get short responses (acknowledgment), sometimes get longer (thoughtful walk-through). Mix.

**Example Discord message (Garrett):**
> Good catch. I hadn't looked at the autopsy that way. Walking through it next office hours.

Note: no exclamation, no "great point!", no padding.

### Galaxy email (transactional + retention)

**Surfaces:** Vault welcome sequence, retention check-ins, founding-50 invitations.

**Source docs:** `copy/vault-welcome-emails.md`, `copy/vault-retention-checkins.md`, `week-minus-1/07-founding-50-selection-framework.md`.

**Voice rules:**
- First-person "I." Closes "— G" or "— Garrett."
- Under 250 words per email.
- Same restraint as public surfaces. Plus warmth — these emails are personal, not transactional.

### Almanac (annual print)

**Surfaces:** The annual book, January ship.

**Voice rules:**
- Headline essay (Chapter 1): full founder voice, "I," personal. Most personal Galaxy surface annually.
- Supporting essays: mostly "we," method-led, but more developed than public web copy.
- Data chapters (Loss Room archive, Pass List archive, etc.): minimal narration. The data does the talking.

### Press / podcasts / interviews (spoken external)

**Surfaces:** Podcast appearances, written interviews, press quotes.

**Source docs:** `copy/vault-launch-press-pack.md`, `copy/outlier-competitive-battlecard.md`.

**Voice rules:**
- Same brand-safety vocabulary rules.
- Garrett speaks for Galaxy; "we" frame even though "I" pronouns appear.
- Counter-position by stating what Galaxy IS, never by saying competitors are bad.
- Refuse to make claims Galaxy can't verify ("We're the most accurate" — refused).

### Social (Twitter/X, Threads, Instagram captions)

**Surfaces:** @GalaxySportsAI (X), @galaxysportsedge (other platforms).

**Voice rules:**
- Drop sentences. Galaxy social posts are 1–3 sentences max.
- No emojis except 🪐 used very sparingly (every 20+ posts at most).
- Never trash-talk competitors.
- Link to specific Galaxy surfaces (Loss Room, autopsies, Pass List) more often than to homepage.
- No "engagement bait" formats (numbered lists with cliffhangers, "comment to win," etc.).

**Example tweet:**
> A pass from this morning: Brooklyn-Boston, 58% confidence Brooklyn cover. Pass List entry filed at 9:42 AM.
> galaxysportsedge.com/passes/[id]

## Pronoun governance (when "we" vs "I" vs "Galaxy")

| Speaker | Surface | Default pronoun | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Galaxy as brand | Public web pages | "Galaxy" or "we" | "We" is the soft default; "Galaxy" for emphasis |
| Garrett as operator | Vault digest, Almanac essay, founder emails | "I" | The product IS the founder thinking |
| Galaxy as institution | Press releases | "Galaxy Sports Edge" | Formal register |
| Garrett in podcasts | Spoken | "I" and "we" interchangeably | "We" when describing decisions, "I" when describing reasoning |

**Rule of thumb:** "I" is for decisions only Garrett can make. "We" is for decisions the operating discipline makes. "Galaxy" is for the brand-as-institution.

Wrong:
> "I publish the Loss Room every week."

Right:
> "We publish every settled loss with an autopsy. The Loss Room is the place." (Galaxy as method)

OR

> "I write the Vault digest myself, every Wednesday." (Garrett as operator)

## Words and phrases that ARE allowed (and well-used)

Some vocabulary is brand-aligned and should be reinforced:

- **"Rationale"** — what Galaxy publishes; what Vault deepens.
- **"Restraint"** — the operating discipline, the brand position.
- **"Autopsy"** — the term of art for what Galaxy does to every loss.
- **"Pass List"** — the proprietary surface name.
- **"Methodology"** — the public reasoning layer.
- **"Calibration"** — when Galaxy talks about whether the model's confidence numbers hold over time.
- **"Confidence"** — Galaxy's word for probability output. Used carefully; always with a number.
- **"The board"** — Galaxy's term for the live publication slate.
- **"Settled"** — past-tense for picks that have a result.
- **"The record"** — Galaxy's term for the year captured (used in Almanac framing).
- **"Math you can read"** — the contrarian tagline. Use occasionally; don't overuse.
- **"Process beats outcome"** — Galaxy reasoning frame for assessing individual calls.

## Tonal contrast — what Galaxy reads like vs what it doesn't

### Galaxy reads like

- Bill James's writing (sports analytics with intellectual restraint).
- Stripe Press catalog copy (clean, considered, premium without flash).
- Baseball Prospectus's better essay sections.
- The Pudding's data journalism (specific, restrained, evidence-driven).
- Ben Thompson's Stratechery (analytical, brand-position-driven).

### Galaxy does NOT read like

- Pat McAfee Show transcripts (personality-led, big energy).
- DraftKings marketing copy (enthusiastic, urgency-driven, prize-focused).
- Generic "founder thread" Twitter content ("This week I learned..." with growth-hacker arc).
- Sports talk radio (hot takes, certainty performance).
- Cryptocurrency project whitepapers (overconfident, jargon-heavy).
- Most affiliate-driven sports betting content (selective memory, sales-driven).

When in doubt: ask whether the sentence reads like Bill James wrote it or like a sports betting affiliate wrote it.

## Failure modes (when voice drifts)

Voice drift happens predictably. Three patterns to watch for:

### Drift 1: Marketing pressure → enthusiasm creep

When growth slows, the natural urge is to inject enthusiasm. Headlines get punchier; CTAs get pushier; "limited time" creeps in.

**Counter:** the brand-safety scanner catches the worst. The harder catch is tone — exclamation-free isn't the same as enthusiasm-free. Run drafts past a person who'd notice.

### Drift 2: Founder-personality creep

When Garrett is in the press cycle or doing podcast tours, the Galaxy voice can absorb more "I" energy than it should. Public-facing surfaces start sounding like Garrett's personal Twitter.

**Counter:** quarterly voice audit. Read every public-facing surface end-to-end. Mark any sentence that reads more like "Garrett thinks" than "Galaxy publishes." Rewrite.

### Drift 3: Competitor-position bleed

When competitors do something interesting (Outlier ships a feature, etc.), the urge to react in copy is strong. Galaxy starts using language that defines itself against the competitor.

**Counter:** state what Galaxy IS in every paragraph. Counter-position only by what Galaxy does, never by what competitors don't.

## Voice audit checklist (quarterly)

Garrett or a delegate audits the following surfaces once per quarter. ~2 hours total.

- [ ] Homepage
- [ ] /board
- [ ] /ledger
- [ ] /loss-room (current month's entries)
- [ ] /passes (current month's entries)
- [ ] /methodology
- [ ] /vault landing
- [ ] /almanac pre-order (if active)
- [ ] Most recent 4 Vault digests
- [ ] Most recent Vault Discord operator posts
- [ ] Most recent press appearance (if any)
- [ ] Most recent Twitter/X post

For each surface, check:

1. Did any banned vocabulary slip in?
2. Did any sentence read enthusiast / salesperson / influencer in tone?
3. Did any "I" pronoun show up on a method-led surface?
4. Did any "we" pronoun show up on a founder-led surface where it weakened the personal register?
5. Did any competitor get named in a way that gave them brand-relevance Galaxy didn't intend?

If any audit item flags, fix immediately. Log corrections in decision log if structural.
