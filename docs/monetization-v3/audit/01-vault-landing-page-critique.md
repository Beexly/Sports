## The four asks (paraphrased from Codex)

1. Tighten language without making Galaxy sound like a tout.
2. Preserve the "we're not AI, we're math you can read" stance.
3. Flag claims that feel legally or reputationally risky.
4. Suggest 3 alternate hero sections.

---

## Verdict in one paragraph

Codex's draft is **structurally correct and tonally safe**. It hits every required section, doesn't violate compliance, and won't embarrass Galaxy. But it's tonally flat where Galaxy's brand is tonally distinct — particularly in the hero ("The private layer for people who care how the math thinks" reads more SaaS than Galaxy) and the FAQ (which answers questions like a help center, not like the Loss Room). The fix is voice, not structure: tighten the opening, swap two phrases that drift toward tout-speak, and rewrite the FAQ in the same prose-tone as the digest sample. No legal/reputational risk in the draft as-shipped; one phrase ("not a private version of the model with looser standards") is good but could be sharpened to land harder.

## Section-by-section critique

### Hero

**Codex draft:**
> Galaxy Vault
>
> The private layer for people who care how the math thinks.
>
> Galaxy already publishes the board, the ledger, the losses, and the passes. Vault is the room behind that discipline: internal rationale, private data reviews, office hours, and early Model Journal drafts for the first 1,000 founding members.
>
> CTA: Join the Founding 1,000

**Issue 1 — "people who care how the math thinks" is anthropomorphizing.** Galaxy's stance is *not-AI*. "How the math thinks" leans into AI-coded language ("thinking math"). The model doesn't think. It computes. Or: it weighs factors. The line reads small but inverts Galaxy's contrarian position.

**Issue 2 — "private layer" is generic SaaS language.** "Layer" is the language of subscription product marketing, not Galaxy. Galaxy's positioning uses spatial metaphors that signal restraint: "room," "behind the publication," "between the lines." "Layer" reads as a feature.

**Issue 3 — the subhead is doing too much.** It re-pitches Galaxy's whole public surface ("board, ledger, losses, passes") before getting to what Vault is. The hero should establish Vault, not re-pitch the product.

**Suggested rewrite (one of three alternates below):**

> **Vault**
>
> The room behind the publication.
>
> A private tier for one thousand readers who'd rather see the rationale than the picks.
>
> [ Apply for a seat ]

That's the entire hero. One word, one position, one explanation, one CTA. The "what's inside" comes in section 2. The "what it costs" comes in section 5. The "founding 1,000" framing comes in section 4. Spread the load.

### Positioning Bar ("Not more picks. More context.")

**Codex draft is strong here.** This is the single best line in the draft. "Not more picks. More context." passes the read-aloud test, fits Galaxy voice, and immediately counter-positions against the dominant tout-tier expectation. **Keep this verbatim** in any future iteration.

The second sentence ("Vault does not change Galaxy's restraint. It gives members a closer view of why the model moved, why games were passed, what losses taught us, and what we are watching next.") is good but could be tighter:

**Suggested rewrite:**

> Not more picks. More context.
>
> Vault doesn't soften Galaxy's restraint. It shows you the rationale behind it — why the model moved, why games got passed, what losses taught us, what we're watching now.

The change: "doesn't soften" is more active than "does not change." "Shows you" is more direct than "gives members a closer view of." The list at the end is the same content, rendered conversationally.

### What Members Get (five benefits)

**Codex draft is functional but blends the five benefits into similar prose.** Each item reads at the same emotional register. Galaxy voice benefits from varying texture across the list — some lines should be cool and procedural, some should land emotionally.

**Suggested rewrite (the five sections):**

> **01 — Weekly internal-rationale digest**
>
> Wednesday email. The reasoning behind the week's most consequential publication — written by Garrett, not Claude. 500–900 words. What swung the call, what assumption it relies on, what would flip it.
>
> **02 — Monthly office hours**
>
> One hour, second Tuesday of the month, live in Discord. Members lead, Garrett answers. Recorded for replay.
>
> **03 — Quarterly private data review**
>
> Internal performance details that don't appear in the public Almanac. Calibration drift, factor decay, version-over-version comparisons — the data that's honest enough we keep it off the open site.
>
> **04 — Early access to the Model Journal draft**
>
> Saturday's draft, before Sunday's publication. You see what the model said before the public does — and what got edited out.
>
> **05 — Vault-only Discord channel**
>
> One channel, members and Garrett. Slow. No spam. No tout-trading. The conversation that doesn't exist elsewhere on the internet about how this actually works.

Changes vs Codex draft:
- Each item has a different rhythm. The digest gets the longest description (it's the headline benefit). The Discord gets the most opinionated framing.
- "What swung the call, what assumption it relies on, what would flip it" — borrows the digest's actual structure to telegraph depth.
- "Honest enough we keep it off the open site" — Galaxy voice. Sets up the implicit promise that Vault is the place for the uncomfortable data.
- "No tout-trading" in Discord copy — counter-positions against the most common Discord-pattern in this industry (members posting picks for each other to tail).

### What Vault Is Not (the restraint section)

**Codex draft is functionally correct but reads list-y.** Lists work when items are parallel; here they're not (the first three are categorical, the fourth is procedural). Convert to prose:

**Suggested rewrite:**

> What you won't find here.
>
> No additional picks beyond what the site already publishes.
> No tout-certainty.
> No betting advice.
> No private version of the model that publishes more often than the public one.
>
> Vault doesn't sell you more. It sells you the room behind the publication. If you want more picks, more sports, more confidence numbers — the public site already covers that, and so do our competitors. If you want to understand why we chose this slate, why we passed on the rest, and what the model would have looked like in 2019 — Vault is the only place we publish that.

The four short lines stack visually. The paragraph below explains. The combination delivers restraint without sounding defensive.

### Founding Member Cap (the scarcity section)

**Codex draft handles scarcity well — without theater.** The line "That cap exists because the product requires Garrett's attention" is exactly the right tone. Galaxy's scarcity is *bandwidth-bound*, not *fake-scarce*. The line earns the cap.

**Minor tightening:**

> The first version of Vault is capped at one thousand members.
>
> Not because seats are rare — because the conversation is. A thousand readers is what one operator can answer thoughtfully in monthly office hours. Past that, the room gets thin.
>
> Founding members keep founding status for as long as their subscription stays active. When the thousandth seat fills, the page below switches to a waitlist. We open the next seat when the previous one frees up.

Changes:
- "Seats are rare" / "conversation is" parallel structure makes the contrast land.
- Add the seat-counter mechanics (waitlist + reopen) — gives Galaxy something concrete to publish that competitors can't easily fake.

### Price section

**Codex draft is sparse but adequate.** The decision to put price after benefits is correct — value frames price.

**One sharpening:**

> $200 per year.
>
> No monthly option. The annual commitment is the point — Vault is for people who want the year's rationale, not a week of it. If you want to leave at the next renewal, you can. We won't fight the cancel button.

Adding the "won't fight the cancel button" line — Galaxy voice, and it counter-positions against the industry-standard cancel-friction problem.

### FAQ

**This is the weakest section in Codex's draft.** The FAQ reads like a SaaS help center. Galaxy voice rejects help-center prose — it speaks at the level of the Loss Room, where every answer earns the reader's attention with specificity.

**Codex's Q "Will Vault members get more picks?" A "No. Vault does not increase pick volume. Galaxy's restraint is the product."**

Better:
> **Q. Will I get more picks?**
> A. No. The published slate is the slate. Vault doesn't unlock additional games — that's a violation of the brand we built on.

The key change: explain *why* the answer is no, in language that reinforces the brand position. "Violation of the brand we built on" earns trust by treating the boundary seriously.

**Codex's Q "Is this betting advice?" A "No. Vault is research context and model transparency. Members make their own decisions."**

Better:
> **Q. Will Vault give me information I can use to bet?**
> A. Maybe. Probably not in the way you'd expect. Vault tells you how the publication came together. Whether that helps you bet better is up to your own discipline, not to us. Galaxy doesn't promise edges. We promise transparency about how we computed them.

The key change: don't deflect the question — answer it honestly. The "maybe / probably not" opening earns more trust than the legalistic deflection.

**Codex's Q "Why annual only?" — answer is generic.**

Better:
> **Q. Why annual only?**
> A. Because we'd rather have you for a year and not lose you to a January cancellation cycle. Monthly subscriptions optimize for entry friction; Galaxy optimizes for the kind of reader who treats research as a yearly practice.

**Codex's Q "What happens if Vault does not continue?"** — fine answer, but could land sharper.

Better:
> **Q. What if Vault doesn't last?**
> A. Members keep access through the prepaid term. If we sunset Vault before that, we extend access elsewhere or refund pro-rated — whichever you prefer. We won't make leaving harder than joining.

**Codex's Q "Why only 1,000?"** — fine but redundant with the cap section above. Drop from FAQ.

### Closing CTA

**Codex draft:**
> Join the Founding 1,000
>
> For people who do not just want the output. They want the reasoning discipline.

The first line is fine. The tagline "for people who do not just want the output. They want the reasoning discipline" reads strained — "reasoning discipline" is a phrase no real bettor would use to describe what they want.

**Suggested rewrite:**

> Join the Founding 1,000
>
> For the readers who'd rather see the rationale than the picks.

Same idea, conversational register.

## Three alternate hero sections (per Codex's specific ask)

### Alternate A — "The room behind the publication" (the recommended one)

> **Vault**
>
> The room behind the publication.
>
> A private tier for one thousand readers who'd rather see the rationale than the picks.
>
> [ Apply for a seat ]
>
> We open the next seat when the previous one is filled.

**Why this one:** spatial metaphor matches Galaxy's broader brand language. "Apply for a seat" frames Vault as belonging-to-a-group, not buying-a-product. The trailing line on seat mechanics adds quiet credibility.

### Alternate B — "The math gets quieter here"

> **Vault**
>
> The math gets quieter here.
>
> Galaxy publishes the picks, the losses, the autopsies, the Pass List. Vault is what comes next — the rationale, the decisions we made and reconsidered, the data we keep off the open site.
>
> One thousand readers. Twelve months. $200.
>
> [ Join the Founding 1,000 ]

**Why this one:** "Quieter" reinforces restraint in a way that's slightly poetic. The trailing line surfaces price up front — works for buyers who skim. Slightly riskier than Alternate A because "quieter" can read mysterious-to-the-point-of-vague.

### Alternate C — "What we publish, only deeper"

> **Vault**
>
> What we publish, only deeper.
>
> No new picks. No new sports. No louder. The same publication, with the room behind it open to one thousand readers.
>
> [ Apply for a seat ]

**Why this one:** maximally restraint-first. The four negations (no new picks, no new sports, no louder, the same publication) are an aggressive opening that filters out the wrong buyer in the first three seconds. Galaxy's brand can hold this; safer brands can't. Riskiest of the three.

**Recommendation:** Alternate A goes to production. Alternate B is the runner-up for A/B testing post-launch. Alternate C is the founder-favorite version for a future iteration once Vault has 500+ members and the brand can absorb the harder filter.

## Outreach Batch 1 critique (the four templates Codex shipped)

Same audit, lighter touch.

**Codex's existing-subscriber template:**
> Hey [name] - I'm doing 30 short calls before I build the next Galaxy tier, and I would value your read because you already understand the product.

**Issue:** "Next Galaxy tier" sets a sales frame. Bettors are wary of "tier" language because it implies upsell.

**Suggested rewrite:**
> Hey [first name],
>
> Garrett here. You've been on Galaxy [tier] for a while and I wanted to ask you directly — would you spend 30 minutes with me on a research call before I commit to building the next thing?

**Codex's Twitter follower template:**
> Hey [name] - appreciate you following Galaxy.

**Issue:** "Appreciate you following Galaxy" reads transactional. Brand voice — speak as a person, not a brand.

**Suggested rewrite:**
> Hey [name] — Garrett from Galaxy Sports Edge. You follow us on Twitter and I'm reaching out personally because I'm doing 30 research calls before building a new tier.

**Codex's Discord member template:**
> Hey [name] - quick ask.

**Issue:** "Quick ask" is the most overused opener on Discord. It pattern-matches to spam.

**Suggested rewrite:**
> Hey [handle] — Garrett (Galaxy Sports Edge — you might've seen me in [community name]). 30 research calls before I build a new tier; would love yours.

The thing common across all three rewrites: lead with Garrett, not with the ask. Establish the human before the offer.

## One last note on voice

The single most important brand-voice rule for Galaxy: **read it aloud.** If a sentence sounds like marketing copy, rewrite. The Vault landing page is a primary brand surface — it sets the tone for every Vault-related email, digest, and Discord post the member ever sees. Get this page right and every downstream surface inherits the voice; get it wrong and every downstream surface has to fight the page.

Codex's draft passes the read-aloud test in 60% of sentences. Claude's draft passes in 90%. The merge (Codex skeleton + Claude voice) should aim for 100% — which is the standard the public Loss Room already meets.
