# Galaxy Character Media Strategy

Date: 2026-05-23
Status: Internal R&D
Related decision: DEC-NEXT-021
Supersedes: photoreal human-host-first testing in prior short-form R&D docs.

---

## Decision log entry

### DEC-NEXT-021 - Shift synthetic media R&D from photoreal host to obvious Galaxy character

**Decision:** Galaxy's short-form character test should start with an obviously synthetic, Galaxy-owned character rather than a photoreal human-presenting host.

**Rationale:** Disclosure solves a compliance problem, but it does not solve the visual brand collision. A realistic woman in a domestic setting talking about sports research pattern-matches too closely to the tout and low-quality synthetic-content ecosystem. Once labeled, the realism advantage also collapses into a saturated category. A stylized Galaxy character makes the synthetic identity obvious, defensible, ownable, and more aligned with "math you can read."

**Guardrail:** The character may introduce Galaxy artifacts. The character does not replace Garrett, does not sell private picks, does not imply real-person expertise, and does not appear on core proof surfaces as the reason to trust Galaxy.

---

## The strategic correction

The original R&D lane considered photoreal SFW hosts as a way to increase social distribution. That path is now downgraded.

Why:

1. **Visual collision beats legal disclosure.** Viewers parse category before they read labels. The photoreal domestic-host aesthetic collides with the exact ecosystem Galaxy is trying not to resemble.
2. **Disclosure removes the realism premium.** If the presenter is labeled synthetic, "very realistic" stops creating parasocial lift and starts competing with thousands of disclosed synthetic-content accounts.
3. **Vault trust gets harder.** Vault sells founder access and operational transparency. A photoreal synthetic acquisition layer makes the funnel feel less direct.
4. **A named character can compound.** Photoreal humans are fungible. A distinctive Galaxy character can become an owned media asset.

The new path: own the synthetic identity visually.

## Ranked execution paths

### 1. Mascot path - recommended V1

**Cost:** ~$500 for a strong first design if outsourced; lower if drafted internally.
**Speed:** fastest.
**Risk:** lowest.
**Brand fit:** highest.

Shape:

- A stylized non-human Galaxy character.
- Visual identity derived from astronomy, calibration, sports research, and the public record.
- Garrett's real voice or a clearly labeled voice treatment.
- Character hosts artifact recaps, not personality content.

Why it works:

- The aesthetic is the disclosure.
- No one thinks the character is a real analyst.
- It cannot be mistaken for a tout influencer.
- It can sit beside Galaxy's method-led voice without corrupting it.

### 2. Vtuber path - second test if mascot gets signal

**Cost:** ~$3k-$5k design plus ongoing rigging/tooling.
**Speed:** medium.
**Risk:** medium.
**Brand fit:** possible, but needs discipline.

Shape:

- Stylized animated character, not photoreal.
- Sports analytics host with clear synthetic framing.
- More expressive than mascot, but still method-led.

Why it might work:

- The format is established with the same broad demographic Galaxy can reach.
- The character can host longer clips or live explainers.

Why it waits:

- Higher production cost.
- Easier to drift into creator-personality content.

### 3. Stylized human-ish character - later only

**Cost:** ~$10k-$30k if done well.
**Speed:** slowest.
**Risk:** highest of the three, but lower than photoreal pretending-to-be-real.
**Brand fit:** only if it loudly owns its synthetic nature.

Shape:

- Human-ish but clearly stylized.
- Named public character.
- Built as a long-run media asset, not a quick conversion trick.

Why it waits:

- Too expensive before proof.
- Requires visual direction, lore discipline, and platform-native execution.
- Still risks becoming the product.

## V1 character recommendation: Vega

Working name: **Vega**.

Why:

- Short.
- Astronomical.
- Easy to say in video.
- Not a betting word.
- Doesn't imply certainty.

Role:

- Galaxy's artifact guide.
- Explains one public artifact at a time.
- Points viewers to the record.

Not role:

- Not mascot for betting confidence.
- Not a picks host.
- Not a replacement for Garrett.
- Not a community moderator.
- Not a member-support persona.

Voice relationship:

- Garrett's real voice is preferred for V1.
- If voice treatment is used, it should be light and clearly branded.
- Do not create a fake human voice identity pretending to be a separate real person.

## Vega visual brief

Concept:

A stylized Galaxy research character built from three ideas:

- constellation map
- telescope / lens
- calibration dial

Visual attributes:

- Simple silhouette readable at phone size.
- One or two accent colors from the Galaxy palette.
- Expressive enough for short-form thumbnails.
- Not cute to the point of childishness.
- Not aggressive.
- Not a gambling mascot.
- No sportsbook visual language.
- No team colors as primary identity.

Possible forms:

1. **Constellation analyst:** a simple geometric character with star-map faceplate and small orbit/dial elements.
2. **Telescope guide:** a compact telescope/lens character with screen-like expressions and subtle celestial accents.
3. **Calibration mark:** an abstract circular character built from a gauge, crosshair, and star field.

Rejected forms:

- Photoreal woman.
- Sports bettor caricature.
- Dollar-sign / money character.
- Team-logo parody.
- Casino-chip visual system.
- Anything that reads juvenile.

## V1 test

### Production

- One character design.
- Five short videos.
- 60-90 seconds each.
- Garrett voice.
- Character appears as visual host, not narrator identity.
- Captions carry the artifact logic.

### Source topics

1. Loss Room: one sample autopsy.
2. Pass List: one below-floor example.
3. Methodology: confidence floor.
4. Methodology: calibration.
5. Model Journal: Pass List hindsight.

### Distribution

Platforms:

- YouTube Shorts.
- TikTok.
- X video.
- Instagram Reels if account exists.

Post cadence:

- Two weeks.
- Five total posts per platform maximum.
- No paid boost.
- No daily posting after the test batch.

### CTA

Every post points to a public proof surface.

No direct checkout.

### Kill criteria

Kill the character if, after 2 weeks:

- zero platform shows meaningful traction,
- comments focus on the character in a way that weakens the artifact,
- the visual language feels inconsistent with Galaxy proof surfaces,
- Garrett would not want the character associated with the Methodology page,
- production time exceeds the value of the traffic.

Continue only if:

- at least one platform sends measurable traffic to proof surfaces,
- comments show comprehension of Galaxy artifacts,
- the character feels like a guide, not the product.

## Compliance and policy baseline

This document is not legal advice.

Current policy read as of 2026-05-23:

- EU AI Act Article 50 creates transparency obligations for certain synthetic/deepfake audio, image, and video content. The official AI Act Service Desk text states that deployers of systems generating or manipulating deepfake image/audio/video content must disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated.
- YouTube requires altered/synthetic-content disclosure in YouTube Studio when realistic altered or synthetic content could be mistaken for reality.
- TikTok provides generated-content labeling and treats realistic synthetic media as notice-sensitive.
- X prohibits deceptive synthetic/manipulated media likely to cause harm and prohibits misleading fake identities.
- FTC endorsement guidance emphasizes clear and conspicuous disclosure of material connections and advertiser responsibility.

Sources checked:

- [EU AI Act Article 50](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/de/ai-act/article-50)
- [YouTube altered/synthetic content disclosure](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- [TikTok generated-content guidance](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content)
- [X Rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-rules)
- [X authenticity policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/twitter-impersonation-and-deceptive-identities-policy.html)
- [FTC Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides)

Strategic conclusion:

Disclosure is necessary but insufficient. Galaxy's V1 solution should make the synthetic identity obvious in the design itself.
