## Three pitch tones, written separately

### Variant 1 — Warm intro (a mutual contact connects Garrett to the target)

The warm intro is the highest-yield format. It's what runs to Sketch via someone in Sketch's orbit (a fellow Twitch creator, a manager, a sports-betting podcast that already partners with Galaxy or follows it closely). The pitch is shorter because the introducer has already done the credibility work.

> Hey [first name],
>
> [Mutual contact] suggested I reach out. Quick context:
>
> Galaxy Sports Edge is a sports research platform that publishes its losses (we have a Loss Room — every losing pick gets an autopsy) and its passes (the games we considered and didn't publish). Different shape than the usual sports-betting tools.
>
> I'm building a live-stream overlay (OBS plugin) that shows our Edge Index and factor breakdown during sports content. Recruiting 5 founding streamer partners. Zero cost for the founding cohort; 10% revenue share on subscribers attributable to your audience, first 12 months.
>
> Not a tout product. Not a "tail my picks" overlay. The pitch is: your audience sees the rationale behind the call as you're watching the game.
>
> Open to 20 minutes this week? Happy to walk through the demo and the data. galaxysportsedge.com if you want context first.
>
> — Garrett
>
> P.S. The Loss Room is the page that earned [mutual contact]'s vouch — worth a 60-second look before we talk.

**Why this voice:**
- Opens by naming the mutual — single most important credibility lever in BD.
- "Quick context" frames the message as setup, not pitch — reduces resistance.
- Names the actual differentiator (Loss Room) twice, not buried in signature.
- "Not a tout product" — direct counter-positioning. Streamers know what they're being pitched ten times a week. Galaxy's negation is the differentiator.
- "20 minutes" — half the typical ask. Costless to say yes.
- P.S. drives the curiosity click on Loss Room specifically. Once they've read it, the brand has done the conversion.

### Variant 2 — Cold DM (no warm intro, Twitter or Twitch DM)

The cold DM is the lowest-yield format. ~3% reply rate is realistic on cold outreach to creators with >100k followers. The DM has to do all the work in two sentences before they bounce.

> Hey [handle] —
>
> Garrett, founder of Galaxy Sports Edge. We publish a Loss Room (every losing pick has a public autopsy) and a Pass List (every game considered, not published). I'm recruiting 5 founding streamer partners for an OBS overlay that shows our live Edge Index during games — zero cost, 10% revenue share for 12 months.
>
> Worth 20 min if the framing fits your audience. galaxysportsedge.com/loss-room is probably the fastest way to figure that out.
>
> If not — no worries. Appreciate you reading.

**Why this voice:**
- 90 words. Reads in 25 seconds.
- Loss Room mentioned in the first sentence — that's the hook for creators who already distrust tout-style products.
- Direct URL to Loss Room (not homepage). The link does the conversion that the DM can't.
- "If not — no worries" — disarms the implicit pressure. Increases reply rate from creators who would otherwise ghost.
- No "Best, Garrett" sign-off — Twitter DMs don't need them and they read formal.

### Variant 3 — Agent / manager email

The agent/manager email runs to people whose job is filtering creator outreach. They process hundreds of pitches a week. The pitch has to be commercially specific, fast, and frictionless to forward to the client.

> Subject: Founding streamer partnership — Galaxy Sports Edge (Sports research overlay, no fees)
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Reaching out about a founding-partner opportunity for [client's handle] that I think is worth a 15-minute review.
>
> **What it is.** Galaxy Sports Edge is a sports research platform. We're building an OBS overlay that displays our live Edge Index and factor breakdown directly on streams during sports content. Streamers configure position, opacity, and which data shows; it doesn't crash streams; it can be hidden instantly.
>
> **The offer for [client].** Zero cost during the founding period. 10% revenue share on any Galaxy subscribers attributable to [client's] audience for 12 months. UTM-tracked.
>
> **Why we're approaching [client] specifically.** [One specific reason — e.g., "[client]'s audience engagement on sports-betting content shows the depth-of-research tolerance Galaxy is built around." Or: "[client]'s segments on [specific game/topic] read like the kind of analytical voice our product complements rather than competes with."]
>
> **Why this isn't a tout pitch.** Galaxy publishes its losses with autopsies (galaxysportsedge.com/loss-room) and the games it considered and didn't publish (galaxysportsedge.com/passes). Different shape than the products that typically reach you. The overlay carries that same posture into streams.
>
> **Commercial details we can confirm on a call.** Subscription tier pricing, revenue-share mechanics, attribution architecture, exclusivity window if relevant.
>
> If [client] is open to it, happy to demo via Zoom — 30 minutes, end-to-end. If you want to vet me before that, this is the company background: galaxysportsedge.com/about.
>
> Best,
> Garrett Baxley
> Founder, Galaxy Sports Edge
> garrett@galaxysportsedge.com · (XXX) XXX-XXXX

**Why this voice:**
- Subject line includes specific value ("no fees") + clear category. Easy to triage.
- Headers (bold "What it is," etc.) let the manager scan in 20 seconds. They will scan, not read.
- "Why we're approaching [client] specifically" — the personalization beat. This sentence is where most outreach fails. The Galaxy version needs to be real and specific, not flattering and generic.
- "Why this isn't a tout pitch" — names the manager's primary filter explicitly. Managers reflexively reject gambling-adjacent pitches; Galaxy needs to disqualify itself from that bucket on first read.
- "Commercial details we can confirm on a call" — signals professionalism. Managers prefer pitches that imply contract literacy.
- Phone number in signature — managers respond at 2x rate to creators who include direct phone.

## Sketch-specific outreach plan (the single highest-leverage target)

Sketch deserves a separate document beyond a templated pitch. The reasons:

1. Sketch's 1.69M Twitch follower count makes him the brand-leverage moment for the entire Live track.
2. Sketch's content isn't sports-betting-primary; the overlay would feel like an integration into broader gaming/lifestyle content rather than a sports-only product. Galaxy needs to read that distinction in the pitch.
3. Sketch's audience demographic (younger, gaming-led, lifestyle-tolerant) is different from the analyst-streamer demo that the other 4 founding partners represent. Galaxy's pitch to Sketch needs to acknowledge the audience-fit question rather than dodge it.

### Recommended Sketch outreach sequence

**Step 1 — Map the warm-intro network.** Before any direct outreach, run a 2-week soft audit of who in Garrett's network has connection to Sketch's orbit. Targets:
- Other Twitch creators in Sketch's collab universe.
- Streaming managers / agents who represent creators in adjacent tiers.
- Sports-betting podcasts that Sketch has appeared on (if any).
- Twitch BD / partnership managers (the Twitch staff who curate partner relationships).

If a warm intro is identified, Variant 1 (above) goes via that path.

**Step 2 — If no warm intro materializes within 2 weeks**, run the 1099 BD consultant hire from v3 plan Part 4 Track 3. Budget $10-25k for 90 days. The consultant's job is to identify a warm path Garrett couldn't find from his own network. If the consultant also fails at 90 days, switch to a direct cold approach.

**Step 3 — Direct cold outreach (last resort).** Send Variant 2 (cold DM) to Sketch's public Twitter or Discord. Expected reply rate: <5%. If no reply within 14 days, send a single follow-up. Past that, accept that Sketch isn't a founding partner and pivot to recruiting the 5 mid-tier partners first; revisit Sketch in V2.

### Sketch-personalized cold DM (if it gets to that stage)

> Hey Sketch —
>
> Garrett, Galaxy Sports Edge. I'm reaching out because of the sports-content arc your streams have been on — the depth of analysis when you cover games doesn't look like the standard sports-betting integration audience.
>
> Galaxy is a sports research platform that publishes its losses (every losing pick has a public autopsy) and its passes (every game we considered, didn't publish). Different posture than the products that probably reach you.
>
> I'm recruiting 5 founding streamer partners for an OBS overlay that shows live model rationale during games. Zero cost during founding window. 10% revenue share for 12 months on subscribers from your audience.
>
> If the framing fits, would love 20 minutes. If not — appreciate you reading and no follow-up from me.
>
> galaxysportsedge.com/loss-room is the page that tells the rest.

**Why this voice:**
- Opens with a specific, observable detail about Sketch's content (not flattery — actual observation). Streamers can tell within three words whether the message is personalized or templated.
- "Different posture" — signals Galaxy understands the typical pitch ratio (most sports-content products are tout-shaped) and is positioned against it.
- Same revenue-share commercial as everyone else — Sketch doesn't need a special deal; he needs the right framing.
- The closing line uses "rest" deliberately — leaves the reader curious about what the page contains.

Sketch is the upside swing. The realistic founding 5 are mid-tier creators. Here's tailored language for each archetype.

>
> Your stream's the kind of analytical voice this would fit. Worth 20 minutes if it's interesting; if not, no follow-up.
>
> galaxysportsedge.com/loss-room walks through the posture.

### Archetype B — YouTube sports analyst (50k–500k subscribers)

YouTube-side outreach reads more long-form than Twitch DMs. The reply rate to email is higher than DM on YouTube; cold email also lets you attach a 30-second demo video, which converts at meaningful lift.

> Subject: Founding streamer partner — Galaxy Sports Edge overlay
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Quick note on a founding-partner opportunity that I think fits your channel's analytical bent.

DFS streamers have a different audience expectation than betting streamers — the audience tunes in for lineup-building, not pick-tailing. The Galaxy pitch needs to acknowledge that the overlay supports DFS reasoning, not betting reasoning.

### Archetype D — YouTube sports betting podcast (50k–200k subscribers)

Podcasts are a different format than visual streams. The overlay isn't directly relevant to podcast content (audio-only), but the podcast hosts often have YouTube video uploads where an overlay applies, AND the podcast format gives Galaxy a sponsorship-shaped relationship that overlays alone can't.

## Likely objections from streamers and managers

### From the streamers themselves

**Objection 1: "Sports betting integrations damage my brand."**

Reality: many streamers are pitched by DraftKings, FanDuel, PrizePicks, and 50 tout services. They've learned to default-reject. Galaxy's pitch needs to immediately read as not-that.

Mitigation: lead with the Loss Room. Mention "not a tout product" or "different posture" explicitly. The streamer's brand-protection reflex disarms when they see Galaxy is willing to publish losses — that's the opposite of every other gambling-adjacent pitch.

**Objection 2: "My audience doesn't bet on sports at scale."**

Likely true for general-content streamers like Sketch. The audience-fit question is honest.

Mitigation: don't argue this objection. Accept it. Reposition the overlay as analytical-content support, not betting-content support. Galaxy's factor breakdown can be discussed as game analysis (which any sports-content audience appreciates) rather than as bet-supporting data. The streamer's bet-supporting audience is the subset that converts; the rest get a slightly more interesting stream.

**Objection 3: "$0 sounds great but what's the actual upside?"**

Realistic question. The 10% revenue share on attributable subscribers is the answer, but most streamers haven't seen Galaxy subscription pricing yet.

Mitigation: do the math in the demo call.
- Galaxy Pro: $19/month → $2.28/year revenue share per attributable Pro subscriber.
- Galaxy Elite: $49/month → $5.88/year revenue share per attributable Elite subscriber.
- Galaxy Vault: $200/year → $20/year revenue share per attributable Vault subscriber.

A streamer with 100k followers who converts 1% to Galaxy at the Elite tier is earning $5,880/year passive. At 10% on subscribers, that's not life-changing money — but it's not zero, and it accumulates. The honest pitch frames this as "brand-aligned + supplemental revenue," not "monetization upside."

**Objection 4: "What if Galaxy goes under?"**

Legitimate. Galaxy is pre-revenue at launch; streamer is associating their brand with a startup.

Mitigation: be upfront. "Galaxy is small. The product is real. The losses we publish are real. The subscribers we have are real. If we go under, the overlay simply stops working — your stream doesn't depend on us." That honesty earns more than a fake assurance would.

**Objection 5: "Will the overlay crash my stream?"**

Technical objection. The single most important thing the demo must demonstrate.

Mitigation: closed beta with the founding 5 partners specifically tests this. The OBS plugin must fail-closed (overlay disappears) rather than fail-loud (overlay errors). The product roadmap should treat "zero stream crashes attributable to overlay" as the launch gate.

### From managers / agents

**Objection 6: "Is this a gambling sponsorship? My client doesn't take those."**

The most common manager filter. Galaxy is gambling-adjacent but isn't a sportsbook or betting affiliate; the distinction matters legally and reputationally.

Mitigation: in the email subject line, name "Sports research overlay" not "Sports betting partnership." In the body, name the Loss Room early. Be ready in the call to clarify that Galaxy doesn't accept bets, doesn't process wagers, doesn't pay affiliate commissions to sportsbooks (or — clarify whatever the actual Galaxy affiliate posture is; ensure consistency with the brand promise).

**Objection 7: "We don't do exclusive partnerships at this fee level."**

True. Founding 5 partners aren't exclusive to Galaxy.

Mitigation: be explicit on the call. Galaxy isn't asking for exclusivity. Other research overlays don't exist yet, so practical exclusivity is implicit; if a competitor emerges, the founding partner can run both overlays simultaneously.

**Objection 8: "My client gets paid more than $0 for OBS integrations."**

True for top-tier creators (Sketch, etc.). The $0 founding model is for "I want my brand on the product" creators, not "I monetize my OBS for $X/month" creators.

Mitigation: for tier-1 targets, be willing to negotiate a flat partnership fee instead of revenue share. Cost of $5-15k/quarter for tier-1 creators is reasonable if it unlocks the brand moment. Budget this contingency in v3 plan Track 3 economics.

**Objection 9: "Can we see the contract before we agree to the demo call?"**

Reasonable. Managers want to see the legal shape before investing their client's time.

Mitigation: prepare a concise founding-partner agreement draft before any manager outreach, then route it through lawyer review before partner-facing use. If a manager asks to see legal shape before a demo, send the lawyer-reviewed draft or a non-binding deal-points summary. Do not attach an unreviewed agreement to cold outreach.

**Objection 10: "What's your existing partnership track record?"**

Honest answer: Galaxy is recruiting its first 5 partners. There's no track record yet.

Mitigation: don't manufacture a fake one. "We're recruiting our founding 5 — you'd be among the first. The track record you can verify is our public Galaxy product (galaxysportsedge.com). The founding-partner shape is a relationship we're committing to building from zero."
