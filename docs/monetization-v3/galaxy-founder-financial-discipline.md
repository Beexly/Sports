# Galaxy Founder Financial Discipline

**Audience:** Garrett. Private. Not for public Galaxy surfaces.

**Why this exists:** Galaxy is bootstrapped, single-operator, no outside capital Year 1. The financial discipline that protects the operator is distinct from the operational cash-flow model Codex maintains in `05-cashflow-capital.md`. This document covers Garrett's personal financial discipline as the operator.

**Scope:** Founder owner-draw, tax setting, runway personal-finance check, when-to-pay-from-Galaxy-vs-personal, accountant engagement.

**Read time:** 6 minutes.

---

## The founder financial setup

### Galaxy entity vs personal

Galaxy operates as a [LLC / S-Corp / Sole Proprietorship — Garrett confirms]. Member subscriptions flow through Stripe → Galaxy bank account → Garrett's owner draws (operating expenses paid through Galaxy account).

### What goes through Galaxy:

- Stripe revenue (Vault, Pro, Elite, Almanac).
- Operating expenses (Vercel hosting, Postmark, Anthropic API, Discord nitro, Stripe fees).
- Contractor payments (1099s per `galaxy-contractor-playbook.md`).
- Galaxy-business legal/accounting fees.

### What goes through personal:

- Garrett's personal living expenses.
- Personal taxes.
- Personal phone, internet (unless Galaxy-allocated portion claimed for business use).
- Health insurance.
- Personal investments unrelated to Galaxy.

The Galaxy/personal separation matters for tax efficiency + legal protection + clarity in any future acquisition due diligence.

## Founder owner draw

### Year 1 draw

Per master plan v3 Part 9 cost model, Year-1 net margin is ~-$36k (investment year). Galaxy generates ~$465k in revenue, ~$501k in operating costs. Galaxy isn't paying Garrett a salary in Year 1 — the founder's compensation is the equity / future-value of building Galaxy.

If Galaxy's bank balance allows: Garrett may take modest periodic owner draws ($1k-3k/month) to cover personal expenses while keeping operating runway. These draws are NOT tax-deductible Galaxy expenses; they are equity withdrawals.

If Galaxy can't sustain owner draw: Garrett operates Galaxy and meets personal expenses from prior savings.

### Year 2 onward

By Year 2, if Galaxy's revenue trajectory holds (per master plan):
- Vault Year-2 revenue: $1M+.
- Almanac Year-2 revenue: $0.5M+.
- Live revenue: $0.5M+ (Scenario C).
- Total Year-2 revenue: $2-3M.
- Year-2 net margin: $2-2.5M.

At that point, Galaxy can support an annual founder salary or larger owner draws. The decision becomes structural:
- Salary (taxable + standard) vs.
- Distribution to owner (tax-advantaged for LLC/S-Corp).

Lawyer + accountant advise on the right structure by Year-2 Q1.

## Tax setting

### Quarterly tax payments

If Galaxy generates net income, Garrett pays estimated quarterly federal + state taxes.

Default approach: set aside 25-30% of net revenue (after operating expenses) for taxes. Hold in a separate Galaxy savings sub-account.

Quarterly schedule:
- Q1 estimated tax: April 15
- Q2 estimated tax: June 15
- Q3 estimated tax: September 15
- Q4 estimated tax: January 15 (following year)

Garrett's accountant handles the actual filings. Garrett just sets aside the cash + reviews accountant's draft.

### Year-end tax considerations

- 1099-NEC issued to each contractor who earned >$600.
- Stripe issues Garrett a 1099-K (if applicable) for processing fees.
- Galaxy's books close December 31; accountant completes tax filing by April 15.

### Specific to early-stage bootstrap

- Year 1 may show a tax LOSS (operating expenses > revenue). The loss may offset other Garrett income in the same year, depending on entity structure + filing.
- Lawyer/accountant advises on whether to elect S-Corp treatment for Galaxy (tax-advantaged for higher earners).

This is accountant territory. Don't make decisions without the accountant in the loop.

## Personal runway check

Galaxy's brand position requires Garrett to operate from financial stability, not desperation. Desperation triggers brand-position drift.

### The personal-runway threshold

Garrett's personal financial situation should support at least 6 months of personal expenses without Galaxy income. This is the "operator stability" floor.

If personal runway drops below 6 months:
- Galaxy's brand-position discipline is at risk (desperate operator drifts toward marketing tactics that violate brand position).
- Founder-resilience playbook escalation may fire (per `founder-resilience-playbook.md` § "personal kill criteria").
- Consider: tighter Galaxy operating expenses, owner draw adjustment, personal expense cuts.

Galaxy never compromises the brand position to fund personal runway. The brand position is the moat.

### The cash sources hierarchy

When personal cash is needed, the hierarchy:

1. Garrett's prior savings.
2. Sustainable Galaxy owner draws (only if Galaxy's runway supports it).
3. Personal credit (last resort).
4. Outside capital (only if explicitly aligned with brand position — see Year-2 strategic question #5).

Don't reverse the hierarchy. Specifically: don't take outside capital to fund personal runway. Don't drain Galaxy operating cash to fund personal expenses if Galaxy needs it for operations.

## When to pay from Galaxy vs personal

### Galaxy pays for:

- Direct business expenses (hosting, software, contractor fees, legal/accounting).
- Tools used >50% for Galaxy (e.g., Anthropic API subscription).
- Travel for Galaxy business (founding-partner meeting, press cycle).
- Insurance specific to the business (general liability, professional liability).

### Personal pays for:

- Garrett's living expenses (rent, food, personal transportation).
- Health insurance (until Galaxy can support a group health plan, which is a Year-2+ consideration).
- Personal phone, internet (unless allocated portion for Galaxy use is claimed).
- Personal income tax (paid from Galaxy distributions to Garrett, not directly from Galaxy).
- Anything that's not specifically Galaxy operational.

### Gray-area items

Office space, computer equipment, professional development, conferences — these can be Galaxy-paid OR personal-paid depending on use ratio. Default: 75%+ Galaxy-use → Galaxy pays; <50% Galaxy-use → personal pays; in-between → split or personal.

Don't over-claim Galaxy expenses. Conservative is the right posture for a single-founder business with future acquisition optionality.

## Accountant engagement

### When to hire

Galaxy needs an accountant by Q1 of Year 2 at latest. Some bootstrap founders defer; Galaxy's brand-position discipline suggests don't.

Reasons to hire by Year-2 Q1:
- Tax filings get complex by Year 2 (1099 issuance, S-Corp considerations, contractor reporting).
- Quarterly estimated tax payments require expertise.
- Books need to be in audit-ready state if acquisition conversations begin.

Cost: $1,500-$3,000/year for a competent small-business accountant. Worth it.

### What the accountant does

- Quarterly bookkeeping review.
- Quarterly estimated tax calculation.
- Annual tax filing.
- Year-end financial statements.
- Advice on entity structure decisions.

### What the accountant does NOT do

- Make Galaxy operational decisions.
- Set founder compensation (that's Garrett + lawyer).
- Engage with members or partners directly.
- Touch the methodology, factor model, or any Galaxy product surfaces.

## What Galaxy's financial discipline protects against

### Risk 1: Brand-position compromise under cash pressure

When founders run out of cash, they take partnerships that violate brand position. The personal-runway floor prevents this.

### Risk 2: Tax surprises

When founders defer accountant engagement, they surprise themselves with $20k+ tax bills at filing. Quarterly set-aside prevents this.

### Risk 3: Mixed Galaxy/personal expenses

When Galaxy and personal cash mix, acquisition due diligence becomes painful. Clean separation prevents this.

### Risk 4: Founder financial stress

When Garrett worries about personal finances, Galaxy operating quality drops. Personal runway floor prevents this.

## When founder financial situation changes structurally

### Trigger: Garrett's personal runway drops below 6 months

- Per `founder-resilience-playbook.md` personal kill criteria, this is the threshold for activating the operator-protection escalation.
- Galaxy operations may need to compress (defer optional spending, pause partnership outreach, etc.).
- Founder might need to take a part-time outside job to extend runway.
- Decision-log entry required if this becomes structural.

### Trigger: Galaxy can support founder salary

- Year-2 Q1 typically. Lawyer + accountant set up structured founder compensation.
- Decision-log entry capturing the salary decision + rationale.

### Trigger: Garrett's personal financial situation improves materially

- Galaxy operations may benefit (less pressure on Vault revenue; more capacity for restraint discipline).
- Don't expand Galaxy operations recklessly just because personal cushion grew. Brand position requires deliberate, not opportunistic, scaling.
