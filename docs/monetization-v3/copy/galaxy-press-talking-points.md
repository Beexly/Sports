# Galaxy Press Talking Points

**Audience:** Garrett. Internal.
**Pairs with:** `galaxy-press-kit.md` (the static press surface) + `copy/vault-launch-press-pack.md` (proactive outreach).
**Purpose:** When Garrett does a press interview, podcast, or written Q&A, he needs talking points that are pre-thought-through. The framework below is the brand-aligned phrasing for the questions journalists actually ask.

**Use:** Pre-interview prep. ~10 minutes of review before any external conversation.

---

## The 10 most-likely questions + scripted responses

### Q1 — "Tell me about Galaxy Sports Edge."

**Brand-aligned response (~60 seconds):**

> Galaxy Sports Edge is a sports research platform built on a deterministic factor model. We publish calibrated probability assessments of sports outcomes — but the more interesting thing is what we publish around them. Every losing pick gets a public autopsy. Every game we considered and didn't publish gets archived. The methodology is open. The 2026 record gets bound in print in an annual called the Galaxy Almanac.
>
> The brand position is restraint over volume. We cap publications at about five a day. We don't do tout-certainty content. The discipline of saying no — visible in the Pass List — is the product.

### Q2 — "How is Galaxy different from [competitor]?"

**Brand-aligned response (~45 seconds):**

> [Competitor name] is a strong product with a real audience. The market is large enough for several platforms in this category. The difference is in posture more than features.
>
> Galaxy optimizes for what we publish AROUND the calls — the autopsies, the Pass List, the methodology page. Most platforms optimize for what they publish — the calls themselves. Different bets on what serious bettors want.
>
> We're not trying to be [competitor]. We're trying to be the platform that's still around in 5 years because we built the discipline to admit when we're wrong.

### Q3 — "What's your win rate?"

**Brand-aligned response (~30 seconds):**

> I'd answer that differently. Galaxy publishes calibrated probabilities, not predictions. A 60% confidence call should hit 60% of the time. The honest question is whether the calibration holds — and the answer is at galaxysportsedge.com/methodology. That data updates continuously.
>
> Aggregate win-rate framing tends to be the question that platforms use to sell themselves; it's not the metric I use to evaluate Galaxy.

### Q4 — "Did you make money this year?"

**Brand-aligned response:**

> Galaxy is bootstrapped. I don't share specific revenue or growth figures publicly. What I can say is the business operates with multiple subscription tiers — Pro at $19/month, Elite at $49/month, and Vault at $200/year (capped at 1,000 founding members). The Almanac launches January 15. I'm comfortable with the trajectory we're on.

### Q5 — "Are you raising money?"

**Brand-aligned response:**

> Galaxy is not currently raising. The bet is bootstrap-and-build, and the model the business is built on means we can probably continue that. Whether that changes at some point is a decision I'd make based on specific reasons rather than because outside capital is available.

### Q6 — "Have you considered being acquired?"

**Brand-aligned response:**

> Galaxy is not currently positioned for acquisition conversations. The honest framing: we need more public track record before that becomes a useful conversation. Year 2 or 3 might be the right window if the metrics support it. For now, the work is the work.

### Q7 — "Tell me about your autopsies / Loss Room."

**Brand-aligned response (~60 seconds):**

> The Loss Room is at galaxysportsedge.com/loss-room. Every losing pick we've ever published has an autopsy — a forensic reconstruction of which factor drove the call, which assumption broke, what we'd do differently in retrospect.
>
> We tag each autopsy with one of five root causes: factor underweighted, factor-interaction blind spot, sample-size noise, line-movement misread, or model-version known weakness. Across hundreds of autopsies, patterns emerge — and those patterns feed into the next model version.
>
> The publication discipline depends on this. If losses weren't honestly examined, the model couldn't improve. That's the operating discipline.

### Q8 — "What's the Pass List?"

**Brand-aligned response:**

> The Pass List is at galaxysportsedge.com/passes. Every game we considered publishing and chose not to. Archived with the confidence the model saw, the reason we passed, and (sometimes) a short note on what made it instructive.
>
> Most platforms hide their passes. We don't, because the discipline of not publishing is as important as the discipline of publishing. The Pass List makes that discipline visible.
>
> Some passes are obvious; the model saw 50% confidence and we held back. Some are close calls — 64% confidence on a mid-series game, just below our 65% mid-series floor. Both belong on the list.

### Q9 — "Are you using AI?"

**Brand-aligned response:**

> The factor model is deterministic. It's hand-built; given the same input, it produces the same publication. There's no large-language-model inference in the publication-generation path.
>
> Internally, I use Claude (Anthropic's commercial API) as a writing assistant. It drafts internal documents, helps structure autopsies from factor data, drafts supporting essays for the Almanac. Garrett edits everything before it ships.
>
> The "we're not AI" position refers to the factor model. The writing around the model is Claude-assisted. The distinction matters and we're explicit about it on the methodology page and internal tooling policy.

### Q10 — "What's the hardest thing about Galaxy?"

**Brand-aligned response:**

> The restraint discipline. Every day there's a temptation to publish more, to claim more, to chase the marketing tactics that competitors use. The honest answer is that the brand position requires saying no to most of those tactics. The Pass List is the operational version of that discipline; the marketing discipline is the harder version.

## Anti-patterns — what NOT to say in press

### "We're going to crush [competitor]."

Galaxy doesn't trash-talk competitors. The brand position rejects this framing.

### "Our win rate is X%."

Galaxy publishes calibrated probabilities; aggregate win-rate framing is a marketing convention. Redirect to calibration.

### "I'd love to share more details about our subscriber growth..."

Galaxy doesn't share growth metrics publicly. Redirect to the work.

### "We're disrupting sports analytics."

No "disrupting" language. Galaxy operates against the grain; doesn't claim to disrupt.

### "Our founders are passionate about sports..."

Galaxy is method-led, not personality-led. Don't make the interview about Garrett's personal story.

### "We're going to be huge..."

Galaxy doesn't claim future scale. The bet is restraint compounds; whether that produces "huge" is downstream of doing the work.

### "Outlier's win rate claims are misleading."

Don't speak to competitor claims. Galaxy speaks only to Galaxy's claims.

### "Our customers love us."

Galaxy doesn't speak for customers. The Ledger speaks; the Loss Room speaks; the Pass List speaks.

### "AI is changing everything..."

Galaxy isn't a take-on-AI commentator. Don't take broad industry positions.

### "We have the best methodology..."

Galaxy doesn't claim "best." Galaxy claims "the methodology we built." Different framing.

## Handling difficult questions

### "But isn't the Loss Room just marketing?"

> Maybe. The honest framing: the Loss Room is what Galaxy spends time and effort producing. If a platform claims "we publish our losses" but the autopsies are 80 words long with generic explanations, the Loss Room is marketing. Galaxy's autopsies run 300-500 words with specific factor analysis and root-cause tagging. The quality is the test of whether it's marketing or not.

### "How do I know your data is real?"

> The Ledger is timestamped publicly. Calibration data is continuously updated. The methodology page documents the framework. If you find a discrepancy between what Galaxy claims and what the public data shows, please reach out — I read every email at garrett@galaxysportsedge.com.

### "Most sports analytics platforms are just touts in better clothing. What makes Galaxy different?"

> That's a fair concern. The simple answer is: read the Loss Room. If it looks like the work of a tout service, you have my permission to say so publicly. Galaxy's brand position depends on the artifacts holding up to scrutiny.

### "Doesn't restraint discipline scale poorly?"

> That's an open question I'm watching. Restraint compounds slowly compared to volume-led platforms. Whether it compounds enough to produce a sustainable business is the bet this company is built on. The Vault founding cohort + Almanac sales + Live partnership numbers in Year 1 will tell us whether the bet is paying off. I'm comfortable having that question answered publicly.

### "Are you afraid of [competitor] with $10M in funding?"

> No. They built a real product. Their funding lets them deploy resources Galaxy doesn't have. But the bet I'm running is that brand position compounds differently than UA-spend does. We'll see in a couple of years which model produces the more durable business.

## When to bring specific examples

Specific examples (named games, specific autopsies, specific Pass List entries, specific methodology updates) reinforce brand position in interviews.

When to use them:
- When the journalist asks for specifics. ("Can you give me an example of a recent autopsy?")
- When a general statement risks sounding abstract. Galaxy's brand depends on the artifacts; reference them.
- When a competitor comparison surfaces. Concrete Galaxy examples are stronger than abstract differentiation.

How to use them:
- Name the game + date.
- Describe the factor briefly.
- Reference the autopsy URL (`galaxysportsedge.com/loss-room/[entry]`).
- Don't over-explain. Two sentences max.

The reference itself does the work.
