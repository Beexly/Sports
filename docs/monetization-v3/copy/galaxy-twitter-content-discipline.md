# Galaxy Twitter (@GalaxySportsAI) — Content Discipline

**Audience:** Garrett. Internal.
**Why this exists:** @GalaxySportsAI is Galaxy's primary social media surface. Most platforms over-post on social; Galaxy under-posts on purpose. This document codifies the cadence, topic categories, and what Galaxy doesn't post.

**Update cadence:** quarterly review.

---

## Posting cadence

**Target:** 2-4 posts per week. Not daily.

**Anti-pattern:** daily posting. Galaxy's brand position rejects social media volume.

**Typical week:**
- 1 post linking to the Sunday Model Journal (Sunday morning).
- 1 post linking to the Wednesday digest (Wednesday morning).
- 1-2 posts responsive to the week's Galaxy work (autopsy reference, Pass List highlight, methodology change).

That's it. No daily engagement. No "good morning" posts. No takes on sports outcomes without a Galaxy artifact to link.

---

## Topic categories Galaxy posts

### Category 1 — Publication linking (~50% of posts)

Posts that link to specific Galaxy surfaces:
- New Loss Room autopsy → 1 tweet linking + 1 sentence framing.
- New Pass List entry that's instructive → 1 tweet linking + 1 sentence why.
- New Model Journal → 1 tweet with the headline thread + link.
- New Vault digest landing → 1 tweet linking to the public-side preview (if there is one; mostly digest stays Vault-only).

**Voice:** Galaxy. Restrained. The link is the value; the tweet is the introduction.

**Sample:**
```
Autopsy from yesterday's game: galaxysportsedge.com/loss-room/2026-X-X-game

64% confidence call that lost. Tagged "factor-interaction blind spot." Walkthrough explains what the model composed wrong.
```

### Category 2 — Methodology context (~25% of posts)

Posts that explain Galaxy's methodology in single-tweet form. Not promotional; educational.

**Sample:**
```
Some sports research platforms hide their losses; some bury them. Galaxy publishes every losing pick with a public autopsy.

The Loss Room is at galaxysportsedge.com/loss-room. The discipline is the work.
```

### Category 3 — Responding to industry discussion (~15% of posts)

When sports analytics or methodology questions come up in broader public discussion, Galaxy can contribute — without naming competitors directly.

**Sample (NOT this):**
> "Outlier's win-rate claims are misleading because..."

**Sample (Galaxy voice):**
```
A question that comes up in sports analytics: what does "calibrated" actually mean?

A model is calibrated when, over enough publications, the actual hit rate matches the confidence band. A 60% confidence call should hit 60% of the time. The check is at galaxysportsedge.com/methodology.
```

### Category 4 — Reflection / commentary (~10% of posts)

Galaxy occasionally posts a single-tweet reflection on sports analytics, restraint, or operating discipline. These are the highest-leverage posts but require careful execution.

**Sample:**
```
The discipline of saying no is more visible when it leaves a record. That's what the Pass List is for.

galaxysportsedge.com/passes
```

## What Galaxy explicitly does NOT post

### Anti-pattern 1: Win celebrations

```
"Big W today on our Lakers call! 64% confidence cashed."
```

Galaxy doesn't celebrate wins publicly. The Ledger records them; that's enough.

### Anti-pattern 2: Loss explanations without the autopsy

```
"Tough loss on the Lakers today. Sometimes the variance just doesn't go your way."
```

Galaxy doesn't explain losses in social posts. The autopsy is the explanation; the social post (if any) links to the autopsy.

### Anti-pattern 3: Hot takes on game outcomes

```
"That fumble was the worst football I've ever watched. How does that even happen?"
```

Galaxy is not in the sports-commentary business. Galaxy publishes calibrated probabilities; it doesn't react to game outcomes.

### Anti-pattern 4: Competitor commentary

```
"Just saw Outlier's claimed win rate. We're going to keep doing it differently."
```

Galaxy doesn't mention competitors publicly. Brand-position violation.

### Anti-pattern 5: Founder-personality posts

```
"As a founder, here are 7 things I've learned about building a sports research business..."
```

Galaxy is method-led, not personality-led. No "founder threads."

### Anti-pattern 6: Engagement bait

```
"What's the one factor you think Galaxy should weight more heavily? Reply with your take!"
```

Galaxy doesn't run engagement-optimized prompts. Real factor discussions live in Vault Discord or office hours.

### Anti-pattern 7: Trending-topic chasing

```
"With the Super Bowl coming up, here's why Galaxy's approach is different..."
```

Galaxy posts on Galaxy's cadence, not on the news cycle. Trending-topic posts are reactive; Galaxy is operational.

### Anti-pattern 8: Marketing language

```
"Limited-time founding member rate at Vault. Don't miss your chance to join the room behind the publication!"
```

Galaxy doesn't run marketing campaigns on Twitter. The Vault landing page handles conversion; Twitter doesn't promote.

### Anti-pattern 9: Subtweets

Galaxy doesn't subtweet competitors, critics, or industry figures. Subtweets are a brand-position violation.

### Anti-pattern 10: Emoji-heavy posts

Galaxy uses no emojis on Twitter. Period.

## When Galaxy responds to mentions / replies

### Responding to thoughtful engagement

If a member or non-member replies to a Galaxy post with a substantive question:

- Galaxy responds within 24 business hours.
- The response is brief (1-2 sentences) + links to a Galaxy surface where the full answer lives.
- If the question requires more depth than fits in a tweet thread, Galaxy says "Thread isn't the right place for this — happy to discuss in the next Vault office hours" or "Drop me an email at garrett@galaxysportsedge.com."

### Responding to critical engagement

If a critic posts a thoughtful critique:

- Galaxy responds once. With the answer or with a link to the artifact that answers.
- Galaxy does NOT engage in tweet-thread debate. Single reply; then move on.
- Per `galaxy-crisis-communications-playbook.md` Category 3 + 6.

### Responding to hostile engagement

- Galaxy does NOT respond to hostile tweets, trolls, or low-effort attacks.
- Block sparingly — only for clear harassment.
- Galaxy's brand position depends on staying out of social media slap-fights.

### Responding to media mentions

When a journalist or notable account mentions Galaxy:

- Galaxy retweets only if the mention is brand-aligned (specific Galaxy surface referenced, no win-rate framing, no comparison-table treatment).
- Galaxy doesn't retweet generic mentions.
- Garrett may engage further via email (per `galaxy-press-kit.md`); the retweet is separate from the journalist relationship.

## Tweet voice rules

Apply `galaxy-brand-voice-canonical.md` § "Voice register by surface" — social media section.

Specific to Twitter:
- 1-3 sentences max per tweet (longer goes to Galaxy site, not Twitter).
- No emojis except 🪐 sparingly (every 20+ posts at most).
- No exclamation marks.
- Sentences end. Drop sentences. Galaxy social posts are 1-3 sentences max.
- Link to specific Galaxy surfaces (Loss Room, autopsy, Pass List) more often than to homepage.

## What Galaxy does when something big happens externally

The sports analytics world has occasional big moments — PFF gets acquired (April 2026), Outlier raises $10.7M (December 2025), a competitor ships a controversial feature.

Galaxy's default: stay silent on external news.

The exception: if the external news directly affects Galaxy operations (a regulatory ruling, a major competitor moving on Galaxy's positioning), Galaxy may publish a single tweet linking to a longer Galaxy post (Model Journal or methodology page) addressing the implications.

Default response to "Galaxy, what do you think about [external news]?": "We don't react to news cycles. The work is the work."

## When Garrett posts personally (vs Galaxy posts)

Garrett has a personal Twitter (separate from @GalaxySportsAI).

Personal posts can:
- Reference Galaxy occasionally.
- Discuss sports more casually.
- Comment on industry developments more freely.

Personal posts cannot:
- Reveal Galaxy operational data not public.
- Speak for Galaxy as the brand.
- Engage in personal beefs that reflect on Galaxy.

Galaxy's brand position is "method, not personality." Garrett's personal Twitter is allowed to be more personality-led; just stays distinct from the Galaxy brand voice.

## Quarterly Twitter audit

Once per quarter:

- Read every Galaxy tweet from the past 90 days.
- Mark which fit the categories above (substantive, methodology-aligned, restraint-aligned).
- Mark which drifted (toward marketing, toward founder-personality, toward reactive commentary).
- Compute: what % of tweets were brand-aligned?

**Healthy:** 90%+ brand-aligned.
**Mixed:** 75-89% brand-aligned. Investigate drift patterns.
**Drifting:** <75% brand-aligned. Reset the discipline; reread this document; consider 4-week posting hiatus.

---

## Engagement metrics — what Galaxy ignores

Galaxy does not optimize for:
- Follower count growth rate.
- Tweet impressions.
- Reply rate.
- Engagement rate (likes + replies + RTs).

These metrics drift Galaxy toward engagement-optimization tactics that violate brand position.

Galaxy tracks one thing: did the post link traffic to a Galaxy surface (Loss Room, Pass List, methodology page, Vault landing)?

That's the only metric. Everything else is noise.

---

## When Galaxy should consider not posting that week

Some weeks, Galaxy doesn't have a Loss Room autopsy worth featuring, doesn't have a Pass List highlight, doesn't have a methodology change. The Model Journal still goes up Sunday; the digest still goes to Vault members Wednesday.

But Twitter posts that week may be 1-2 instead of 3-4. That's fine.

The discipline: don't post to fill the calendar. Post because there's something specific to point at.

If 4+ consecutive weeks pass without a Galaxy tweet: investigate. Either Galaxy is undersharing (the discipline became silence) or there's nothing to share (Galaxy's output has slowed).
