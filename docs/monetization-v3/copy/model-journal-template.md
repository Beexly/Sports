# Galaxy Model Journal — Weekly Template

**Cadence:** Saturday morning draft → Sunday morning publish.
**Vault benefit:** Vault members get Saturday's draft, before Sunday's publication. Public Galaxy site shows only the Sunday-published version.
**Author:** Garrett (founder-voice). Claude may draft Saturday's first cut from model output; Garrett edits before either surface sees it.
**Length:** 800–1,400 words. Reads in 6–10 minutes.

---

## What the Model Journal is (and what it's not)

The Model Journal is Galaxy's **weekly reflective publication on what the model believed, learned, or changed**. It is NOT a pick. It is NOT a forecast. It is NOT a methodology page update.

It's a public reflection on a single thread from the week — something the model surfaced, something a publication revealed, something a pass illustrated, something the data pattern is teaching across multiple publications.

**Compared to other Galaxy publications:**

| Surface | Cadence | Voice | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Board | Live | Method | Current publications |
| Ledger | Continuous | Method | Settled picks |
| Loss Room | Per loss | Forensic | Autopsies |
| Pass List | Daily | Compact | Games considered, not published |
| Methodology | Continuous | Structural | Framework + factor weights |
| Vault digest | Weekly Wed | Personal | Deep dive on one publication |
| **Model Journal** | **Weekly Sun** | **Reflective** | **Public weekly thread on what we're seeing** |
| Quarterly review | Quarterly | Internal data | Vault-only calibration deep-dive |
| Year-end report | Annual Dec 31 | Year retrospective | Free public summary |
| Almanac | Annual Jan 15 | Bound record | Paid 300-page reference |

The Model Journal is the **only weekly public Galaxy long-form publication.** It's where Galaxy is most likely to gain new readers via search + word-of-mouth.

## Structural template

Every Model Journal entry has 4 sections in this order. Section lengths flex; total target 800–1,400 words.

### Section 1 — The thread (~150 words)

The week's reflective thread, stated plainly. What's the one thing this Model Journal is going to walk through?

This is the section that gets indexed by search engines + quoted in member discussions. Spend the most time on the first paragraph.

**Template:**

> This week's Model Journal is about [specific thread]. It came up in [specific publication / pass / autopsy / methodology question]. The reason it's worth a whole publication is [specific argument].
>
> Before the walk-through: [brief context the reader needs to follow what comes next].

### Section 2 — The walk-through (~400–700 words)

The deepest section. Walk through the specific publication, pass, autopsy, or data pattern that drove the thread. Specific. Quantified. Honest.

**Acceptable angles for the walk-through:**

- A specific publication: what happened, why the model published, what we'd reconsider with hindsight.
- A specific pass: why we passed, what the game taught (or didn't), whether the publication threshold did its job.
- A specific autopsy: which root cause tagged, what the pattern is across recent autopsies, whether a methodology change is shipping.
- A specific factor: which factor the week revealed something about, how its weighting is or isn't holding up.
- A specific methodology question: a member-raised or operator-surfaced question that benefits from depth.

**What the walk-through always includes:**

- The specific game(s) or publication(s) being discussed (named, dated).
- The factor weighting reasoning (described qualitatively; no exact weights).
- The data that supports or undermines the call (calibration check where relevant).
- Honest uncertainty markers ("we don't know yet whether..." / "the sample is small enough that...").

**What the walk-through doesn't include:**

- Predictions about future games.
- Win-rate boasts.
- Comparisons to specific competitors by name.
- Promotional language about Vault, Almanac, or other Galaxy products.
- Exclamation marks, generic enthusiasm, founder-personality detours.

### Section 3 — The harder question (~150–300 words)

The hardest part of the publication — and the part that makes the Model Journal uniquely Galaxy.

This section names a question the week's walk-through DOESN'T fully answer. The Model Journal is Galaxy's "what we don't know" publication. Each week, name one specific open question with intellectual honesty.

**Template:**

> The harder question I'm sitting with this week: [specific question].
>
> [2-3 paragraphs exploring why the question is open. What evidence we'd need to answer it. What we currently believe but can't yet verify. Why the question matters for the next 30-90 days of Galaxy's operations.]
>
> If you have a read on this — drop it in [#vault-feedback for members / direct email to Garrett for non-members]. The Model Journal is Galaxy's most open-ended publication on purpose.

### Section 4 — What I'm watching (~100–250 words)

The closing section. Brief. Forward-looking but not predictive.

Three to five specific things Galaxy is watching in the coming week — factors, publications, data patterns, market moves — that might surface in next week's Model Journal.

**Template:**

> What I'm watching in the coming week:
>
> 1. [Specific factor / pattern / publication]. Why it matters: [one line].
> 2. [Same].
> 3. [Same].
>
> Next Model Journal: Sunday [date].
>
> — Garrett · [Saturday draft date]

The "what I'm watching" list serves two purposes:
- Tells the reader what next week's Journal might explore.
- Tells Garrett what to track during the week — the list functions as Garrett's own reading list for the next 7 days.

## Sample Model Journal (specimen)

Below is a Claude-drafted specimen of a complete Model Journal entry. Galaxy specifics are placeholders; Garrett's edit pass fills in the year-specific reality.

---

```
# Model Journal — Week of 26 May 2026
## The factor we keep underweighting

This week's Model Journal is about a factor the model has been undervaluing for at least eight weeks. It surfaced in last week's autopsy on Atlanta-Tampa Bay, surfaced again in the Boston-Miami autopsy ten days earlier, and showed up implicitly in the Knicks-Pacers G6 pass that we walked through in the public Pass List.

The factor: **the interaction between defensive line health-adjusted EPA and the opposing team's late-game rapid-scoring efficiency in one-score game scripts.**

Before the walk-through: when a defense is healthy and getting after the quarterback, the model assumes the offense becomes one-dimensional and clock-management becomes easier for the leading team. That's a Category 2 (situational) factor, weighted at medium. When the leading team is up by 4-7 in the fourth quarter and the trailing team has the ball in their own territory, the model has historically given 75-80% probability to the leading team running out the clock successfully.

That model assumption has been failing. Three of the last five "factor underweighted" autopsies tag this interaction. The leading team's defense holds; the trailing team scores anyway — usually via one explosive play that wasn't priced into the late-game script.

## The walk-through

Atlanta-Tampa Bay was the cleanest example. Atlanta led by 6 with 4 minutes left. Tampa Bay had ~80 yards to go in their own territory. Atlanta's defensive line was healthy and pressuring Tampa Bay's QB at the rate we'd expect. The factor said: ~78% Atlanta closes out.

What actually happened: Atlanta's defense did hold. Tampa Bay had to convert two fourth downs and a 47-yard touchdown drive that ended with 11 seconds left. The factor was correct on the assumption (Atlanta's defense was as effective as expected) but the assumption produced the wrong outcome (Atlanta covered -3.5 if the score had stayed). The cover failed because Tampa Bay's offense in compressed time was outside the factor's distribution.

Across the three autopsies tagging this pattern, the common element is: leading team's defense was effective; trailing team's offense converted at a rate the model didn't expect in one-score game scripts.

The honest read: the model has been weighting "defense holds" as if it correlates 1-to-1 with "lead protected." Empirically, in one-score late-game scripts, a 4-7 point lead can survive even when the trailing team scores rapidly — but the cover often doesn't. The factor model treats lead-protection and cover-protection as the same outcome. They aren't.

A fix is shipping in v5.4. The fix splits the factor into two:
- "Defense holds" → predicts the trailing team's average scoring rate.
- "Cover survives" → predicts the trailing team's *variance* in scoring rate (specifically, the right-tail rapid-scoring scenarios).

v5.4 will publish 2-4% lower confidence on similar one-score game-script publications going forward. We'll watch calibration over the next 8-12 weeks.

## The harder question

The harder question I'm sitting with this week: how do you know when a factor is genuinely a blind spot versus when the pattern across autopsies is sample-size noise?

Three autopsies tagged with the same factor-interaction issue is suggestive. It's not definitive. Three independent autopsies share a tag could mean: (a) the factor really is broken in this specific case, (b) the autopsy taxonomy is over-categorizing toward our recent attention bias, or (c) we're seeing a pattern that would also appear in a randomized counterfactual model with no underlying flaw.

We don't have the tools to distinguish (a) from (b) from (c) yet. v5.4's fix is a bet that (a) is the right read. If calibration improves over the next 12 weeks, that's confirmation. If it doesn't improve — or worse, if calibration drifts further away — we'll have to revisit whether the fix is correct.

The harder question behind the question: at what point does Galaxy's operational discipline (admit factor-interaction issues quickly, ship fixes, watch calibration) overshoot into noise-chasing? I don't have a clean answer. Open in the Vault Discord if anyone has a read.

## What I'm watching

What I'm watching in the coming week:

1. Whether the Atlanta-Tampa Bay factor-interaction pattern shows up in the upcoming Brooklyn-Boston rest-differential publications. Different sport, possibly related interaction.
2. Whether the late-line-movement check (Gate 4 of the publication process) caught any cases where the line moved in a direction the model's factor weighting wouldn't have predicted.
3. NHL playoff publications — small sample, less factor data, but the same one-score-game-script pattern might surface in OT contexts.
4. Whether v5.4 ships on schedule. The factor-interaction split is non-trivial; the changelog should reflect both the fix and its rationale.

Next Model Journal: Sunday 2 June 2026.

— Garrett
Saturday, 31 May 2026 (Vault preview)
```

## Saturday draft → Sunday publish cadence

The Model Journal has two timestamps:

**Saturday morning (Vault preview):** Draft published to Vault Discord + Vault digest archive. Members can read, comment, and push back. Garrett may incorporate member input into the Sunday version.

**Sunday morning (public publish):** Public version published at `/journal/[YYYY-MM-DD]`. Linked from homepage + Twitter + Galaxy email list.

The Saturday-to-Sunday gap is part of what Vault members pay for. Three things can happen between Saturday draft and Sunday publish:

1. **No changes.** Garrett's Saturday draft is publication-ready. Sunday version is identical except for the dateline.
2. **Light revision.** Vault member raises a clarifying question or surfaces a typo. Sunday version incorporates.
3. **Material rewrite.** A Vault member raises a substantive concern about the walk-through. Garrett rewrites the affected section. The Saturday draft is preserved in Vault archive as the original; Sunday version is annotated with a brief "revised after Vault discussion" footer if the change is material.

Material revisions should be rare — once a quarter at most. If they happen weekly, the Saturday-Sunday gap isn't producing better content; it's producing operator instability. Tighten the Saturday draft quality.

## What the Model Journal is NOT (in order of importance)

1. **Not a methodology page update.** The methodology page describes the framework. The Model Journal describes a thread within it.
2. **Not a Vault digest.** The digest is deeper, member-only, structured around one specific publication. The Model Journal is public, reflective, weekly.
3. **Not a Loss Room autopsy.** Autopsies are per-loss forensic reports. The Journal might reference an autopsy as part of a thread.
4. **Not a pick.** Galaxy doesn't publish picks in the Model Journal.
5. **Not a podcast script.** The Journal stands as a written publication. If Garrett uses Journal material on a podcast, the podcast references the Journal; the Journal is not the podcast.
6. **Not a Twitter thread.** Galaxy doesn't break Journal content into engagement-optimized thread format. The Journal is long-form, single-page.

## Production timeline (per week)

**Wednesday–Friday (during the week):** Garrett notes 2-4 candidate threads in a running journal-prep doc. Pull factor data, autopsy patterns, pass list patterns as they accumulate.

**Saturday morning, 09:00–11:00 (Garrett's peak energy block):** Pick the thread. Write the Section 2 walk-through first (the hardest part). Then Sections 1, 3, 4 in order. Draft length: 800–1,400 words.

**Saturday morning, 11:00–11:30:** Read aloud test. Brand-safety scanner pass. Fix anything that doesn't sound like Galaxy voice.

**Saturday 12:00 noon:** Publish Saturday draft to Vault Discord + members-only archive. Members get notification.

**Saturday 12:00 – Sunday 08:00 (~20 hours):** Vault members read, optionally respond.

**Sunday 08:00–09:00 (Garrett):** Read Vault Discord responses. Decide whether to revise. Update Sunday version.

**Sunday 09:00:** Public publish at `/journal/[date]`. Email to Galaxy subscribers. Twitter + Discord public announcement.

**Total Garrett time:** ~3-4 hours per week, primarily Saturday morning.

## Failure modes to watch for

### Failure 1: Journal becomes formulaic

Each week's thread starts to follow the same template too rigidly. The walk-through gets predictable; the harder question gets shallow.

**Counter:** quarterly audit. Read the prior 12 entries. Do they sound like the same person writing the same article 12 times? If yes, deliberately break the template next quarter — write a Journal entry that doesn't follow the 4-section structure (with explicit "this week's Journal departs from the standard format because..." opening).

### Failure 2: Journal becomes promotional

Galaxy's brand position rejects promotional content. The Journal must not drift into "join Vault to see deeper" or "buy the Almanac" pitches.

**Counter:** brand-safety scanner pass before every publish. Specifically scan for marketing language. If a sentence feels promotional, rewrite.

### Failure 3: Journal becomes safe

Section 3 (the harder question) is the highest-risk section to write. It's tempting to make the question soft, abstract, or already-answered.

**Counter:** the harder question must be something Garrett genuinely doesn't know the answer to. If Garrett can give a confident answer, the question is too soft. Pick a different question.

### Failure 4: Journal gets late

Saturday morning peak energy block gets eaten by reactive work. Garrett writes the Journal Saturday evening or Sunday morning.

**Counter:** Saturday morning is reserved. If genuinely unavoidable (illness, family emergency), publish a brief "Journal delayed this week — back next Sunday" post. Don't ship a sloppy Journal.
