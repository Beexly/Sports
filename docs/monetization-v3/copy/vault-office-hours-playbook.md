# Vault Office Hours — Playbook

**Cadence:** Second Tuesday of every month, 8:00 PM Eastern.
**Duration:** 60 minutes live. Run over by ≤10 minutes if conversation is strong; do not run under 50 minutes (members value the full hour).
**Format:** Discord stage in `#vault-office-hours`. Audio-first; Garrett's camera on. Members audio-on optional.
**Capacity:** V1 cap at 100 attendees per session (Discord stage limit). At >100, set up overflow Discord voice channel + post recording within 24 hours.
**Attendance expectation:** ~30–60% of active Vault members attend each session live. 80–90% watch recording within 7 days.

---

## Why office hours exist (the brand-position lens)

Office hours are the live counterpart to the digest. The digest is Garrett LEADING — picking the publication, explaining the rationale, naming the assumption. Office hours invert that: members lead, Garrett answers.

If office hours becomes Garrett presenting more content, the product has drifted. The goal is the conversation Galaxy can't have on its public surfaces — questions that take more than a tweet to answer, methodology details that surface in real Q&A, autopsy walk-throughs that get deeper because members push.

Done right, office hours are the single most-cited reason in member NPS surveys that Vault is worth $200/year. Done wrong, they become content-marketing webinars that members skip.

## Pre-session prep (week-of, ~90 minutes total)

### Monday before OH (60 min)

- [ ] Open `#vault-feedback` and read every message posted in the last 4 weeks.
- [ ] Open `#vault-lounge` and read every thread that didn't get a Garrett reply.
- [ ] Compile a "questions members might ask" list (≥10 candidates). These are the questions Garrett should be ready for, even if members don't bring them up.
- [ ] Identify 2-3 specific publications/passes/losses from the month worth walking through if members ask. Pull the underlying factor data for each (so Garrett can speak specifically, not generically).
- [ ] Note any methodology change or planned change worth surfacing during OH if a member opens that door.

### Day-of (30 min, 1 hour before session)

- [ ] Test Discord stage audio.
- [ ] Confirm cloud recording is enabled.
- [ ] Open the relevant tabs: factor model output for the month, Loss Room recent entries, Pass List recent entries, methodology page, Vault decision-log entries from the month.
- [ ] Phone on do-not-disturb.
- [ ] Quiet space. Door closed. Water nearby.
- [ ] Notes pad open — capture every question asked + every commitment made during OH.

## The 60-minute structure

The structure is loose by design. Pre-rehearsed talking points kill the format. Below is the rhythm, not a script.

### Minutes 0–5 — Open

```
"Hey, glad you're here. This is [month] office hours. Two ground rules I'll set every time:

One — members lead. I'll try to talk less than half the hour. If I'm dominating, push back.

Two — anything's fair game. Methodology, losses, passes, the model, the roadmap, things I haven't published about, things I should be publishing about. If it's not a question I can answer publicly, I'll say so."
```

Then **stop and wait**. Silence is fine. The first question always feels slow. Don't fill the space.

### Minutes 5–45 — Q&A flow

Members raise hands (Discord stage feature) → Garrett brings them up to speak.

**Rules for Garrett:**
- Answer the question that was asked, not the question Garrett wishes was asked.
- If you don't know, say "I don't know." Don't extrapolate to look knowledgeable.
- If a question requires data you don't have at hand, commit to a follow-up in `#vault-lounge` within 48 hours. Track the commitment in notes pad.
- If a question opens a methodology question that benefits from deeper walk-through, walk through it (5–10 minutes is fine). If the same question gets asked recurrently, that's the digest topic for next Wednesday.
- If a question is too narrow (one specific bet outcome, one specific player), reframe to the underlying factor or assumption. "Specifically that bet is in the Ledger; the broader pattern you're surfacing is X" — then talk about X.

**Rules for managing the room:**
- One member per turn. Don't let one voice dominate.
- If a question is hostile or off-brand, acknowledge it directly. "I hear the frustration. The model isn't perfect; the autopsy on [recent loss] is in the Loss Room. Want to walk through that?" Redirects without shutting down.
- If multiple members are aligned on a question Garrett hasn't surfaced before, that's signal. Capture it.

### Minutes 45–55 — Garrett-led wrap

This is the 10 minutes where Garrett leads. Three sub-segments:

**45–48 — One methodology note.** Pick something the month surfaced — a factor that got reweighted, a new tag in the autopsy taxonomy, a question Vault Discord raised that changed how Garrett thinks about a publication. 2-3 minutes max. Not a presentation; just a brief.

**48–52 — One commitment.** Name one thing Garrett is doing differently in the next 30 days because of this month's office hours conversation. Specific. Concrete.

**52–55 — Calendar.** Next office hours date. What's planned for the coming month's digests. Whether there's a quarterly data review coming up.

### Minutes 55–60 — Soft close

```
"Last 5 minutes — anything we didn't get to that should be in next month's session? Drop it in #vault-feedback or just shout it here.

Thanks for the hour. The recording goes up in #vault-office-hours by tomorrow morning."
```

Wait 60 seconds for any final questions. End the recording. Post a 1-sentence thank-you in #vault-lounge.

## Post-session protocol (within 24 hours)

- [ ] Upload recording to `#vault-office-hours`. File-name format: `vault-oh-2026-MM.mp4`.
- [ ] Write a 3–5 sentence summary post in `#vault-lounge`: "What we talked about in office hours. Main thread: [topic]. One commitment I made: [specific]. Replay link: [URL]."
- [ ] Follow up on every commitment made during the session. Post in `#vault-lounge` thread for each.
- [ ] Add any new "would members ask this" patterns from the session to the Monday prep list for next month.
- [ ] Note any member who asked a particularly sharp question — they're candidates for early Vault advisory channel involvement (Phase 6+ feature).

## Question patterns that recur (and how to handle them)

### Pattern 1: "What's the model's record this month?"

Galaxy voice rejects "win rate as primary metric" framing. But the question is legitimate.

**Response approach:**
- Don't dodge. Pull the actual Ledger data and walk through it: "X picks settled this month, Y won, Z lost. The pattern in the losses is the more interesting story — three of the five losses share [specific factor pattern]."
- Reframe from "record" to "calibration" if you can: "The more honest question is whether the confidence numbers calibrated. A 65% confidence call should hit 65% of the time over a long enough sample. This month was [over/under] calibration."
- Don't celebrate or apologize. Just describe.

### Pattern 2: "What's your real win rate? You can tell us in here."

Sometimes asked playfully, sometimes seriously. The implication: public Ledger numbers are softened.

**Response approach:** Direct contradiction.
- "Public Ledger numbers are the actual numbers. Nothing in Vault sees better data than the public site. The thing Vault sees better is the reasoning behind the public numbers — not different numbers."
- If member doesn't believe it, walk them through the Loss Room methodology + the data flow. Repeat until they do.

### Pattern 3: "Will you cover [sport Galaxy doesn't currently cover]?"

Most-asked Vault question. Demand is real but execution requires substantial infrastructure work.

**Response approach:**
- Honest about constraints: "Adding [sport] means [N hours] of data integration + [factor model rework] + [methodology page expansion]. Currently, that capacity is allocated to [current priority]."
- Honest about timeline: "Realistically, [sport] is Year 2 if it happens, not this year."
- If 3+ members ask the same sport recurrently, that's signal. Capture in #vault-feedback.

### Pattern 4: "What does Galaxy do that you're embarrassed about?"

Brand-aligned question. Members are inviting Garrett to publicly self-criticize.

**Response approach:**
- Don't deflect. Pick something real.
- Example: "The methodology page is dense. Members tell me they read the first 3 pages and skim the rest. I haven't figured out how to fix that without losing the precision."
- Example: "We don't publish a Pass List annotation for difficulty — every pass looks equivalent on the public page, even though some are obvious passes and some were close calls. We've talked about adding the annotation; haven't shipped it."

The self-criticism doesn't have to be world-shaking. It just has to be honest.

### Pattern 5: "Have you considered [competitor's feature]?"

Outlier and other competitors ship features Galaxy doesn't. Members will ask.

**Response approach:**
- Don't trash the competitor. Galaxy's brand requires civility.
- Honest about scope: "I've seen [feature]. The version we'd build wouldn't look like [competitor]'s version because [Galaxy-specific posture reason]. Whether we build it depends on whether the room is asking for it."
- If 3+ members ask, capture as Vault advisory signal.

### Pattern 6: "Can you predict [upcoming game]?"

Galaxy's brand position rejects "predictions" framing. Members occasionally don't internalize this distinction.

**Response approach:**
- Soft redirect: "Galaxy doesn't predict — Galaxy publishes calibrated probabilities. Predictions imply a single outcome with confidence; probabilities imply a distribution with uncertainty. The publication for [game] is on the site (or it isn't — that's the Pass List)."
- Don't lecture. State once and move on.

### Pattern 7: "Is [recent loss] a sign the model needs to change?"

Vault members come from a high-engagement cohort; they're paying attention to results.

**Response approach:**
- Reference Loss Room autopsy. "The autopsy is in the Loss Room. Tagged [root cause]. The pattern question — does this loss point to a structural model issue — is what I'm watching. So far, no."
- Honest about uncertainty: "If three more similar losses show up in the next 4 weeks, that's a pattern. One loss is a data point."

## Quarterly data review office hours (special format)

Every third month, office hours doubles as the public-discussion-portion of the quarterly data review.

**Modified structure:**
- 0–10 min: Garrett walks through the quarterly data deck (NOT the full deck — the highlight version).
- 10–50 min: Standard Q&A.
- 50–60 min: Garrett-led wrap with quarterly commitment.

The full quarterly data review PDF + recorded walkthrough is delivered separately (per Vault PRD). The quarterly OH is the live counterpart.

## Conference-mode office hours (V2 milestone)

When Vault hits V2 cap (5,000 members), monthly office hours can't accommodate everyone live. Possible adaptations:

- **Multi-session OH:** Two sessions per month, different time zones.
- **Garrett + invited guest format:** Once a quarter, invite a Vault member to co-host a topic-specific session.
- **Annual Vault Conference:** The yearly capstone event. 200 in-person, 1,500 livestream.

V1 doesn't worry about scale. Solve at V2 milestone.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

1. **Prepared talking points / slides.** Office hours is not a webinar. If Garrett walks in with a deck, the format has drifted.
2. **Solo dominance.** If Garrett talks ≥75% of the hour, members feel like spectators.
3. **Promises Garrett can't keep.** If a question requires data or infrastructure that doesn't exist, don't promise it's coming next month. Honest timelines or honest uncertainty.
4. **Defensive responses to criticism.** If a member criticizes a publication, the right response is "yes, here's the autopsy and what I'd do differently" — not "well, the model still landed at 64% which is calibrated."
5. **"That's a great question."** Empty validation. Just answer.
6. **Side conversations in chat that distract from the speaker.** Discord stage has a chat sidebar; members will use it. Garrett should mostly ignore the chat during the session — focus on the speaker, address chat topics in the wrap if relevant.

## Compliance during office hours

Office hours are live and unscripted. The brand-safety scanner can't run on them in real-time. Garrett applies the discipline:

- **No predictions of specific game outcomes.** Talk about model output, not future results.
- **No claims that Vault members get better picks.** Vault is depth, not access.
- **No comparison to Outlier metrics.** Brand position, not benchmarking.
- **No language that promises returns or edge in dollars.** Galaxy doesn't promise edges.
- **No naming of competitors in a hostile frame.** Different posture, not "better."

If Garrett slips during OH (it happens), correct in `#vault-lounge` within 24 hours with a clarification post. Members see the correction; brand position recovers.

## Recording transcription + searchable archive

Every OH recording gets auto-transcribed (Otter, Granola, or equivalent). Transcripts get indexed and searchable in `#vault-office-hours`.

Why this matters: by Year 2, the OH archive becomes a corpus that supports:
- Almanac source material (members can search "what Garrett said about [topic] in [month]").
- Methodology page expansions (recurring questions become published methodology FAQs).
- New-member onboarding (new founding members can read the most-watched 3 OHs as backfill).

Codex action: integrate Otter or equivalent into Discord OH recording pipeline. ~$15/month tool. Make searchable inside Discord.

## Office hours success metrics (rolling 6-month)

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Live attendance (% of active Vault members) | ≥40% |
| Recording watch (within 7 days of session) | ≥80% |
| Member questions per session | ≥15 |
| Garrett talk-time (% of session) | ≤55% |
| Member NPS specifically asking about OH | ≥60 |
| Commitments made by Garrett kept within stated timeline | ≥90% |

Below threshold on any metric for 2 consecutive months → investigate. Most likely: OH format has drifted; reset by re-reading this playbook.
