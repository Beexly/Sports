# Outlier vs Galaxy — Competitive Battlecard

**Date:** 2026-05-22
**Author:** Claude
**Purpose:** Strategic content for Galaxy operators and creators (Garrett, partners, Vault members, future BD hires) on how to position Galaxy against Outlier. Internal-only by default; specific sections can be lifted into public surfaces (Vault office hours, methodology page) once reviewed.

## Who Outlier is

- **Company:** Outlier (outlier.bet) — sports betting research platform.
- **Headquarters:** New Orleans, LA.
- **Funding:** $10.7M Series A in December 2025. Led by Discerning Capital. Approximately $5.7M equity + $5M user-acquisition financing.
  - (Note: v3 master plan originally cited "November 2025" — Codex's source spot-check in `08-source-assumptions.md` corrects this to December 3-4, 2025 per public reporting.)
- **Subscribers:** "Tens of thousands of paying subscribers." "Hundreds of thousands of users" across US, UK, Australia.
- **Product:** App-based subscription. Multi-sport research tool. App Store presence with 14k+ five-star reviews.
- **Positioning:** "Market-leading sports betting research tool." "Data-driven." "Customer-focused." Subscription-only per primary positioning.

## Why Outlier is Galaxy's most direct competitor

1. **Same category framing:** subscription-based sports research, not affiliate-based picks service.
2. **Same buyer profile target:** weekly+ sports bettors who want research before bets.
3. **Same product structure:** odds + analytics + research content behind a subscription.
4. **Same scale ambition:** Outlier raised UA capital, signaling intent to grow subscriber count aggressively in 2026.

## Why Outlier is NOT Galaxy

The differences matter more than the similarities.

| Dimension | Outlier | Galaxy |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand position** | "Data-driven research tool" (generic) | "Math you can read" (contrarian) |
| **Loss transparency** | Not publicly emphasized | Loss Room is a primary surface — every loss has an autopsy |
| **Pass transparency** | No public equivalent | Pass List — every considered game, archived |
| **Methodology transparency** | Limited public disclosure | Methodology page open + frozen annually in Almanac |
| **Pick volume** | Broad coverage across markets | Restraint-led — ~5 picks/day cap |
| **Founder visibility** | Team-led | Founder-led editorial voice |
| **Revenue model** | Subscription (multi-tier, multi-app feature) | Subscription + Vault premium tier + Almanac product + Live track |
| **Acquirer profile** | Single-product sportsbook ecosystem buyer | Multi-acquirer optionality (Vision Pro / Vanta-shape / Better Collective) |
| **Scale advantage** | 2+ years ahead on subscribers | None yet — pre-scale |
| **Capital advantage** | $10.7M warchest | Bootstrapped, time-pressed |

## The strategic read

Outlier is at the **scale-and-distribute** phase of company development. They've validated product-market fit (tens of thousands of subscribers prove the category works) and are now investing $5M in user acquisition to convert that PMF into category leadership before competitors emerge.

Galaxy is at the **brand-depth-and-restraint** phase. We can't match Outlier's UA spend; we don't need to. The path Galaxy can win is a path Outlier structurally cannot take — radical transparency + restraint discipline + multi-line acquisition optionality. Outlier optimized for subscriber scale; Galaxy optimizes for brand uniqueness + portfolio.

**The market is large enough for both.** US sports bettors: 52M. Weekly bettors: 15M. Research-buying segment: 750k. Both Outlier (tens of thousands) and Galaxy (target 30k Vault + 150k Pro/Elite at maturity) fit inside that addressable pool with room to spare.

## Counter-positioning rules — what to say

When Outlier comes up in conversation (with a prospect, in a press interview, on a podcast, in Vault office hours), here's the language to use.

### What to say:

1. **"Different product to different audiences."**
   > "Outlier is the tool you use because you bet often and want data. Galaxy is the philosophy you subscribe to because you don't trust the industry. They're both legitimate. They just attract different readers."

2. **"They scaled subscribers; we're scaling brand."**
   > "Outlier raised $10.7M in December and they're investing it in user acquisition. We respect the build. We're taking a different path — restraint-led brand depth instead of scale-led market share."

3. **"They publish data. We publish accountability."**
   > "Outlier publishes a lot of useful data. The thing we do differently is publish what didn't work. The Loss Room and the Pass List don't have public equivalents on Outlier's product. That's not a flaw of Outlier's — it's a different choice."

4. **"We're not trying to be Outlier."**
   > "We're not chasing subscriber count. Galaxy's optimization function is multi-track — Vault, the Almanac, the Live streamer overlays, the methodology layer that B2B buyers will eventually license. Outlier optimizes for one number. We optimize for the portfolio."

### What NOT to say:

1. **Don't say Outlier is bad.** Outlier is a real product with a real audience. Trash-talking competitors violates Galaxy's brand position. The right competitive posture is "different shape," not "we're better."

2. **Don't compare picks accuracy.** Outlier doesn't publish detailed accuracy data; neither do most competitors. Direct accuracy comparisons require apples-to-apples calibration data Galaxy can't externally verify. Don't make claims that depend on comparisons Galaxy can't defend.

3. **Don't claim Outlier hides their losses.** We don't know Outlier's internal practices. Galaxy can describe what GALAXY does (publish losses) without making accusations about what OUTLIER doesn't do.

4. **Don't engage on price comparison.** Outlier's pricing is in the $20-50/month range based on tier; Galaxy's is similar. Price comparisons are non-strategic — they put both products on the same shelf instead of differentiating them.

5. **Don't say "we're the anti-Outlier."** Counter-positioning is more effective when it's self-defining (what Galaxy IS) than when it's against-defining (what Galaxy ISN'T). Anti-language gives Outlier more relevance to Galaxy's brand than they deserve.

## Specific scenarios + scripted responses

### Scenario 1: A potential Galaxy subscriber asks "How are you different from Outlier?"

**Response:**
> "Outlier is a strong product — they raised $10.7M in December because the category they're in is real. The difference is in posture, not features. Outlier optimizes for breadth of coverage and data depth. Galaxy optimizes for transparency about what didn't work. Our Loss Room — every losing pick has a public autopsy — is the surface I'd point to that doesn't have a direct Outlier equivalent. If you value research-as-tool, you'll probably like both. If you value research-as-accountability, Galaxy's specifically built around that."

### Scenario 2: A reporter asks "Doesn't Outlier already do what Galaxy does?"

**Response:**
> "There's overlap, but the products optimize for different things. Outlier is in the scale-and-distribute phase — they're investing the $10.7M they raised in user acquisition. Galaxy's path is different. We're not chasing subscriber count as the primary metric. The Loss Room, the Pass List, the public methodology page, the annual Almanac — these are surfaces that prioritize trust over reach. The market's large enough for both products. We're not trying to be Outlier."

### Scenario 3: A Vault member asks "Why should I be in Vault instead of paying for Outlier?"

**Response:**
> "You can do both. Outlier gives you a research tool. Vault gives you closer access to how Galaxy specifically thinks — the internal rationale digest, the monthly conversations, the quarterly internals that don't get published anywhere. If you're using Galaxy's site already and want the depth behind it, Vault is the upgrade. If you want broader research coverage, Outlier's product probably serves that better. They solve different problems."

### Scenario 4: A streamer partner asks "Why should my audience pay for Galaxy if they can get Outlier?"

**Response:**
> "Your audience is going to make their own decision on that — and some will end up with both, some will end up with neither, some with one or the other. What I'd focus on with your audience is what makes Galaxy specifically relevant. If your audience values research-as-accountability — they care that someone publishes what didn't work — Galaxy reads more naturally for them than Outlier does. Both products are legitimate. We just sell to different sensibilities."

### Scenario 5: A potential investor asks "What's your moat against Outlier with $10.7M to spend?"

**Response:**
> "Outlier can spend their warchest on user acquisition; they cannot buy Galaxy's brand position. The 'we're not AI, we're math you can read' framing is a specific operator's voice — Garrett's. It can be copied structurally but the brand depth comes from years of consistent restraint, the kind that money can't accelerate. The acquisition optionality is different too — Outlier is a single-product subscription company chasing a single exit. Galaxy is a portfolio: Vault, the Almanac, Live streamer track, and the eventual Vision LLC + Integrity B2B + Compliance vertical. Each line has its own acquirer profile. Outlier optimizes for one number; Galaxy optimizes for many."

### Scenario 6: A subscriber says "Outlier just released X feature — when will Galaxy do that?"

**Response:**
> "I won't speak to Outlier's roadmap because I don't know it. What I'll say is Galaxy doesn't ship features because competitors ship them. The roadmap is driven by what Vault advisory tells us, what the methodology page audits reveal, and what the data shows is the next honest direction. If [feature X] is something a lot of Galaxy users want, that's worth knowing — drop it in #vault-feedback or DM me. If it's not, we'll keep doing what's in front of us."

## What to watch as Outlier evolves

The Series A funding will go somewhere. Reasonable bets on how Outlier deploys $10.7M over 2026-2027:

1. **Major UA push.** $5M earmarked for user acquisition is explicit. Expect aggressive paid social, podcast sponsorships, influencer partnerships.
2. **App + platform expansion.** Likely additions to product breadth — possibly multi-language, expansion to additional markets (UK + Australia already covered; Canada/Europe likely).
3. **Hiring engineering + data science team.** Series A money typically goes to headcount.
4. **Potential acquisition activity.** Outlier may acquire smaller competitors, content brands, or data sources to consolidate position.
5. **Possible Series B targeting.** $10.7M Series A usually buys 18-24 months of runway before next raise.

### What Galaxy watches for that would shift strategy

- **Outlier publishes a Loss Room equivalent.** Major brand-position threat. Galaxy needs to deepen the autopsy + Pass List + methodology surfaces before being matched at the structural level.
- **Outlier acquires a sports analytics annual or Almanac-shaped product.** Threatens the Almanac track. Galaxy must accelerate Almanac V1 launch.
- **Outlier launches an OBS overlay or streamer integration.** Threatens the Live track directly. Galaxy must accelerate Sketch outreach + founding-partner commitments.
- **Outlier announces a B2B / API tier.** Threatens the long-tail B2B optionality (deferred track 7 — Sportsbook Quant Licensing). Less urgent than the above.
- **Outlier raises Series B at unicorn-level valuation.** Signals Outlier achieved category-leader scale. Galaxy's investor narrative becomes harder; pivot to acquisition-shape conversations sooner.

### Counter-moves Galaxy can make

- **Vault depth.** Galaxy can build a Vault tier that Outlier cannot easily replicate — operator-led group conversations, founder editorial voice, quarterly data review. Outlier's team-led product structure makes a "founder-in-Discord" tier hard to copy.
- **Almanac as durable brand asset.** A printed annual builds brand depth that App Store ratings can't match.
- **Public methodology page.** Galaxy can be more transparent about the model than Outlier wants to be (because Outlier's competitive moat is opacity). Lean into the transparency advantage.
- **Multi-track portfolio.** Galaxy's Vision LLC + Integrity + Compliance tracks open exit paths Outlier doesn't have. Galaxy's defense isn't matching Outlier feature-for-feature; it's making Galaxy unmatchable as a portfolio bet.

## What this battlecard is NOT

- **Not a marketing comparison page.** Galaxy will not publish a "Galaxy vs Outlier" comparison page on the public site. Doing so creates false equivalence and gives Outlier brand-relevance Galaxy doesn't benefit from sharing.
- **Not a sales script.** This is strategic content for operators and creators, not a sales tool. Frontline sales conversations should reference at most one talking point from above, not lift the structure wholesale.
- **Not anti-Outlier.** The battlecard treats Outlier with respect. They built a real product, raised real capital, serve real subscribers. Galaxy's position is "different shape," not "better." That framing is brand-aligned.
