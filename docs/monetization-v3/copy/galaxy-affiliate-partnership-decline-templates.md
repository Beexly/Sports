# Galaxy Affiliate + Partnership Decline Templates

**Audience:** Garrett. Internal.
**Purpose:** Galaxy will receive a steady stream of affiliate, partnership, sponsorship, and content-syndication asks once Vault has visibility. Most of these should be declined cleanly. The templates below handle the declines without burning bridges.

**Why this matters:** Galaxy's brand position is restraint-driven. Saying yes to the wrong partnership compromises the whole. Saying no in a way that closes the door damages the network. The templates separate the two.

---

## The seven typical inbound shapes

### Shape 1: Sportsbook affiliate program

A sportsbook (DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, Caesars, smaller offshore books) offers Galaxy a referral-revenue arrangement: Galaxy promotes the book, members sign up via Galaxy's affiliate link, Galaxy earns CPA or rev-share.

**Default response:** Hard no. Permanent.

### Shape 2: Picks-syndication partnership

A media company (sports website, podcast network, newsletter) offers to syndicate Galaxy's picks in exchange for cross-promotion or licensing fees.

**Default response:** Conditional consideration. Usually no for Year-1; revisit Year-2+.

### Shape 3: Influencer co-promotion ask

A sports-betting Twitter personality or YouTube creator asks to co-promote with Galaxy: they push Galaxy, Galaxy pushes them.

**Default response:** Hard no for cross-promotion. Open to discrete content collaboration if methodology-aligned.

### Shape 4: Data-licensing inbound

A sports-data provider or analytics company asks Galaxy to license a data feed (factor scores, historical autopsy data, calibration tables).

**Default response:** Soft no for Year-1. Open to discussion Year-2+ on commercial terms.

### Shape 5: Sponsorship of Galaxy content

A non-sportsbook brand (insurance, finance, productivity tool) wants to sponsor Galaxy's digest, podcast, or annual report.

**Default response:** Hard no. Galaxy doesn't accept sponsorship.

### Shape 6: Podcast or media appearance request

A podcast host invites Garrett on as a guest. A journalist requests an interview.

**Default response:** Selective yes per `galaxy-press-kit.md` criteria.

### Shape 7: Speaking engagement

A conference or industry event invites Garrett to speak.

**Default response:** Default no through Year-2. Selective yes for highest-leverage venues.

## Template for Shape 1 (sportsbook affiliate program)

```
Subject: Re: Galaxy partnership opportunity

Hi [first name],

Thanks for the note about [sportsbook]'s affiliate program.

Galaxy doesn't accept sportsbook affiliate revenue. The reason is structural: our brand position requires that we never have a financial incentive tied to what books our readers use. Affiliate revenue would compromise that.

This is a permanent posture, not a "right now" answer. There isn't a number that would change it.

For what it's worth: I think the strongest sports content platforms in the next decade will be the ones who are independent of book referrals. Galaxy is making that bet structurally.

Best of luck with the program.

— Garrett
```

### Why this works

- It's permanent + reasoned. Sportsbook teams will mark Galaxy as "do not pursue."
- It doesn't moralize. The decline is positioned as Galaxy's strategic choice, not a judgment on the asker.
- It signals the structural reasoning that's repeatedly cited in Galaxy's brand position.

### What this DOESN'T do

- Make exceptions for "free trial" or "no-fee" sportsbook arrangements.
- Accept book-branded content (sponsored posts, "in partnership with" arrangements).
- Allow sportsbook logos on Galaxy properties.
- Quote a sportsbook in an article without explicit factual relevance.

## Template for Shape 2 (picks-syndication partnership)

```
Subject: Re: Content partnership with Galaxy

Hi [first name],

Thanks for the interest in syndicating Galaxy's picks via [publication].

For Year-1 (through end of 2026), Galaxy is keeping content distribution narrow — galaxysportsedge.com + the Vault subscription + our Twitter presence. The reasoning: we're still building the methodology track record, and broader syndication right now would dilute the integrity of where our calls actually originate.

If we revisit syndication in Year-2 (Q1 2027 or later), here's the kind of arrangement that would fit our brand position:
- Galaxy's picks publish on Galaxy first; syndication is 24-48 hours delayed.
- Methodology attribution + link to galaxysportsedge.com/methodology accompanies each syndicated pick.
- No book-affiliate links in the syndicated piece.
- Commercial terms (we don't accept cross-promotion-only deals).

If [publication] is interested in revisiting in Year-2, drop me a note in January 2027 and we'll talk.

— Garrett
```

### Why this works

- It defers without closing.
- It surfaces specific structural constraints that filter out partners who can't meet them.
- It puts the next-step responsibility on the asker.

### What this DOESN'T do

- Agree to Year-1 syndication "to see how it goes."
- Accept syndication with embedded sportsbook affiliate links.
- Accept cross-promotion-for-cross-promotion arrangements (no monetary exchange).

## Template for Shape 3 (influencer co-promotion ask)

```
Subject: Re: Collab idea

Hi [first name],

Thanks for reaching out about a Galaxy [collab / cross-promo].

The honest answer: Galaxy doesn't do cross-promotion with sports-betting personalities. It's not personal — it's that Galaxy's brand position is built on the methodology + transparency rather than personality, and a cross-promotion arrangement positions Galaxy as one of the personalities. That trades brand-position value we can't afford to spend.

If you're interested in a different kind of collaboration — say, you'd like to discuss the Galaxy methodology publicly with us (a podcast we both join, a written exchange about a specific call) — that's a conversation I'd be open to. The constraints would be:
- Methodology-focused content, not picks-focused.
- No mutual cross-promotion in the format.
- Both sides keep their own audiences; nobody asks for follow-for-follow.

If that frame works for you, drop me a note. If not — best of luck with what you're building.

— Garrett
```

### Why this works

- It declines the cross-promotion without insulting the asker.
- It opens a narrow path for methodology-aligned collaboration.
- It pre-filters: only askers serious about substance pursue further.

### What this DOESN'T do

- Agree to mutual follow-counts as success metric.
- Appear on a podcast where the host's content is framed around tout-certainty or outcome promises.
- Co-sign anything where Galaxy's name appears alongside picks Galaxy didn't make.

## Template for Shape 4 (data-licensing inbound)

```
Subject: Re: Data feed inquiry

Hi [first name],

Thanks for the interest in Galaxy's data.

For Year-1, Galaxy isn't licensing data externally. The methodology is still maturing + commercial data partnerships require a maturity bar we haven't crossed yet.

In Year-2, when we revisit data licensing, the structure we'd consider:
- Calibration data + historical factor scores: commercially licensable.
- Live picks feed: not licensable (Vault-only).
- Pass List + Loss Room data: not licensable individually (galaxysportsedge.com is the public surface).
- White-label cockpit for B2B partners: open to discussion at commercial terms.

If [your company] is interested in revisiting in Q1 2027 or later, drop a note then.

Best,
Garrett
```

### Why this works

- It clearly separates what is + isn't on the table.
- It defers the commercial discussion to a stage where Galaxy has leverage.
- It signals what Galaxy IS willing to do, opening the door without committing.

### What this DOESN'T do

- License live picks data to anyone (Vault-only).
- License the Loss Room data outside of the galaxysportsedge.com surface.
- Accept "exclusive" data partnership offers.

## Template for Shape 5 (sponsorship of Galaxy content)

```
Subject: Re: Sponsorship inquiry

Hi [first name],

Thanks for the note about sponsoring [Galaxy property].

Galaxy doesn't accept sponsorship of our content — for the digest, podcast (none planned), annual report, or any other channel. This is a permanent posture; the reasoning is structural rather than tactical.

If there's a different commercial relationship that interests [your company] — say, white-label B2B intelligence for your team, or a research partnership on a specific question — that's a different conversation. The terms would be commercial (cash for delivered work), not sponsorship.

Best,
Garrett
```

### Why this works

- Hard no on the sponsorship.
- Opens a narrow alternative if the asker is willing to switch shapes.
- Preserves the brand position absolutely.

### What this DOESN'T do

- Accept "native content" or "presented by" arrangements.
- Accept exclusive sponsorship in exchange for editorial-section access.
- Make exceptions for nonprofit or mission-aligned sponsors.

## Template for Shape 6 (podcast appearance request)

```
Subject: Re: Podcast invite

Hi [first name],

Thanks for the invite to [podcast].

I'm selective about podcast appearances + want to give you an honest read on whether this is a fit.

Galaxy's brand position is methodology-first, not personality-first. So my best appearances are on shows where the host wants to dig into methodology — how factor models work, what we got wrong, what calibration means, etc. They're less useful on shows looking for hot takes, prop predictions, or game-of-the-week framing.

Tell me a bit about how you'd want the conversation to go + I'll give you a clean yes or no. If the fit's right, I'm happy to do it.

— Garrett
```

### Why this works

- Filters out predictions-focused podcasts.
- Establishes the methodology-first frame before the conversation.
- Lets Garrett decline politely if the fit isn't there after one exchange.

### What this DOESN'T do

- Agree to a podcast without knowing the format.
- Show up on a show that markets Garrett as a "betting expert" rather than methodology operator.
- Use the appearance to promote specific picks.

## Template for Shape 7 (speaking engagement)

```
Subject: Re: Speaking invitation for [event]

Hi [first name],

Thanks for the invitation to speak at [event].

Through Year-2, Galaxy is keeping public speaking very limited — the operator is single-person, our time is the constraint, and we're prioritizing the Vault + Almanac builds.

A few questions that would help me decide:
1. What's the talk format (panel / keynote / interview)?
2. What's the audience size + composition?
3. Is travel involved + when?
4. Are speakers compensated, or is this a profile-building event?

If the fit's strong, I'll consider it. If not, I'll pass cleanly + we can revisit Year-3.

— Garrett
```

### Why this works

- Sets expectations: most speaking invitations get declined.
- Surfaces practical criteria the asker can answer + Galaxy can evaluate.
- Defers without closing.

### What this DOESN'T do

- Agree to speak without seeing the event spec.
- Accept speaking gigs that involve sportsbook sponsors or affiliate booths.
- Pay-to-speak arrangements (some industry events charge speakers).

## When to actually say yes

Galaxy says yes to inbound when the partnership/appearance:

1. **Reinforces the brand position** rather than diluting it.
2. **Reaches readers Galaxy can't reach otherwise** (legitimate audience expansion).
3. **Doesn't compromise the methodology's independence** (no book money, no celebrity dynamic).
4. **Doesn't displace higher-leverage work** (Vault, Almanac, member-time).
5. **Has clear constraints** (format, compensation, attribution) defined upfront.

Less than 5% of inbound passes all 5 tests. The discipline is the gate.

---

## The reply Garrett doesn't write

The trap is the "well, maybe this one would be different" exception. Each shape above is documented because the reasoning is repeatable. The discipline is treating each decline the same way every time.

If a specific inbound is genuinely different, the override needs:
- A decision-log entry per `galaxy-decision-rights-matrix.md`.
- A justification that the brand position is preserved.
- A sunset clause if the partnership starts drifting.

Without those: the answer is the template above.

## When the decline is the wrong response

Some inbound deserves a different shape than the templates above:

- **A genuine peer in the methodology space** (academic researcher, transparent quant-sports model operator) reaches out for substantive intellectual exchange. The conversation is the value; no decline needed.
- **A regulatory or compliance contact** (state gaming commission, journalist on a compliance investigation, lawyer on a related matter). Engage via `galaxy-crisis-communications-playbook.md`.
- **A genuine member reaching out informally.** Not a partnership ask; treat as member outreach per `copy/vault-member-support-playbook.md`.
- **An acquisition inquiry.** Use the variant in `copy/galaxy-investor-inbound-response-template.md`.

Read the inbound carefully before applying a template.
