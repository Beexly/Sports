# Galaxy Almanac — Essay Outlines

**Audience:** Garrett. Internal.
**Purpose:** The Galaxy Almanac (January 15, 2027 ship) contains a headline essay plus supporting essays alongside the data tables, autopsies, and calibration appendices. The outlines below are a candidate essay bank Garrett can select from so the writing phase has structure.

**Status:** First-pass candidate outlines. The production pack currently budgets 7 supporting essays; Garrett selects the final set during October-November 2026.

---

## Almanac structure overview

The Almanac is organized in 5 parts:

1. **The Year in Numbers** — Quantitative summary (calibration, sport breakdowns, Loss Room aggregates).
2. **The Methodology Year** — How the factor model evolved across 2026.
3. **The Essay Section** — headline essay plus selected supporting essays. The candidate outlines are below.
4. **The Loss Room: A Year of Autopsies** — Full Loss Room re-print with quarterly framing.
5. **Appendices** — Calibration tables, factor categories, glossary, methodology summary.

The essay section is the Almanac's voice. It's where Galaxy demonstrates the brand position rather than asserting it.

## Essay 1: "What 2026 Got Right (and Wrong) About Sports Forecasting"

**Length target:** 2,500 words.
**Voice:** Reflective, evidence-led.

### Outline

- Opening: The year started with a confident industry consensus about what would happen in the AFC, NFC, NBA East, NBA West, NHL. List the 5 most-confident consensus calls + show what actually happened.
- Section 1: The 3 places consensus was right + why (probably not luck).
- Section 2: The 3 places consensus was wrong + what the wrong calls had in common.
- Section 3: How Galaxy's methodology responded to each — where the factor model anticipated the divergence, where it missed.
- Section 4: The bigger pattern: when does consensus become a contrarian signal? When does it remain a baseline?
- Closing: What this implies for 2027 forecasting. Not predictions; principles.

### Why this essay

It's the Almanac's mission statement. Galaxy demonstrates that the methodology actually engaged with reality + can be honest about both wins and misses.

## Essay 2: "The Loss Room: One Year In"

**Length target:** 1,800 words.

### Outline

- Opening: A Loss Room call from January 2026, with the autopsy still readable.
- Section 1: The 12-month Loss Room aggregate. Total losses. Distribution by sport. Distribution by confidence band. Distribution by factor-cause.
- Section 2: What the year of autopsies taught Galaxy about the model's blind spots.
- Section 3: Three specific patterns that emerged across multiple autopsies (e.g., factor X was over-weighted in division games, or the model under-priced injury report ambiguity).
- Section 4: How methodology v2.x evolved in response to autopsy patterns.
- Section 5: Why we publish the Loss Room (philosophically).
- Closing: The Loss Room continuing into Year-2.

### Why this essay

It anchors the Loss Room as a permanent Galaxy artifact. Members + outside readers understand it's not a marketing gimmick.

## Essay 3: "Pass List: 200 Games We Chose Not To Publish"

**Length target:** 1,200 words.

### Outline

- Opening: The Pass List concept. Why a model that says "we don't have a defensible call here" is more useful than one that fills the void with fabricated confidence.
- Section 1: Pass List 2026 aggregate. ~200 games not published. Distribution by sport. Distribution by reason.
- Section 2: The 5 most common Pass List reasons + an example of each.
- Section 3: A reframe: what would Galaxy have looked like if we had pushed every Pass List game out as a low-confidence pick? The hypothetical comparison.
- Section 4: Why Pass List > false confidence, even when it makes Galaxy look like it has less to say.
- Closing: Pass List as discipline, not weakness.

### Why this essay

Pass List is one of Galaxy's strongest brand differentiators. Most platforms publish on everything; Galaxy publishes only when the methodology says publish. This essay makes that visible.

## Essay 4: "How a Factor Model Works (For Readers Who Don't Want Math)"

**Length target:** 2,000 words.

### Outline

- Opening: The simplest possible explanation. A factor is a thing the model measures. A weight is how much that thing matters. A call is the model's output when you combine all the factors.
- Section 1: The 4 factor categories Galaxy uses. One short explanation of each, with one concrete example.
- Section 2: How weights are set (calibration against historical data, not gut).
- Section 3: What a factor model can do well. What it can't do. The honest list.
- Section 4: The most common misconception readers have ("it's just AI" / "you can't predict sports") + the actual answer.
- Section 5: How readers can build their own factor sense for sports they follow closely.
- Closing: The methodology is on galaxysportsedge.com/methodology. This essay is the warm-up.

### Why this essay

It's the on-ramp for new readers + members. It's also the most-shareable Almanac piece — likely to circulate beyond the existing audience.

## Essay 5: "The Year Sports Betting Tried (and Failed) to Become Black-Box Prediction"

**Length target:** 2,200 words.

### Outline

- Opening: The black-box prediction platform boom of 2024-2026. What got promised. What got built. What members actually got.
- Section 1: The technical reality. What black-box prediction usually means (and what it usually doesn't mean).
- Section 2: The economic reality. Why black-box marketing was profitable for the platforms but bad for the members.
- Section 3: The Galaxy alternative. Deterministic factor model. Methodology published. Losses published. Pass List as principle.
- Section 4: Three platforms that quietly walked back their black-box claims in 2026 (concrete examples).
- Section 5: What "math you can read" means + why that frame outlasts opacity.
- Closing: The bet for 2027 + beyond.

### Why this essay

It's Galaxy's clearest competitive positioning statement. Not for the existing membership (they get it); for new readers in 2027 who arrive via the Almanac.

## Essay 6: "Calibration: The Most Honest Number In Sports"

**Length target:** 1,500 words.

### Outline

- Opening: Define calibration in plain English. A 70%-confidence call should hit ~70% of the time. Not 85%. Not 55%.
- Section 1: Why calibration is rarer than win-rate as a published metric.
- Section 2: Galaxy's 2026 calibration. Numbers by confidence band. Numbers by sport.
- Section 3: What miscalibration looks like + why it usually reveals overfitting or coverage gaps.
- Section 4: How Galaxy maintains calibration — backtest discipline, factor weight constraints, the no-snap-judgments rule.
- Section 5: What members should ask any prediction platform: "Show me your calibration curve." If they can't: they don't actually know.
- Closing: Calibration as the discipline that compounds.

### Why this essay

It teaches the most-important methodology concept. Members + non-members come away with a tool for evaluating any prediction source.

## Essay 7: "What the Founding 50 Taught Us"

**Length target:** 1,200 words.

### Outline

- Opening: The founding-50 cohort joined Vault before there was a track record. They paid $200 on faith.
- Section 1: What the founding-50 had in common (anonymized; no names). Sport-following patterns. Methodology-curiosity. Skepticism of tout-certainty content.

- Section 3: What Galaxy got wrong + how the founding-50 surfaced it.
- Section 4: What "the founding-50 lesson" means for Year-2 + how Galaxy honors that lesson when membership scales.
- Closing: The founding-50 status is permanent. The lesson is permanent. The bet on member-relationship continues.

### Why this essay

It honors the founding cohort + signals to future readers that Galaxy treats member-relationships seriously. It's also brand-aligned restraint: no names, just patterns.

## Essay 8: "Off-Days, Off-Weeks, Off-Seasons"

**Length target:** 1,000 words.

### Outline

- Opening: The sports media incentive to be on every day. The Galaxy alternative: be off when the methodology doesn't have a call worth publishing.
- Section 1: Galaxy's off-day discipline in 2026. Saturday-off. Pass List as off-statement.
- Section 2: Why off-days matter for the operator (single-operator burnout risk) AND for the methodology (forcing yourself to publish breeds bad calls).
- Section 3: Why off-seasons matter — sport-specific dormancy that lets the methodology rebuild for the next cycle.
- Section 4: What "restraint as product" means in practice.
- Closing: The off-day commitment continuing into 2027.

### Why this essay

It frames restraint as professional discipline. Brand-aligned + a quiet differentiator from the always-on content cycle most platforms run.

Essays 1-4 are the **must-ships**. Essays 5-8 are the **strong-ships**. Essays 9-11 are the **maybe-ships**.

## Brand-voice constraints for all essays

Per `galaxy-brand-voice-canonical.md`:

- No first-person Garrett-personality voice. Galaxy speaks; not "I."
- No certainty claims. No "we predicted" or "we called it." Use "Galaxy's methodology indicated" or "the factor model surfaced."
- No celebrity. No named members in essays. No named bettors.
- No competitive smear. Categories of competitors can be named (Essay 5, 11); specific platforms cited only when sourced + factual.
- Loss Room essays are honest, not self-flagellating. The brand position is calibrated honesty, not performative humility.

---

## What the Almanac DOESN'T do

1. **No Galaxy financial figures.** ARR, subscription counts, revenue — none of this appears in the Almanac.
2. **No member testimonials.** Member voice appears only in aggregate Founding-50 essay, anonymized.
3. **No "look how far we've come" framing.** Year-1 was Year-1; restraint applies.
4. **No predictions for specific 2027 outcomes.** Galaxy doesn't publish standings forecasts or season-long calls in the Almanac.
5. **No marketing language.** The Almanac is the antidote to marketing-tone content.
