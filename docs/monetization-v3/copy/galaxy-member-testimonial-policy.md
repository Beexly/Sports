# Galaxy Member Testimonial + Case Study Policy

**Audience:** Garrett. Internal.
**Purpose:** Most subscription businesses publish member testimonials, case studies, and "success stories" as core marketing material. Galaxy's brand position changes the calculus. The policy below specifies what Galaxy does + doesn't do with member-voice content.

**Default posture:** Restrained. Galaxy doesn't use member voice as marketing fuel. Member voice may appear in narrow, brand-position-aligned ways with explicit consent.

---

## Why the default is restraint

Galaxy's brand position rests on methodology + transparency, not personality or social proof. Standard testimonial marketing (member quotes, "join thousands of happy subscribers," etc.) tends to:

- Position the platform as personality-driven rather than method-led.
- Imply outcomes that the methodology doesn't promise.
- Pressure members into being content for the platform.
- Compete with the methodology page as the substantive surface.

A platform that publishes its losses publicly + asks for testimonials reads as inconsistent. Restraint is the brand-aligned posture.

---

## What Galaxy DOES do (with explicit consent)

### 1. Aggregate, anonymized member input in the annual report

The Year-end annual report (per `copy/galaxy-year-end-annual-report-template.md`) may reference:

- "N members renewed at Year-1 close."
- "Most-cited feedback theme this year was [X]."
- "Founding-50 advisory channel input shaped N product decisions."

No individual member is named. The aggregate frames the relationship without exploiting it.

### 2. Anonymized member feedback in the Model Journal

The Model Journal occasionally references:

- "A member asked about [methodology question]."
- "Multiple members surfaced [observation pattern]."

No names. No specific testimonial framing. The reference is methodology-substantive, not marketing-emotive.

### 3. Named-with-consent acknowledgment in the Almanac

Galaxy's annual Almanac may include a brief named acknowledgment section ("With gratitude to founding members [first names only, if consented]") — only if every named member explicitly opts in. The list is alphabetized, not ranked. No quotes; just names.

### 4. Press-context member quotes with explicit consent

If a journalist or podcaster reaches out wanting to interview Galaxy + asks if they can talk to a member, Galaxy:
- DOESN'T proactively offer a list of members.
- DOES facilitate the introduction if a specific member has separately expressed interest in being a Galaxy reference.
- DOESN'T edit or curate what the member says.
- The member retains full control over their quote.

---

## What Galaxy DOESN'T do

### 1. No testimonial pull-quotes on the landing page

The Vault landing page (per `copy/vault-landing-page.md`) does NOT include:

- "Join 500 happy members" social-proof claims.
- Photo + quote member testimonials.
- "Member of the month" features.
- "What our members are saying" sections.

The landing page is substantive — methodology, what Vault includes, Loss Room, Pass List, pricing.

### 2. No "case studies" of member success

Galaxy doesn't publish:

- "How [member] turned $X into $Y using Galaxy" — outcome promises violate brand position.
- "Member [name] doubled their bankroll" — same.
- Case studies of any kind that frame Galaxy as causal to member outcomes.

The reason: Galaxy publishes methodology + transparency. Members do what they want with it. Galaxy doesn't take credit for member outcomes.

### 3. No "review aggregator" or trust-badge surface

Galaxy doesn't display:

- Trustpilot widgets.
- "X% would recommend" stars.
- Verified-purchaser badges.

The reason: aggregator surfaces invite the social-proof framing Galaxy rejects.

### 4. No "What Galaxy members say" video content

Galaxy doesn't produce or commission member-voice video content for marketing purposes.

### 5. No incentivized review requests

Galaxy doesn't ask members to:

- Leave reviews on third-party platforms.
- Tweet positive things about Galaxy.
- Tag Galaxy in success-story posts.

If members do these things voluntarily — fine. Galaxy doesn't direct or amplify.

### 6. No "Member of the Founding 50" public feature

Founding-50 members get a permanent founding number (1-50). Galaxy does NOT publicly feature them in any way unless explicitly opted in.

## When members proactively offer content

Sometimes a member will reach out asking to:

- Write a public review of Galaxy.
- Tweet about their Vault experience.
- Mention Galaxy on a podcast.
- Share screenshots of digest content.

### Default response

```
Hey [first name],

Thanks for the offer. To be honest, Galaxy doesn't have a strong testimonial / public-voice program — it's a brand-position thing. The platform is methodology-driven; we resist becoming personality-driven through member voice.

That said, your voice is your voice. If you want to write something publicly about your experience as a Galaxy member:
- You don't need our permission.
- We won't reshare or amplify what you write (also brand-position discipline).
- We won't ask you to remove it unless it shares Vault-gated content (digest specifics, etc.).

If there's a specific reason you want to publish — say, a podcast where you'd like to mention Galaxy — let me know + I'll help with context. But mostly: do what feels right to you.

— Garrett
```

### Why this works

- Honors the member's intent without commercializing it.
- Maintains the brand-position discipline.
- Preserves the gated-content boundary.

---

## When journalists want to talk to members

A reporter or podcaster reaches out, wanting member voices for a Galaxy story.

### Default response

```
Hey [journalist name],

Galaxy doesn't typically facilitate member interviews for press. The reasoning is that Galaxy's voice is the methodology, the Loss Room, and the Pass List — those surfaces are what we want press to engage with.

That said, if there's a specific member you've already connected with who's willing to talk, that's their decision + not ours to influence either way. We don't direct members to specific reporters.

For the substantive Galaxy story, here's the press kit: [link]. The methodology page + Loss Room are publicly accessible. If you have specific factor-model or calibration questions, I'll answer them directly.

— Garrett
```

### What we won't do

- Provide a member contact list.
- Curate members based on "success story" potential.
- Coach members on what to say.
- Suppress members from talking to press independently.

## When members appear in member-led content

Some Galaxy members create their own content (blog posts, podcasts, YouTube videos) that mention Galaxy. Examples:

- A member writes a Medium post about their experience with deterministic factor models, mentioning Galaxy as one example.
- A member starts a podcast and references Galaxy episodes.
- A member's Twitter following grows + they regularly cite Galaxy in their posts.

### Default response

**Galaxy doesn't amplify, retweet, or feature member-created content** — even brand-aligned content.

Rationale:
- Amplification creates implicit endorsement, which compromises the methodology-first frame.
- Selectively amplifying creates winner-take-all dynamics among members.
- Members should create content because it's worth creating, not because Galaxy will signal-boost.

Exception: methodology-substantive content that Galaxy chooses to engage with on its merits, treated like any other intellectual input. Engagement is in the form of a Model Journal reference or a substantive Twitter reply — not amplification.

---

## When a member becomes a public figure

Rare but possible: a Galaxy member becomes a public personality (existing sports media figure subscribes; existing influencer joins Vault).

### Default response

- Galaxy doesn't publicize their membership.
- Galaxy treats them like any other member — no special access, no special discount, no special content.
- If they publicly mention being a Galaxy member, Galaxy doesn't comment.

The reason: Galaxy's brand position depends on treating every member as equal. A public-figure-as-member dynamic compromises that.

---

## Founding-50 acknowledgment in the Almanac

Per the Almanac production pack, the annual book may include a brief "with gratitude" section listing founding-50 first names. Conditions:

1. **Each founding-50 member explicitly opts in.** Default is NOT included.
2. **First name only.** Surnames omitted.
3. **Alphabetized, not ranked.** No founding number associated with names.
4. **No quotes.** Just names.

This is the only place in Galaxy's published content where founding-50 members are named.

---

## What members get instead of testimonial-marketing

Galaxy invests in member experience rather than member-voice-as-marketing:

- Personal Day-7 check-ins.
- Office hours (live access to Garrett).
- Founding-50 advisory channel (founding-50 specific).
- Quarterly aggregate feedback acknowledgment.
- Year-end annual report aggregate reference.

The investment compounds. Members feel valued because they actually are. Marketing-testimonial dynamics extract; member-experience-investment compounds.

---

## Why this policy holds in Year-2 + beyond

Even when Vault scales to 5,000 members + the temptation to "social proof" grows:

- The brand position depends on methodology > social proof.
- The Loss Room is the social proof. The transparent track record is the social proof. The Pass List is the social proof.
- Adding testimonial marketing to a methodology-first platform reads as inconsistent + erodes trust.

The discipline holds.
