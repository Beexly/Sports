# Galaxy Sports Edge — /about Page Copy

**Route:** `/about`
**Audience:** Public. First-time visitors who clicked through from press, podcasts, or search; existing subscribers who want to verify what Galaxy is; journalists doing background.
**Voice:** Third-person Galaxy. Method-not-personality. Restrained.
**Update cadence:** Quarterly review. Major edits only when something structural changes (track activation, headcount changes, brand-position evolution).

---

## Page-level structure

The /about page has 8 sections in this order. Each section is short — total page reads in 4-6 minutes.

---

## Section 1 — What Galaxy is

```
About Galaxy Sports Edge

Galaxy Sports Edge is a sports research platform built on a deterministic factor model.

We publish calibrated probability assessments of sports outcomes. We publish them with the factors that drove each call. When we lose, we publish a public autopsy. When we consider a game and choose not to publish, we archive it.

Galaxy operates from the position that the discipline of saying no is as important as the discipline of saying yes. The Pass List makes that discipline visible.

The brand is built around what we publish, not what we say about what we publish.
```

That's the opening. Three short sentences setting position; one defining sentence about restraint; one closing line that's the brand statement.

## Section 2 — What we publish

```
What we publish

Five public surfaces:

01 — The Board
   Live publications. Currently active probability assessments.
   /board

02 — The Ledger
   Every settled pick with its result. Permanent record.
   /ledger

03 — The Loss Room
   Every losing pick with a public autopsy explaining what the model got wrong.
   /loss-room

04 — The Pass List
   Every game we considered publishing and chose not to.
   /passes

05 — The Methodology
   How the factor model works. What we weight. Open documentation, updated continuously, frozen annually.
   /methodology

Two subscription tiers:

— Pro ($19/month): full board access, full ledger, full loss room, full pass list, methodology page.
— Elite ($49/month): everything Pro includes + earlier publication access + market analysis.

One premium tier:

— Vault ($200/year, capped at 1,000 founding members): the rationale behind the publications. Weekly internal-rationale digest. Monthly group office hours. Quarterly private data review. Early access to the Model Journal. Vault-only Discord.
/vault
```

Section 2 is the catalog. Functional. Specific URLs included so the page works as a hub.

---

## Section 3 — What we don't publish

```
What we don't publish

— More than approximately five picks per day.
— Certainty slogans or outcome promises.
— Picks earlier than Pro / Elite tier rules allow.
— Sponsored picks. Galaxy doesn't accept payment for publication position.
— Picks for sports we haven't validated factor coverage on.
— Nonpublic information. There's no information layer behind Galaxy's published rationale; what's on the methodology page is what's in the model.

The restraints above are not aspirational. They are operational rules with brand-safety enforcement.
```

The "what we don't" section is uniquely Galaxy. Most platforms wouldn't write this section. The restraint posture lives here.

## Section 4 — How Galaxy is built

```
How Galaxy is built

The model is deterministic. Factors are documented at /methodology. Each publication is computed from a defined set of factors with category-level weights documented publicly (exact weights are proprietary).

Each publication has an operator review layer. Garrett reviews the candidate publication, checks the dominant factors, and decides whether the model output clears Galaxy's publication discipline or belongs in the Pass List.

The public methodology documents the factor categories, publication gates, confidence thresholds, autopsy taxonomy, Pass List categories, calibration checks, and model versioning rules. The exact numerical weights stay private because they are the competitive work.

Galaxy operates from [city, state]. The company is bootstrapped, single-operator-plus-1099-contractors, and not raising outside capital through Year 1.
```

This section explains the build without exposing internal tooling. If a journalist or member asks about internal writing assistance, answer from the internal policy; do not make the public /about page carry that disclosure by default.

## Section 5 — Who runs Galaxy

```
Who runs Galaxy

Garrett Baxley founded Galaxy in 2024.

Galaxy is single-operator. Garrett writes the publications, runs Vault office hours, hosts the digest, authors the Almanac essays, and operates customer support directly. Engineering scales via 1099 contractors when needed; editorial scales via 1099 copyeditors and cover designers for the annual Almanac.

Galaxy doesn't run a founder-personality brand. The work is the work; Garrett happens to be the person doing it. The Almanac and the Model Journal carry first-person voice because those surfaces require it. The rest of Galaxy speaks as a method.
```

Brand-aligned framing of the founder. Names Garrett (necessary for press kit / Stripe / legal compliance) without making Galaxy "Garrett's brand."

## Section 6 — What Galaxy is not

```
What Galaxy is not

Galaxy is not a sportsbook. We do not accept wagers. We do not process bets. We are not a betting service.

Galaxy is not an affiliate of any sportsbook. We do not earn commissions from bettor activity at any specific operator.

Galaxy is not a tout service. We do not sell picks. The subscription tiers grant access to the same publications; no member receives picks that other members don't see.

Galaxy is not a black-box prediction feed. The factor model is documented at the category level; the publication process is visible; the Loss Room and Pass List are permanent records.

Galaxy is not financial advice. The publications are calibrated probability assessments. What a reader does with those probabilities is their own discipline.
```

The "what Galaxy is not" section is the legal / compliance / brand-position triple-purpose. Each negation matters for one of those reasons.

## Section 7 — The annual artifact

```
The annual artifact

Each year, Galaxy publishes the Galaxy Almanac — a ~300-page hardcover annual that binds the year's settled picks, losses with autopsies, Pass List archive, methodology snapshot, model changelog, and supporting research essays.

The 2026 Almanac ships January 15, 2027. Hardcover $99. Digital $39. Pre-orders open in October.

The Almanac exists because a year is a different unit than a week. Patterns visible across hundreds of publications aren't visible in individual posts. The Almanac turns the year's accumulated data into a structure that can be read, referenced, and re-read months later.

The Almanac is Galaxy's most durable brand asset across years. The first one is the seed. Each subsequent one inherits the discipline.

/almanac
```

The Almanac mention earns its place on /about because it's a defining Galaxy artifact, not a marketing CTA.

## What the /about page deliberately does not include

1. **No founder photo.** Galaxy is method-led, not personality-led.
2. **No "our values" / "our mission" section.** Galaxy's operating values are a separate (internal-leaning) document.
3. **No subscriber count.** Galaxy doesn't measure itself by subscriber numbers externally.
4. **No revenue or growth figures.** Bootstrapped, private.
5. **No press logos / "as seen in" section.** Brand-position dilution risk; press kit is at /press.
6. **No customer testimonials.** Galaxy doesn't run testimonials.
7. **No subscription CTA / pricing comparison table.** /pricing exists for that.
8. **No FAQ.** /vault has Vault FAQ; the main pages handle their own questions.
9. **No "join our newsletter" form.** Galaxy's email list is implicit in subscription tiers.
10. **No social proof beyond the Loss Room / Pass List itself.** Those surfaces ARE the social proof.

## Drift patterns to watch for

### Drift 1: /about gets longer over time

Each quarterly review tempts adding "one more thing." After 2 years, /about is 4,000 words.

**Counter:** target stays at ~1,000 words / 4-6 minute read. If a new addition is necessary, something else gets cut.

### Drift 2: Founder section gets personal

"Garrett's journey from..." creeps in. Brand-position violation.

**Counter:** Section 5 stays factual. No journey arc. Garrett's personal story belongs (at most) in interviews + the Almanac.

### Drift 3: Marketing language

"Galaxy is the leading..." / "Industry-first..." / "Pioneering approach to..." — all rejected.

**Counter:** brand-safety scanner. Specifically check Section 1 + Section 7 + Section 8 for marketing drift.
