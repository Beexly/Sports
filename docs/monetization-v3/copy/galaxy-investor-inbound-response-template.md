# Galaxy Investor Inbound Response Template

**Audience:** Garrett. Internal.
**Purpose:** Galaxy will receive inbound interest from VCs, angels, and operator-investors once Vault gets traction. Most of those conversations should be politely closed, not pursued. The template below handles the response without burning the relationship.

**Why this matters:** Galaxy's capital plan is bootstrap-through-Year-2 per `05-cashflow-capital.md`. Raising before Year-2 ARR is established locks in a worse valuation + dilutes the founder's strategic optionality. Politely deferring inbound is brand-aligned + economically right.

---

## The three typical inbound shapes

### Shape 1: Cold VC outreach (associate-level)

A VC associate finds Galaxy via Twitter or press coverage, sends a templated email asking for a chat.

**Reality:** This is a top-of-funnel meeting for the associate, not a real check signal. Galaxy doesn't owe the meeting.

### Shape 2: Angel / operator-investor warm intro

Someone in Garrett's network introduces a sports-industry operator or angel investor who has expressed interest in Galaxy.

**Reality:** This is a real signal worth a 30-minute conversation. Decline only after the conversation, not before.

### Shape 3: Partner who wants a stake

A potential business partner (sportsbook, media company, sports-data provider) wants to invest as part of a partnership conversation.

**Reality:** Highest-risk inbound. Investment-as-strategic-lever can compromise brand position. Default response is no.

---

## Template for Shape 1 (cold VC outreach)

```
Subject: Re: [their subject]

Hi [first name],

Thanks for reaching out and for the kind words about Galaxy.

We're not raising right now. Bootstrap through Year-2 is the plan — the math works at our current revenue trajectory, and an outside check at this stage would dilute strategic optionality more than it would unlock.

If that changes in 2027 or later, I'll loop back to firms I've stayed in light contact with. If you'd like to be on that list, no objection — just reply yes and I'll note it.

For what it's worth, the things I'd find useful from a VC partner (whenever the time is right):
- An operator-led firm with sports / consumer-subscription pattern recognition.
- A check size in the $2-5M range, not $15M+.
- Patient capital — 7-10 year horizon, not 4-5 year fund-clock pressure.

Thanks again for the note.

— Garrett
```

### Why this works

- It's specific. The VC knows exactly why this isn't the right moment.
- It opens a door for the future without committing to a meeting now.
- It signals Galaxy knows what kind of partner it wants — most VCs respect the discipline.

### What this DOESN'T do

- It doesn't agree to a 30-minute "get-to-know-you" call. Those calls are the cost; Galaxy's time is the constraint.
- It doesn't share confidential numbers (ARR, member count, etc.) to a stranger.
- It doesn't promise the firm priority access when Galaxy does raise.

## Template for Shape 2 (warm intro, real signal)

```
Subject: Re: Intro from [mutual connection]

Hi [first name],

Thanks for the intro from [mutual connection] and for the interest in Galaxy.

Happy to chat for 30 minutes. Galaxy is bootstrap-through-Year-2 by design — we're not raising — but I'd like to learn what you're seeing in the space and share a bit about where we're heading. Real conversations with operator-investors are useful even when capital isn't the topic.

Two questions before we set time:

1. Are you looking at this as a potential investment, an advisory relationship, or a sector-knowledge conversation? Any of those is fine; I want to make sure the conversation matches your interest.

2. Is there anything Galaxy could send you in advance to make the call more substantive? I can share the master plan summary publicly available; specific numbers would come later.

If 30 minutes next week works, here's my Calendly: [link]

— Garrett
```

### Why this works

- It gates the conversation on real interest, not just curiosity.
- It establishes the bootstrap posture early — the investor knows the deal isn't open.
- It treats the operator-investor as a peer, not a supplicant.

### After the 30-minute conversation

If the investor is genuinely valuable (sports-industry pattern recognition, useful network, patient capital):
- Stay in light quarterly contact.
- Share Galaxy's monthly KPI summary if they want.
- When Year-3 raise happens, they're on the short list.

If the investor is fishing:
- Polite thank-you note.
- No further contact.

## Template for Shape 3 (partner wants a stake)

```
Subject: Re: Investment + partnership discussion

Hi [first name],

Thanks for the conversation about a potential partnership between Galaxy and [their company]. A few thoughts.

On the investment side: Galaxy is bootstrap-through-Year-2 by design. Outside capital — even strategic — at this stage compromises the brand-position discipline that makes Galaxy worth partnering with. The honest answer is no on the investment piece.

On the partnership side: if [their company] sees mutual value in a commercial relationship — content syndication, data licensing, white-label cockpit access — those are conversations Galaxy is open to. The terms would be commercial (cash for services), not equity.

If we can build the commercial relationship first, the investment question can return in Year-3 or Year-4 when Galaxy is raising. By then we'll have proof-of-relationship to make the investment case stronger.

If a commercial relationship doesn't work for [their company] without the equity component, the honest answer is we should hold and revisit later.

Open to either path.

— Garrett
```

### Why this works

- It separates the partnership from the investment cleanly.
- It declines the equity entanglement without closing the relationship.
- It demonstrates Galaxy's discipline — which is the brand position.

### What this DOESN'T do

- It doesn't make exceptions for "strategic value." Strategic investors take board seats; board seats compromise brand position.
- It doesn't accept advisory-shares-for-introductions deals. Those distort incentives.
- It doesn't agree to a co-marketing arrangement that implies investment is coming.

## When to actually take a meeting

Galaxy takes investor meetings only when:

1. **Warm intro from someone Garrett trusts.** No cold meetings, regardless of fund size.
2. **The investor's stated interest is operator-level, not check-writing-level.** "I'd like to learn about the space" is fine. "I have $X to deploy" is a sign to defer.
3. **The 30-minute conversation has a defined purpose** — sector knowledge, hiring intel, distribution intel — beyond just rapport-building.
4. **The conversation doesn't displace a Vault customer-dev interview or member call.** Member-time > investor-time at Galaxy's stage.

---

## When to decline

Decline immediately when:

1. **The inbound is from a fund whose portfolio includes companies Galaxy publicly competes with.** Conflict of interest poisons the well.
2. **The inbound is from a fund whose portfolio includes companies whose brand position Galaxy explicitly opposes.** (Sportsbook cap-table investors, black-box prediction tool funds, etc.)
3. **The inbound is from a fund Garrett has never heard of and the warm intro is third-degree.** Galaxy's time is the constraint.
4. **The inbound is from a fund operating on a 4-5 year clock pressure.** Galaxy's compounding is multi-year.

## The acquisition variant

Same template structure when acquirers reach out (typically media companies or sports-data companies):

```
Subject: Re: [their subject]

Hi [first name],

Thanks for the note about [their company]'s interest in Galaxy.

Galaxy isn't for sale. The 24-month plan is to build to $10M+ ARR while preserving brand-position independence; an acquisition before that compounding plays out would underprice the asset for both sides.

If the conversation is about a commercial partnership (content licensing, data feed, white-label) — those are different conversations and we're open to them.

If the conversation is about acquisition specifically — let's hold and revisit in 18-24 months when the numbers are more mature. I'll keep [their company] on a small list of acquirers I'd consider talking to when the time is right, if that's of interest.

— Garrett
```

### Why this works

- It compliments the acquirer's interest without committing to a process.
- It opens a separate commercial conversation.
- It defers the acquisition discussion to a point where Galaxy has leverage.

---

## What Garrett doesn't say in these replies

- Galaxy's ARR figures.
- Galaxy's member count.
- Galaxy's churn or retention figures.
- Galaxy's runway scenario or burn rate.
- Galaxy's specific cap table.
- The names of any specific advisors or angels.

These are confidential. Investor inbound is not the channel for sharing them.

## Saying yes (eventually)

When Galaxy does raise in Year-3 or beyond, the process should be:

1. **Decide the size.** Galaxy's first outside check is likely $2-5M, not larger.
2. **Decide the structure.** Common shares, modest preferred, or convertible note. No participating preferred. No multiple liquidation preferences. No board control concessions.
3. **Decide the lead.** Galaxy chooses the lead investor based on operator pattern-recognition + brand-position alignment, not check size.
4. **Run a tight process.** 3-5 firms maximum. Decision in 30 days. No drawn-out auction.
5. **Save runway.** Galaxy raises 18-24 months of runway, not 36-48. Compounding above the cap is too valuable to dilute.

These constraints are pre-committed in the capital model. The investor inbound replies above ensure Galaxy gets to that future moment with options intact.
