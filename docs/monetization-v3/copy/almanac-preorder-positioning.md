## Section 2 - Why the Year

Sports analytics disappears.

A model publishes a confidence number Tuesday morning. The game happens Wednesday. By Friday the result is forgotten, rebranded as a hit, or quietly never mentioned again if it was a miss.

Galaxy's public surfaces already refuse that drift. The Ledger preserves every settled pick. The Loss Room preserves every loss with its autopsy. The Pass List preserves the games we considered and did not publish. The methodology page preserves the model that ran the year.

The Almanac is what happens when all of that gets bound.

Not the year's highlights. Not what worked. The full record, including what did not, including what we changed, including what we passed.

The reader who buys this book is buying a commitment we already made.

## Section 4 - What This Is Not

Not a 2027 prediction guide. Galaxy does not predict. Galaxy publishes calibrated probabilities at the time of decision. The Almanac is the 2026 record, not a 2027 forecast.

Not a best-of highlight reel. The losses get the same page count as the wins.

Not a substitute for the live publication. The site is the live product. The Almanac is the year that fell into amber.

## Price Defense

### Objection: "$99 is more than comparable sports annuals."

Mitigation: lead with hardcover plus digital bundling. If customer development shows price stickiness, test $79 hardcover plus a $129 premium tier with signed hardcover and Vault founder-list priority.

### Objection: "I can read this for free on the website."

Mitigation: invest in physical quality and include Almanac-exclusive essays. The headline Year-in-Review essay and 2-3 supporting research essays stay exclusive for roughly 6 months post-publish.

### Objection: "$99 is a considered purchase."

Mitigation: accept that considered-purchase pricing is correct for Galaxy. Add a 30-day hardcover refund policy to reduce conversion friction without cheapening the product.
