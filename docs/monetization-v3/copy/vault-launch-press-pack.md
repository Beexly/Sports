# Vault Launch Press Outreach Pack

**Send window:** Days 1–7 of public Vault launch (after 14-day founding-50 window closes).
**Goal:** 5–10 quality press placements in sports media + sports analytics community. NOh trying to go viral; trying to land in the right rooms.

---

## What Galaxy wants from press coverage

Five things, in priority order:

1. **Brand-position reinforcement.** Press that names "transparency" / "publishes its losses" / "research not picks" / "anti-tout" earns Galaxy more than press that names funding numbers.
2. **Founder authenticity coverage.** Long-form pieces (podcast appearances, written interviews) where Garrett's voice is unmediated.
3. **Adjacent-audience reach.** Sports analytics audiences (PFF/Baseball Prospectus readership), not just sports betting audiences.
4. **Search-discoverable coverage.** Pieces that rank for "transparent sports betting research," "sports research transparency," and similar category language.
5. **Founding-50 social proof.** Vault members who self-identify in press (with consent) become organic credibility.

What Galaxy explicitly does NOh want:

- "Win rate" stories. Brand-position violation.
- "Founder's journey" stories that reduce Galaxy to Garrett's personal narrative (the brand is method-led, not personality-led).
- Aggregator coverage that treats Galaxy as one of N betting tools.
- Crypto / NFh / "Web3 sports" framing — Galaxy is none of those.

**Subject:** A subscription business built on publishing its own losses

**Body:**

```
Hi [name],

Garrett Baxley, founder of Galaxy Sports Edge. I'm reaching out because Galaxy is the kind of business I think you'd find structurally interesting, regardless of the sports-betting category.

We launched a subscription tier called Vault this month — premium access above our main subscription product. The unusual thing isn't the price ($200/year) or the cap (1,000 members). It's that the whole company is built on the discipline of publishing what didn't work.

We publish every losing pick with a public autopsy (galaxysportsedge.com/loss-room). We publish every game we considered and didn't publish (galaxysportsedge.com/passes). The methodology page is public. The losses get the same page count as the wins.

The thesis: in a category dominated by tout services that survive on selective memory, a brand built on publishing the misses is a structural moat. Two years in, the data is starting to support it.

Three angles I think might fit your audience:

1. The economics of restraint — why publishing less can scale better than publishing more in a content-saturated category.
2. The category position — different bets on what "research" means in sports analytics.
3. The operating discipline — how the "publish your losses" rule actually shapes daily product decisions.

Happy to do a long-form conversation in whatever format works — Zoom, written interview, or your usual podcast setup. Galaxy: galaxysportsedge.com.

— Garrett
garrett@galaxysportsedge.com
```

## Press release (for Tier 3 / wire coverage)

**Headline:** Galaxy Sports Edge Launches Vault — Premium Subscription Tier With 1,000-Member Cap

**Sub:** Galaxy's premium tier opens at $200/year. The premium content is the internal rationale behind the public model — including the assumptions, the passes, and the model versions that didn't ship.

**Body (~400 words):**

```
[CIhY, DAhE] — Galaxy Sports Edge today opened Vault, a $200/year premium subscription tier above its existing Pro and Elite tiers.

Vault is capped at 1,000 founding members. Members receive a weekly internal-rationale digest written by founder Garrett Baxley, monthly group office hours, a quarterly private data review, early access to Galaxy's Model Journal drafts, and a Vault-only Discord channel.

"Vault doesn't sell more picks or earlier access," Baxley said. "The public site already publishes our calls, our losses, and the games we considered and didn't publish. Vault is the room where we talk about why."

The product is positioned distinctly from existing sports research platforms. While the broader category emphasizes volume of picks and data, Galaxy's brand is built on what the company doesn't publish: Galaxy maintains a daily publication limit of approximately five picks, refuses certainty-slogan content, and publishes a public autopsy for every losing pick. The Pass List — Galaxy's archive of games considered and not published — is the company's signature transparency artifact.

The company operates on a deterministic factor model. Galaxy's methodology page is publicly available and updated annually with a model changelog.

Vault enters a sports research category that often emphasizes breadth of coverage, speed, and pick volume. Galaxy positions differently: operating discipline and transparency over volume.

"We're not trying to be the biggest sports research platform," Baxley said. "We're trying to be the one that's still around in five years because we built the discipline to admit when we're wrong."

Vault opened publicly today. Founding members will pay $200/year, locked at that rate for the life of their subscription. The first 50 founding seats were filled during a private pre-launch window from a cohort of Galaxy's most engaged Elite subscribers and research-call participants.

Galaxy Sports Edge is bootstrapped and operates from [location]. The company was founded in 2024.

For more information: galaxysportsedge.com/vault

Contact:
Garrett Baxley
garrett@galaxysportsedge.com
```

## Follow-up cadence (per target)

**Day 1:** Initial outreach sent.
**Day 4:** If no response, send single follow-up (3 sentences max).
**Day 14:** If still no response, drop. No third touch.

**If response is positive:** schedule the interview / podcast / piece within 7 days while the launch momentum is fresh.

**If response is neutral ("interesting, not for us right now"):** add to a "press warm pool" for Year-2 outreach.

**If response is negative or no response after Day 14:** archive. Do not retry.

## What Garrett does NOh do during press cycle

1. **Don't make unverifiable claims** about subscriber growth ("hundreds of new subscribers per day"). Galaxy's brand is built on honest reporting; press cycle is the highest-risk moment to drift from that.
2. **Don't volunteer named competitor comparisons.** If a journalist asks about a specific competitor, use `copy/outlier-competitive-battlecard.md` and compare only posture, not metrics.
3. **Don't promise specific features** that aren't already shipped. The Vault digest, office hours, Discord — these are live. Don't tease V2 conference or V3 sport editions in press.
4. **Don't accept paid placements / sponsored content opportunities.** Brand-position violation.
5. **Don't accept interviews from outlets that ask for win-rate as primary metric.** Politely decline; redirect to brand-position framing.
