# Sample Twitter Content — 7-Day Galaxy Voice Week

**Audience:** Garrett. Internal.
**Purpose:** Demonstrates Galaxy's Twitter voice in a full week of content per `copy/galaxy-twitter-content-discipline.md`. The week below is illustrative, not a real schedule.

**Status:** Voice-calibration reference. Shows tone, cadence, and restraint.

---

## Cadence overview

Galaxy's Twitter cadence target: **3-5 substantive tweets per week**, NOT one tweet per day.

The week below has 4 tweets across 7 days. Some weeks have 3; some have 5. Never daily.

---

## Monday — Methodology note

**Tweet:**

```
Quick methodology note for the week.

The factor model treats divisional games differently in late-season play. Reasoning: divisional opponents have more film on each other; situational factors have more variance; market lines have already absorbed most of the season-long info.

So our publication threshold rises 3 percentage points for divisional games after Week 10.

If you've wondered why our December NFL Pass List is longer than September's — that's why.

galaxysportsedge.com/methodology
```

### Why this works

- Substantive methodology content.
- Not promotional.
- Links to the public methodology page (the substrate).
- Brief — fits the timeline without spinning out a thread.

### What it avoids

- No "🔥" or "📈" emoji.
- No "Here's why most platforms get this wrong" framing.
- No call-to-action to subscribe.

## Thursday — Reflection on a public conversation

**Tweet:**

```
Saw a thread arguing that "every prediction model is just regression dressed up."

Half-true.

The half that's true: predictive models are math applied to historical patterns. Always have been.

The half that's not: there's a real difference between methodology that's documented, calibrated, and audited — vs methodology that's marketed as a black box.

Galaxy bets on the first.
```

### Why this works

- Engages with a real argument substantively.
- Acknowledges the partial truth of the critique.
- Surfaces Galaxy's brand-position differentiator without being defensive.
- "Bets on the first" is concise + memorable.

### What it avoids

- No subtweeting the specific account.
- No calling out competitors.
- No claim of superiority — just specific positioning.

## Sunday — A genuine observation about a game

**Tweet:**

```
The Vikings/Lions game tonight produced an interesting line behavior.

Opened at Lions -3. Moved to -4. Moved back to -3 by kickoff after Vikings injury news flipped the calculus.

The factor model surfaced 67% confidence on Lions -3.5 in the morning report, which fell below threshold when the line moved to -4 (gap closed), and rose back above threshold when the line returned to -3.

Published the call on the second move.

Whether it lands or not, the autopsy will show the methodology in motion.
```

### Why this works

- Genuinely substantive game-day content.
- Demonstrates the methodology engaging with real market dynamics.
- Pre-commits to autopsy regardless of outcome.
- No certainty claim; no "I love this play" framing.

### What it avoids

- No tout-certainty.
- No "🎯" or fire-emoji framing.
- No promotional pitch.
- No suggestion the reader should bet alongside.

## What Galaxy's Twitter is FOR

Per `copy/galaxy-twitter-content-discipline.md`:

1. **Distribute methodology substance.** Make the model visible in fragments that compound across many tweets.

2. **Demonstrate restraint.** Quiet days demonstrate the brand position as much as substantive days.

3. **Engage with real arguments.** Public conversations about sports forecasting get Galaxy's substantive engagement; bad-faith engagement gets silence.

4. **Anchor public surfaces.** Methodology page, Loss Room, Pass List are referenced repeatedly; Twitter is the funnel, not the substance.

---

## What Galaxy's Twitter is NOT FOR

1. **Daily posting.** Cadence is content-led, not calendar-led.

2. **Quote-tweet pile-ons.** Galaxy doesn't dunk on competitors or critics.

3. **Promo cycles.** No "Vault is open — subscribe by tonight" pressure.

4. **Member engagement bait.** No "tag 3 friends" or "RT for a free pick" content.

5. **Celebrity referencing.** No name-dropping bettors, sports personalities, or media figures.

6. **Win celebration.** When Galaxy hits a call, Twitter doesn't celebrate. The Loss Room exists because losses are part of the record; celebrations would imply wins are something more.

---

## Common Twitter scenarios + responses

### A specific call hit

Don't tweet "we called it." The win is in the record. The methodology page + the Vault digest acknowledged the call ahead of time. Twitter celebration is brand-position drift.

### A specific call missed visibly

The autopsy goes in the Loss Room within 48 hours. A single Twitter acknowledgment if the loss is highly visible:

```
Last night's [game] didn't hit. Autopsy in the Loss Room tomorrow.

galaxysportsedge.com/loss-room
```

That's it.

### A competitor publicly attacks Galaxy

Per `copy/galaxy-twitter-incident-response-protocol.md`, 24-hour buffer. If response needed: substantive, brief, brand-position-anchored. Then disengage.

### A piece of Galaxy content goes viral positively

Don't capitalize. Don't post a thread "explaining why this resonated." Don't ask for follows or subscriptions. Treat the viral moment as data, not opportunity.

### A piece of Galaxy content goes viral negatively

24-hour buffer per `copy/galaxy-twitter-incident-response-protocol.md`. Then proceed per protocol.

## Tweet voice patterns to maintain

Across all tweets:

- **Sentences over emojis.** Galaxy is a word brand.
- **Specific over generic.** Numbers, dates, factor categories.
- **Calibrated over confident.** "67% confidence" beats "great spot."
- **Public-surface-anchored.** Methodology page, Loss Room, Pass List get linked.
- **Brief over thread-y.** Most tweets stand alone; threads are reserved for substance.
