# Galaxy Year-1 Qualitative Success Markers

**Audience:** Garrett. Internal.
**Purpose:** KPI metrics tell Garrett whether Galaxy is hitting numerical targets. The qualitative markers below tell Garrett whether Galaxy is *being Galaxy* — whether the brand position is compounding, or whether it's quietly degrading under operational pressure.

**Read cadence:** Monthly review, in addition to KPI ritual. Quarterly: deep audit against this document.

**Why this matters:** A Galaxy that hits KPI targets but loses brand position is sunset-shape, not success-shape. The qualitative markers catch what numbers miss.

---

## The 12 qualitative markers

Each marker is a signal that Galaxy is operating with brand discipline. Twelve markers, grouped into four categories.

### Category A — Member culture (how Vault feels)

#### Marker 1: Vault Discord conversations stay substantive

**Signal of health:** Vault Discord threads regularly contain questions about methodology, factor reasoning, autopsy patterns. Members debate substance, not gossip.

**Signal of drift:** Discord conversations devolve into pick-tailing requests, hot-take debates, or generic sports talk.

**Monthly check:** read the last 4 weeks of #vault-lounge. Count threads tagged "methodology" or "autopsy" vs threads tagged "general sports." Healthy ratio: 60/40 methodology/general or better.

#### Marker 2: Members share losses publicly in Discord

**Signal of health:** members occasionally post their own honest losses (separate from Galaxy's publications). Other members engage with substance.

**Signal of drift:** members only share wins. The room becomes a celebration channel.

**Monthly check:** how many member-shared losses appeared in #vault-lounge this month? Target: at least 2-3 / month. Zero across 3+ months is a warning.

#### Marker 3: Member-initiated methodology questions reach office hours

**Signal of health:** office hours members ask specific factor questions, methodology questions, calibration questions. The room cares about the model itself, not just outcomes.

**Signal of drift:** office hours members ask "what's the next pick?" or "tell us the win-rate this month."

**Monthly check:** in office hours, what percentage of member questions were methodology-focused vs outcome-focused? Healthy: 70%+ methodology.

#### Marker 4: Members defend Galaxy's restraint in their own words

**Signal of health:** when external critics surface (Twitter, sports betting forums), Vault members occasionally defend Galaxy's restraint — without being prompted by Garrett. They paraphrase the brand position in their own language.

**Signal of drift:** members don't defend Galaxy externally. Or worse: members defensively explain Galaxy's restraint to non-members in ways that don't land.

**Quarterly check:** search for Vault members' public mentions of Galaxy across Twitter / sports betting forums / Discord communities. Is the language Galaxy-aligned?

### Category B — Operating discipline (how Garrett operates)

#### Marker 5: Garrett can articulate the brand position in 3 sentences

**Signal of health:** at any given moment, Garrett can state Galaxy's brand position concisely without consulting documents. The position is internalized, not memorized.

**Signal of drift:** Garrett pauses, hedges, or needs to look up the brand position. The position has become detached from operational instinct.

**Monthly check:** at the start of the monthly KPI ritual, write the brand position from memory. Compare to `galaxy-brand-voice-canonical.md`. If significant drift: re-read the canonical voice doc + operating values + AI policy. Reset.

#### Marker 6: Decision-log entries get written on the same day decisions are made

**Signal of health:** when a meaningful decision happens (override, methodology change, partnership engagement, sunset consideration), the decision-log entry is written that day.

**Signal of drift:** decision-log entries pile up as backlog. Garrett writes them "when he has time" — which is rarely.

**Monthly check:** how many decisions made in the prior month had next-day-or-sooner log entries? Healthy: 90%+.

#### Marker 7: Garrett honors the Saturday OFF discipline

**Signal of health:** Garrett doesn't check Galaxy email, Discord, or admin cockpit on Saturdays. Period.

**Signal of drift:** Garrett "just checks" Saturday email or Discord. The slope starts gentle and ends with the founder-resilience playbook's kill criteria firing.

**Weekly check:** Saturday discipline held? Yes/no. Track in weekly retrospective.

#### Marker 8: The peak-block 09:00-11:00 produces the day's most important deliverable

**Signal of health:** Monday-Tuesday peak blocks produce the Wednesday digest. Thursday peak block produces autopsies or Almanac essays. The pattern holds across weeks.

**Signal of drift:** peak blocks get eaten by reactive work — email, partnership conversations, urgent press inquiries. Galaxy's compounding output suffers.

**Weekly check:** in the weekly retrospective, mark whether each weekday's peak block produced a peak-quality deliverable. Healthy: 4-5 of 5 weekdays.

### Category C — Content quality (how Galaxy publishes)

#### Marker 9: The Wednesday digest reads like the same operator wrote it 13 weeks in a row

**Signal of health:** the digest voice stays consistent. Length stays in the 500-900 word target. The 5-section structure holds. Members can identify a Galaxy digest from one paragraph.

**Signal of drift:** the digest gets shorter, slangier, more rushed. Marketing language creeps in. Sections get dropped.

**Quarterly check:** read 4 random Vault digests from the prior quarter. Are they tonally consistent? Length-consistent? Structure-consistent?

#### Marker 10: Loss Room autopsies stay specific and honest

**Signal of health:** every autopsy names the specific factor, the specific assumption, the specific outcome. No hedging. No padding. Honest assessment of process vs variance.

**Signal of drift:** autopsies start sounding like generic post-mortems. Specific factors get replaced with vague references. "What we'd do differently" sections become formulaic.

**Quarterly check:** read 5 random autopsies from the prior quarter. Are they specific? Could a reader learn something concrete from each one?

#### Marker 11: The Pass List shows genuine consideration

**Signal of health:** Pass List entries show range — some narrow misses (64% confidence, 1% below the floor), some clean passes (58% confidence). The mix tells a story about the publication discipline.

**Signal of drift:** Pass List becomes a routine output. Entries cluster around the same confidence band. The discipline of saying no becomes mechanical rather than thoughtful.

**Quarterly check:** review the Pass List distribution. Are the confidence bands diverse? Are the categories (A-E from `copy/pass-list-page-copy.md`) showing real variation?

#### Marker 12: The methodology page evolves with the work

**Signal of health:** the methodology page updates as the model evolves. New factor categories appear. Old factors get refined. The changelog reflects real change.

**Signal of drift:** the methodology page becomes stale. It says the same things in month 12 that it said in month 1.

**Quarterly check:** when was the last methodology page substantive update? Healthy: at least 2 substantive updates per quarter.

### Category D — Brand impact (how Galaxy is perceived)

#### Marker 13 (bonus): External writers cite Galaxy's specific surfaces

**Signal of health:** journalists, podcast hosts, Substack writers reference Galaxy's Loss Room, Pass List, or methodology page by name when discussing sports analytics.

**Signal of drift:** Galaxy gets mentioned generically ("a sports research platform") without specific surface citation.

**Annual check:** count external mentions in the past 12 months. How many cite specific Galaxy surfaces vs generic mentions? Healthy trajectory: increasing share of specific citations.

#### Marker 14 (bonus): Sports analytics professionals reach out unsolicited

**Signal of health:** analysts, researchers, sports media producers occasionally email Galaxy without a specific transaction in mind. "I've been reading your Loss Room — appreciate the discipline."

**Signal of drift:** unsolicited outreach is rare or zero. The brand isn't compounding in the analyst community.

**Annual check:** how many unsolicited "appreciation" or "let me know if you need X" emails arrived in the past 12 months? Trajectory matters more than absolute number.

## Galaxy's "quiet success" pattern

The qualitative markers above describe what Galaxy looks like when it's compounding correctly. The pattern is recognizable across founders who've built sustainable subscription businesses (Bill James, Pro Football Focus pre-acquisition, Baseball Prospectus).

The pattern:

1. **The work itself is impressive.** Members can point to specific Galaxy artifacts (Loss Room entries, autopsies, Pass List, methodology page) and say "this is unusually good."

2. **The culture compounds.** New members joining 12 months in arrive into a community that's been forming. They learn the conventions from existing members, not from onboarding emails.

3. **The founder is present but not central.** Garrett's voice appears in specific surfaces (digests, office hours, Almanac essay). The rest of Galaxy speaks as a method, not as Garrett.

4. **Word-of-mouth is the growth.** Members refer other members because the product is brand-aligned, not because there's a promotional incentive (though the referral program exists per `copy/vault-referral-program.md`).

5. **External validation is incidental.** Press, podcast appearances, social mentions happen — but Galaxy doesn't optimize for them. They're a side effect of the discipline, not a measure of it.

When 8+ of the 12 markers are healthy: Galaxy is in the compounding-correctly pattern. Continue operating; trust the work.

When 4-7 markers are healthy: investigate which markers are weak. The pattern of weak markers tells Garrett what to address.

When 0-3 markers are healthy: the brand position is in trouble. Re-read the operating values + brand voice canonical + founder resilience playbook. Reset.

## The single most important qualitative question

At any given monthly KPI ritual, ask:

> If a thoughtful sports analytics reader who'd never heard of Galaxy stumbled onto galaxysportsedge.com this month, would they find a platform that:
> - Reads as serious and disciplined?
> - Shows what most platforms hide?
> - Operates with restraint they can verify?

If yes: Galaxy is being Galaxy.
If no: the qualitative markers above tell you which dimension to fix.

The KPI numbers can be on-target while the answer above is no. That's the gap qualitative markers fill.

## How this document differs from the KPI ritual

The KPI ritual (per `audit/kpi-operator-ritual.md`) is mechanical: numbers vs thresholds vs decisions.

This qualitative-markers document is interpretive: signals vs patterns vs brand position.

The two complement each other. A healthy month has:
- Green numerical KPIs (per the KPI ritual).
- 8+ healthy qualitative markers (per this document).

A red-flag month has:
- KPIs on track but qualitative markers degrading (warning).
- Or qualitative markers healthy but KPIs failing (investigate; the model may not be calibrated to brand realities).

The combination tells Garrett more than either alone.

## What this document deliberately does NOT include

1. **No member-NPS tracking.** Galaxy doesn't run NPS surveys. The qualitative markers capture the underlying signal without the survey overhead.

2. **No vanity-metric markers.** Email open rates, Twitter followers, podcast download counts — none of these are markers of Galaxy being Galaxy.

3. **No specific quantitative thresholds.** The markers are interpretive. Garrett uses judgment, not a scoring rubric.

4. **No public reporting of the markers.** This document is internal. Some markers (member behavior in Discord) involve member data that stays private.

5. **No tying markers to founder compensation or rewards.** Galaxy is single-operator; there's nothing to tie. The markers are operating discipline, not performance management.
