## 1. Research question
Is recorded playing time a neutral denominator for injury rates? Where recorded minutes track the workload under study, dividing injury counts by them removes part of the association the rate is supposed to scale — quantify the two defects (outcome truncation, exposure-dependent playing time) via the "denominator gradient" γ = slope of log(recorded minutes) on recent exposure.

## 2. Dataset / schema
Retrospective observational cohort from public records. Reference: 88,573 appearances by 1,208 established EPL players, 1 Jul 2017–7 Apr 2025 (eligibility: positive minutes, 900+ earlier club minutes; 576 same-day spell starts, 0.65% of appearances). Replication: 628,487 men's + 29,799 women's appearances across 15 European leagues (appearance records alone, no injury data needed for γ). Women's data from Soccerdonna match reports (starters + substitution minutes reconstructed; identity: 22 starters × 90 min = 1,980 recorded minutes per match). Derived de-identified data openly available at DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21940596.

## 3. Method / model
Poisson model log E[y] = log(m) + a + b·x vs per-appearance logistic; the identifying comparison is fixed-90 vs recorded-minutes offset, holding family/link/rows fixed (only the offset changes). γ fitted as OLS of log recorded minutes on previous-seven-day club minutes per 90, with weekly/half-weekly calendar-phase terms, covariance clustered on player (refit under club-season and fixture clustering as checks). Two defects separated empirically: (a) outcome truncation isolated by replacing event rows' minutes with the non-event lineup-role mean and refitting; (b) exposure dependence isolated by refitting denominators within every lineup stratum and by adjusting vs restricting on squad role. Attenuation measured by fitting one Poisson model twice, changing nothing but the offset, with 1,000 player-resamples for intervals. Attenuation-vs-γ relationship traced by sweeping a floor on recorded minutes (Figure 2).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Denominator identity (to first order): log E[y] = log(m) + a + b_off·x and log E[y] = a′ + b_app·x imply b_off = b_app − γ, where γ = ∂log(m)/∂x. Poisson with log workload entered as offset ≡ log workload as covariate with coefficient fixed at 1 (Shrier et al.); where a risk factor changes workload, including workload biases the estimate — overadjustment / control for a variable on the exposure→outcome path. Negligible threshold γ < 0.05 (a reporting convention: at γ=0.05 the measured attenuation ratio is 1.20, i.e. ~4.1% of the rate ratio). Assumptions: (1) Poisson/log-linear mean structure for injury counts; (2) same-day spell start = public injury report (explicitly NOT a clinical diagnosis or verified time loss; outcome sensitivity unknown — second blinded audit queue returned no verdict); (3) minutes reconstructed from reports are accurate; (4) identity approximations hold while γ is small (predicted/observed attenuation ratio runs 0.94 at γ=0.001 to 2.01 at γ=0.303, so γ alone overstates bias as it grows).

## 5. Features / target
Features: recent exposure (previous-seven-day club minutes per 90; robustness window 31–37 days earlier), lineup role (starter/substitute/unknown). Targets: same-day injury/absence spell start (reference cohort only); γ uses no injury data at all.

## 6. Validation design
Measurement study, explicitly not hypothesis-testing ("These are measurements from public records, not a tested hypothesis... nothing here is confirmatory; the reference association is a worked example"). Replication: gradient refit in 15 leagues, two sexes, two providers (Frauen-Bundesliga via Soccerdonna 0.456 vs FBref 0.509, overlapping intervals). Clustering variants (player / club-season / fixture) leave estimates untouched. Negative-control exposure window (31–37 days prior) reproduces the decomposition (gradient 0.201, ratio 1.99 vs 2.01). Automated test suite at 100% statement/branch coverage recomputes every number from deposited data.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Reference cohort: changing only the offset moved the seven-day estimate from rate ratio 1.27 (95% CI 1.11–1.44, fixed-90) to 1.09 (0.95–1.25, recorded minutes) — log-scale attenuation 0.151 (0.140–0.163), i.e. 14.0% of the rate ratio divided away ("two thirds of the excess association" above the null is lost: 0.09 of 0.27 survives). Outcome truncation explains essentially none of it (−0.095% of the attenuation, interval −0.41 to 0.20; γ 0.303 → 0.304 with truncation removed). Exposure dependence explains it: pooled γ=0.303; within starters an order of magnitude smaller (attenuation all but vanishes, Δ=0.139, 0.128–0.150). Gradient across leagues: 0.431–0.655 men's (median 0.536), 0.444–0.647 women's (median 0.466) — above the 0.05 threshold in all 15 leagues. Restricting to starters brought γ below threshold in 8/8 men's leagues (0.020–0.031) but only 5/7 women's (Spain bound 0.050, Sweden 0.058 — report per appearance there). Adjusting for role instead of restricting left 0.071 of the 0.151 attenuation (six times what restriction leaves). Independent-source attribution check: 24 of 27 resolved same-day records confirmed (88.9%).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Stated: no pre-registration, all specifications chosen after inspecting data (intervals cover sampling uncertainty only); public records fail surveillance consensus standards; same-day records may be delayed coding/training events/unrelated absence; women's panel is one season per league vs multi-season men's; Spain/Sweden exceptions are single-season observed boundaries; ascertainment audit's frame random in neither. Added: soccer only — no NFL replication; the remedy (restrict to starters) does not generalize to NFL where rotation is positional and minutes are far less bimodal; outcome is report-date based, not time-loss.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Adapt as a reporting discipline for GSE's own injury-rate computations, and as a guardrail if/when an availability sub-model is built:
1. Before reporting any injury rate per snap/minute, fit the gradient: OLS of log(snaps) on the workload variable of interest (e.g. snaps in prior N games), with player-season-clustered SEs. Report γ with its interval.
2. If the lower bound of γ exceeds 0.05, do not publish per-snap/per-minute rates — report risk per game (per-appearance) instead, or restrict to a low-variance-minutes stratum (e.g. every-down players) and re-verify γ there. Note the paper's finding that covariate adjustment for role does NOT substitute for restriction.
3. Do not interpret γ itself as the bias magnitude (identity over-predicts as γ grows); use the floor-sweep calibration (Figure 2B) to convert γ to attenuation share — ~4% of the rate ratio at the threshold.
4. NFL caveat: starter/substitute stratification is soccer-shaped; the NFL analogue needs its own stratification (e.g. snap-share deciles) and league-specific verification — the authors' core instruction: "run the check locally."

## 12. Reproducible test
Replication target: reconstruct γ from play-by-play snap data (e.g. nflverse): regress log(offensive snaps) on prior-4-game snap load per player-season with player-clustered SEs; confirm whether γ > 0.05 (expected: yes, given rotation patterns) and whether restriction to every-down starters (≥90% snap share) collapses it. Pass criterion: gradient table by position group + a decision (per-snap vs per-game reporting) recorded in the repo. Second test: apply to GSE's own workload→availability estimates if an availability model is commissioned — verify attenuation before/after denominator choice.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADAPT: the denominator gradient is a concrete, cheap, one-regression diagnostic with quantified consequences (14% of a rate ratio divided away in the reference cohort) and an actionable remedy. It is methodology, not a plug-in model — hence ADAPT, not ADOPT. Rejection of the original 1491 stands: pediatric pain data had no path to NFL value; this replacement fills the causal_injury lane with workload–injury measurement rigor that does transfer.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Beyond the paper: use the denominator-gradient diagnostic as an *audit tool* on a published NFL injury-rate study. Take one published snap-normalized injury-rate claim, reconstruct γ from the same data source, and re-report the rate under the floor-sweep calibration (Figure 2B) — quantifying how much of the published effect survives the attenuation correction. The paper validates the diagnostic on its own soccer cohorts but never applies it retrospectively to someone else's published number; a successful audit would make the γ-check a standard citation in GSE's availability/injury content, with a documented precedent for when a per-minute rate claim should have been per-appearance.
