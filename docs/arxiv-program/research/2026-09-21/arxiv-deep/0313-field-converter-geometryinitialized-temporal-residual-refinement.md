# [0313] Field Converter: Geometry-Initialized Temporal Residual Refinement for World-Grounded Player Pose Estimation from Soccer Broadcasts (arXiv:2609.10498v1)

**Citation:** Simon Khan, Laurent Gajny, Jennyfer Lecompte, Sébastien Laporte (2026). *Field Converter: Geometry-Initialized Temporal Residual Refinement for World-Grounded Player Pose Estimation from Soccer Broadcasts*. arXiv:2609.10498v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10498v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 1268 lines, main paper + supplement).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — the geometry-initialized residual-refinement pattern (calibrated ray–ground intersection plus a temporal network that predicts only the residual correction) is the right template for GSE's NGS-replacement lane deriving world-grounded player movement from NFL broadcast film; it must be re-engineered for NFL camera calibration, occlusion, and the lack of a fixed ground plane reference.

## 1. Research question
The paper asks: can monocular soccer broadcast footage be converted into metric-accurate, world-grounded 3D player trajectories without per-frame manual calibration? It proposes initializing each player's root position by calibrated ray–ground-plane intersection, then refining with a temporal neural network that predicts only the residual correction on top of the geometric initialization, and validates the approach on the FIFA Skeletal Tracking Light 2026 dataset.

## 2. Dataset / schema
FIFA Skeletal Tracking Light 2026: 89 clips from 8 matches, approximately 2.41M valid player-frame observations (paper's stated scale). Inputs per frame: broadcast video, 2D pose keypoints, bounding boxes, camera calibration (intrinsics/extrinsics per clip). Reference: relative 3D pose from SAM 3D Body; ground-truth world positions from the dataset's skeletal tracking. Split is match-disjoint: 62 clips / 6 matches train, 12 clips (BRA_KOR) validation, 15 clips (ENG_FRA) test. Access: not stated as public in the paper — availability/licensing terms are not given; treat as restricted unless verified.

## 3. Method / model
Two-stage pipeline: (a) Geometry initialization — calibrated ray–ground intersection gives a metric root initialization per player per frame (rays cast from camera center through the detected root keypoint, intersected with the pitch plane); relative pose comes from SAM 3D Body; outputs are transformed into shared world coordinates. (b) Temporal residual refinement — a network (MLP / TCN / Transformer variants) predicts a camera-space residual correction on top of the geometric initialization, rather than predicting absolute position. TCN: 41-frame windows, stride 8, five residual blocks, dilations (1, 2, 4, 8, 16), 1.278M parameters. Transformer: two layers, four attention heads, 1.252M parameters. Ablations remove: valid-joint mask, ground-intersection initialization, relative 3D pose input, 2D pose + box cues, camera descriptor. Throughput measured on a single NVIDIA L40S (PyTorch 2.6, CUDA 12.4): mean training throughput (2.95 ± 0.03)×10³ / (25.5 ± 20.8)×10³ / (61.4 ± 25.5)×10³ input player-frames/s for MLP/TCN/Transformer; end-to-end batched evaluation 530±290 / 467±107 / 551±141 video frames/s (excluding upstream tracking, calibration, and SAM3DBody inference).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
The paper's key formal content (as stated): root initialization via ray–ground-plane intersection from calibrated cameras; residual prediction formulated as correcting the geometric initialization in camera space; temporal aggregation over 41-frame windows. (No single numbered master equation is quoted in the extract beyond the architectural specifications; the geometric construction is described procedurally.) Stated assumptions: (a) the pitch is a known plane (flat ground-plane assumption — violated when players are airborne); (b) camera calibration is available per clip; (c) 2D pose/bbox detections and SAM 3D Body relative poses are given upstream; (d) residual corrections are small relative to the initialization, justifying the residual formulation. The airborne violation is quantified: error rises to ~37 cm in the highest foot-clearance interval vs. grounded frames.

## 5. Features / target
Input features per player-frame: 2D pose keypoints, bounding box, camera descriptor (calibration parameters), valid-joint mask, relative 3D pose (SAM 3D Body), ground-intersection root initialization. Target: metric 3D root position in world coordinates (and thereby world-grounded full-body pose). Evaluation metrics: root translation error (cm), world MPJPE (cm), local MPJPE (cm), reprojection error (px), plus temporal-consistency metrics (root velocity error cm/s, root acceleration error m/s²).

## 6. Validation design
Match-disjoint split (train: 62 clips/6 matches; val: 12 clips BRA_KOR; test: 15 clips ENG_FRA) — temporally and structurally clean, no leakage across matches. Baselines: pure geometry initialization (no learning), frame-wise MLP residual, TCN residual, Transformer residual, and direct absolute-root prediction (no geometric init) for MLP/TCN. Metrics: root error, world/local MPJPE, reprojection error on the held-out matches, with bootstrap confidence intervals on temporal-consistency metrics. Motion-regime breakdowns: grounded vs. airborne, high-speed (top 25%), large-init-error (top 25%), distance from image center, bounding-box height, pelvis height.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Paper's main results (Table 3, test matches): Geometry-only init — root error 48.58 cm, world MPJPE 48.36 cm, local MPJPE 7.74 cm, reprojection 5.39 px. MLP residual — 13.63 / 15.76 / 7.74 / 3.69. TCN residual — 10.12 / 13.20 / 7.74 / 3.49. Transformer residual — 11.04 / 13.21 / 7.74 / 3.43. Direct absolute-root prediction (no init): MLP 256.00 cm, TCN 63.05 cm — showing the geometric initialization does most of the work. Temporal consistency (Table 8): root velocity error 131.9 [124.1, 142.2] cm/s (MLP) → 100.6 [95.3, 107.6] (TCN); root acceleration error 48.3 [45.6, 51.7] → 39.6 [37.7, 42.2] m/s². Airborne degradation: ~37 cm error in the highest-clearance interval; geometry-only approaches ~1 m. Depth (camera z-axis) remains the dominant error component after refinement. These are the paper's claims on a match-disjoint test set — the strongest validation design in this wave.

## 8. Code / data availability
Code: https://github.com/KhanSimon/field_converter (stated in paper). Data: FIFA Skeletal Tracking Light 2026 — public availability/licensing not stated in the paper; verify before use.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
(a) The match-disjoint split is genuinely clean — no leakage found in the validation design. (b) Ground-plane assumption: airborne players (jumps, dives, tackles) break the initialization; error ~37 cm at high clearance and ~1 m for geometry-only. For NFL, airborne/contact phases (jump balls, tackles) are exactly the high-leverage moments. (c) Upstream dependence: results exclude tracking, calibration, and SAM3DBody errors — end-to-end error on raw broadcast video will be larger than reported. (d) Soccer-specific: fixed camera geometry per clip, open-field sport, 22 players spread out; NFL has denser formations, heavier occlusion, and sideline/All-22 vs. broadcast view mixing. (e) Local MPJPE is identical (7.74 cm) across all variants — the paper's contribution is purely global localization, not pose quality. (f) Dataset licensing unclear; FIFA data may not be usable commercially.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Adaptation plan for NFL broadcast film: (a) data — licensed NFL broadcast clips (or All-22 where available) with a small hand-annotated calibration/ground-truth set; camera calibration per play via field-marking detection (yard lines/hash marks give the plane + scale, replacing soccer's pitch-plane assumption); (b) upstream — off-the-shelf 2D pose (e.g., RTMPose) + a 3D lifter, with the paper's valid-joint-mask idea retained for occluded joints (heavy in NFL line play); (c) model — TCN residual refiner (41-frame windows, stride 8) on top of ray–field-plane intersection init; start from the paper's open code and retrain; (d) training — game-disjoint splits (never split plays from the same game across train/test); loss on root error + reprojection; (e) serving — batch/offline feature extraction for weekly model inputs (not real-time); outputs feed route/separation/speed features into the projection engine; (f) estimated effort: 4–6 weeks for a prototype on a few games, assuming licensed film access. Legal: verify film-licensing terms before building.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: 3–5 licensed NFL games with broadcast film; hand-annotate or license ground-truth player positions for a sample of plays (or cross-check derived speed/separation against published NGS values for the same plays where available). Metric: root translation error (cm) and derived speed error (mph) on game-disjoint test plays. Baseline to beat: pure ray–plane geometric initialization (no learned refinement). Time window: one full game held out from training. Success = learned residual refinement reduces root error by ≥25% vs. geometry-only on the held-out game, with no degradation on airborne/contact plays beyond the paper's reported ~37 cm regime.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
Adopt the pipeline into GSE's feature factory only if: (a) on game-disjoint NFL test film, world-grounded root error ≤ 20 cm median (paper achieves 10–14 cm in soccer; NFL occlusion justifies the looser bar); AND (b) derived per-play speed/separation features correlate r ≥ 0.90 with NGS-published values on overlapping plays; AND (c) film licensing permits the use case. If any condition fails, keep the NGS-replacement spec on its current public-data path and shelve the video pipeline.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Beyond the paper: replace the flat-plane assumption with a learned contact/airborne classifier that switches the initialization — ground-plane intersection when the player is grounded, ballistic-motion prior (constant-acceleration extrapolation from takeoff pose/velocity) when airborne. Rationale: the paper's own ablations show airborne frames are the dominant residual error source (~37 cm), and NFL's highest-leverage plays (contested catches, tackles) are disproportionately airborne; a regime-switching initializer attacks the exact failure mode the paper documents but does not fix.
