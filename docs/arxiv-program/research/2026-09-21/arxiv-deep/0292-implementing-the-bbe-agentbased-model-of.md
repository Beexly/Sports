# [0292] Implementing the BBE Agent-Based Model of a Sports-Betting Exchange (arXiv:2108.02419v1)

**Citation:** Cliff, D., Hawkins, J., Keen, J., & Lau-Soto, R. (2021). *Implementing the BBE Agent-Based Model of a Sports-Betting Exchange*. arXiv:2108.02419v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02419v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 1339 lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — the agent-based simulator is a horse-racing in-play exchange tool, but its core ideas (synthetic-data-generator with known ground truth for training data-hungry models; rational-predictor bettors calibrated by dry-run count d; opinion-dynamics/order-flow mechanics) adapt to NFL live-betting research once the race physics are replaced with an NFL game-state simulator.

## 1. Research question
Data-hungry ML methods for profitable automated betting need far more in-play exchange data than real betting exchanges (Betfair, Smarkets, Betdaq) sell or even possess. The paper implements BBE (Bristol Betting Exchange), an agent-based model of an in-play betting exchange for track-race events (horse/greyhound-style races), as a synthetic data generator (SDG): a generative model that produces data preserving key statistical features of real data while the ground truth (causal mechanics behind every datum) is known. Three independent implementations are built and compared: single-threaded Python (STP), multi-threaded Python (MTP), and multi-core mixed-language (MCM: Python + OpenCL on GPU).

## 2. Dataset / schema
No empirical dataset: synthetic only. Race simulator outputs: per-timestep position d_c(t) of each of n_r competitors on a 1-D track of length L_r, until the slowest crosses the finish line; bettor-level: time series of each agent's decimal-odds beliefs (re-evaluated every 10 s in the example), order book (aggregated back/lay stakes at each decimal odds per competitor, top-3 prices displayed, full ladder available), matched/unmatched bet records (arrival time, bettor identity, amount). Example: 5-competitor, 2000 m race lasting < 6 min; PMF over n_r! finish-order outcomes estimated by R repeated runs with different seeds. Implementations on GitHub: Yepadee/Bristol-Betting-Exchange (MCM), keenjam/BettingExchange (MTP), RobertoLauSoto/BristolBettingExchange (STP).

## 3. Method / model
Race dynamics (Eq. 1): d_c(t+δt) = d_c(t) + S_c(t); S_c(t, f⃗_r, d⃗) = R_c(t,d⃗)·P_c(f⃗_r,p⃗_c)·δ(v⃗_c) if Δ_c(t) > θ_c (clear track ahead), else R_c(t,d⃗)·δ_min (blocked by slower competitor ahead; δ_min = min of own and blocker's previous step). P_c(·) ∈ [0,1] penalises mismatch between race factor vector f⃗_r (track dry/wet, flat/undulating) and competitor preference vector p⃗_c; R_c models pacing (fast start/slow finish etc.); δ draws stochastic step sizes, e.g. U(10,20) for one competitor vs U(1,25) for another. Matching engine: standard limit-order-book semantics — backs matched to oldest unmatched lays at the same price and vice versa; partial matches stay active; unmatched bets expire at close with stakes returned; ~5% commission on winnings only. Bettor agents: RP(d) Rational Predictors (d dry-run race simulations aggregated by frequentist statistics; d=0 = random, d→∞ ≈ omniscient); LinEx (linear extrapolation of per-competitor mean speed over past N seconds); LW (leader wins); UD (underdog: P2 wins unless gap > D, then P1); BTF (back the favourite = lowest market odds); RB (representative bettor: stake clustering on multiples of 2/5/10, favourite-longshot bias, per Brown & Yang 2016); ZI (zero-intelligence noise traders). The three implementations differ only in execution: STP (single-threaded Python), MTP (one Python thread per bettor-agent, asynchronous), MCM (OpenCL race simulations on a 640-core NVIDIA GTX 1050 GPU, Python orchestration).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
d_c(t+δt) = d_c(t) + S_c(t); S_c(t) > 0 always (race must end). S_c(t,f⃗_r,d⃗) = {R_c(t,d⃗)·P_c(f⃗_r,p⃗_c)·δ(v⃗_c) if Δ_c(t) > θ_c; R_c(t,d⃗)·δ_min otherwise}, Δ_c(t) = d_{i⁺}(t) − d_c(t), i⁺ = argmin_{i:d_i>d_c} Δ_c(t), δ_min = min(S_c(t−δt), S_{i⁺}(t−δt)). Race state summarised by vector d⃗(t) with |d⃗| = n_r. Decimal odds convention (e.g., $1 stake returning $11 total = 11.0). Assumptions: 1-D track with point competitors; blocking only via step-size cap; bettors re-evaluate at fixed intervals; MVP implementations keep preference/factor vectors length-1; win-bets only (no place/each-way markets); no latency, no partial fills beyond stake splitting; commission flat 5% on winnings.

## 5. Features / target
Features (for bettor agents): current positions d_c(t), per-competitor mean step size over trailing N seconds (LinEx), market stake distribution (BTF), leader/P2 identities and gaps (LW, UD), competitor preference/factor mismatch (RP dry-runs implicitly). Target: race winner (and implied finish-order PMF). Horizon: in-play, sub-second to 10-second resolution over the race duration (~6 min example).

## 6. Validation design
Cross-implementation replication: PMF estimates over n_r! finish orders from R i.i.d. runs per implementation, compared via Kruskal–Wallis nonparametric test (noise vs consistent variation). Performance scaling: wall-clock time per single race vs number of competitors (STP: mean of 1,000 runs; MCM: mean of 100,000 runs on GPU), log-scale comparison; Figure 4 close-up with coefficient of variation ~0.005. No comparison to real exchange data — stylized-facts alignment is explicitly future work (no published stylized facts for in-play track-race markets exist; closest is Restocchi 2018 on political prediction markets). Illustrative single-race figures (space-time plot, RP bettor odds time series) are narrative, not validation.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
MCM runs ≈1000× faster than STP/MTP (≈ three orders of magnitude; STP and MTP essentially identical since MTP parallelises bettors, not races). Figure 3: log-scaled wall-clock per race across competitor counts; Figure 4 shows nonlinear MCM scaling once GPU SIMD lanes saturate, CoV ≈ 0.005. Illustrative: Horse1 starts favourite, finishes last; Horse5 starts longest odds, wins. Dry-run cost illustration: one 5-min race with B rational RP(d=50) bettors updating once per second needs 1 + 15,000×B dry-run simulations per race — motivating the GPU implementation. No accuracy/profit numbers — this is an implementation/MVP paper, not a strategy paper. No code-verified replication statistics beyond the Kruskal–Wallis proposal.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
(a) The race physics (1-D point competitors with uniform step draws) is a toy — no calibration of generated data to real exchange statistics is performed; the "stylized facts" alignment is entirely future work. (b) Bettor rationality is implemented via dry-run counts d — a convenient fiction, not a model of how humans bet; RB/ZI agents encode only a few known biases. (c) Win-markets only; no place/each-way markets, no cross-market interplay. (d) No latency, no exchange suspension at race end (real in-play markets suspend on key events). (e) The 1000× GPU speedup is for embarrassingly parallel race sims, not for the asynchronous bettor/exchange interaction — MTP (the more faithful deployment model) is no faster than STP. (f) Domain is horse-racing-style in-play win betting; nothing in the paper transfers to NFL except the architecture concept.

## 10. GSE overlap
Extension, not duplicate. The corpus has no synthetic-data or agent-based-model work: gap #7 (in-play/live NFL spread & total modeling) names iWinRNFL as the only in-game WP coverage and calls live spread/total surfaces thin; the ML brief lists "automated discovery" and "continuous learning" as commissioned topics. BBE's SDG philosophy (known ground truth, unlimited labelled data for data-hungry models) is the missing piece for training any GSE live-betting model where real second-by-second NFL odds + game-state data are prohibitively expensive or unavailable. The RP(d) dry-run idea (bettor rationality = number of pre-simulations) is also a ready-made mechanism for modelling the *market's* information level in steam-move studies (gap #3, market microstructure).

## 11. GSE implementation spec
(1) Build an NFL-flavoured BBE: replace the 1-D race with a drive-level NFL game simulator (nflverse-based: down/distance/yardline state, EP/WP transition model from existing repo assets) and replace bettor agents with archetypes relevant to NFL live markets — sharp (low d-error), public (favourite/longshot bias, stake clustering), steam-chaser (BTF analog on line moves). (2) Generate synthetic live odds tapes (spread/total moneyline ladder per minute of game time) with known ground-truth game scripts; use them to train/validate GSE's live-betting models and to stress-test the Kelly sizing module under adversarial market conditions (flash crashes, steam runs). (3) Use the RP(d) mechanism to parameterise market efficiency: sweep d to find the efficiency level at which GSE's live edge disappears — a direct measure of how sharp the real market must be to erase GSE's edge. Stack: Python (STP-style for accessibility); GPU port only if race-count demands it. Effort: ~2–3 engineer-weeks for the MVP simulator (the matching engine is ~200 lines; the game simulator reuses repo EP models).

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: 2024 NFL season nflverse play-by-play + Odds API live/historical lines (whatever granularity is available). Metric: (a) generate 10,000 synthetic games with known scripts and verify the live-spread simulator reproduces the empirical distribution of closing-line moves within ±0.5 points mean absolute deviation; (b) train GSE's live total model on synthetic tapes only, then evaluate log-loss on real 2024 games vs the same model trained on real data — gap must be < 10% relative for the SDG to be useful. Window: 2024 regular season.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT the synthetic-live-market simulator if the synthetic-trained live-total model achieves within 10% relative log-loss of the real-trained model on the 2024 test window AND the simulator reproduces closing-line-move distributions within ±0.5 points MAD; REJECT as a training source (keep only as a stress-test toy) if the log-loss gap exceeds 25% — synthetic data that doesn't transfer is worse than no data.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Go beyond the paper by adding cross-market interplay the authors defer: simulate the live spread, total, AND moneyline books jointly with a shared game-state process so that arbitrage constraints (e.g., moneyline/spread consistency) hold by construction. Then study "synthetic steam": inject a known informed-bettor agent (high-d RP) mid-game and measure how fast the BTF/follower agents propagate the move — giving GSE a laboratory for detecting real steam in NFL live markets, a direct attack on market-microstructure gap #3.
