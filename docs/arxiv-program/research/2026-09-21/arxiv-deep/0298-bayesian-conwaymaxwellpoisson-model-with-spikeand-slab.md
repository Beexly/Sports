# [0298] Bayesian Conway-Maxwell-Poisson model with spike-and-slab priors for dispersed count data with application to football scores (arXiv:2607.18009v1)

**Citation:** Zhang, N., Rastelli, R., & Friel, N. (2026). *Bayesian Conway-Maxwell-Poisson model with spike-and-slab priors for dispersed count data with application to football scores*. University College Dublin / Insight Centre. arXiv:2607.18009v1 [stat.ME]. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18009v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (1710-line text extract, incl. appendices A–C).
**Verdict:** ADOPT — this is the count-data model GSE should be running for NFL scoring distributions. The SAS prior is an elegant way to let team-level dispersion declare itself instead of assuming Poisson. Most directly applicable: totals (over/under) modeling, where dispersion is the entire game.

## 1. Research question
Poisson models of football scores assume equidispersion (mean = variance), yet team-level goal frequencies across a season show substantial heterogeneity in dispersion. The authors generalize the Maher (1982) Poisson goal model with a Conway-Maxwell-Poisson (CMP) likelihood whose team-specific dispersion parameters ν_i carry a spike-and-slab (SAS) prior: spike at ν=1 (equidispersed Poisson regime), slab LogNormal around 1 (non-equidispersed regime). This yields interpretable posterior probabilities P(Z_i=1|Y) of each team's non-equidispersion plus the magnitude of departure, with a thresholding rule for classification. Inference via a tailored Metropolis-within-Gibbs sampler handling the doubly-intractable CMP likelihood via the Exchange algorithm. Evaluated on simulations and 5 EPL seasons (2020/21–2024/25).

## 2. Dataset / schema
Simulations: synthetic 20-team leagues, 10 equidispersed (ν_true=1) + 10 non-equidispersed (ν_true=k, 0.2≤k≤4), 5 replicates per k; WAIC settings n=760 per league. Real: EPL seasons 2020/21–2024/25, analyzed independently (2023/24: 380 matches, record 1,246 goals, 3.28/match; Man City champions, 91 pts). Data from football-data.co.uk. Out-of-sample: parameters estimated through game-day t (starting mid-season, 190 games), predict next 10 matches, roll forward — 19 training sessions per season. Code: https://github.com/nzhang98/compoisson_goal.

## 3. Method / model
Likelihood (Eq. 3): y^H_{i,j} | μ^H_{i,j}, ν_i ~ COM-Poisson(μ^H_{i,j}, ν_i); y^A_{j,i} | μ^A_{j,i}, ν_j ~ COM-Poisson(μ^A_{j,i}, ν_j) — dispersion follows the scoring team. Centring via Maher log-linear: log(μ^H_{i,j}) = α_i + β_j + γ; log(μ^A_{j,i}) = α_j + β_i (Eq. 4); sum-to-zero constraints Σα_i = Σβ_i = 0 (Eq. 6) with N−1 hyperplane proposals (Eqs. 7–8). SAS prior (Eq. 5): Z_i|p_i ~ Bernoulli(p_i), p_i ~ Beta(1,1), η_i ~ Normal(0,1), ν_i := exp(Z_i η_i) — i.e., (ν_i|Z_i) ~ (1−Z_i)δ_1 + Z_i LogNormal(0,1). Priors: γ, α_i, β_i ~ Normal(0,100). Inference: Metropolis-within-Gibbs (Algorithm 1): Z_i via binary-flip MH + Exchange (auxiliary draws from CMP, Eq. 11); p_i Gibbs (Eq. 12); (α_i, η_i) jointly via positively correlated bivariate Gaussian proposal (ρ=0.85, s_α=0.10, s_η=0.4, Eqs. 9–10, acceptance Eq. 13); β_i via Eq. 14; γ via Eq. 15–16. Doubly-intractable likelihood handled by Exchange algorithm (Benson & Friel 2021) with rejection sampler (geometric envelope for ν<1, Poisson envelope for ν≥1, Eqs. 19–21, Algorithm 2); unbiased likelihood estimator from rejection counts (Eq. 22, Appendix A.3). 250,000 MCMC iterations, 50,000 burn-in. Comparators: Poisson baseline, CMP-Full (Z_i≡1). Model fit: WAIC (Eq. 17, S=5,000 posterior samples); prediction: Ignorance score IGN = −log2(f̃(y*)) (Eq. 18) on Outcome / Over-Under 2.5 / Goal-Difference; RPS checked, similar conclusions.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Eq. 1: f(y|μ,ν) = (μ^y/y!)^ν / Z_f(μ,ν); Z_f(μ,ν) = Σ_{x=0}^∞ (μ^x/x!)^ν. Eq. 2: ν=1 recovers Poisson (Z_f = e^μ). Moments: E(Y) ≈ μ + 1/(2ν) − 1/2, Var(Y) ≈ μ/ν. Eq. 3: team-specific ν on scoring team. Eq. 4: log(μ^H_{i,j}) = α_i + β_j + γ; log(μ^A_{j,i}) = α_j + β_i. Eq. 5: π(Z_i|p_i) ~ Bern(p_i); π(p_i) ~ Beta(a,b); π(η_i) ~ N(0,σ_η²); ν_i := exp(Z_i η_i). Eq. 6: Σα_i = Σβ_i = 0. Eqs. 9–10: correlated (α_i, η_i) proposal, ρ=0.85. Eq. 11: A_Z exchange acceptance for Z_i. Eq. 17: WAIC = −2(lppd − p_WAIC). Eq. 18: IGN = −log2(f̃(y*)). Assumptions: conditionally independent scores (dependence only via team parameters; justified by Egidi et al. 2018 / McHale & Scarf 2011 low correlation); dispersion is a team-season trait; home advantage γ constant across teams; seasons analyzed independently (team turnover); equidispersed teams sit at posterior P(Z_i=1)≈0.25 baseline, not 0.

## 5. Features / target
Features: match scores (home/away goals), team identities, home/away indicator. Targets: team-level attack α_i, defence β_i, home γ, dispersion ν_i and regime indicator Z_i; predictive: exact scoreline probabilities, {p_H, p_D, p_A}, Over-Under 2.5, Goal Difference. Horizon: season-level (38 games/team), out-of-sample weekly rolling forecasts from mid-season.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Simulation (Table 1): FP ≈ 0.04–0.16 (avg ~10%) stable across k; TP = 1.00 at ν_true=0.2, 0.94 at 0.3, drops sharply (0.58 at 0.5, 0.14 at 0.8); under-dispersed TP 0.94–0.98 at ν_true ≥ 3.2, 0.42 at 1.6. WAIC (Table 2): extreme dispersion — CMP models vastly beat Poisson (e.g., ν=0.3: Poisson 2836.5 vs CMP-SAS 2640.5, rep 1); mild (ν=0.6/2): CMP-SAS best all replicates; near-equidispersion: Poisson best but CMP-SAS very close (2130.5 vs 2131.3 at ν=0.9), CMP-Full decays more — SAS robustness confirmed. EPL 2023/24 (threshold 0.5): 3 over-dispersed (Fulham P(Z)=0.94, ν̂=0.323; Brighton 0.58, ν̂=0.729; Wolves 0.51, ν̂=0.766) + 1 under-dispersed (Nott'm Forest 0.53, ν̂=1.395); threshold 0.4 adds Liverpool, Bournemouth, Chelsea. Fulham's attack parameter shifts from −0.090 (Poisson) to −1.831 (CMP-SAS) — extreme overdispersion (boom/bust scoring) masked by Poisson. In-sample WAIC 2023/24 (Table 4): CMP-SAS 2370.0 (lppd −1141.7, pWAIC 43.3) < CMP-Full 2386.1 < Poisson 2400.3. Out-of-sample 2023/24 IGN (Table 5): Outcome 1.326 vs 1.327; O/U 2.5 0.929 vs 0.974 (largest gain); GD 2.937 vs 2.940; CMP-Full worse than Poisson on Outcome/GD. Man Utd–Fulham 1–2: CMP-SAS exact-score P = 4.68% vs Poisson 4.22%; away-win prob 0.270 vs 0.196 (+8pp). Five seasons (Tables 6–7): CMP-SAS WAIC best every season (e.g., 2024/25: 2293.1 vs Poisson 2312.8); CMP-SAS IGN ≤ Poisson in every season × market (e.g., 2024/25 Outcome 1.397 vs 1.415; O/U 1.004 vs 1.025); ~3 dispersed teams/season, mostly overdispersed; Liverpool dispersed 3/5 seasons (direction changes); Nott'm Forest underdispersed 2 consecutive seasons.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
(a) Soccer goals, not NFL points — counts are low (mean ~1.4/team/match); NFL team scores (mean ~22) are a different count regime where CMP behavior must be re-validated. (b) Conditional independence assumption under-predicts draws (authors' own note) — the NFL analog (correlated team scores within a game) is stronger in football with fewer possessions; a Dixon-Coles-style dependence correction is flagged as future work. (c) No recency weighting — all matches weighted equally (authors flag this). (d) Detection power is limited near equidispersion: TP collapses for 0.7≤ν≤1.2 — mild dispersion is hard to identify with 38 games/team; NFL's 17-game season is even thinner. (e) Posterior baseline P(Z_i=1)≈0.25 for equidispersed teams means the 0.5 threshold is doing real work; threshold choice (0.4 vs 0.5) changes conclusions. (f) MCMC cost: 250k iterations × exchange auxiliary sampling — heavier than GSE's current stack; a variational or Laplace approximation would be needed for weekly production. (g) Seasons analyzed independently — no temporal persistence modeling of dispersion (though Liverpool/Forest show multi-season hints).

## 10. GSE overlap
Strong, direct, and missing from the corpus. No Bayesian count-data scoring model has been read in depth: the 15-area ML brief mentions score prediction as an area but no CMP/dispersion work exists in the repo or Drive dossiers. Garrett's engine predicts spreads/totals/moneylines (v5.2.7) — its totals model is the immediate consumer: team-level dispersion in points scored is exactly the quantity that separates a good totals model from a market-average one. The corner-kicks paper (paper 3, this wave) handles count dispersion (Poisson vs geometric-Poisson) but for soccer corners, not team scoring. Duplicate check: none — no CMP or spike-and-slab scoring model in the corpus.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
(1) Port the CMP-SAS model to NFL team points: y^H/ y^A = home/away points (scaled counts; or model touchdowns+field goals as counts). Team-specific dispersion ν_i on points scored; Maher structure log(μ^H_{i,j}) = α_i + β_j + γ. Start with the released repo code (verify live) and re-fit on 2020–2024 NFL seasons independently. (2) Add the two Dixon-Coles enhancements the authors flag: score-dependence correction and recency weighting (exponential decay on game weights). (3) Compare against GSE v5.2.7's totals predictions: out-of-sample IGN on O/U lines and ATS, rolling from mid-season exactly as in §3.5.2. (4) For production, replace full MCMC with a faster approximation (Laplace/VI) once the model is validated. Effort: ~2 engineer-weeks to port and validate; MCMC runtime is the bottleneck.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: 2022–2024 NFL regular seasons (nflverse play-by-play aggregated to scores). Metric: out-of-sample Ignorance score on (a) Over-Under vs closing line, (b) exact total-points buckets, (c) ATS — CMP-SAS vs Poisson-Maher vs GSE v5.2.7 baseline, rolling weekly from mid-season. Window: full seasons 2022–2024.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT into the GSE totals pipeline if CMP-SAS beats the Poisson-Maher baseline on out-of-sample IGN for totals in ≥2 of 3 seasons AND identifies ≥3 teams/season with P(Z_i=1)>0.5 (dispersion heterogeneity exists in NFL scoring); REJECT if no team crosses the 0.5 threshold in any season (NFL scoring is effectively equidispersed at team level) or if IGN gains vanish — the complexity is not justified.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Go beyond the paper in two directions the authors explicitly flag: (1) add a Dixon-Coles-style dependence parameter between the two teams' scores (NFL scores are more correlated than soccer — pace and game script couple them) and test whether the dependence term interacts with dispersion (e.g., overdispersed teams create higher-variance game scripts); (2) model dispersion as *persistent across seasons* with a hierarchical prior (ν_i,t ~ LogNormal centered on ν_i,t−1) instead of the paper's independent-season fits — the paper's own Liverpool/Forest multi-season patterns suggest dispersion is a team trait, and a hierarchical model would borrow strength across the NFL's short 17-game seasons, directly addressing the paper's low-power-near-equidispersion limitation.
