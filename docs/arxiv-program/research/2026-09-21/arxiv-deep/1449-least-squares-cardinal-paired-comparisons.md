# [1449] Least squares for cardinal paired comparisons data (arXiv:2401.07018)

**Citation:** Rahul Singh, George Iliopoulos, Ori Davidov (2025). *Least squares for cardinal paired comparisons data*. arXiv:2401.07018v3 [stat.ME]. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07018
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF, 69 pages incl. supplement with proofs of Theorems 2.1, 3.1, 3.2 and Propositions 2.1–2.2, Corollary 2.1, converted via pdftotext).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — (a) adopt the graph-Laplacian precision machinery for GSE's least-squares (Massey-type) ratings: Var(μ̂) = σ²N⁺ gives correct standard errors for every team-strength estimate and every pairwise difference, accounting for schedule-induced dependence — GSE currently has no such SEs on its LS ratings; (b) use Theorem 4.3's near-orthogonality conditions as a formal gate for when GSE may omit covariates (e.g., home field) from rating models: omitting is safe iff XᵀM/n = o(1), i.e., the schedule is balanced — the NFL's unbalanced schedule fails this, so GSE must keep the home covariate, and the paper's NBA application proves the point (Cayley distance 23 between with/without-covariate rankings); (c) deploy the Hotelling-type χ² tests (eqs. 13–14) to test "all teams equal" and "team A > team B" with graph-correct standard errors; (d) use the algebraic-connectivity diagnostic λ₂(N): rating precision is O(1/m) where m is the max-min comparisons along a spanning tree — a principled audit of whether 17-game NFL schedules identify all pairwise merit differences.

## 1. Research question
Least-squares estimation of merits from cardinal paired-comparison data (Y_ijk = score differential of game k between teams i and j) is widely used (Massey ratings) but its statistical properties were never rigorously established. Under what graph-based conditions is the LSE consistent, asymptotically normal, and rank-consistent? When can covariates (home field, past standings) be omitted? What happens when the number of items K → ∞ on sparse comparison graphs? How does the theory compare to Bradley–Terry models?

## 2. Dataset / schema
Theory paper; empirical content: (i) simulations — complete/path/cycle graphs with K=8, μ₀=(−7,−5,−3,−1,1,3,5,7)×10^{−γ}, γ∈{0,1,2}, m∈{10,…,1000} comparisons per edge, errors N(0,1), T3/√3 (variance 1), T2 (no finite variance); covariate simulations with β=(1,1)ᵀ and Rademacher covariates; large-graph simulations K∈{100,…,500} with Erdős–Rényi edge probabilities p∈{1, 0.5, K⁻¹(log K)³, K⁻¹√(log K)³}; (ii) NBA 2022–23: 1,320 regular-season games, 30 teams (basketball-reference.com), outcome = point spread, covariates = home indicator, expanded standings of 2020–21 and 2021–22. R scripts at https://github.com/rahulstats/GLM-I.

## 3. Method / model
Model (1): Y_ijk = μ_ij + ε_ijk, μ_ij = μ_i − μ_j (merits/scores), ε_ijk independent zero-mean. LSE (5): μ̂ = argmin{Q(μ) : vᵀμ=0}, Q(μ)=Σ_{i<j}Σ_k(Y_ijk−(μ_i−μ_j))² (eq. 4). Closed form (Thm 2.1, eq. 6): μ̂ = N⁺S − (vᵀN⁺S/vᵀ1)1, unique iff comparison graph G is connected; N = graph Laplacian (N_ii=Σ_j n_ij, N_ij=−n_ij), S_i=Σ_{j≠i}Σ_k Y_ijk. With covariates (16): Y_ijk = μ_i−μ_j + x_ijkᵀβ + ε_ijk; β̂ = (Xᵀ(I−MN⁺Mᵀ)X)⁻¹Xᵀ(I−MN⁺Mᵀ)Y (eq. 22), μ̂ = N⁺(S−MᵀXβ̂) (eq. 21), unique iff Cond 4.1 (rank(M)=K−1, rank((I−MM⁺)X)=p).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
- Var(μ̂) = σ²N⁺ (eq. 7); MSE = σ²trace(N⁺). Precision depends on the graph through the Laplacian pseudoinverse — scaled-graph study (Fig. 2): path graph most variable, complete graph least; star/wheel/knockout near-complete; central vertices estimated more precisely.
- Consistency (Thm 3.1): Var(μ̂_n)→0 iff Condition 3.1 — min comparisons along some spanning tree →∞; Var(μ̂_{i,n}) = O(1/m), m = max_T min_{(i,j)∈T} n_ij (eq. 9); equivalently algebraic connectivity λ₂(N)→∞; λ₂ = O(m). Counter-example: path 1−2−3−4 with n_12=n_34=k→∞ but n_23=1 ⇒ λ₂≤1, LSE inconsistent despite n_i→∞ for all i.
- Strong consistency (Thm 3.2): μ̂_n→μ a.s. iff Cond 3.1, needing only zero-mean errors (no variance).
- Asymptotic normality: under stronger Cond 3.2 (min tree n_ij/n→c>0): √n(μ̂_n−μ)⇒N(0,σ²Θ⁺), Θ=lim N/n (Thm 3.3, Lemma 3.1); under 3.1 alone: √m(μ̂_n−μ)⇒N(0,σ²Ψ) with smaller rate and lower-rank limit (Thm 3.4, Ex. 3.1).
- Ranks (Thm 3.5): P(r̂(μ̂_n)≠r(μ))→0; with subgaussian errors + Cond 3.2: ≤C₁exp(−nC₂) — exponential rate.
- Covariates: consistency needs Cond 4.2 (principal angle φ_n between im(M) and im(X) bounded away from 0, smallest eigenvalue of XᵀX diverges); normality needs Cond 4.3 (lim XᵀX/n positive definite) + Cond 4.4 (Hájek–Sidak negligibility). Prop 4.1: a covariate with x_ijk = z_i−z_j lies in im(M) and is unidentifiable.
- **Covariate omission (Thm 4.3):** if the true model has covariates but you fit without them, the AKL divergence is minimized at μ_m = μ_t+o(1) when XᵀM/n=o(1) (near-orthogonality); the asymptotic bias b=lim N⁺MᵀXβ vanishes entirely if MᵀX/√n=o(1); with IID centered random covariates, estimation of μ and β decouples (block-diagonal asymptotic variance). Balanced round-robin + home indicator ⇒ M⊥X ⇒ home effect can be ignored safely.
- Infinite items: complete graph, n_ij=1: √K(μ̂_{i1..ir}−μ)⇒N(0,σ²I), E[max|μ̂_i−μ_i|]=O(√(log K/K)) (Thm 5.1). Erdős–Rényi: need only p_K≥(1+c)log K/K (connectivity threshold) for consistency+normality; uniform consistency needs p_K=(log K)^{1+c}/K (Thm 5.2, eq. 29).
- vs Bradley–Terry (Sec. 8): cardinal LSE needs only connected G; BT additionally needs the strong-connection condition (every partition has a cross-win) for MLE existence; Conds 3.1/3.2 are necessary and sufficient for BT too (new); covariate conditions 4.1–4.4 are more natural (design-matrix only) than Fan et al. 2022's parameter-space orthogonality; for K→∞, cardinal needs only connectivity while BT needs parameter-space restrictions (M_K=o(√(logK/K)) etc.) — cardinal LSE works on much sparser graphs.
- Assumptions: errors IID zero-mean (Cond 2.1); linear transitive ordering μ_i−μ_j (non-transitive/cyclical models deferred to a separate paper); fixed K for Sections 2–4; n_ij≤1 for Section 5.

## 5. Features / target
Inputs: pairwise score differentials Y_ijk on the comparison graph (NFL: game score margins; NBA application: point spreads), optional covariates x_ijk (home indicator ±1, prior-season standings differences). Targets: merit vector μ (team strengths), derived ranks r(μ), regression coefficients β, hypothesis-test decisions.

## 6. Validation design
Simulations across graph topologies (complete/cycle/path/star/wheel/tournament), error distributions (Gaussian, t3, t2), merit spacings (γ=0,1,2), and scales (m=10..1000; K=100..500); 1,000 runs for MSE, probability of correct ranking, max estimation error. NBA 2022–23: four nested models (I: no covariates; II: +home; III: +home+2021–22 standing; IV: +home+both past standings); Cayley distances between derived rankings; nonparametric bootstrap (200 samples) for rank variability boxplots.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Quoted exactly. **Simulations:** MSE decays with m for N(0,1) and T3/√3 errors (Gaussian always smaller, gap largest at small m); T2 errors (no variance) decay slower with spiky empirical MSE yet remain consistent per Thm 3.2. Correct-ranking probability →1 as m grows; small merit spacing (γ=2) is hard. **Covariates:** at m=1000, γ=2, P(correct ranking) ≈0.4 with covariates vs ≈0.1 without — omission is consistent (Thm 4.3) but materially less efficient. **Large sparse graphs:** max error decreases in K and in p; p=O(K⁻¹(log K)²) (just above the threshold) converges very slowly. **NBA 2022–23:** Models I and II give identical rankings; III and IV similar to each other but Cayley distance 23 from I/II (Table 2: I–II 0, III–IV 8, cross 23); full model highly significant ⇒ M and X not orthogonal here ⇒ omitting covariates gives an improper ranking (Thm 4.3 conditions fail). Bootstrap: middle-ranked teams most variable; Boston IQR (1,2) in Model I vs (2,7) in Model IV; Oklahoma City (11,19) in I vs (3,6) in IV; Model IV slightly less variable overall.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- Pure theory: no predictive-accuracy comparison against ELO/BT/ML on held-out games — the NBA section is illustrative (rankings, not forecasts); no Brier/RPS/log-loss numbers.
- Heavy-tailed errors: T2 → consistent but slow; Cauchy (no mean) → inconsistent — LS on raw margins needs robustness checks; the authors flag Huber/LAD extensions as future work.
- Linear transitivity assumed (μ_i−μ_j); cyclical/non-transitive structure (the subject of ledger [1452]'s companion area) is explicitly out of scope here.
- Cond 3.2 (needed for √n normality) is strong: requires all tree-edge counts to grow at the same rate — NFL schedules with fixed 17 games and growing seasons approximate this across seasons but not within one season.
- The covariate-omission safety result needs near-orthogonality; real sports schedules (unbalanced, persistent home/away asymmetries vs specific opponents) routinely violate it — which is exactly when the NBA example shows rankings change (distance 23).
- Infinite-item results assume n_ij≤1 and K→∞; NFL has fixed K=32, so Section 5 applies by analogy (sparse cross-conference comparisons) rather than directly.

## 10. GSE overlap
Existing-research map: Massey, Colley, ELO variants, Bradley–Terry, Harville (2003) covariate model inventoried; Dixon-Coles, Keener/Perron methods mentioned. What's NEW: (a) the first rigorous consistency/normality/rank-convergence theory for LS ratings — GSE uses Massey-type ratings without any of these guarantees or SEs; (b) Var(μ̂)=σ²N⁺ as an implementable precision engine: correct SEs for team strengths and pairwise differences on any schedule graph; (c) the covariate-omission gate (Thm 4.3) — a formal test for when GSE's home-field (or rest, altitude) covariate can be dropped, replacing heuristics; (d) the χ² test battery (all-equal, pairwise, all-different, is-team-1-best) with graph-correct variances — GSE has no principled "are these two teams really different" test; (e) the λ₂(N) connectivity diagnostic: quantifies whether the NFL schedule identifies every pairwise merit difference and at what rate; (f) optimal experimental design via Θ⁺ (A/D-optimality) as a future content lane — "which games most reduce rating uncertainty."

## 11. GSE implementation spec
1. Build the LS rating engine with precision: per week, form N (Laplacian of games played) and S (score-differential sums); μ̂=N⁺S (constraint Σμ=0); report σ̂²=Q(μ̂)/n and Var(μ̂)=σ̂²N⁺; publish team strengths WITH standard errors (sqrt of diag) — a GSE differentiator.
2. Pairwise difference tests: for any team pair, z = (μ̂_i−μ̂_j)/√(σ̂²(θ⁺_ii+θ⁺_jj−2θ⁺_ij)) with θ⁺ from (N/n)⁺ — use in matchup content ("model says KC is better than BUF at 95% confidence, accounting for schedule connectivity").
3. Covariate gate: fit home covariate β̂ (eq. 22); compute XᵀM/n — if not o(1) (NFL: it won't be), keep the covariate; document the decision with the NBA distance-23 precedent. Add rest-day and altitude covariates under the same gate.
4. λ₂(N) weekly diagnostic: track algebraic connectivity of the season-to-date game graph; report the effective rate m (eq. 9) — early-season ratings get wide SEs honestly instead of point estimates.
5/5. Rank-tiers with exponential confidence: use Thm 3.5's exponential bound form to attach "probability the top-4 order is correct" to GSE power-tier content.
6. Effort: 2–3 days (engine + SEs + tests); 1 day (covariate gate); 1–2 days (λ₂ diagnostic dashboard).

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: nflverse 2014–2025 (scores, home/away). Test 1: LS-Massey engine with N⁺ SEs vs GSE's current rating on spread-prediction MAE and win-probability Brier, 3 season-group walk-forward; adopt if Brier improves ≥0.002 or MAE improves ≥0.1 points. Test 2: coverage test — 95% CIs from σ̂²N⁺ on held-out pairwise differences should cover at ~95% (validates the SE machinery; cf. [1444]'s calibration audit). Test 3: covariate gate — fit home+rest covariates; if XᵀM/n is not small, confirm the with/without-covariate ranking distance is material (else the gate is vacuous). Test 4: early-season λ₂ diagnostic — verify SE widths shrink monotonically as connectivity grows and that week-4 CIs are honestly wide.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADAPT the N⁺ precision engine if the coverage test (Test 2) passes at 93–97% — then ship SEs on all GSE LS ratings and the pairwise z-tests for content. REJECT the "omit home covariate" simplification permanently for NFL (unbalanced schedule ⇒ Thm 4.3's near-orthogonality fails; NBA precedent: ranking distance 23). REJECT using LS point estimates without SEs in early season (λ₂ small ⇒ Var large; point estimates then are the path-graph fallacy of Ex. 2.1). REJECT the exponential rank-confidence claim for weekly tiers unless the subgaussian-error check on NFL margins passes — else report bootstrap rank intervals (the paper's own Fig. 6 approach) instead.

## 14. Improvement experiment
The paper's open lane most valuable to GSE is optimal design (future direction #2): use Θ⁺ to compute A-optimality (minimize trace of Var) over the remaining schedule — i.e., which upcoming games most reduce total rating uncertainty. GSE should build a weekly "highest-leverage games for rating precision" feature: for each unplayed game, compute the reduction in trace(σ²N⁺) from adding that edge, rank games, and publish as content ("these 3 games will resolve the AFC seeding picture most"). A second experiment: robustify the LSE with Huber weights and re-run the T2-style simulation on NFL margins with garbage-time/blowout contamination — if Huber-LS beats OLS on spread MAE by ≥0.15 points, adopt it as the default engine (the paper proves OLS is consistent but slow under heavy tails; GSE cares about the constant, not just the rate).
