# [0565] Analyzing and Forecasting Success in the Men's Ice Hockey World (Junior) Championships Using a Dynamic Ranking Model (arXiv:2409.05714)

**Citation:** Holý, V. (2025). *Analyzing and Forecasting Success in the Men's Ice Hockey World (Junior) Championships Using a Dynamic Ranking Model*. arXiv:2409.05714. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05714
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 4063 lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — the score-driven dynamic Plackett–Luce model with fixed-effects + mean-reverting dynamic component + regression covariates is directly portable to NFL final-standings/seeding forecasting; the finding that penalized MLE (λ=0.01) beats unpenalized on rolling one-step-ahead log-likelihood while the static model wins unpenalized is a concrete fitting discipline for small-sample tournament forecasting. Ice-hockey specifics don't transfer, but the model structure does.

## 1. Research question
Which factors explain national-team success in the Men's Ice Hockey World Championships (WC) and World Junior Championships (WJC) — home advantage, past tournament results, physical attributes, roster experience — and can a score-driven dynamic Plackett–Luce ranking model with those covariates forecast tournament outcomes (winner, medalists, playoff qualifiers)?

## 2. Dataset / schema
WC: 45 editions 1976–2024 (missing 1980, 1984, 1988, 2020), 24 teams retained (Netherlands, Romania, South Korea dropped for single last-place appearances), 8/12/16 teams per edition. WJC: 48 editions 1977–2024, 15 teams retained, 8/10 teams per edition. Data source: IIHF (iihf.com). Per team-edition: final rank, host dummy, reciprocal ranks of last U18/WJC/WC, roster-average height/weight/age, roster experience (games) in IIHF/NHL/other leagues. Table 2 lists per-team host counts, participation counts, mean reciprocal rank, best/worst ranks (e.g., WC: Russia MRR 0.504, Canada 0.453, Czechia 0.438; WJC: Canada 0.626, Russia 0.557).

## 3. Method / model
Score-driven dynamic ranking model extending Holý & Zouhar (2022): PL distribution with time-varying strengths split into fixed effects (long-term intrinsic level), mean-reverting AR(1) dynamic component (medium-term deviations), and regression covariates (short-term, interpreted w.r.t. current strength only — unlike Holý & Zouhar's ARMAX-style intertwining). Key differences from Holý & Zouhar: (1) strengths computed only for participating teams P_t (no covariates needed for absent teams; handles Russia/Belarus 2022+ suspensions); for non-participants the dynamic component u_{i,t} is still tracked, decaying by φ toward its long-term value 0; (2) canceled tournaments (missing years) move strengths toward long-term values rather than being ignored. ML with bounds φ∈[0,1), α≥0; penalized ML L_Pen for forecasting.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
- PL probability (Eq. 1): P[y|f]=Π_{r=1}^N exp f_{r^th}/Σ_{s=r}^n exp f_{s^th}; three-team worked example (Eq. 2): P[y|f]=[exp f_1st/(exp f_1st+exp f_2nd+exp f_3rd)]·[exp f_2nd/(exp f_2nd+exp f_3rd)]·[exp f_3rd/exp f_3rd]; log-likelihood ℓ(f|y) = Σ_i f_i − Σ_{r=1}^N ln(Σ_{s=r}^N exp f_{s^th}) — Eq. (3); score ∇_i(f|y) = 1 − Σ_{r=1}^{y(i)} exp(f_i)/Σ_{s=r}^N exp(f_{s^th}) — Eq. (5), E[∇]=0, Var(∇)=Fisher information.
- Strength dynamics (Eq. 7): f_{i,t} = ω_i + Σ_j β_j x_{i,t,j} + u_{i,t}, u_{i,t} = φu_{i,t−1} + 1_{i∈P_{t−1}} α∇_i(f_{t−1}|y_{t−1}); for non-participants the strength is not computed but u_{i,t} is tracked and decays toward 0 when φ∈(−1,1); u_{i,0}=0. Identification Σ_i ω_i = 0, i.e. ω_N = −Σ_{i=1}^{N−1} ω_i (Eq. 9) — without covariates this also gives Σ_i f_{i,t} = 0.
- Log-likelihood (Eq. 8): L(θ) = Σ_t ℓ(f_t|y_t), θ = (ω_1,…,ω_N,β_1,…,β_M,φ,α)ᵀ; standard errors ŝe[θ̂] = √diag[−T·H(θ̂)^{−1}] (Eq. 10).
- Penalized log-likelihood (Eq. 11): L_Pen(θ) = Σ_t ℓ(f_t|y_t) − λΣ_tΣ_{i∈P_t} f_{i,t}².
- Hunter (2004) partition condition required for a finite MLE maximum; filter invertibility needs φ∈(−1,1) for consistency/asymptotic normality (full conditions beyond paper scope; refs Blasques et al. 2014/2018/2022).
- Assumptions: PL invariance to constant shifts in f; scores drive the dynamics via the exact log-likelihood gradient; penalization needed for the Hunter (2004) partition condition in rolling estimation; physical/experience covariates are team-season aggregates, linear in log-strength; ties not modeled (WC/WJC standings resolved by tiebreakers); IIA (Luce's choice axiom) assumed.
- Medal context (App. Table 7): WC golds — Canada 28, Russia 27, Czechia 13, Sweden 11, Finland 4, US 2; WJC golds — Canada 20, Russia 13, US 6, Finland 5. Fixed-effect examples (final models, App. Tables 10–11): WC — Canada 2.268***, Russia 2.109***, Sweden 2.622***, Finland 1.852***, US 1.279***, Japan −2.469***; WJC — Canada 2.797***, Russia 2.720***, Czechia 1.791***, Sweden 1.967***, Finland 1.530*** (all SE ≈ 0.26–0.5).
- EDA details (§4.1, Figs. 2–3): WC lag-1 rank autocorrelation 0.715 declining gradually; WJC 0.566; WC–U18 0.523, WJC–U18 0.542, WJC–lagged-WC positive but <0.5 (hockey-development-system signal captured by intercept + dynamic component).

## 5. Features / target
Target: complete final standings ranking of each edition (PL likelihood). Features: host dummy; reciprocal rank of last WC/WJC/U18; roster-average height, weight, age; roster-average experience (games) in IIHF, NHL, other leagues.

## 6. Validation design
In-sample: ML fit of 7 specifications (static, dynamic, tournament, physical, experience, full, final=AIC-minimal), AIC comparison, significance at 0.1/0.05/0.01. Out-of-sample: rolling one-step-ahead forecasts over the last 16 editions, penalized ML over a λ grid, metrics = average predicted log-likelihood (primary), P(champion), P(medals), P(playoffs), MAE/RMSE vs modal ranking.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
- Autocorrelations: WC ranks 0.715 at lag 1 (stable), WJC 0.566 (fluctuating); WC–WJC concurrent correlation 0.585; WC–U18 0.523.
- WC (Table 3, N=45, log-lik/AIC): static −765.832/1577.664 → dynamic −759.578/1569.155; final model (AIC 1531.978): Last WJC 0.853***, Last WC 0.674**, Avg. Age −0.191*** (younger better), IIHF Exp. 0.053***, NHL Exp. 0.002* — IIHF coefficient ~25× the NHL coefficient despite 11× fewer games/season, so one WC tournament ≫ one NHL season; hosting insignificant (no home advantage); autoreg. φ = 0.736 (medium persistence).
- WJC (Table 4, N=48): hosting significant in every model (0.488**–0.673***); past-tournament results insignificant; Avg. Height −0.142* (shorter better), Avg. Weight 0.123* (heavier better); IIHF Exp. 0.897***; autoreg. φ = 0.796–0.850; final-model AIC 888.157 vs static 913.877.
- Forecasting (Tables 5–6, λ=0.01 optimal for both): WC — final model avg. log-lik −22.783 vs static −23.321; P(champion) 0.166, P(medals) 0.045, P(playoffs) 0.027, MAE 1.999, RMSE 2.627. WJC — log-lik −10.794 vs −11.004; P(champion) 0.212, P(medals) 0.071, P(playoffs) 0.303, MAE 1.353, RMSE 1.805. Without penalization (λ=0) the static model beats the final model — penalization is required for the covariate model to win.
- Unbounded optimization warning (§4.4): without bounds φ∈[0,1), α≥0, the MLE drifts to a persistent model (φ→1) with negative α — log-likelihood looks concave in the positive-α region but develops narrow irregular peaks in the negative-α region, one of which becomes the global maximum; standard errors collapse to ≈0 (unreliable), optimization needs multiple starting points; the same pathology appears with and without penalization and in Holý & Zouhar (2022)'s WC sample (unreported; their Nelder–Mead start values landed on the well-behaved mean-reverting optimum). Recommendation: always bound φ∈[0,1), α≥0.
- Conclusion key findings: WJC success is a better predictor of future WC performance than the most recent WC itself; home advantage significant only in WJC (younger players more environment-sensitive; rink size/shape Europe vs North America); IIHF experience ≫ NHL experience (with the caveat noted); younger teams do better in WC given sufficient experience.
- Fixed-effect hierarchy (§3/Appendix): the model is panel regression with dynamic errors; fixed effects capture intrinsic skill, institutional support, culture, fan base.

## 8. Code / data availability
Data: IIHF (iihf.com) tournament results and hosting; Elite Prospects (eliteprospects.com) physical characteristics. Code: https://github.com/vladimirholy/ice-hockey-wc (R; simplified functions adapted from the `gasmodel` package of Holý 2024a, which handles joint/separate regression+dynamic modeling but not varying participant sets or canceled tournaments). Optimization via the Sbplx algorithm (nloptr package, Subplex re-implementation).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Adversarial notes: (a) one-step-ahead only — multi-year forecasts impossible since covariates (rosters, experience) are unknown in advance; (b) physical/experience variables only available after final rosters are announced, shrinking the practical forecasting window to days; (c) modal-rank MAE/RMSE is admittedly unreliable (WJC's lowest MAE at λ=1 while lowest log-lik at λ=0.01); (d) the NHL-experience finding is confounded: WC occurs during the NHL playoffs, so WC rosters systematically miss the best NHL players — the IIHF≫NHL effect may be selection, not causation; (e) N is tiny (24 WC / 15 WJC teams, 45/48 editions) — 10-covariate models with per-team fixed effects are heavily parameterized; penalization is doing the heavy lifting (unpenalized, static wins); (f) PL's independence-of-irrelevant-alternatives (Luce choice axiom): a subset's internal ranking is unaffected by other teams' presence — violated if teams have systematic matchup-specific edges beyond fixed effects; (g) unbounded (φ,α) estimation reliably finds a spurious overfitted persistent model with negative α — a trap any re-implementation must avoid via the φ∈[0,1), α≥0 bounds.

## 10. GSE overlap
Extension. The map inventory covers Plackett–Luce and state-space team-strength models, but not a score-driven PL with fixed-effects + AR(1) dynamic + covariates for tournament standings, and not IIHF hockey. The paper's structure (static vs dynamic vs penalized-forecasting comparison) is a new capability for GSE's standings/seeding models.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
1. Port the model to NFL: replace editions with seasons (2002–2025), teams with the 32 franchises, final rank with end-of-season order within conference or division (8-team divisions or 16-team conference hyperedges — choose conference: K=16 richer); covariates = pre-season analogues available before week 1: prior-season reciprocal rank, roster-average age/experience, market-implied pre-season odds as a fixed-effect prior, rest/net-travel dummies as the "hosting" analogue. 2. Fit static / dynamic / full / AIC-final exactly as the paper does, with bounds φ∈[0,1), α≥0 and the Σω=0 identification. 3. Forecast next season's division winners / playoff field from pre-season covariates with rolling one-step-ahead evaluation over 2015–2025, primary metric average log-likelihood, penalization grid λ∈{0, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1}. Effort: ~2 weeks (season-level data assembly is the cost; fitting is a small likelihood optimizer).

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: NFL seasons 2002–2025: final conference standings (Pro-Football-Reference), pre-season Super Bowl odds (market archives), roster-average age/AV (PFR/OTC). Protocol: rolling one-season-ahead forecasts for 2015–2025; compare static vs dynamic vs AIC-final (penalized, λ tuned on log-lik). Metrics: avg. log-likelihood of the observed standings order, P(division winner correct) per team-season, MAE vs modal rank.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
Adopt the score-driven dynamic PL for GSE's pre-season standings model if, on 2015–2025 rolling forecasts, the AIC-final penalized model beats the static model on average log-likelihood by ≥0.05/season AND achieves P(division winner) ≥ market-implied baseline within 0.03 — then the dynamic component earns its keep. Reject if the static model wins unpenalized and penalized dynamic only wins on MAE: that's the paper's own unreliable metric, and the NFL's 32-team single-season structure may not support the dynamic component.

## 14. Improvement experiment
The paper's own limitation — MAE/RMSE vs modal ranking being unreliable — can be fixed for GSE: replace modal-rank evaluation with expected-rank metrics computed by simulating from the fitted PL (10k draws per season), yielding E[rank_i] and P(rank_i ≤ k) per team. Compare E-rank MAE against modal-rank MAE over the 2015–2025 backtest: if expected-rank metrics select different λ than log-likelihood, report the conflict and default to log-likelihood (a proper scoring rule); if they agree, GSE gets a defensible rank-accuracy metric the paper lacked.
