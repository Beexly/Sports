# [0290] Machine learning for sports betting: should model selection be based on accuracy or calibration? (arXiv:2303.06021v4)

**Citation:** Walsh, C., & Joshi, A. (2024). *Machine learning for sports betting: should model selection be based on accuracy or calibration?* Machine Learning with Applications. arXiv:2303.06021v4. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06021v4
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 1696 lines).
**Verdict:** ADOPT — direct mandate for GSE: run model selection on classwise-ECE (with an 80%-nonempty-bins guard), not accuracy, for any moneyline/spread probability model; calibration-driven selection beat accuracy-driven by +34.69% vs −35.17% average ROI and saved Kelly from ruin.

## 1. Research question
In sports outcome prediction, models are almost universally selected on classification accuracy. The authors argue that for the *sports betting* problem — where profit depends on estimating the true probability of an outcome to find mispriced odds and to size Kelly bets — calibration (how close predicted probabilities are to true frequencies) is the more appropriate selection criterion. They test this by building two identical betting pipelines on NBA data that differ only in the selection metric (accuracy vs classwise expected calibration error) and comparing season ROI.

## 2. Dataset / schema
NBA data 2014/15–2018/19 from basketball-reference.com (box-score statistics, basic + advanced, per game; team-level features). Closing moneyline odds for the 2018/19 NBA season from Las Vegas sportsbook Westgate (via sportsbookreviewsonline.com). Schema: per-game records with home/away team-total box-score stats (averaged as season-to-date *differences vs previous opponents*, then differenced home-minus-away), plus "Previous Season Winning Percentage" (home-minus-away difference). First 10 games of each team-season excluded from training instances (used only to compute later features). Proprietary-ish only in that odds were scraped from a public archive; reproducible from basketball-reference + odds archives.

## 3. Method / model
Two-branch predictive-modelling pipeline (Fig. 1): candidate algorithms = logistic regression, random forest, SVM, MLP. Common filter: drop one of each Spearman-|ρ|>0.7 correlated pair (keep the feature most correlated with target). Branch A (accuracy): sequential forward selection (SFS, LR learner) maximising accuracy → subset C; BO-TPE hyperparameter optimisation minimising negative accuracy; pick the model with best test accuracy. Branch B (calibration): SFS minimising classwise-ECE (20 bins) with an 80%-nonempty-bins guard (if violated, ECE set to 1) → subset B; BO-TPE minimising classwise-ECE; pick the model with lowest test classwise-ECE. Betting: strategy = bet every value bet (model P > bookmaker implied 1/O); rules = fixed $100 stakes or eighth-Kelly sizing; starting bankroll $10,000; games processed chronologically. Feature engineering novelty: features are season-to-date averages of *differences* in box-score stats vs opponents (relative "out-performance"), claimed more robust to league drift than absolute averages (citing Dutta et al. 2017); covariate shift handled by two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests at 1% on validation vs training distributions — failing features dropped.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Eq. 1 (Kelly): k = (pb − q)/b, where p = model probability, q = 1 − p, b = odds − 1. Eq. 2 (perfect calibration): P(Ŷ = Y | P̂ = p) = p ∀ p ∈ [0,1]. Eq. 3 (classwise-ECE): (1/k)Σ_i Σ_j (|B_{j,i}|/n) |y_i(B_{j,i}) − p̄_i(B_{j,i})|, with k classes, m bins of equal width over [0,1], bin-size-weighted. Eq. 4 (LR): P(Y_i = C|X_i) = exp(X_iβ)/(1 + exp(X_iβ)). Eq. 5: negative log-likelihood −Σ[y_i log f(X_iβ) + (1−y_i) log(1−f(X_iβ))]. Eq. 6 (SVM): argmin_w {(1/n)Σ max(0, 1 − y_i f(x_i)) + C w^T w}. Kelly simulation (Table 1): K = (P × Bankroll − (1−P))/Bankroll (i.e., Kelly fraction on stake scale), stake = (1/8)×K×Bankroll (eighth-Kelly) or $100 fixed. Assumptions: bookmaker implied probability treated as proxy for true probability (citing Wheatcroft 2020) in the scatter analysis; covariate shift tested at 1% significance; ≥80% of ECE bins must be non-empty else ECE := 1 (prevents "predict the base rate" degenerate calibration).

## 5. Features / target
Subset B (calibration branch): 3P, BLK, FT%, ORB, AST%, Previous Season Winning Percentage. Subset C (accuracy branch): STL%, eFG%, DRB, AST, DRtg, ORtg, 3P, Previous Season Winning Percentage. (Features are home-minus-away differences of season-to-date average out-performance vs opponents; box-score stat set listed in a supplementary document.) Target: binary home-team victory in a given NBA game (no draws possible). Horizon: pre-game, single game.

## 6. Validation design
Chronological splits (cross-validation explicitly rejected as invalid for sports): initial training 2014/15–2015/16 (feature selection, HPO; 2016/17 as validation); extended training 2014/15–2016/17 → model selection on test set 2017/18; final fit 2014/15–2017/18; betting simulation on the single 2018/19 season with published Westgate closing moneylines. Baselines: accuracy-branch vs calibration-branch systems under identical strategy/rules; rules compared (fixed $100 vs eighth-Kelly). Metrics: accuracy, classwise-ECE (20 bins), % games bet on, % bets won, final bankroll, ROI. No statistical test of ROI differences (e.g., no bootstrap); significance argument is qualitative.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Test-set (2017/18) scores — calibration branch (classwise-ECE): SVM 3.23%, MLP 3.59%, LR 3.61%, RF 4.39% (SVM selected). Accuracy branch: SVM 66.55%, LR 65.69%, MLP 65.69%, RF 65.34% (SVM selected). Betting simulation on 2018/19 ($10,000 start): fixed betting — calibration SVM $13,244.51 (ROI 32.45%), accuracy SVM $10,556.29 (ROI 5.56%). Eighth-Kelly — calibration SVM $13,692.86 (ROI 36.93%), accuracy SVM $2,409.66 (ROI −75.9%). Summary (Table 6): calibration-driven SVM — 87.55% of games bet, 38.8% bets won, accuracy 64.27%, classwise-ECE 4.46%, max ROI 36.93%, average ROI 34.69%; accuracy-driven SVM — 89.89% of games bet, 38.46% won, accuracy 64.62%, classwise-ECE 5.03%, max ROI 5.56%, average ROI −35.17%. Supporting observation: accuracy-driven model identified more false-positive value bets; its value-bet predictions formed two clusters at probability extremes (variance 0.036 vs 0.034 for the calibrated model). Note the paper's abstract reports average ROIs "+34.69% versus −35.17%" and best case "+36.93% versus +5.56%".

## 9. Leakage & limitations
(a) Single betting season (2018/19) — no guarantee of repeatability; no cross-season replication, no significance test on ROI. (b) Both final models were SVMs — the calibration/accuracy contrast may partly reflect HPO/feature-subset differences (subsets B and C differ in 6/8 features) rather than the selection metric alone; the "selection paradigm" bundles feature selection + HPO + model choice, so attribution is confounded. (c) The 80%-nonempty-bins ECE guard is acknowledged by the authors as "somewhat arbitrary." (d) NBA moneyline only; transfer to NFL spreads/totals untested. (e) Eighth-Kelly on the *accuracy* model lost 75.9% — the ROI gap is driven heavily by the Kelly interaction with miscalibration, which is the authors' point but means the fixed-stakes contrast (32.45% vs 5.56%) is the cleaner number. (f) No transaction-cost/slippage/limits modelling; closing odds assumed obtainable (Westgate published odds ≠ always bettable at scale).

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT the selection-metric change if, on the 2024 test window, the ECE-selected model achieves ≥3 pp lower classwise-ECE than the accuracy-selected model AND its season ROI beats the accuracy-selected model's ROI under both fixed-stakes and 1/8-Kelly rules (any magnitude — the paper's gap was huge); REJECT if the accuracy-selected model wins ROI under either rule by more than 5 pp. Pre-registered before running.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Hybrid selection: replace single-objective ECE minimisation with two-stage SFS — first constrain to feature subsets whose 20-bin ECE is within 1 pp of the minimum, then pick the most accurate among them (or the Pareto frontier of accuracy vs ECE). Hypothesis: accuracy still carries signal once calibration is satisfised, and the paper's bundled design never tested whether its subsets B/C confound the comparison. A second follow-up: repeat the experiment on NFL spreads (three classes: cover/push/fail) to test whether the calibration advantage survives away from binary moneylines.
