**Verdict:** ADAPT

Rationale: interaction information is the cheapest principled pair-selection prior in the lane (O(|F|²) entropy computation vs model retraining per candidate), and the paper's Section IV-C is a methodological corrective the whole lane needs: it quantifies how CV-only reporting (+5.7pp inflation), OpenFE's transductive test-sample leakage (+14.01% on airfoil), and missing pre/post hyperparameter tuning distort published AutoFE comparisons. GSE adopts the II pair-prior *and* the hygiene rules.

## 1. Research question
The AutoFE search space explodes combinatorially with transformation order (OpenFE caps at order 2, AutoFeat at 3, EAAFE at 6, DIFER at 5). Can an information-theoretic quantity — interaction information, measuring how much two features *synergize* in predicting the target beyond their individual contributions — pre-select which feature pairs are worth combining, so the search only explores high-synergy pairs? Secondary contribution: audit the experimental hygiene of the AutoFE literature (EAAFE, DIFER, OpenFE papers) and quantify how much reported gains are inflated by setup flaws.

## 2. Dataset / schema
Public datasets "randomly chosen from the EAAFE, DIFER, and OpenFE papers" (Table III): Airfoil (1,503×5, reg), Credit Default (30k×23, clf), Bikeshare (17,389×13, reg), Wine Quality Red (999×12, clf), California Housing (20,640×8, reg), OpenML 586 (1k×25, reg), JM1 (10,885×22, clf), Jungle Chess (44,819×6, clf) + a proprietary Allstate-scale regression dataset (100s of thousands of samples, ~1,000s of features). Downstream models: LR/Lasso, RF, LightGBM — hyperparameter-tuned (random search, 100 iterations, 5-fold CV) *before* AutoFE and *after* AutoFE. 25 runs per dataset/model (5 train/test seeds × 5 algorithm seeds). Time: 2024.

## 3. Method / model
**Interaction information (Algorithm 1):** τ_ij = I(F_i; F_j; Y) = I(F_i; F_j | Y) − I(F_i; F_j) = H(F_i,F_j) + H(F_j,Y) + H(F_i,Y) − H(F_i) − H(F_j) − H(Y) − H(F_i,F_j,Y). Symmetric in all three variables; interpretable as I(F_i; Y | F_j) − I(F_i; Y): the information F_i shares with Y *given* F_j minus without — i.e. synergy beyond isolation.
**IIFE (Algorithm 3):** (1) compute II for all pairs (O(|F|²); prefilter to F̃ = top-50 by RF impurity importance if |F| too large); (2) loop: take K highest-τ pairs, apply all bivariate functions B, evaluate each candidate by cross-validated downstream-model performance V_M *in parallel*, keep the argmax; (3) apply univariate functions U to the new feature, keep argmax; (4) add to pool; next iteration computes II only for (new feature × existing) — O(|F|). Stop: patience P — stop if mean of recent P/2 CV scores ≤ mean of previous P/2 (Algorithm 2). Order cap optional for interpretability (pop pairs that hit max order).
**Key property:** builds *increasingly complex* features iteratively (Jungle Chess: best feature reached order 10 — beyond any competitor's cap) while also stopping at low order when the model doesn't benefit (Airfoil/RF case).
**II as accelerator (§IV-D):** for expand-reduce methods (OpenFE, AutoFeat), restrict expansion to high-II pairs → "OpenFE-II"/"AutoFeat-II": pair set reduced ~5×, similar-or-better test scores, much shorter runtimes.

OpenFE occasionally beats IIFE (Cal Housing Lasso 0.5260 > 0.5076; Jungle Chess LGBM 0.9481 < EAAFE 0.9873 > IIFE 0.9701). Runtimes (Table V): TOTAL avg — IIFE 1.58h, OpenFE 1.45h, AutoFeat 1.53h, EAAFE 1.31h, DIFER 4.55h (slowest everywhere; LGBMclass avg 7.07h); IIFE comparable to OpenFE/AutoFeat, much cheaper than DIFER. ## 4. Equations & assumptions
Equations: τ_ij = I(F_i;F_j|Y) − I(F_i;F_j); entropy expansion above; Algorithm 2 stop condition (mean(S_{c−P/2..c}) ≤ mean(S_{c−P..c−P/2})); Algorithm 3 loop. Named statistics: F1-micro (classification), 1 − relative absolute error (regression); RF impurity importance for prefiltering. Assumptions (stated): high-II pairs are good combination candidates (validated synthetically); entropy estimation on discretized/continuous features is reliable (Mesner & Shalizi nearest-neighbor conditional MI, ref [14]); the patience stop detects convergence; prefiltering to top-50 doesn't drop synergistic pairs.

## 5. Features / target
Inputs: raw tabular features. Generated: bivariate combinations of high-synergy pairs (B set) + univariate transforms of winners (U set), iterated to arbitrary order. Example: log(F1·F2) built in one iteration, then combined further. Targets: dataset-native (F1-micro / 1−RAE). No time horizon (static tabular).

## 6. Validation design
The paper's own hygiene prescriptions, applied to itself: 80/20 holdout test sets (not CV-as-metric), inductive setting, hyperparameter tuning before AND after AutoFE, 25 runs, test metric on held-out data. Comparators: OpenFE, EAAFE, AutoFeat, DIFER (open-source implementations). Large-scale: subsampled-row evaluations (1/20th rows) for IIFE; OpenFE/AutoFeat required II-acceleration to finish at all ("cannot complete within several weeks" otherwise). Synthetic verification: targets y_ij = f(F_i, F_j) for 105 credit-approval pairs, f ∈ {+, ×, sin(+), exp(|max|)} — II ranks the true pair at/near rank 0 across all f (Fig. 5 histograms).

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
- Table I (all public datasets/models): IIFE **10 top ranks**, average rank **2.50 (1.64)** vs OpenFE 3.38, EAAFE 3.13, AutoFeat 3.08, DIFER 3.75, baseline 4.46. Average % change over baseline: **26.88% (90.6)** vs AutoFeat 20.62%, DIFER 9.02%, OpenFE 8.26%, EAAFE 7.11%. Excluding the OpenML-586 outlier: 8.74% vs 5.63–7.16%. By model: linear-only **72.42%** (AutoFeat 59.44%), RF-only 5.20%, LGBM-only 3.02% (OpenFE marginally better at 3.11%).
- Linear-model gap closure (Fig. 3): IIFE+LR reduces the gap to RF* to **4.99%** and to LGBM* to 5.35%; IIFE+Lasso → RF* 4.94%, → LGBM* 10.15% — linear models reach near-nonlinear performance while staying explainable (the paper's "major contribution").
- Large-scale proprietary (Table II): IIFE **+6.21% in 5.33h** vs OpenFE-II +1.16% (4.17h) vs AutoFeat-II +4.23% (5.75h).
- Literature-hygiene quantifications (§IV-C): CV-as-final-metric inflates gains 21.03% → 15.33% on holdout; OpenFE's transductive groupby-then-* (test samples in aggregates) inflates airfoil by **+14.01%** and jungle chess by +1.95% vs the inductive fix.
- Combining methods (§IV-E): IIFE+AutoFeat (LR) +1.51%, IIFE+EAAFE (RF) +0.31%, IIFE+OpenFE (LGBM) +0.20% over the second-best single method.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Adversarial read: (a) The paper's own critique is its strength — but note IIFE's *internal* selection still uses CV scores on the train set for the keep decision (V_M in Algorithm 3), with the holdout only at the end; the 26.88% headline still carries selection bias, just less than competitors'. (b) The transductive-leakage finding is damning for OpenFE's published numbers specifically, and the paper re-runs OpenFE in inductive mode — but the *fix* (train-only aggregates applied to test) is exactly what ledger 1842's OpenFE implementation spec must enforce; cross-ledger dependency noted. (c) The 72.42% linear-only gain is dominated by datasets where linear baselines were terrible (huge std 153) — the gap-closure framing is fairer than the raw %. (d) II estimation on continuous features via nearest-neighbor MI is noisy at small N; no sensitivity analysis on discretization. (e) Prefiltering to top-50 by RF importance can drop pure-interaction pairs (XOR-type: individually useless, jointly predictive) — the exact pairs II is supposed to find; the paper doesn't test this failure mode. (f) Proprietary dataset is unverifiable. (g) Anonymous code link in v1 may be dead; check before adopting.

## 10. GSE overlap
Two adoptions in one paper. (1) **The II pair-prior as a search pruner**: GSE's candidate interaction space (team stats × situational splits × market features) is combinatorial; computing II for all pairs is O(|F|²) entropy work — orders of magnitude cheaper than LightGBM retrains per candidate — and the paper proves it finds the synergistic pairs (synthetic rank-0 result) and accelerates expand-reduce methods 5× without score loss. It slots directly into the GSE pipeline *before* the OpenFE/DIFER-style expansion. (2) **The hygiene rules as GSE law**: holdout test (never CV-as-metric — quantifies the inflation at 5.7pp), inductive-only aggregates (the transductive finding is a landmine for any groupby-then-* NFL feature like "team's average EPA" computed over train+test), tune-before-and-after. The linear-model gap closure (4.99% to RF) is strategically valuable: an explainable linear model at near-GBM performance is what Garrett can show publicly ("every pick public, show their work").

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Build "GSE-IIFE": (1) Pair screening: compute τ_ij for all candidate feature pairs on the training seasons only (2020–2022), using the Mesner–Shalizi nearest-neighbor conditional MI estimator; prefilter: skip RF-importance prefiltering (the paper's XOR-blindspot) — NFL feature counts are in the hundreds, O(|F|²) is affordable. (2) Iterative construction: top-K pairs × bivariate set {+, −, ×, ÷, diff-vs-opponent, ratio-to-league-avg} → LightGBM CV on 2023 validation → keep argmax; univariate {log, sqrt, z-score, sigmoid} on winner; add to pool; repeat with patience P = 5. (3) **Inductive enforcement** (from §IV-C): every groupby-then-* aggregate computed on training seasons only, mapped onto validation/test — the transductive bug is a hard failure mode in the audit. (4) Model-specific: run the loop separately for the public explainable LR model and the LightGBM engine model; the LR arm targets the gap-closure result. Effort: ~4–5 days (II estimator + loop + inductive plumbing).

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: nflverse 2020–2024, game-level; kickoff cutoffs enforced. Protocol: IIFE loop on 2020–2022 (II computation) / 2023 (V_M selection); frozen feature set; evaluate LR and LightGBM on held-out 2024 (log-loss) vs no-FE baseline. Controls: (a) hygiene ablation — run one arm with transductive aggregates (train+2024) to *replicate* the paper's +14% inflation finding in the sports domain (expected: inflated 2024 score that collapses under the inductive fix; if it doesn't inflate, say so); (b) random-pair baseline (same K, random pairs) to prove II prioritization beats chance; (c) leakage audit on all groupby features.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADAPT the II pair-prior iff (a) 2024 held-out log-loss improves ≥ 0.003 for LightGBM, AND (b) the II-prioritized arm beats the random-pair arm by ≥ 0.002 (proving the information-theoretic selection, not the bivariate expansion, drives the gain), AND (c) the transductive-control arm demonstrably inflates (confirming the audit can detect the bug class), AND (d) the LR arm closes ≥ 50% of the LR→LightGBM gap (the paper's explainability dividend). REJECT if II prioritization doesn't beat random pairs, if any inductive violation is found, or if the loop needs > 8 hours per model arm.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Beyond the paper: fix its XOR-blindspot — replace the RF-importance prefilter (which the paper uses at scale but which drops pure-interaction pairs) with a **two-stage screen**: stage 1 computes II for all pairs (no prefilter); stage 2 re-estimates II *conditioned on the current pool* (conditional interaction information I(F_i; F_j; Y | pool)) so pairs whose synergy is already captured aren't re-selected. Hypothesis: conditional-II finds the XOR-type sports interactions (e.g. blitz-rate × man-coverage-rate: individually weak, jointly predictive of defensive EPA) that marginal II + RF-prefiltering miss. Second experiment: make the bivariate set *sports-native* (rest-differential interactions, weather × dome/outdoor, divisional-rematch adjustments) and test whether domain-specific B beats the paper's generic arithmetic set — the CAAFE/LLM-FE lesson (1846/1847) applied to IIFE's search.
