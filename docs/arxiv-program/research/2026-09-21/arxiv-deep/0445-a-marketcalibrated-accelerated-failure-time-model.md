# [0445] A Market-Calibrated Accelerated Failure Time Model for In-Play Football Forecasting (arXiv:2605.16066v1)

**Citation:** L. Clegg, J. Cartlidge, Z. Syed (2026). *A Market-Calibrated Accelerated Failure Time Model for In-Play Football Forecasting*. arXiv:2605.16066v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16066v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (§§1–11, full bibliography; 3,349 text lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — soccer-domain, but the machinery transfers cleanly to GSE's NFL engine: (1) market-calibrated team strengths (squared-error fit to 1X2 + over/under prices, eq. 13) as the general recipe for anchoring model parameters to kickoff prices; (2) baseline-subtracted cumulative shot-quality covariates as the design pattern for in-play NFL signal features; (3) the accuracy-vs-profitability distinction with the EV-threshold and winner's-curse evidence, directly relevant to how GSE evaluates picks.

## 1. Research question
Close the gap between in-play football forecasting models and betting-exchange prices by (i) calibrating a Weibull accelerated failure time (AFT) model's team-strength parameters to Betfair Exchange prices at kick-off (financial-model calibration: fit parameters to reproduce observed market prices), and (ii) adding post-shot expected goals (PSxG) as a time-varying in-play covariate. Test whether market calibration — applicable to any intensity-based goal arrival model — is the dominant driver of in-play accuracy.

## 2. Dataset / schema
- Four EPL seasons 2021–2025: WhoScored.com timestamped events (goal arrival times, red cards, added time); FBref.com post-shot expected goals (PSxG — probability a shot scores given location, context, placement, GK positioning; on-target shots only); Betfair Exchange minute-by-minute historical data (last-traded prices; minute M+2 used as crude executable-odds estimate — 99% of price reactions to goals absorbed within 2 min per a 470-goal timing analysis).
- 1,520 scheduled fixtures; 3 excluded; 1,517 remaining → 1,377 training, 140 evaluation (all gameweek 25+ of 2024–25).
- Stoppage-time means from 240 pre-evaluation training matches: 3.1 min first half, 6.2 min second half (FIFA 2023 effective-playing-time directive forced same-season restriction).
- Note: FBref has since lost access to the underlying Opta statistics (Jan 2026 blog), so PSxG replication requires a new source.

## 3. Method / model
- Weibull AFT for goal inter-arrival times (Babootor 2021 framework): TH∼Weibull(γ,λH), TA∼Weibull(γ,λA); log 𝔼[T] = μ + βhome + a + d with sum-to-zero identifiability; fitted by MLE on interval-censored goal times (not Bayesian), BIC for model comparison.
- In-play adaptations: exponential time decay of team strengths (wk=exp(−ξ·dk/3.5), ξ=0.0065/half-week ≈ 1-year half-life, re-estimated per gameweek); half-specific shape parameters; conditional Weibull sampling via inverse transform (eq. 7); stoppage-time means; half-time clock reset justified by γ̂1H≈1 (memoryless); covariates via βᵀx added to log-expected times (eqs. 10–11).
- Market calibration (eq. 13): per match, minimise squared error between Weibull-simulated and Betfair-implied probabilities over 1X2 outcomes plus over/under thresholds {0.5,1.5,2.5,3.5,4.5} — the over/under markets resolve an identifiability problem (outcome probs alone consistent with many (λH,λA) pairs at different scoring levels); solved with Powell's method from model-estimated η. Shifts ranged to >0.47 (42-min increase in Leicester's expected time to score vs Arsenal); predominantly positive (uncalibrated model overestimates scoring).
- In-play MC: N=10,000 paths per prediction minute (Algorithm 1), PSxG deviation held fixed across paths (future shots not simulated).
- Comparisons: Zou (Bayesian birth process, conjugate Gamma update θ̂1=(r1+X(T))/(r1+EH(T))·θ̂01, eq. 14, r1=EH(45)) and Maia (Cox process with dynamic regressors, eq. 15, plus stoppage/red-card joint models), both calibrated to the same pre-match odds with the same objective.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
(1)–(2) TH∼Weibull(γ,λH), TA∼Weibull(γ,λA); (3) score-state γ from Babootor (0.943/1.044/1.731 — not replicated on this data: 1.19/1.19/1.21, so dropped); (4)–(5) log 𝔼[TH]=μ+βhome+aH+dA, log 𝔼[TA]=μ+aA+dH; (6) time-decay weights; (7) Tremaining=(s^γ−log(U)/λ)^{1/γ}−s; (8)–(9) cumulative PSxG Sk(t) and deviation xPSxG,k(t)=Sk(t)−S̄(t) vs a linear population baseline; (10)–(11) covariate-augmented log-expected times; (12) RPS; (13) calibration loss ℒ=Σo(p̂o−po^mkt)²+Σg(p̂>g−p>g^mkt)²; (14) Zou's Gamma-posterior update; (15) Maia's Cox intensity. Assumptions: independent Weibull inter-arrivals (no improvement from Marshall–Olkin bivariate or random effects per Babootor); first half approximately memoryless; Betfair last-traded ≈ executable; red-card difference linear; covariates static during forward simulation; no modelling of the market's own information set beyond prices.

## 5. Features / target
Features: per-team attack/defence strengths (time-decayed), home advantage, half indicator, red-card difference (own−opp), cumulative PSxG deviation vs population baseline (and cumulative-goals deviation variant). Targets: 1X2 outcome probabilities at each minute; simulated final scores; Kelly-bet profitability.

## 6. Validation design
Training on 1,377 matches; evaluation on 140 held-out matches (gameweek 25+ 2024–25) at every minute kick-off→whistle (13,832 prediction points/model); metrics = accuracy, RPS, log-loss; log-loss-by-minute vs Betfair (Fig. 4); nested covariate models compared by BIC + LRT; betting simulation vs Betfair in-play odds with 2% commission, unit vs Kelly staking (f=(bp−q)/b, bankroll reset per bet); EV-threshold plot (Fig. 6); goal-window vs non-goal-window split to rule out stale-price exploitation (3,212 goal-window bets ROI 4.7% vs 14,246 outside 4.9% — edge not from timing lag). Explicitly caveats: 140 matches is small for betting evaluation (ROI/Sharpe outlier-sensitive); accuracy results more robust than profitability claims.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
- Hazard spec (Table 1): half-specific γ (γ̂1H=0.983 [0.953,1.012], γ̂2H=1.395 [1.325,1.465], Table 2) ΔBIC=−302.9 vs score-state baseline (BIC=41,629); single γ ΔBIC=−16.7; Babootor's score-state γs not replicated.
- Covariates (Table 3, BIC=29,883 baseline): M1 red cards β̂red=−0.41 (0.06), ΔBIC=−37.1, LRT p=7.0×10⁻¹²; M3 PSxG β̂psxg=−0.10 (0.02), ΔBIC=−53.4, p=2.9×10⁻⁷ (best); M2 goals deviation β̂goals=−0.07 (0.01), ΔBIC=−49.4.
- Pre-match (Table 4): uncalibrated Weibull 56.4% vs Betfair 61.4% accuracy; all calibrated variants lock to 61.4% (RPS ≈0.182–0.184, log-loss ≈0.911–0.920).
- In-play (Table 5): Weibull κ,ψ 70.2% accuracy / RPS 0.1294 / log-loss 0.6933 vs Betfair 70.6% / 0.1254 / 0.6714; Maia κ,ψ 69.4% / 0.1303 / 0.6963; Zou κ 68.2% / 0.1412 / 0.7569 (its Bayesian update overrides calibrated strengths — in 0–0 matches scoring rates fall 30% by minute 20, 50% by half-time, over-shooting to draws); PSxG's out-of-sample effect small (RPS 0.1353→0.1347 Weibull; 0.1376→0.1375 Maia) — calibration dominates.
- Betting (Table 6): Weibull κ,ψ Kelly ROI +4.5% (Sharpe 5.94) over 17,458 bets, 49% win, net +158.15; unit staking −3.4% (late-match short-odds bets wipe mid-game gains); EV≥0.20 → 8.5% ROI over 3,241 bets; EV-threshold peak near 0.2 at >20% ROI (3,377 bets) then declines — winner's curse; Maia κ,ψ Kelly ROI only 1.3% despite similar accuracy (comparable forecast quality ≠ comparable economic value).
- Comparisons: Weibull κ,ψ RPS 0.1294 competitive with SOTA in-play (0.1338, ref 28).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- 140-match single-half-season evaluation; ROI/Sharpe outlier-sensitive (authors' own caveat — accuracy claims more robust than profitability).
- Three misaligned data sources (WhoScored/FBref/Betfair timestamps); last-traded prices ≠ executable.
- PSxG held fixed across MC paths (no future-shot simulation); in-play covariate set weak vs market from minute 20 onward, especially at the second-half restart (suggests half-time market re-calibration as a second calibration point, and substitutions/expected-threat as better covariates).
- Zou's failure mode is instructive: updating strengths from sparse in-match evidence destroys the calibrated prior — a caution for any GSE in-play updating scheme.
- Accuracy-optimal ≠ profit-optimal (refs 39, 13, 38): profitability can arise from decorrelating from market prices rather than from better forecasts — the authors suggest fitting team strengths to maximise expected returns instead of market-fit.
- EPL-only, single season; market calibration's value may vary with league liquidity.
- Bibliography follow-ups for GSE (from the full reference list, now read in full): Wheatcroft (2021), "Evaluating probabilistic forecasts of football matches: the case against the ranked probability score" (cited by §6 — a direct critique of RPS as an evaluation metric, relevant given this paper and GSE both report RPS); Wunderlich & Memmert (2020), "Are betting returns a useful measure of accuracy in (sports) forecasting?" (cited by §§10–11 — argues profitability ≠ accuracy, the paper's own §11 theme); Wunderlich et al. (2026, in press), "Does the consideration of market prices in model selection increase model profitability?" (cited by §§10–11); Leriou & Ntzoufras (2025), "Survival modeling of goal arrival times in English Premier League" (cited in §§1, 4, 5, 7, 11 — the closest competing survival-model approach).

## 10. GSE overlap
Strong structural overlap with the ML brief and engine-benchmark lane in existing-research-map.md: GSE's engine produces NFL SPREAD/MONEYLINE/TOTAL picks (3,411 picks in the Neon DB, model v5.2.7) and the map includes Kelly staking, market-efficiency, and line-movement topics — but no in-play forecasting work and no formal market-calibration-of-parameters step. This paper fills exactly that methodological gap: (i) fitting team-strength parameters to kickoff market prices via a squared-error calibration objective is the recipe for GSE's pre-game NFL market anchoring (The Odds API is the connected source; market-implied win totals/team ratings exist in the corpus); (ii) baseline-subtracted cumulative deviation covariates (eqs. 8–9) are the design pattern for any future GSE in-play feature (e.g., EPA deviation vs population baseline); (iii) the accuracy-vs-profitability distinction (Zou's collapse, Kelly>unit, EV-threshold curve, winner's curse, Maia's accuracy-without-profit) is directly load-bearing for GSE's pick evaluation, where the unit of value is bankroll, not log-loss. Dedup: no prior item combines Weibull AFT with market calibration and Kelly evaluation (near misses: Poisson/ Dixon–Coles score models, market-efficiency studies — none with this calibration method).

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Phase A — market-calibrated team strengths (pre-game NFL): (1) define GSE's kickoff calibration objective analog of eq. 13: ℒ = Σ(game lines: spread/moneyline/total implied probs − engine probs)² + Σ(over/under 0.5/1.5/… equivalents − engine)² over calibrated team-strength parameters, minimised per game from model-estimated values via Powell; (2) use The Odds API consensus at kickoff as the market source; (3) the totals market fixes the absolute scoring level exactly as over/under fixes total goals — replicate that identifiability argument for NFL (moneyline alone underdetermines (offense, defense) strength pairs); (4) report the calibration shift distribution per team (Fig. 3 analog) as the "market-vs-model information gap" diagnostic. Phase B — deviation covariates: implement cumulative-EPA-deviation-vs-population-baseline as the NFL analog of xPSxG for any in-play work. Phase C — profit-based gating: evaluate every model change under both log-loss/RPS and Kelly-vs-Betfair-style ROI simulation against closing lines; adopt the paper's EV-threshold plot as the standard pick-filter diagnostic (ROI vs EV threshold, watching for the winner's-curse turnover); never let sparse in-match evidence override the calibrated prior (Zou lesson).

## 12. Reproducible test
(1) Refit GSE NFL team strengths on a training window with exponential time decay (ξ≈0.0065/half-week); (2) calibrate per-game to kickoff consensus lines via eq. 13's analog; (3) backtest on a held-out season: accuracy, RPS, log-loss vs market-implied probs, plus Kelly ROI simulation against closing lines with 2% vig analog. Expected pattern from the paper: calibration closes most of the market gap; residual edge, if any, shows up in Kelly ROI not log-loss. Numeric acceptance: calibrated model within 0.5pp of market-implied accuracy on held-out NFL games before any profitability claim is made.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
Accept into the engine lane if: (1) the kickoff-calibration objective is implemented and its identifiability fix (totals-equivalent market) documented; (2) held-out accuracy/RPS/log-loss reported against market-implied probabilities; (3) any profitability claim passes the goal-window-style timing control (edge outside stale-price windows) and the EV-threshold plot shows a non-marginal edge. Reject profitability claims based on <300 games or without the stale-price control; reject any variant that lets in-match updating override the calibrated prior without Zou-style collapse testing.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Directly from §11: fit team-strength parameters to maximise expected Kelly returns against historical closing lines rather than market-fit at kickoff (the 13/38 decorrelation idea) — this tests whether GSE's edge comes from forecast accuracy or from systematic market biases the engine can price differently. Second: add the second calibration point (half-time market prices → NFL halftime lines) as mid-game re-anchoring, the paper's proposed fix for the second-half-restart information gap.
