## 1. Research question
RQ1: What methods and evaluation criteria exist for generating synthetic financial transaction data? (Answered via PRISMA systematic review: 4 databases, 10 years, 1,216 papers screened by ≥2 reviewers, quality criteria from Carrera-Rivera et al. 2022 / Dybå & Dingsøyr 2008 → 30 papers included.) RQ2: Which algorithm is most effective? (Answered via head-to-head benchmark of 5 algorithms on two large datasets with a unified five-category metric framework.)

## 2. Dataset / schema
Two datasets: (1) real anonymized bank transactions — one month, 5,202,003 transactions (5,201,946 after cleaning), features undisclosed (transaction, source/target tokens, amount, transfer details); (2) IBM-simulated public dataset — 4,211,370 rows, similar characteristics. Graph embeddings (Abu-El-Haija et al. 2018) capture network structure. Protocol: hyperparameter search on a 100k-row subset (30 runs), final models trained on full data. NFL translation: transactions → game events (plays); source/target tokens → teams; amounts → EPA/yardage; the graph → the season matchup network.

## 3. Method / model
No new generator — the contribution is the framework. Five algorithms benchmarked: CTGAN (2063's paper), DoppelGANger/DGAN (Lin et al. 2020 — separates time-series and metadata generation, batch generation for temporal correlation), WGAN (gradient penalty the most common variant, 62%), FinDiff (Sattarov et al. 2023 — Gaussian diffusion with embedding layer for mixed-type tabular; **this is ledger 2065**, 2309.01472), TVAE. Review finding: GANs are 70% of proposed architectures, but only 50% of post-2023 top performers — the field is shifting toward diffusion/VAE (corroborates the lane's direction).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Framework definitions: column-wise fidelity via KS statistic (higher = more similar); row-wise fidelity via Pearson correlation between column pairs; synthesis = fraction of generated records NOT replicating originals within a 1% margin (Sattarov et al. 2023 method); efficiency = mean training seconds over 30 hyperparameter runs (100k samples → 10k synthetic); privacy = 5th percentile of DCR (median Euclidean distance synthetic→original) and NNDR (nearest/second-nearest distance ratio) per Zhao et al. 2021 — higher = lower re-identification risk; graph structure = NetSimile score (Berlingerio et al. 2012), higher = more similar networks. Assumptions: (1) fidelity, synthesis, privacy, efficiency, and graph similarity are jointly necessary — no single score suffices; (2) the 1%-margin novelty test captures "copying"; (3) 5th-percentile DCR/NNDR robustly estimates tail privacy risk.

## 5. Features / target
Mixed-type tabular transaction records + their network structure. NFL analog: the game-level table (2062–2069's domain) *plus* the season graph (who played whom, home/away) — the paper's insistence on graph-structure evaluation is its most NFL-relevant point, since a synthetic NFL season that breaks the schedule graph is useless for schedule-aware models.

## 6. Validation design
PRISMA review (reproducible search string given) + controlled benchmark: same 100k subset for tuning, same full-data training, same five metric categories on real and simulated data. Honest reporting includes a failure: WGAN's generated graph collapsed to a single cluster (NetSimile = NaN on real data) — published, not hidden. Cross-dataset check: real vs IBM-simulated results differ notably (lower fidelity on simulated), attributed to the simulated data's sparser structure being easier to memorize (privacy scores drop to 0.0 DCR for CTGAN/FinDiff/TVAE on simulated data).

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Table 1 (real data): FinDiff — column fidelity **0.95429**, row fidelity **0.98524** (beats TVAE by 6.41%/1.18%, beats CTGAN 0.87787/0.95258); synthesis 0.84101 (vs DGAN/WGAN 1.0 — diffusion copies more, a real tradeoff); DCR 0.00033 (worst privacy) but NNDR 0.98781 (nearest synthetic neighbors fall in the same clusters as originals); efficiency 625s. DGAN — DCR **1.81121** (best privacy), synthesis 1.0, but column fidelity 0.47808 (worst). CTGAN — the balance pick: fidelity 0.878/0.953, synthesis 0.99998, DCR 0.01968, but slowest: 2267s (65.3% slower than non-GANs). WGAN — fastest GAN (538s), worst column fidelity 0.24064, graph collapse. Table 2 (IBM simulated): same ranking pattern at lower absolute fidelity (FinDiff 0.43746/0.95798). Verdicts: DGAN for privacy-first sharing; FinDiff/TVAE for augmentation fidelity; CTGAN for balanced general use; all models fail graph replication (real-graph self-similarity 8.026 vs synthetic ~30 — poor).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Adversarial notes: (1) The headline "FinDiff wins fidelity" replicates Sattarov et al. 2023 (2065's own paper) — partially circular, since the framework is "adapted from Sattarov et al. 2023"; treat as corroboration, not independent proof. (2) FinDiff's synthesis score of 0.841 means ~16% of records are within 1% of a real record — for NFL augmentation this is acceptable (we're not publishing the data), but it weakens any privacy claim. (3) Graph structure is poorly replicated by *all* methods — the NetSimile gap is an open problem, and my §14 experiment targets exactly this. (4) No downstream task utility metric (no TSTR) — combine this framework with 2072's predictive score. (5) Efficiency measured only on 100k subsets; NFL scale is smaller, so training cost is a non-issue for us.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Schedule-graph-conditional generation: the paper's open problem is that *no* method replicates graph structure (NetSimile ~30 vs self-similarity 8.026). Experiment: (1) add an explicit graph-reconstruction auxiliary loss to the best tabular generator (FinDiff-2065): a GNN link-prediction head trained jointly, penalizing synthetic tables whose implied matchup graph diverges from the real schedule graph; (2) evaluate with the paper's NetSimile metric — target: close half the gap (NetSimile ≤19) while holding column fidelity ≥0.90. If it works, graph-conditional generation becomes a lane-wide requirement: every synthetic NFL dataset must ship with its NetSimile-vs-real-schedule score, and the engine's schedule-aware modules may only train on synthetic data passing the graph gate.
