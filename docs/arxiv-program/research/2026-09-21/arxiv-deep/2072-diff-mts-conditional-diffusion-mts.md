# [2072] Diff-MTS: Temporal-Augmented Conditional Diffusion for Multivariate Time Series (arXiv:2407.11501)

**Citation:** Lei Ren, Haiteng Wang, Yuanjun Laili (Beihang University, 2024). *Diff-MTS: Temporal-Augmented Conditional Diffusion-based AIGC for Industrial Time Series Towards the Large Model Era*. arXiv:2407.11501. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11501. Submitted to IEEE; no code link in the paper.
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-22. **Read:** full text (ar5iv HTML, 2407.11501, §§I–V + references; Eqs. 1–16, Algorithms 1–2, Tables I–VI, Figs. 1–5 verified).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — the strongest *conditional* time-series diffusion paper in this lane and the one whose evaluation protocol (discriminative + predictive + DTW/Fréchet, three criteria: diversity/fidelity/usefulness) should become the lane standard. Two transferable inventions: (1) classifier-free conditional Ada-MMD — a learnable MMD regularizer that forces condition consistency without a classifier (the generated sequence actually obeys the requested condition); (2) TDR-UNet — trend/peak temporal decomposition with convolutional attention between UNet encoder and decoder. For the NFL this means: condition-consistent trajectory generation (ask for a "road-underdog blowout-win" trajectory and actually get one), which none of the other lane papers can do.

## 1. Research question
Industrial MTS is scarce (privacy, collection cost) yet needed for "industrial large models." GAN-based MTS generators suffer unstable joint generator/discriminator training; plain diffusion lacks temporal inductive bias and controlled (condition-consistent) generation. Questions: does a diffusion model with (a) an adaptive MMD condition-consistency regularizer and (b) a temporal-decomposition-reconstruction denoiser beat GAN-based (TTS-GAN, TimeGAN, GAN-LSTM) and diffusion-based (DiffWave, SSSD) baselines — and TabDDPM — on diversity, fidelity, and *usefulness* across multiple sequence lengths?

## 2. Dataset / schema
NASA C-MAPSS turbofan degradation: 21 sensors → 14 informative (sensors 1,5,6,10,16,18,19 dropped as constant); sub-datasets FD001 (1 operating condition, 1 fault), FD002 (6 conditions), FD003 (2 faults), FD004 (6 conditions + 2 faults). FEMTO bearing wear: 25.6 kHz vibration/force, 3 conditions; bearings 1-1/1-2 under condition 1 (1800 RPM, 4000 N) for generation. Generation lengths 24/48/96 (C-MAPSS) and 80/160/320 (FEMTO). NFL translation: sensor channels → team-week metrics (K channels); operating condition/fault type → matchup regime (home/away, rest edge, dome, divisional); health indicator (the paper's condition x_c) → team strength tier or game-script class.

## 3. Method / model
DDPM backbone (Eqs. 1–3) with a **cosine noise schedule** (Eqs. 4–5, s=0.008, per Nichol & Dhariwal 2021 — chosen because the linear schedule destroys signal information too fast on sensor data). Conditional reverse (Eqs. 6–8): p_θ(x_{t−1}|x_t, x_c) = N(x_{t−1}; μ_θ(x_t,t|x_c), σ_θ I) with μ_θ from ε_θ(x_t, t|x_c). **Ada-MMD** (Eqs. 9–11): L_diff = (1−ω)L_{θ,xc} + ω L_MMD(n,m), where L_MMD(n,m) = K(n,n′) − 2K(m,n) + K(m,m′) with n = true noise ε, m = predicted noise ε_θ(x_t,t|x_c), K a positive-definite kernel, ω a learnable coefficient — a classifier-free way to align conditional and unconditional latent structure, "adaptively learning mutual information." **TDR-UNet** (§III-C): input embedding (Conv1D for x_t; sinusoidal timestep + 2-layer FC; FC + Mask_α for continuous condition x_c — α fraction of condition-embedding entries randomized, a classifier-free-guidance-style dropout); 3-encoder/3-decoder 1D-Conv UNet (conv → GroupNorm → SiLU blocks, residual + skip connections); between encoder and decoder a **temporal decomposition**: X_Trend = AvgPool(Pad(X)), X_Peak = MaxPool(Pad(X)), F_dec = Conv1d(Concat(X_Trend, X_Peak)) (Eq. 15), then convolutional attention Q/K/V = Conv(F_dec), Attention = softmax(QK^T/√d_k)V (Eq. 16), then 1D-conv reconstruction. Algorithms 1 (train, Adam, 70 epochs) and 2 (sample: x_T∼N(0,I), iterate to x_0 conditioned on x_c). Full hyperparameters in Table I.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Core: L_diff = (1−ω)L_{θ,xc} + ω·L_MMD(ε, ε_θ(x_t,t|x_c)) (Eq. 11); x_Trend/x_Peak decomposition (Eq. 15); conv-attention (Eq. 16); cosine schedule f(t) = cos((t/T + s/(1+s))·π/2)² (Eq. 5). Assumptions: (1) condition x_c (health indicator) is available at generation time and continuous — NFL analogs (strength tier, regime) are available pre-game, so this holds; (2) MMD with a fixed kernel captures the noise-distribution mismatch relevant to condition consistency — the ω adaptivity partially relaxes this; (3) trend/peak decomposition via AvgPool/MaxPool is a sufficient temporal prior (kernel_size=3); (4) evaluation metrics (below) proxy generation quality — the paper uses the most complete metric set in the lane.

## 5. Features / target
Targets are full multivariate sequences (24–320 steps × 14 channels). NFL features: team-week trajectory panels (18 weeks × K metrics) conditioned on a continuous "game-script strength" indicator, or drive-level sequences conditioned on pre-game spread. The paper's per-label generation protocol ("generate the same number of series with the same labels (HI) as the original dataset," §IV-E) maps to: generate the same number of trajectories per regime class as the real NFL data.

## 6. Validation design
The lane's best protocol — three criteria, five metric families: (1) diversity/fidelity: t-SNE/PCA overlap (Figs. 4–5); (2) fidelity: **discriminative score** — 2-layer LSTM classifier real-vs-synthetic accuracy, 70/10/20 split (lower better); (3) usefulness: **predictive score** — train 2-layer LSTM on synthetic, test on real, RMSE (lower better; 40 epochs); (4) similarity: DTW and Fréchet distances (Table VI); (5) ablations: Ada-MMD off / pure-MMD (Table V), TDR-UNet components (Table IV). Every experiment repeated 5× and averaged. Baselines: TTS-GAN, TimeGAN, GAN-LSTM (GAN pole), SSSD, DiffWave, TabDDPM (diffusion pole).

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Table II (C-MAPSS): Diff-MTS wins most of 12 length×dataset cells on both scores. Highlights: FD001-96 discriminative **0.611** vs DiffWave 0.904 (−32.4%) vs TimeGAN 0.935 vs TTS-GAN 0.987 (GAN disc scores generally >0.94 — near-perfectly distinguishable, i.e., GAN samples are poor); predictive FD001-96 **14.472** vs DiffWave 21.774 (−33.4%), FD003-96 **15.554** vs 28.433 (−45.2%), FD004-96 **23.167** vs 32.515 (−28.7%). FEMTO (Table III): predictive 0.143/0.148/0.158 vs DiffWave 0.213/0.204/0.239; discriminative 0.683/0.683/0.663 vs 0.864/0.741/0.741. Table VI: avg DTW 5.000 vs DiffWave 5.442 vs GAN-LSTM 10.157 vs TTS-GAN 484.327; avg Fréchet 1.317 vs 1.412. Ablations: removing Ada-MMD degrades predictive scores on all 4 FD sets (Table V, e.g., FD004-48 28.926 vs 27.072); TDR-UNet avg disc 0.726 vs original UNet 0.785 (−7.5%), avg pred 21.070 vs 22.333 (−5.7%) (Table IV). This is genuine, consistent dominance — the paper's numbers are the strongest in this lane.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Adversarial notes: (1) No code released — reimplementation risk; verify Table I hyperparameter semantics (the "conv(4,32/64/64,2)" shorthand is underspecified) against a reimplementation pilot before committing. (2) Discriminative score 0.611–0.897 is still well above 0.5 — samples are detectable; good, not solved. (3) Industrial sensor data is smoother/more periodic than NFL game data — transfer of the trend/peak prior to weekly football stats needs validation (the predictive score will catch it). (4) The Ada-MMD kernel choice is barely discussed — kernel misspecification could silently degrade condition consistency; monitor condition-consistency directly (fraction of generated trajectories whose realized class matches the requested condition). (5) Lengths ≤320 tested; an 18-week NFL panel is comfortably inside. (6) Compute: diffusion sampling × TDR-UNet is heavier than TimeGrad's dilated ConvNet — the N≈10 ablation lesson from 2070 applies here too.

Build plan (effort: ~7 engineer-days incl. reimplementation; the highest-value sequential build in the lane):

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: nflverse 2015–2022 train, 2023–2024 held-out (season-level splits). Baselines: TimeGrad (2070), TTS-GAN (2071), DiffWave reimplementation. Challenger: Diff-MTS reimplementation. Metrics (adopting the paper's suite as the lane standard): (a) discriminative score — 2-layer LSTM real-vs-synthetic accuracy, require <0.75 (paper achieved 0.611–0.897); (b) predictive score — train week-ahead predictor on synthetic, test on real, require RMSE within 15% of real-trained (the paper's usefulness criterion); (c) condition consistency — ≥80% of generated trajectories classified into the requested regime by an independent probe; (d) downstream: real+synthetic vs real-only spread log-loss on held-out weeks; (e) DTW/Fréchet vs baselines.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT Diff-MTS as the lane's primary trajectory generator if: (a) discriminative score <0.75 on NFL data (beats the paper's worst, approaches its best), AND (b) predictive-score RMSE within 15% of real-trained on held-out seasons (usefulness — the paper's core claim), AND (c) condition consistency ≥80% (the Ada-MMD value proposition; if this fails, the paper's headline contribution doesn't transfer), AND (d) real+synthetic spread log-loss beats real-only by ≥0.003 on held-out weeks. REJECT if the reimplementation cannot reproduce the paper's ranking (Diff-MTS ≥ DiffWave on the paper's own C-MAPSS FD001-96 sanity check) — a failed replication kills the build before NFL data is touched. Gate set before running; 5 repeats, averaged, per the paper.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Matchup-graph Ada-MMD: the paper's condition x_c is a per-sample scalar/vector; NFL panels have *relational* structure (who plays whom each week). Experiment: (1) extend x_c to include the week's matchup-graph embedding (opponent strength + home/away encoded as a graph, pooled per team), and (2) apply the Ada-MMD regularizer not just to noise distributions but to the *cross-team correlation structure* of the denoised output — MMD between the empirical correlation matrices of real vs synthetic weekly panels. If correlation-MMD-augmented training improves the predictive score on head-to-head-correlated metrics (margin, total) by ≥10% relative while holding discriminative score, it becomes the standard conditioning upgrade for all joint-panel generators at GSE (and generalizes 2070's §14 GNN idea into the diffusion-sequence setting).
