# [0314] MuyBridge: Mobile Human Center-of-Mass Estimation from Monocular Video via Sparse Fusion (arXiv:2609.02854v1)

**Citation:** Aidan Bradshaw et al. (2026). *MuyBridge: Mobile Human Center-of-Mass Estimation from Monocular Video via Sparse Fusion*. arXiv:2609.02854v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02854v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 888 lines, main paper + appendix).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — a rigorously validated monocular center-of-mass pipeline whose sparse-fusion design (2D pose + sparse joint depth + anthropometry + ballistic/contact anchors) is the right template if GSE ever builds broadcast-derived biomechanical load features for NFL injury/props modeling; adopt only with the paper's own caveats (8 athletes, markerless reference, depth error dominates).

## 1. Research question
The paper asks whether accurate 3D human center-of-mass (CoM) trajectories can be recovered from a single uncalibrated mobile video, without motion-capture suits or multi-camera rigs. It proposes "sparse fusion": dense estimation only where needed (2D pose + monocular depth sampled sparsely at joints), fused with anthropometric body-segment parameters and physics anchors (ground contact, ballistic flight), and validates CoM accuracy against a multiview reference on athletic movements.

## 2. Dataset / schema
AthletePose3D: 8 athletes, approximately 1.3M synchronized multiview frames (paper's stated scale). Test set: 1,028 sequence-camera pairs, 235,183 frames. Modalities per frame: monocular video (mobile-phone capture), 2D pose (Halpe-26), monocular depth (distilled Marigold), multiview 3D reference for evaluation. Movements: running, track & field (jumps/throws), speed skating. Access: paper does not state a public download URL in the extract — verify availability before use; treat as request/restricted.

## 3. Method / model
Pipeline: (a) per-frame 2D pose via Halpe-26 and dense monocular depth via distilled Marigold; (b) sparse depth sampling — depth is read only at joint locations rather than densely; (c) per-frame 3D joint reconstruction by back-projecting 2D joints along camera rays to anthropometrically scaled distances; (d) segment CoM from stature/sex-specific de Leva anthropometric tables (segment masses m_s and proximal ratios ρ_s); (e) temporal fusion — constant-velocity range fusion with ground-contact and ballistic-flight anchors, plus affine range calibration (α, β) fit to anchors; (f) whole-body CoM as the mass-weighted segment mean. On-device profile (iPhone 15): pose 15.58 ms (63 FPS), depth 349.9 ms (2.86 Hz), full pipeline 946.3 MB, peak memory 1,185 MB, full-pass energy 638.4 mJ — the sparse design is what makes mobile deployment feasible.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Paper's equations (copied faithfully): segment CoM `c_s = p_prox_s + ρ_s(p_dist_s − p_prox_s)`; whole-body CoM `C = Σ_s m_s c_s / Σ_s m_s`; back-projection ray `d_f,j ∝ K^-1[u,v,1]^T`; anthropometric range `t_size_f,k = L_k / ||d_f,i − d_f,j||`; affine range calibration `(α,β) = argmin Σ_f w_f(α z̃_f + β − t_anchor_f)^2`. Stated assumptions: (a) de Leva population anthropometry approximates each athlete's segment masses/lengths (requires stature + sex); (b) one-time per-sequence calibration (affine range fit) is acceptable; (c) ground-contact frames and ballistic-flight phases are detectable and physics-constrained (constant velocity / projectile motion); (d) monocular depth is metrically meaningful up to an affine correction.

## 5. Features / target
Inputs: monocular RGB video frames, camera intrinsics K (one-time calibration), athlete stature + sex (for anthropometric tables). Intermediate features: Halpe-26 2D joint positions, sparse Marigold depth at joints, segment endpoints. Target: 3D whole-body center-of-mass trajectory C(t) in metric units, plus derived jump height. Evaluation metrics: 3D MAE (mm), absolute relative error (AbsRel %), per-axis MAE (X/Y/Z), jump-height MAE and correlation.

## 6. Validation design
Test on 1,028 sequence-camera pairs (235,183 frames) held out from AthletePose3D, compared against the dataset's synchronized multiview 3D reference (markerless, not marker-based mocap — a limitation, Section 9). Breakdowns by sport (running, track & field, skating), by contact vs. flight phase, and jump-height validation across 719 pairs. Baselines: the paper compares against ablated variants (no anchors, no affine calibration) rather than external competing systems; no prior monocular-CoM baseline is beaten head-to-head. No time-series leakage issue (per-frame reconstruction + held-out sequences), but the 8-athlete population is the binding constraint.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Paper's results (test set): Running (4.4 m range) — 3D MAE 187 mm, AbsRel 3.6%, X/Y/Z 44/33/166 mm. Track & field (5.4 m) — 185 mm, 2.3%, 94/39/117 mm. Skating (10.1 m) — 707 mm, 6.6%, 167/41/672 mm. Flight-phase error is 1.5–2.3× contact-phase error. Jump height across 719 pairs: 62 mm MAE, r = 0.84, bias +12 mm. Depth (Z) error dominates in all sports (e.g., 166 mm of running's 187 mm; 672 mm of skating's 707 mm). These are the paper's claims vs. a markerless multiview reference; the depth-dominance pattern is the key structural finding.

## 8. Code / data availability
Code: https://github.com/Abradshaw1/Muybridge (stated in paper). Data: AthletePose3D — public availability not confirmed in the extract; verify before use.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
(a) Only 8 athletes — population generalization is unproven; de Leva tables are population averages that misfit outliers (NFL linemen vs. defensive backs differ far more than the paper's athlete pool). (b) The "ground truth" is markerless multiview reconstruction, itself error-prone — the reported MAEs are relative to an imperfect reference. (c) One-time per-sequence calibration (affine range fit to anchors) is required; fully uncalibrated in-the-wild use is not demonstrated. (d) Depth error dominates (Z-axis is 3–4× the lateral error) — monocular depth remains the bottleneck, and skating (10.1 m range) degrades to 707 mm MAE. (e) External validity to NFL: the paper studies running/jumping/skating, not cutting, blocking, or tackling; contact-heavy NFL movements with occlusion are untested. (f) No comparison against a prior monocular-CoM method — the ablations show the anchors help, but absolute standing vs. alternatives is unknown.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Conditional future-lane spec (only if GSE commissions injury/props-risk modeling): (a) data — licensed NFL practice/film clips of specific movement types (cuts, jumps, tackles) with a small validation subset carrying wearable or multi-camera reference; (b) adapt the pipeline — replace Halpe-26 with a sports-pose model robust to pads/uniforms, keep the sparse-fusion structure (2D pose + sparse depth + anthropometry), recalibrate de Leva tables or fit NFL-position-specific segment parameters; (c) anchors — detect ground contact from foot keypoints, ballistic phases from takeoff/landing; (d) outputs — per-play CoM trajectory features (peak CoM acceleration, CoM displacement on contact, jump/landing asymmetry) as injury-risk covariates; (e) serving — offline batch feature extraction; (f) estimated effort: 6–8 weeks including validation data collection. Do not build without a committed consumer (injury model or props desk).

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: 50+ licensed clips of NFL-relevant movements (cuts, contested-catch jumps, tackles) with an independent 3D reference for a subset (wearables or multi-camera). Metric: 3D CoM MAE (mm) and jump/landing-height MAE vs. reference, reported separately for contact vs. flight phases. Baseline to beat: a naive constant-velocity extrapolation of detected 2D pose scale (i.e., the paper's anchors must earn their keep). Window: athlete-disjoint test set (no athlete in both train/calibration and test).

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
Adopt CoM features into any GSE model only if: (a) on athlete-disjoint NFL-movement test clips, contact-phase 3D CoM MAE ≤ 250 mm and flight-phase ≤ 400 mm vs. an independent reference (paper achieves 185–187 mm in its domain; NFL movements are harder); AND (b) the derived features show test-retest reliability (ICC ≥ 0.80) across two camera angles of the same play; AND (c) a downstream injury-risk or props model gains ≥0.02 AUC from the features on a forward window. Otherwise shelve — the 8-athlete evidence does not justify production use.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Beyond the paper: replace population de Leva tables with athlete-specific segment parameters estimated from a short calibration routine (a few seconds of known-geometry movements, e.g., standing + arm span), and test whether personalized anthropometry cuts the depth-dominated error. Rationale: the paper's error is dominated by the Z-axis range estimate, which is exactly where wrong segment lengths inject bias; personalization attacks the largest error component with minimal extra data, and is feasible in NFL settings where athletes are measured anyway (combine data).
