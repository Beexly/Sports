# [0332] FOOTPASS: A Multi-Modal Multi-Agent Tactical Context Dataset for Play-by-Play Action Spotting in Soccer Broadcast Videos (arXiv:2511.16183v1)

**Citation:** Jeremie Ochin, Raphael Chekroun, Bogdan Stanciulescu, and Sotiris Manitsaris (2025). *FOOTPASS: A Multi-Modal Multi-Agent Tactical Context Dataset for Play-by-Play Action Spotting in Soccer Broadcast Videos*. arXiv:2511.16183v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16183v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 1639 lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — don't adopt the soccer dataset; port the two-stage "noisy visual detections denoised by tactical/game-state priors" paradigm to automate NFL broadcast highlight timestamping for GSE's clip pipeline.

## 1. Research question
Automated play-by-play extraction from soccer broadcast video stalls because pure-vision spatiotemporal action detection (STAD) hits a precision wall at the high recall needed for exhaustive coverage. Can tactical knowledge — game state (player positions/velocities, team memberships, roles) and the long-range temporal regularities of soccer — serve as a prior to denoise imperfect visual detections and produce reliable player-centric play-by-play streams? The paper introduces FOOTPASS, the first public benchmark coupling full-length broadcast video with human play-by-play labels and tactical context, and benchmarks three methods to test this thesis.

## 2. Dataset / schema
- **FOOTPASS**: 54 full-length soccer matches from 2023/24 (French Ligue 1, German Bundesliga, Italian Serie A, Spanish La Liga, UEFA Champions League; 50 teams). Broadcast video natively Full HD 1920×1080 at 25 fps, synchronized with annotations; downsampled to 352×640 for the benchmark.
- **Play-by-play**: 102,992 human-annotated on-ball events in 8 classes — drive, pass, cross, shot, header, throw-in, tackle, block — each a tuple (frame, class, team, jersey) anchored at the ball-touch frame. Class distribution: pass 49.9%, drive 39.0%, header 4.0%, cross 2.3%, throw-in 1.9%, block 1.4%, shot 1.2%, tackle 0.3%. Bounding-box coverage of acting player: 81.5% overall (drives 85.7%, shots 81.6%, passes 81.5%; throw-ins 43.8%; 46.8% of throw-ins fall in replay segments).
- **Game-state**: per-frame player positions/velocities on the pitch, team memberships, jersey numbers, and 13 roles (Goalkeeper, Left Back, Left Central Back, Mid Central Back, Right Central Back, Left Midfielder, Right Midfielder, Defensive Midfielder, Attacking Midfielder, Left Winger, Right Winger, Central Forward, Right Back), plus single-player tracklets in screen space. Construction: field-line detection + per-frame camera calibration (field-to-image homography, intrinsics/extrinsics via planar decomposition, nonlinear refinement); player detection/localization/tracking with a custom multi-pass matcher (FIFA EPTS certified); imputation of missing players (avg 9–12 of 22 visible) via speed/acceleration-constrained interpolation with role-based relative-position priors; roles from trajectory analysis + clustering + expert annotation.
- **Splits**: 48 train matches (91,327 instances), 3 validation (indices 18, 24, 47; 6,070), 3 test (indices 0, 36, 50; 5,595). Validation/test chosen so even the rarest class has ≥15 visible instances.

## 3. Method / model
Three benchmarked methods, all retrained on FOOTPASS:
1. **TAAD** (Singh et al. 2023): purely visual Track-Aware Action Detector — detect + track actors, aggregate features along trajectories with fine-tuned X3D-L 3D CNN + ROI Align, then a Temporal Convolutional Network; per-actor per-frame action predictions. Training: 50-frame clips (2 s at 25 fps) containing ≥1 annotated action; ≤500 samples/class/epoch; 4–5 background tracklets as negatives; AdamW lr 10⁻³ (classification head) / 5×10⁻⁵ (pretrained X3D-L), weight decay 10⁻⁴, batch 6, 25 epochs on one RTX A6000, LR ÷10 at epochs 10 and 20; Albumentations augmentation (scaling, rotation, translation, flip, color jitter); label dilation ±1 frame; boosted foreground cross-entropy weights. Inference: overlapping 50-frame windows (stride 25), logits averaged, softmax, per-player per-class temporal NMS (25-frame window), concatenated to play-by-play records.
2. **TAAD+GNN** (Ochin et al. 2025b): TAAD visual features + game-state (positions, velocities, team memberships) encoded as a spatiotemporal graph — X3D-L backbone, Edge Convolution layers with asymmetric edge function, Temporal Convolutional layers between EdgeConv layers, GRU before the classification head; focal loss; same optimizer/augmentation as TAAD (+ flipping position/velocity features with the video).
3. **TAAD+DST** (Ochin et al. 2025a): Denoising Sequence Transduction — post-processes TAAD's averaged logits with role-based structured game-state features over long 750-frame sequences; three cross-entropy losses (frame, player role, action). Training: 2000 random 750-frame sequences per epoch from each of 48 matches; AdamW lr 2.5×10⁻⁴, wd 10⁻⁴, batch 96, 10 epochs, LR ÷10 at epochs 3/6/8, single RTX A6000; pitch mirroring augmentation (X/Y/both, roles and logit ordering remapped). Inference: contiguous 750-frame windows, confidence thresholding only.
4. **Ablated DST**: same training on per-player logit sequences + tactical features but without multi-agent reasoning (two losses: frame, action) — isolates whether gains come from tactical priors vs. better post-processing.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
No equations stated. Losses and operations named but not formalized (cross-entropy ×3 for DST; focal loss for TAAD+GNN; asymmetric edge function referenced). Stated assumptions: (i) on-ball events are temporally instantaneous (single anchor frame at ball touch); (ii) player ground contact at bbox bottom-midpoint for projection; (iii) play-by-play always specifies acting-player identity via jersey number even when no bbox exists; (iv) missing-player imputation with speed/acceleration constraints and role priors; (v) 13 fixed roles sufficient under limited data; (vi) τ=0.15 confidence threshold "slightly above the score expected from a uniform distribution given the number of classes"; (vii) broadcast videos excluded replays for camera calibration; (viii) 25-frame NMS handles successive actions.

## 5. Features / target
Inputs: broadcast video frames; single-player bounding boxes/tracklets; game-state features (positions, velocities, team memberships, 13-class roles, jersey numbers). Target: play-by-play event sequences — (frame, class ∈ {drive, pass, cross, shot, header, throw-in, tackle, block}, team, jersey), matched within ±12 frames temporal tolerance. Evaluation at confidence threshold τ=0.15 (high-recall regime for assisted annotation).

## 6. Validation design
Fixed match-level splits (48/3/3 matches; indices given). Metrics: overall and per-class Precision/Recall at τ=0.15, δ=±12 frames; per-class Average Precision via 11-point interpolation (Everingham et al. 2010). Evaluation on the 3-match test set (5,595 instances). Comparison against the authors' prior private-dataset numbers (240 matches) as a scale reference. Robustness analysis by visibility subset (acting player with/without bounding box). Ablation isolating multi-agent reasoning from temporal post-processing.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Overall at τ=0.15, δ=12 (Table 2, ×10²): TAAD — PR 25.6 / REC 59.9 / F1 35.9; TAAD+GNN — 44.5 / 62.7 / 52.1; **TAAD+DST — 68.2 / 66.8 / 67.5**; ablated DST — 45.0 / 54.0 / 49.1. Per-class F1 (TAAD+DST best except tackle): pass 72.3, drive 68.5, cross 56.8, throw-in 62.0, shot 65.2, header 28.3, block 18.0, tackle 0.0 (no true positives — scarcity + occlusions). Per-class AP: mAP 32.8% (TAAD+GNN) vs 36.8% (TAAD+DST) at δ=12; TAAD baseline has highest AP for header (DST's tactical denoising mildly degrades its high-precision region). Prior private dataset (240 matches): 78.7% precision / 75.8% recall — performance scales with data quantity; FOOTPASS uses ~4× less training data. Visibility robustness (Figure 6): recall without acting-player bbox — TAAD 3.9%, TAAD+GNN 2.6%, **TAAD+DST 33.4%**; ~9–10% of DST true positives lack bboxes vs ~1% for TAAD. Ablation: full DST F1 67.5% vs 49.1% without multi-agent reasoning — gains attributed to role/team-conditioned priors (who does what where) and long-range possession continuity, not just better NMS.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- **Occlusion/replay artifacts are structural**: ~20% of events lack acting-player bboxes; 46.8% of throw-ins occur in replay segments; broadcast editing breaks temporal continuity — the benchmark is realistic but metrics are capped by observability, not model quality.
- **Severe class imbalance**: tackle (0.3%) gets zero true positives under the best model; training mitigations (≤500 samples/class/epoch, focal loss) insufficient at this scale.
- **Match-level leakage risk**: only 3 test matches — team/league-specific styles may not generalize; no cross-competition validation.
- **Imputed game-state**: positions of invisible players are inferred, not observed — the "tactical prior" is partly synthetic, and DST's no-bbox recall may lean on these interpolations.
- **Header/block degradation**: tactical denoising hurts less-intentional actions (headers, blocks) where long-range structure doesn't apply — the prior is not universally beneficial.
- **External validity to NFL**: soccer broadcast conventions, 8 on-ball classes, and pitch geometry don't transfer; only the paradigm transfers.

## 10. GSE overlap
Extension, not duplicate. Per the existing-research map: GSE has an active video/clip lane — Recordly v1.3.0 (render engine for the unified money machine), the x-poster video skill, the standing rule that sports videos use real game footage with telestrated transformative edits, and Grok briefs on X — but **no automated event-spotting from broadcast video** and no game-state-prior machinery. nflverse already supplies NFL play-by-play (the soccer problem of "no structured events" doesn't exist for the NFL), so the value is inverted: use the DST paradigm to automate *timestamping* of highlight-worthy moments in broadcast footage to feed the clip pipeline, with nflverse play-by-play as the tactical prior instead of a learned one.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
1. **Data**: NFL broadcast clips (GSE's existing clip sources); nflverse play-by-play as the structured prior (down, distance, EPA, formation where available); no new annotation — align broadcast video to play timestamps.
2. **Stage 1 (perception)**: off-the-shelf action detector (e.g., a TAAD-style or YOLO-based player/ball detector) producing noisy per-play event candidates from broadcast frames at 1–2 fps.
3. **Stage 2 (denoise with priors)**: sequence model over the candidates conditioned on nflverse play context (down/distance, score differential, time remaining, EPA of the play) — exactly the DST role — to output high-precision highlight timestamps (big plays: EPA > 1.5, turnovers, scores). The nflverse prior replaces the learned tactical graph; role/team priors become down/distance/score priors.
4. **Serving**: batch pipeline — ingest game broadcast → emit timestamped clip candidates → human (Garrett) approves via the existing approve-desk flow; feeds the telestrated-edit workflow.
5. **Effort**: 3–4 weeks (detector integration + alignment + denoiser training on one season of broadcasts).

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: 10 full NFL broadcasts from the 2024 season with aligned nflverse play-by-play. Task: spot "highlight plays" (EPA > 1.5 or turnover or score). Metric: Precision/Recall of predicted timestamps matched within ±2 s of the nflverse play start. Baseline: the stage-1 visual detector alone with confidence thresholding.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
**Adopt** the two-stage pipeline for the clip workflow only if, on the 10-game test set, the denoised pipeline achieves ≥80% recall at ≥60% precision on highlight plays AND beats the detector-alone baseline by ≥20 percentage points of precision at matched recall. **Reject** if the nflverse prior adds <10 pp precision (i.e., the visual detector is already sufficient, or the prior doesn't transfer to football's stop-start structure) — in which case keep detector-only timestamping.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Go beyond the paper's learned tactical priors: since the NFL has *ground-truth* play-by-play (unlike soccer's annotation bottleneck), invert the architecture — train the denoiser **directly supervised** on (noisy detector output → nflverse play labels) as a sequence-labeling task, and add an audio channel (crowd noise energy as a weak excitement signal, which FOOTPASS lists as future work). Test whether audio + structured priors push precision past the 60% gate on the hardest subset (plays where the acting player is off-screen at the broadcast cut). This turns the NFL's structured-data advantage into the prior the soccer paper had to learn.
