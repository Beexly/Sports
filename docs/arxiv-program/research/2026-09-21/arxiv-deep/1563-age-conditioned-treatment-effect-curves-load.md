# [1563] Estimating the age-conditioned average treatment effects curves: An application for assessing load-management strategies in the NBA (arXiv:2402.12400)

**Citation:** Shinpei Nakamura-Sakai, Laura Forastiere, Brian Macdonald (2024). *Estimating the age-conditioned average treatment effects curves: An application for assessing load-management strategies in the NBA*. arXiv:2402.12400. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12400
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (ar5iv HTML).
**Verdict:** ADAPT

the Age-Conditioned Treatment Effect (ACTE) meta-learner framework is directly portable to GSE's NFL/NBA rest-and-schedule adjustments; adopt the X-learner + honest-RF design and the bootstrap-CI age-curve protocol, re-fit on GSE game-level data with rest-day/schedule treatment definitions.

## 1. Research question
How does the causal effect of rest days (load management) on NBA player performance vary by player age, and can game-level data plus causal machine learning (meta-learners) produce more granular, causally identifiable age curves than the traditional season-level regression age-curve literature? The paper answers both the methodological question (how to estimate Age-Conditioned Treatment Effects, ACTE) and the applied one (does rest between games improve NBA performance, and for whom?).

## 2. Dataset / schema
NBA game-level data, ten seasons 2011-2022, obtained through the `hoopR` R package (public: hoopR by Gilani, pulls from ESPN/nba_api). After processing: 827 players; only player-games where the player was active >=25 minutes in the previous game (excludes minimal play). Age range analyzed: 18-39 (40+ excluded for sparsity; Table 2 shows b2b share ~14-16% for ages 19-35, dropping to ~10-13% at 38-39). Treatment: W=0 = back-to-back game (consecutive days), W=1 = at least one day of rest. Treatment probability in data: 0.151 (15.1% back-to-backs), also used in the simulation. Outcomes: net/offensive/defensive ratings; box score stats per 100 possessions (points, assists, total rebounds, steals, blocks, turnovers); shooting (FG%, 3P%, TS%). Covariates: player identity, team, opponent team, home/away, season fixed effects. Public access via hoopR; no proprietary data. Code availability: none stated in paper.

## 3. Method / model
Meta-learner framework (Künzel et al. 2019) under Rubin's potential outcomes. Estimand: ACTE τ(a) = E[Y(1)−Y(0) | A=a] = μ₁(a) − μ₀(a), where μ_w(a) = E_X[μ_w(a,x)] is the Age-Conditioned Expectation Function (ACEF). Three meta-learners compared: S-learner (single μ^obs(a,x,w) with treatment as feature; τ̂(a) = μ̂₁(a) − μ̂₀(a)), T-learner (separate μ̂₀^obs, μ̂₁^obs on control/treated samples), X-learner (fit response functions per arm; impute pseudo-effects D̃¹ᵢ = Yᵢ^obs − μ̂₀^obs(Aᵢ,Xᵢ) for treated, D̃⁰ᵢ = μ̂₁^obs(Aᵢ,Xᵢ) − Yᵢ^obs for control; regress each on (a,x); final τ̂(a) = e(a)·τ̂⁰(a) + (1−e(a))·τ̂¹(a) with propensity e(a) = Pr(Wᵢ=1|Aᵢ=a)). Base learners: OLS-spline (fixed-effect spline, the best performer in Schuckers et al. 2023's age-curve simulation) and honest random forest via the Rforesty package (honesty = separate subsamples for training/prediction to limit overfitting). Confidence: 90% bootstrap CIs per age; ACEF curves smoothed with GAM (df=6) per Schuckers et al. 2023. X-learner results reported only with honest RF (X-learner + OLS-spline would mirror T-learner + OLS-spline given the covariate context). Simulation study (3 scenarios: constant, linear, nonlinear τ(a)) selects the meta-learner × base-learner combo before the real application.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
- Potential-outcome decomposition (Eq. 3): Yᵢ(w) = g(a,w) + fᵢ(x,a,w) + εᵢ, with E_X[f(x,a,w)|A=a] = 0; hence τ(a) = g(a,1) − g(a,0).
- ACTE (Eq. 2): τ(a) := E[Yᵢ(1) − Yᵢ(0) | A=a] = μ₁(a) − μ₀(a) = E_X[μ₁(a,x) − μ₀(a,x)].
- Identification (Theorem 1): under SUTVA + unconfoundedness, τ(a) = E_X[E[Yᵢ^obs|Wᵢ=1,Aᵢ=a,Xᵢ=x]] − E_X[E[Yᵢ^obs|Wᵢ=0,Aᵢ=a,Xᵢ=x]]. (Proof in Appendix A of paper.)
- X-learner weighting: τ̂(a) = e(a)·τ̂⁰(a) + (1−e(a))·τ̂¹(a), e(a) := Pr(Wᵢ=1|Aᵢ=a).
- Simulation DGP: g(a,w) = ω + β₁(a−a_max)² + β₂(a−a_max)²·1(a>a_max) + β₃(a−a_max)³·1(a>t_max) + τ(a)·w, with β₁=−1/9, β₂=−6/1000, β₃=45/10000; σ_β=0.02, σ_ε=1, σ_γ=0.4; scenarios τ(a)=2; τ(a)=0.1(a−a_min); τ(a)=2(a−16)+0.0005·1(a>20)(a−a_max)³−0.0005·1(a>a_max)(a−a_max)⁴.
- Assumptions: SUTVA (no interference between units; one version of treatment — consistency), and unconfoundedness (Yᵢ(0),Yᵢ(1)) ⊥⊥ Wᵢ | Xᵢ. Age range restriction to 18-39 assumed sufficient overlap.

## 5. Features / target
Features (covariates X): player ID (fixed effect), team, opponent team, home/away indicator, season fixed effect, player age a. Target: game-level performance outcomes — net rating, offensive rating, defensive rating; box score stats per 100 possessions (points, assists, total rebounds, steals, blocks, turnovers, fouls); FG%, 3P%, TS%. Prediction horizon: none (causal/associational estimation at game level, not forecasting); the derived object is the age-conditioned curve τ(a).

## 6. Validation design
No train/validation/test split in the usual forecasting sense (causal estimation, not prediction). Validation is by (a) simulation study with known ground truth: 3 scenarios × 6 (meta-learner × base-learner) combos, scored by MSE of τ̂(a) vs true τ(a) by age (Table 1); (b) 90% bootstrap CIs on real NBA data, with non-overlap of treated/control CI bands (ACEF) or CI-excluding-zero (ACTE) as significance evidence; (c) cross-learner consistency (S/T/X learners, OLS-spline vs honest RF) as a robustness check. Splits: not time-ordered; the honest RF uses sample splitting internally. Baselines: the six meta-learner/base-learner combinations are compared against each other; no external causal baseline (e.g., IPW, doubly robust) is reported.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Simulation MSE (Table 1, exactly as printed): Scenario 1 (constant τ): s.ols 0.00, t.ols 1.53, x.ols 1.53, s.rf 0.35, t.rf 0.05, x.rf 0.07. Scenario 2 (linear τ): s.ols 0.44, t.ols 1.44, x.ols 1.44, s.rf 0.29, t.rf 0.07, x.rf 0.08. Scenario 3 (nonlinear τ): s.ols 89.23, t.ols 9.40, x.ols 9.40, s.rf 9.90, t.rf 4.17, x.rf 3.74. Conclusion: X-learner + RF best for complex effects; S-learner + OLS best for simple parallel-trend effects. NBA application (qualitative, from figures; no numeric effect sizes tabulated): net rating significantly improved by rest across most ages 20-38; rest affects defense more than offense (ACTE larger in absolute value on defensive rating); steals the only box-score stat with consistent positive rest effect; FG% and TS% improved by rest mainly for younger players; 3P% least affected. 827 players; treatment prevalence 0.151 (back-to-back share ~15.1%).

## 8. Code / data availability
Data: via the public hoopR R package (Gilani). No code repository link stated in the paper; base-learner implementation references the Rforesty package (Künzel et al.). No replication script URL given.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Unconfoundedness is untestable and likely violated: rest decisions are strategic — coaches rest stars against weak opponents and play them in big games, so "rest" correlates with opponent quality beyond the included fixed effects; injury status, practice intensity, travel, and sleep are unobserved confounders. No-interference (SUTVA) is suspect: a teammate's rest changes a player's role/usage. Age 18 and 40-42 have near-zero treated samples (Table 2: 0-5 b2b games), so tails of τ(a) are unidentified; 90% bootstrap CIs there are noise. Survivorship bias: only players who survive to older ages appear there (e.g., blocks U-shape driven by Tim Duncan's outlier seasons, Table 3 — Duncan holds 8 of top-10 blocks/100 at age 35+). The ±25-minute-previous-game filter conditions on past playing time, which is downstream of rest decisions (potential collider). No external validation (no other league/season holdout; splits not time-ordered). OLS-spline S-learner assumes uniform treatment effect without treatment×covariate interactions, which the paper itself flags as a strong intrinsic bias. Effect sizes are graphical only — no tabulated numeric ACTE values, making external comparison hard.

## 10. GSE overlap
GSE has no rest/schedule causal adjustment layer today; the existing-research map lists "causal injury impact — player-level causal injury effect estimation (synthetic controls on QBs/OL)" as a gap (gap #9), and causal inference is only a "brief topic" in the 15-area ML brief with results pending. Garrett's CEPT (Baxley Causal E-Process Theory) is a publication/theory lane, not a prediction-serving causal-adjustment pipeline — no duplication. FineCausal (2503.23911) is absorbed but is trajectory/diffusion-focused, not a rest-effect estimator. The hoopR/nba_api data route and the nflverse analog (NFL play-by-play + roster/injury + schedule data) give GSE the exact game-level covariates (player, team, opponent, home/away, season) needed. This paper is new capability, not duplicate: GSE can port the ACTE design to "rest days / short-week / travel" treatments on NFL player props and team spread/total models.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Build `gse-acte-rest`: (1) Data: nflverse play-by-play + weekly roster/injury (2016-2025), schedule (days since last game, travel miles, short-week flags, altitude), player age from birthdate. Unit = player-game. Treatment definitions: W=1 short rest (≤3 days since last game, TNF short week) vs W=0 normal rest; second treatment: W=1 travel >2000 miles vs 0. Outcomes: EPA/play, success rate, fantasy points per game, snap-weighted; team-level: offensive/defensive EPA. (2) Covariates: player, team, opponent, home/away/neutral, season+week fixed effects, opponent strength (Elo), weather. (3) Model: X-learner with honest RF base (or GSE's existing gradient-boosted stack with honest subsampling) + S-learner spline baseline; propensity model on age+covariates for e(a). (4) Simulation sanity gate first: replicate paper's 3-scenario DGP with NFL-like treatment prevalence (~TNF short weeks ≈ 6% of games) to confirm X-learner+RF recovers τ(a). (5) Serving: precompute τ̂(a) curves per position group; at prediction time, adjust player projections by age-specific rest/travel treatment effect when the treatment differs from league-average conditions; also feed τ̂ into injury-risk/workload overlay. Effort: ~2-3 engineer-days for the prototype, 1 week with bootstrap CIs and backtest harness. Stack: Python (econml for meta-learners, honest RF via grf/causal-forest or sklearn with manual honesty split).

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: nflverse 2019-2024 regular seasons, player-game rows with >=10 snaps in previous game, WR/RB/TE positions, age 21-36. Treatment: W=1 if days_rest <= 4 (short week incl. TNF) vs W=0 if days_rest >= 6. Outcome: half-PPR fantasy points per game (standardized by position). Baseline to beat: naive two-sample comparison (rest vs short-week mean difference by age) and GSE's current rest-agnostic projection. Metric: simulation MSE on a held-out synthetic τ(a) DGP mirroring the paper's scenario 3 (nonlinear), plus on real data the width/significance of 90% bootstrap CIs and directional consistency of τ̂(a) across S/T/X learners. Window: 2019-2024 seasons pooled; leave-one-season-out for stability.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT the ACTE rest-adjustment into GSE projections only if: (a) on the synthetic DGP, X-learner+RF recovers the true nonlinear τ(a) with MSE at least 30% below the naive difference-in-means estimator; (b) on real NFL data, the 90% bootstrap CI for at least one position group's ACTE excludes zero over a contiguous age band ≥3 years AND the estimated direction matches domain sense (short rest hurts skill-position fantasy output); (c) cross-learner sign agreement: S/T/X agree on sign in ≥70% of age bins with n≥200 games. Otherwise reject — fall back to simple age×rest interaction terms in the existing models.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Upgrade the binary treatment to a continuous dose-response: replace W∈{0,1} with days-of-rest as continuous dose and estimate an age-conditioned dose-response curve τ(a,d) via a generalized propensity score / DR-learner for continuous treatments. Test whether the marginal benefit of the 4th vs 3rd rest day differs by age (older players benefit more from days 5-7; younger players saturate earlier) — the paper's binary design cannot see this. If the dose-response is monotone and age-heterogeneous, GSE gets a graded rest adjustment (e.g., +x% projection bump per rest day, age-specific) instead of a binary short-week flag — strictly more useful for TNF/MNF/bye-week edges.
