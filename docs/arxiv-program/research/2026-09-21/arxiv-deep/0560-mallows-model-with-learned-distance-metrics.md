# [0560] Mallows Model with Learned Distance Metrics: Sampling and Maximum Likelihood Estimation (arXiv:2507.08108)

**Citation:** Alimohammadi, Y., & Asgari, K. (2025). *Mallows Model with Learned Distance Metrics: Sampling and Maximum Likelihood Estimation*. arXiv:2507.08108. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08108
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 17440 lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — a learnable upset-shape parameter α for team-ranking distributions is a genuine new capability for GSE (modeling how often long-shot upsets occur in rankings); port the MLE for α/β/σ to NFL poll-and-market rankings, but the model explains ranking dispersion, not matchup outcomes, so keep it in the poll-aggregation/tournament-simulation role.

## 1. Research question
Can the Mallows ranking model be generalized so that the *distance metric itself is learned from data*, rather than fixed a priori (e.g., Kendall's τ)? The paper proposes the L_α-Mallows model with d_α(π,σ) = Σ_i |π(i)−σ(i)|^α, develops an FPTAS sampler and a joint MLE for (α, β, central ranking σ₀), proves consistency, and validates on college football/basketball power rankings.

## 2. Dataset / schema
- College football power rankings 2019–2021 (Massey Ratings composites of weekly expert power rankings): >4,100 observed rankings. Top-10 set: Georgia, Alabama, Michigan, Cincinnati, Baylor, Ohio State, Oklahoma State, Notre Dame, Michigan State, Oklahoma; also a 100-team evaluation set.
- College basketball power rankings 2020–2021: >1,700 observed rankings. Top-10: Gonzaga, Illinois, Baylor, Michigan, Alabama, Houston, Ohio State, Iowa, Texas, Arkansas; also 100 random teams.
- Sushi preference dataset (5,000 human rankings over 10 sushi types).
- Synthetic data: n=15 items, true (α₀,β₀)=(1.5,0.5), samples from the paper's truncated sampler at truncation k=9; and (α₀,β₀)=(1,1) with 50 training samples.
- Splits: temporal split — train on earlier rankings, hold out final sixth for evaluation; 50 independent trials (football: 800 train/250 test; basketball: 700 train/150 test); sushi 80/20 random splits × 25 trials.

## 3. Method / model
Two-step MLE: (Step 1) estimate the central ranking σ̂ by minimizing Σ_l d_1(π⁽ˡ⁾, σ) — shown (Lemma 2.1) to be consistent for any true α̃>0 — which reduces exactly to a minimum-weight perfect matching on a bipartite graph with edge weights Σ_l |π⁽ˡ⁾(i) − j| (Proposition 2.2). (Step 2) with σ̂ fixed, estimate (α,β) by zeroing the gradient equations Ψ_m(α,β;σ̂) = 0 (empirical vs model-expected distance and its α-derivative), solved with differential evolution; model expectations E[d_α] = −∂log Z_n/∂β, β·E[ḋ_α] = −∂log Z_n/∂α evaluated via the FPTAS sampler. Sampling: Lemma 3.2 proves marginal assignment probabilities decay geometrically for α≥1: P_{α,β}(π(i)=j) ≤ c(α,β)^{|j−i|} for |j−i|≥k, with c(α,β)<1 monotone decreasing in α and β; the only explicit closed form given is c(1,β) ≤ 2e^{−2β}/(1+e^{−2β}) (the α>1 case follows from monotonicity). Truncate the matrix A_n[i,j] = e^{−β|i−j|^α}·1{|i−j|≤k} with k = log(n/ϵ)/(log((1+e^{−2β})/(2e^{−2β}))) giving TV distance ≤ ϵ and permanent within relative error ϵ (Lemma 3.3). [QC 2026-09-21: removed a previously inserted closed-form Γ-expression for c(α,β) with α>1 — it did not appear in the source; the paper gives the explicit bound only for α=1.] Dynamic-programming sampling (App. C.3–C.5): n+1 layers, states as 2k-bit availability vectors (C(2k,k) states/layer, ≤k predecessors); the forward DP and backward sampling both run in O(nk·C(2k,k)); correctness proved by induction in C.4, with DP[n][s_n]=per(A_n^{(k)}) and sampling probability Π_j A_n^{(k)}[j,π(j)]/per(A_n^{(k)}). Total: partition function within (1−ϵ) multiplicative factor and ε-close samples for any α≥1, β>0 in O(n^{1−2/log(C)}ϵ^{2/log(C)}log(n/ϵ)) (Theorem 3.1).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Copied faithfully:
- L_α distance: d_α(π,σ) := Σ_i |π(i) − σ(i)|^α.
- Mallows law: P(π) = exp(−β d(π,σ))/Z, P_{α,β,σ}(π) = e^{−β d_α(π,σ)}/Z_n(β,α), Z_n(β,α) = Σ_{π∈S_n} e^{−β d_α(π,σ)}.
- Log-likelihood: L_m(α,β,σ) = −(β/m) Σ_l d_α(π⁽ˡ⁾,σ) − log Z_n(β,α).
- Score: Ψ_m(α,β;σ̂) = (−(1/m)Σ d_α + E[d_α(Π,σ̂)], −(1/m)Σ ḋ_α + E[ḋ_α(Π,σ̂)]), with ḋ_α(π,σ) = Σ_i |π(i)−σ(i)|^α log|π(i)−σ(i)|.
- Central-ranking error bound (Theorem 2.4): P(σ̂_m ≠ σ₀) ≤ n!·exp(−m/(3n⁴)); with α̃ = n/3, improves to ≤ n!·exp(−m/(54n²)) (Remark 2.5).
- Asymptotic normality: √m((α̂−α₀),(β̂−β₀)) →_d N(0, I_{α₀,β₀}⁻¹) with Fisher matrix [[β²Var[ḋ], βCov[ḋ,d]],[βCov[ḋ,d], Var[d]]] (Theorem 2.4); same holds for approximate score if sup‖Δ‖ = o(m^{−1/2}) (Theorem 2.6).
- Truncation: k = log(n/ϵ)(log((1+e^{−2β})/(2e^{−2β})))⁻¹; per(A_n^{(k)}) approximates Z within relative error ϵ; DP in O(n·C(2k,k)); per-sample O(nk).
- Assumptions: i.i.d. rankings from a Mallows(α₀,β₀,σ₀); estimation theory assumes α>0, β>0; the FPTAS requires α≥1 (paper notes α<1 sampling is open). Intuition: larger α penalizes long-range swaps more.

## 5. Features / target
Inputs: collections of full rankings (ordinal lists over n teams/items) from expert power-ranking sources. No per-team features — the model has only (α, β, central ranking) as parameters. Target: held-out rankings, evaluated via top-1/top-5 hit rate, Spearman ρ, Kendall τ, Hamming distance, pairwise accuracy.

## 6. Validation design
Temporal train/test splits (train on earlier rankings, hold out final sixth) for the two sports datasets — no random cross-validation over time, so no temporal leakage by construction. 50 independent resampled trials with confidence intervals; synthetic-data recovery of known (α,β) with deliberate truncation mismatch (train at k=6 vs data generated at k=9); robustness check varying truncation k. Baselines: classical Mallows with Kendall's τ distance and the Plackett–Luce model (Appendix D): PL MLE via L-BFGS on L(θ)=Σ_lΣ_i[θ_{π⁽ˡ⁾(i)} − log Σ_{j≥i} exp(θ_{π⁽ˡ⁾(j)})] (Eq. 12), Gumbel-Max sampler π=arg sort(−(θ+ε)), ε~Gumbel(0,1)^n (Eq. 13); Mallows-τ closed form Z(β)=Π_j(1−e^{−jβ})/(1−e^{−β}), σ₀ by Borda count, exponential insertion sampler O(n²). Metrics (App. D): Top-k hit rate = Pr(π(1)∈{σ(1)..σ(k)}) (Eq. 11), Spearman, Kendall τ, Hamming H=(1/n)Σ1{π(i)≠σ(i)}, pairwise accuracy; 100 Monte Carlo samples per model. Estimation theory (App. B): σ̂ consistent by uniqueness of the inversion-swap minimizer + uniform convergence over finite S_n (Lemma 2.1); (α̂,β̂) consistent via Hadamard–Caccioppoli global invertibility of f_α (Lemmas B.1–B.3) + van der Vaart Thm 5.9; asymptotic normality via Thm 5.23; Thm 2.6: approximate-score estimate keeps the same limit if sup‖Δ‖=o(m^{−1/2}).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Adversarial notes: (a) headline numbers are held-out but "top-10 teams" were pre-selected using known end-of-season polls (bleacherreport2022apcfp, collegepollarchive2021ap) — survivorship/selection bias: teams outside the top 10 that would have populated the tail are excluded, inflating hit rates; (b) expert power rankings are human-judgment inputs that already encode outcomes — predicting them back is a poll-aggregation exercise, not a test of predicting game results; (c) the striking 100-team results have α̂≈0.002, which the authors admit means "the model adaptively flattens the penalty" — but α→0 makes d_α approach a constant-per-disagreement metric, i.e., the model degenerates toward a uniform-ish weighting, so "learning the metric" nearly learned it away; (d) evaluation metrics like Hamming distance on n=100 rankings are saturated (0.973–0.986) and uninformative; (e) NFL transfer: the paper has no NFL data; the NFL has 32 teams and far fewer ranking sources per week, so the "tall" ranking corpus (4,100 football rankings from Massey's many contributors) does not exist for the NFL; (f) the paper's own stated limitation bites exactly in the regime GSE cares about: the FPTAS sampler requires α ≥ 1, and "developing computationally efficient sampling for α < 1 remains open" (Limitations and Future Work) — yet college football's estimated α̂ ≈ 0.44 is below 1, so sampling/estimation in the most sports-relevant regime runs on the approximate score machinery without the sampler's guarantees. Any GSE port must check that the α̃=1 two-step MLE and the truncation sampler behave at estimated α̂ < 1 before trusting them in the NFL lane.

## 10. GSE overlap
New capability, not a duplicate. GSE's metrics inventory lists Elo, Glicko (mentioned), TrueSkill (mentioned), Bradley–Terry, Plackett–Luce, Massey/Sagarin/Colley — but no Mallows-model work and no learned ranking-dispersion modeling. The ML brief area 5 lists "learning-to-rank" as commissioned but with no results in the repo. The L_α-Mallows family gives GSE two things nothing in the corpus has: (1) a single parameter α characterizing a sport's upset profile (long-range vs local swap frequency), and (2) a sampler for generating full-team-ranking distributions. Relevant to GSE's poll-aggregation and NFL power-ranking consensus work.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
1. Data: weekly NFL power rankings from multiple public sources (ESPN, PFF, SumerSports, benbbaldwin-style objective ratings as ordinal rankings, market-implied rankings from odds via benbbaldwin tiers) 2018–2026 → 32-team rankings, ~15–20 sources/week. 2. Fit L_α-Mallows via the paper's two-step MLE using the published code as reference; estimate α̂, β̂, σ̂ per season/week. 3. Use α̂ as the sport-league's upset-profile parameter: compare NFL α̂ against the paper's football (0.44) and basketball (1.10) benchmarks — does the NFL look more like football (frequent long-range upsets) or basketball? 4. Sample ranking distributions for Monte Carlo season-simulation priors: generate perturbed consensus rankings as inputs to playoff-probability models. 5. Guard: validate only on rankings derived from pre-kickoff information (no post-game polls) if used near betting. Effort: ~1 week (data assembly dominates; the MLE is off-the-shelf from the GitHub repo).

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: NFL weekly power rankings 2022–2025 (ESPN/PFF/market-implied, 32 teams). Train L_α-Mallows on weeks 1–17 of each season 2022–2024, hold out 2025 season rankings. Metrics: top-5 hit rate and Kendall's τ on held-out rankings (same metrics as the paper). Baselines: (a) fixed-α Mallows with Kendall's τ; (b) Plackett–Luce. Pass criterion stated in §13.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
Adopt L_α-Mallows for GSE's NFL ranking-aggregation lane if, on the 2025 held-out season, it beats both baselines by ≥2 percentage points on pairwise accuracy AND by ≥5 percentage points on top-5 hit rate, in at least 3 of 4 seasons when repeated in a rolling setup. Reject if α̂ estimates are unstable across seasons (SE > 0.3) or if the fixed-τ Mallows matches it within 1 pp — then the learned metric adds nothing for the NFL's 32-team, few-source ranking corpus.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Replace the single global α with team-specific or cluster-specific α (e.g., playoff-tier vs mid-tier vs bottom-tier teams may have different displacement profiles — bottom-tier teams in the NFL move more). The paper's FPTAS proof is for a single α; a mixture-of-α Mallows (one α per tier) would let GSE model the empirically obvious fact that #28-vs-#32 swaps are cheap while #1-vs-#5 swaps are rare. Test whether a 3-tier α model beats single-α on held-out Kendall's τ, penalizing the extra parameters with a held-out likelihood-ratio test.
