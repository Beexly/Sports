**Verdict:** ADAPT — the first time-series entry in this lane and its design fits NFL structure unusually well: autoregressive per-step conditional diffusion with covariates *known for the whole forecast horizon* (day-of-week, lags, categorical embeddings) — the NFL schedule, rest days, and venues are exactly such known-future covariates. The paper's task is forecasting, not synthesis, so the adaptation is to run the same per-step conditional diffusion as a *generator* of synthetic multivariate stat trajectories (team-week EPA/success/pace panels, within-game state sequences) rather than as a forecaster.

## 1. Research question
Can energy-based models (specifically denoising diffusion, Ho et al. 2020) replace the tractable-but-restrictive emission distributions (Gaussian, low-rank copula, normalizing flows) in multivariate probabilistic time-series forecasting? The technical questions: does a per-time-step conditional diffusion model, autoregressively conditioned on an RNN hidden state, beat copula/flow/VAE baselines on CRPS_sum across thousands of correlated dimensions; and does the EBM form avoid the failure modes of flows (spurious density between disconnected modes) and VAEs (continuous-to-disconnected mapping) on real-world series?

## 2. Dataset / schema
Six real-world multivariate datasets (Table 1), preprocessed as in Salinas et al. 2019a: Exchange (8 series, ℝ+, daily, 6,071 steps, predict 30), Solar (137, ℝ+, hourly, 7,009, predict 24), Electricity (370, ℝ+, hourly, 5,833, predict 24), Traffic (963, (0,1), hourly, 4,001, predict 24), Taxi (1,214, ℕ, 30-min, 1,488, predict 24), Wikipedia (2,000, ℕ, daily, 792, predict 30). Covariates: time-dependent (day-of-week, hour-of-day), time-independent embeddings, and lag features by frequency — all known for the forecast period.

## 3. Method / model
Factorization (Eq. 8): q(x_{t0:T}^0 | x_{1:t0−1}^0, c_{1:T}) = Π_{t=t0}^{T} q(x_t^0 | x_{1:t−1}^0, c_{1:T}). Temporal encoder (Eq. 9): h_t = RNN_θ(concat(x_t^0, c_t), h_{t−1}) — 2-layer LSTM, h_t ∈ ℝ^40. Each factor learned by a *conditional* DDPM: ε_θ(√ᾱ_n x_t^0 + √(1−ᾱ_n)ε, h_{t−1}, n) (Eq. 7 simplified), Σ_θ = β̃_n. ε_θ is a conditional 1-D dilated ConvNet (WaveNet/DiffWave style): 8 residual blocks, 8 residual channels, gated activation σ(·)⊙tanh(·), bidirectional dilated convolution with dilation 2^{i%2}, 8 skip connections summed; noise index n encoded by Transformer Fourier positional embeddings into ℝ^32 (N_max=500). N=100 diffusion steps, linear β schedule 1e−4→0.1. Training (Algorithm 1): random context+prediction windows, batch 64, Adam 1e−3, early stopping on validation. Scaling (§3.3): each series divided by its context-window mean (or 1 if zero) — the Salinas et al. 2019b trick, reported as significantly improving performance. Inference (Algorithm 2): annealed Langevin sampling per step, autoregressive rollout, S=100 trajectories for empirical quantiles. Single Nvidia V100 16GB.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
DDPM backbone (Eqs. 1–7, following Ho et al. 2020): forward q(x^n|x^{n−1}) = N(x^n; √(1−β_n)x^{n−1}, β_n I); closed-form q(x^n|x^0) = N(x^n; √ᾱ_n x^0, (1−ᾱ_n)I) (Eq. 3); reverse p_θ(x^{n−1}|x^n) = N(x^{n−1}; μ_θ(x^n,n), Σ_θ I) (Eq. 1) with μ_θ(x^n,n) = (1/√α_n)(x^n − (β_n/√(1−ᾱ_n))ε_θ(x^n,n)); training objective E‖ε − ε_θ(√ᾱ_n x_t^0 + √(1−ᾱ_n)ε, h_{t−1}, n)‖². Sampling: x_t^{n−1} = (1/√α_n)(x_t^n − (β_n/√(1−ᾱ_n))ε_θ(x_t^n, h_{t−1}, n)) + √Σ_θ z (Algorithm 2). Metric: CRPS(F,x) = ∫(F(z) − 1{x≤z})²dz; CRPS_sum = E_t[CRPS(F̂_sum(t), Σ_i x_{i,t}^0)] — proper scoring rule (de Bézenac et al. 2020), used instead of likelihood since not all baselines yield analytic distributions. Assumptions: (1) covariates known for all forecast steps; (2) the autoregressive factorization captures cross-series dependence entirely through h_{t−1} + the joint per-step diffusion (no explicit cross-series graph); (3) context-mean scaling suffices for series with order-of-magnitude scale differences (Traffic Fig. 4).

## 5. Features / target
Per dataset (§2); targets are the future values of all D series. NFL translation (my inference): entities i = teams (D=32) or team-game stat streams; time steps = weeks (season trajectory) or plays/drives (within-game trajectory); covariates c_t = schedule-derived and known in advance (opponent, home/away, rest days, dome, week number, bye) + categorical embeddings of team identity (the paper's §3.4 categorical-embedding trick); the multivariate vector x_t^0 = per-team weekly EPA margin, success rate, pace — a 32-dim joint distribution per week capturing correlated outcomes (if KC blows out LV, both teams' stats move jointly — exactly the cross-entity dependence the paper targets).

## 6. Validation design
CRPS_sum on rolling-window forecasts vs 11 baselines: classical (VAR, VAR-Lasso, GARCH, VES) and deep (KVAE, Vec-LSTM-ind-scaling, Vec-LSTM-lowrank-Copula, GP-scaling, GP-Copula, Transformer-MAF). TimeGrad metrics are mean ± standard error over 10 independent retrainings. Ablation (Fig. 3): diffusion steps N ∈ {2,…,256} on Electricity — N≈10 already near-optimal, N≈100 optimal, larger not beneficial. No synthesis-quality metrics (it's a forecasting paper); uncertainty via 100 sampled trajectories.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Adversarial notes: (1) It's forecasting, not synthesis — the paper never generates unconditional trajectories; the NFL synthesis use requires re-purposing the conditional model as a generator (sample with teacher-forced history from real seasons, then roll forward). (2) Autoregressive rollout compounds error over long horizons (30 steps tested; an 18-week NFL season is within range, but a full play-by-play sequence is not). (3) No discrete/categorical emission modeling — Taxi/Wiki counts are treated as continuous; NFL needs discrete outcomes (scores, covers) — combine with a discrete head or the lane's categorical machinery (2067). (4) Cross-entity dependence is only via shared h_{t−1} and the joint per-step noise — no explicit team-interaction structure (a GNN extension is the paper's own suggested future work, §6). (5) Sampling cost: N=100 Langevin steps × horizon × S=100 trajectories is heavy; the N≈10 ablation mitigates it. (6) Exchange (the smallest dataset, 8 series) is the one loss — small-D regimes are not its strength; the NFL's D=32 is closer to Solar/Electricity where it wins. External validity to NFL: high for trajectory synthesis — the known-covariate design matches the NFL's fully-known schedule, and CRPS_sum is directly reusable as the proper scoring rule for GSE's own multivariate probabilistic forecasts (e.g., the joint distribution of all Week N spreads).

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Build plan (effort: ~5 engineer-days; first sequential-synthesis prototype):
1. Data: nflverse weekly team stats 2015–2024 → panel of 32 teams × 18 weeks × K metrics (EPA/play margin, success rate, pace, pressure rate). Context = weeks 1–12, prediction = weeks 13–18.
2. Covariates c_t (all known in advance, per §3.4): opponent embedding, home/away, rest days, dome/outdoor, week index, bye flag — categorical features via learned embeddings as the paper does.
3. Model: 2-layer LSTM (h=40, or scaled up) + conditional dilated-ConvNet ε_θ; N=100 steps (ablate down to ~10 per Fig. 3); context-mean scaling per team-metric.
4. Training: random context+prediction windows across seasons (never split a season across train/test — season-level splits only).
5. Use as generator: seed with real weeks 1–12 of a season, roll out 100 synthetic weeks-13–18 trajectories per team; the joint 32-team weekly vectors preserve correlated outcomes (blowouts move both teams).
6. Feed synthetic trajectories as augmentation for the engine's late-season spread model.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: nflverse team-week panel 2015–2022 train, 2023–2024 held-out (season-level splits). Baseline: Vec-LSTM-lowrank-Copula analog (or the engine's current late-season model) forecasting weeks 13–18 from weeks 1–12. Challenger: TimeGrad trained per §11. Metrics: (a) CRPS_sum over the 32-team joint weekly vector on 2023–2024 (the paper's own metric); (b) downstream: engine spread-model log-loss on weeks 13–18 trained on real vs real+TimeGrad-synthetic trajectories; (c) calibration of the 50%/90% intervals (Fig. 4 style) on held-out weeks.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT TimeGrad-trajectory augmentation if: (a) CRPS_sum on 2023–2024 weeks 13–18 beats the copula baseline by ≥5% relative (the paper's Taxi/Wiki margins were 23–36%; we require a fraction), AND (b) real+synthetic spread log-loss beats real-only by ≥0.003 on held-out late-season weeks, AND (c) the 90% empirical intervals achieve 85–95% coverage (calibration sanity — the paper shows intervals but doesn't report coverage; we require it). REJECT if autoregressive rollout error compounds (CRPS_sum degrades vs baseline by week 18 by >20% relative to week 13) or if N=100 sampling makes trajectory generation >10× the cost of the tabular backbones without meeting gate (a). Gate set before running; 10 retrainings for se, per the paper.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Graph-conditioned emission: the paper's §6 suggests GNNs "when the relationships between entities are known" — the NFL *has* a known relationship structure (the week's matchup graph: who plays whom). Replace the shared-h_{t−1} cross-team dependence with a per-week matchup-graph attention layer inside ε_θ: each team's denoising step attends to its opponent's noisy state (and division rivals' states) at the same diffusion step. Test: does matchup-graph conditioning beat the paper's shared-hidden-state conditioning on CRPS_sum for weeks 13–18, especially on head-to-head-correlated metrics (margin, total)? If yes, this becomes the standard architecture for any multi-team joint generative model at GSE.
