# [0294] Training-Free Long-Term Multi-Object Tracking for Sports Video Analytics (arXiv:2608.15688v1)

**Citation:** Stanczyk, T., Yoon, S., & Bremond, F. (2026). *Training-Free Long-Term Multi-Object Tracking for Sports Video Analytics*. Inria / Université Côte d'Azur. arXiv:2608.15688v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15688v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (2134-line text extract).
**Verdict:** ADOPT — McByte++ is the best training-free, deployable pipeline for player tracking from broadcast video. GSE's video/content operation needs exactly this (All-22/coaches-film player tracks without annotated training data).

## 1. Research question
Sports multi-object tracking (MOT) fails on long-term identity: players are occluded, leave the frame (camera cuts), get substituted, and reappear; most trackers terminate identities and produce fragmented trajectories. The authors extend McByte (their 2025 training-free tracker) into McByte++, a training-free tracking-by-detection framework that adds (a) online re-identification invoked only at new-tracklet initialization, (b) conditional camera motion compensation (CMC) with spatial downscaling, and (c) lightweight mask propagation (EdgeTAM replacing heavier Cutie), while keeping the "no training, no tuning" philosophy and achieving an order-of-magnitude speed increase.

## 2. Dataset / schema
Benchmarks: SportsMOT (basketball, volleyball, soccer sequences; diverse indoor/outdoor views, strong camera motion; test split), SoccerNet-tracking 2022 (soccer broadcast video, continuous camera motion; oracle detections on test split; 25 FPS), SoccerNet-tracking Challenge 2023 (no provided detections; authors use YOLOX trained on SportsMOT; eval server was unavailable during prep, so comparisons limited to McByte variants). Metrics: HOTA (detection + localization + association), IDF1 (identity preservation), MOTA (detection accuracy, interpreted cautiously under oracle detections). Detections: YOLOX pre-trained per dataset, fixed confidence threshold 0.6, no retraining or per-sequence tuning.

## 3. Method / model
Built on ByteTrack (high/low-confidence detection staging, IoU association, Kalman filter propagation, Hungarian matching). Three new components: (1) **Regulated mask-guided association**: EdgeTAM propagates per-tracklet segmentation masks; mask cues enter the cost matrix only in *ambiguity* (similar IoU scores across row/column) or *isolation* (all IoU costs above threshold) cases, and only when the mask is visible, mask fill ratio mf ≥ 0.05, and bbox coverage mc ≥ 0.90 (fixed across all datasets); cost update costs^{i,j} = costs_IoU^{i,j} − mf^{i,j} when conditions satisfied (Eq. 3), else costs_IoU^{i,j}. (2) **Selective online re-ID**: appearance features from a pre-trained OSNet backbone (no fine-tuning), ℓ2-normalized (Eq. 4), temporally aggregated at tracklet level (Eqs. 5–6); invoked only when a new tracklet initializes — cosine similarity (Eq. 7) against a long-term identity memory of terminated tracklets; identity transfer only on mutual best-match with similarity ≥ 0.8. (3) **Conditional CMC**: global camera motion as affine transform from ORB feature matching on frames downscaled ×4 (ds=4; original used ×2); compensation applied only when the estimated transform is physically plausible (no degenerate scaling/translation); most recent reliable estimate retained, stale estimates not applied. CMC applied after Kalman prediction, before association.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Eq. 1: mc^{i,j} = |mask(tracklet_i) ∩ bbox_j| / |mask(tracklet_i)| (bbox coverage, gating only). Eq. 2: mf^{i,j} = |mask(tracklet_i) ∩ bbox_j| / |bbox_j| (fill ratio, cost modifier). Eq. 3: costs^{i,j} = costs_IoU^{i,j} − mf^{i,j} if conditions (1)–(3) satisfied, else costs_IoU^{i,j}. Eq. 4: f̃_t = f_t / ‖f_t‖₂. Eq. 5: f_avg = (1/N)Σ f̃_i; Eq. 6: f̃_avg = f_avg / ‖f_avg‖₂. Eq. 7: sim(f̃^{(p)}, f̃^{(m)}) = f̃^{(p)} · f̃^{(m)} (cosine on ℓ2-normalized vectors). Assumptions: ByteTrack IoU + Kalman baseline sufficient for short-term; affine model sufficient for global camera motion; re-ID embeddings reliable only from full/partial crops in favorable frames (hence exclusion from frame-level matching); mutual best-match at sim ≥ 0.8 is a safe identity-transfer criterion; fixed thresholds (0.6 detection, mf 0.05, mc 0.90, sim 0.8) generalize across sports without tuning.

## 5. Features / target
Features: per-frame YOLOX detections (boxes, confidence); EdgeTAM-propagated masks; ORB-estimated affine camera motion; OSNet appearance embeddings from bbox crops. Targets: long-term player identities with consistent IDs across occlusions/camera exits; association quality measured by HOTA/IDF1 on sports benchmarks. Runtime measured in FPS. Horizon: whole sequences (long temporal gaps, minutes of absence).

## 6. Validation design
Ablations on SoccerNet 2022 test (Table 1) and SportsMOT test (Table 2): mask-module swap (Cutie→EdgeTAM, dagger variant), CMC strategy (unconditional vs conditional) × downscale ds ∈ {2,4,6}, re-ID similarity threshold ∈ {0.6,0.7,0.8,0.9}, offline GTA-link post-processing. State-of-the-art comparison under identical detections (Tables 4–5) vs ByteTrack, OC-SORT, McByte (training-free) and with-own-detections trained methods (GTR, CenterTrack, MeMOTR, MOTIP, MotionTrack, DiffMOT, Deep-EIoU, SportMamba). Segmentation-based comparison on SportsMOT validation (Table 6): DEVA, Grounded SAM 2, MASA under original and YOLOX settings. No training, identical parameters everywhere.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
SoccerNet 2022 (Table 1): McByte 85.0 HOTA / 79.9 IDF1 / 96.8 MOTA @ 1.04 FPS; McByte++ no re-ID (cond CMC, ds=4): 84.1 / 78.9 / 97.1 @ 10.71 FPS; + online re-ID (sim 0.8): **87.5 / 84.5 / 97.1 @ 8.69 FPS**; + offline GTA: 88.6 / 87.2 / 97.1 @ 7.46 FPS. SportsMOT (Table 2): McByte 76.9 / 77.5 / 97.2 @ 3.60 FPS; McByte++ re-ID 0.8: **79.9 / 83.6 / 96.9 @ 14.57 FPS** (+4.1 HOTA, +7.6 IDF1 over no-re-ID 75.8/76.0); GTA offline: 81.5 / 86.0. Challenge 2023 (Table 3, YOLOX SportsMOT detections): McByte 64.1 / 76.5 @ 1.46 FPS; McByte++ re-ID: 64.3 / 78.6 @ 11.13 FPS (≈7.6× speedup). SOTA (Tables 4–5): McByte++ online best among tracking-by-detection under identical detections (SoccerNet: 87.5/84.5 vs OC-SORT 82.0/76.3, ByteTrack 72.1/75.3); SportsMOT 79.9/83.6 vs Deep-EIoU 77.2/79.8, SportMamba 77.3/77.7. Mask-based (Table 6, SportsMOT val): McByte++ 85.5/87.6 vs Grounded SAM 2 w/ YOLOX 66.1/70.2, MASA 73.6/71.2. Re-ID threshold 0.8 dominates 0.6/0.7/0.9 on HOTA+IDF1 both datasets; ds=4 is the accuracy–speed sweet spot; re-ID gains leave MOTA unchanged (association-only effect).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
(a) No NFL/American-football data: soccer/basketball/volleyball — 22-player broadcast soccer is the closest, but NFL has tighter bunching at the line, more occlusion density, and distinct uniforms/pads. (b) re-ID was ablated but only on two datasets; sim=0.8 fixed across sports is a claim of generalization the paper supports empirically but does not bound. (c) Challenge-2023 comparisons are McByte-vs-McByte only because the eval server was down — no independent external validation there. (d) Author's own follow-up analysis (Stanczyk & Bremond 2025, ref [39]) shows re-ID can degrade frame-level association — their selective-invocation design dodges this, but the threshold remains a single point of failure for identity merges (0.6 caused false merges of similar-looking teammates). (e) Runtime 8.7–14.6 FPS on benchmark hardware — near-real-time but not live 60fps; hardware unspecified. (f) SAM3 could not be evaluated (VRAM limits) — mask-based SOTA comparison is incomplete against the newest generation. (g) No detector training, but still dependent on YOLOX pre-trained per sport.

## 10. GSE overlap
New capability, high priority. The corpus has no broadcast-video tracking work: the computer-vision lane is thin (only the video-grounding papers in this wave, none deployed). Garrett's operation needs player tracks from broadcast/All-22 for telestrated content (per the standing video rule: real footage, seconds-long clips) — McByte++ is the deployable path: training-free, no per-league tuning, runs on standard hardware, code forthcoming. NGS tracking is licensed; this gives an in-house alternative for play-level trajectory extraction from video Garrett already has (YouTube-sourced footage, coaches film). Duplicate check: none — no MOT/tracking paper read in depth; SportsGrounder (this wave, paper 8) is video *reasoning*, not identity tracking; they are complementary, not overlapping.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: 50 NFL broadcast/All-22 plays with manual player-ID ground truth across camera cuts (Garrett's existing footage library). Metric: IDF1 and ID-switch count vs ByteTrack baseline with identical detections; runtime FPS. Window: one week's worth of plays.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT for the content/analytics pipeline if McByte++ reduces ID switches vs ByteTrack by ≥40% at ≥8 FPS on the 50-play set; REJECT if IDF1 is within 5 points of ByteTrack (no meaningful long-term gain) or FPS < 5 on available hardware.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Go beyond the paper by adding a *team-color/number-OCR prior* to the re-ID stage: NFL uniforms have high-contrast jersey numbers; an OCR-on-crop confidence score as a hard constraint on the mutual best-match (only allow identity transfer when number-OCR agrees or is inconclusive, never when it contradicts) would directly address the paper's failure mode of merging visually similar teammates — a much bigger problem in football than soccer given 22 same-uniform players bunching at the line of scrimmage.
