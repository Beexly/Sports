# [0363] SoccerChat: Integrating Multimodal Data for Enhanced Soccer Game Understanding (arXiv:2505.16630)

**Citation:** Gautam, S., Midoglu, C., Thambawita, V., Riegler, M. A., Halvorsen, P., Shah, M. (2025). *SoccerChat: Integrating Multimodal Data for Enhanced Soccer Game Understanding*. arXiv:2505.16630. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16630
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (text extract, 2562 lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — the video-instruction-tuning recipe (QA taxonomy, jersey-color anonymization, joint multi-dataset training, LLM-as-judge scoring) is a portable blueprint for building a GSE soccer/NFL video-understanding QA system; the SoccerChat weights themselves are soccer-specific and not directly usable. Key transferable finding: joint training on SoccerChat+XFoul beat sequential fine-tuning on both classification and referee tasks, and strong general pretraining (Qwen2-VL) beat narrowly fine-tuned X-VARS — pretraining strength dominates narrow fine-tuning data.

## 1. Research question
Can a multimodal conversational AI framework that integrates visual (video), auditory (ASR commentary), and textual (captions) data improve soccer video understanding — specifically action/event classification and referee decision-making QA — relative to general-purpose vision-language models and narrow referee-only models?

## 2. Dataset / schema
- **SoccerNet-v2** (Deliège et al. 2021): 500 full-length matches from major European leagues, ~764 hours of footage; ~300,000 manually annotated events across 17 action classes; camera-shot segmentation (replay vs real-time). Source of clips and event labels.
- **SoccerNet Dense Video Captioning** (Mkhallati et al. 2023): timestamped textual captions from online game portals; player/team names referenced.
- **SoccerNetEchoes** (Gautam et al. 2024): ASR transcripts of human commentary (noisy; may reference off-screen elements).
- **Jersey color annotations** (home/away per game): used to replace real player/team names in captions (e.g., "red-jerseyed team") so the model generalizes to event dynamics rather than memorizing names.
- **Derived SoccerChat instruction dataset:** 90,834 event clips extracted (±5 s window centered on SoccerNet-v2 actions, no camera transitions, ≤10 s); 12,827 valid paired-event clips (consecutive events 1–7 s apart); after caption/commentary alignment (captions matched within +3 s to +10 s after event; replay-segment ASR filtered), **49,120 question-answer pairs** total. Clip pool with text: 10,615 single-event + 2,982 paired-event clips. Long descriptions 250–300 words generated with GPT-3.5 Turbo; two QA types: overview-based (high-level narrative) and detail-based (paired-event before/after facts). All artifacts released: dataset, fine-tuned weights for all variants, evaluation code, intermediate generations/scores (https://github.com/simula/SoccerChat).

## 3. Method / model
SoccerChat = **Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct** (ViT ~675M params + Qwen2 LLM; Naive Dynamic Resolution mapping images to visual tokens; M-RoPE decomposing positional embeddings into temporal/spatial components) fine-tuned on the instruction dataset. Adaptations: per-patch max dimension 28×28, ≤128 visual tokens/frame (16:9 → 112 patches); **24 frames per segment at 2.4 FPS** → 24×112 = **2,688 tokens per 10-s segment** (§4.1.2). Audio not directly processed (Qwen2-VL has no audio path); ASR enters only as text. Six variants evaluated: **SoccerChat** (fine-tuned on the instruction set), **SC+XF** (joint training with XFoul), **SC-FT-XF** (sequential: SoccerChat then XFoul fine-tune), **Q2VL** (base Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct baseline), **Q2VL-XF** (X-VARS public checkpoint trained on XFoul), **X-VARS** (fine-tuned solely on XFoul — referee baseline).

## 4. Equations / algorithms
No closed-form equations; the "math" is token-budget arithmetic and the training-dataset design. Token budget per segment (§4.1.2): frames = 24, patches per 16:9 frame ≈ 112 ≤ 128 limit → total tokens = 24 × 112 = 2,688 per segment. Evaluation via the **QwQ Scorer** model assigning scores 0–10 for alignment with ground truth (0 = no alignment, 10 = perfect alignment and justification), visualized as grouped violin plots; classification tasks additionally report weighted precision/recall/F1, Cohen kappa, MCC, Hamming loss.

## 5. Key results
**XFoul QA validation (referee decision-making), mean QwQ score** (§5.1): Q2VL-XF **6.81** (best), SC+XF **6.46**, SC-FT-XF 6.14, SoccerChat 3.37, X-VARS **2.91**, Q2VL 2.58. Takeaways: models trained on XFoul dominate; joint SoccerChat+XFoul training (SC+XF) beats sequential fine-tuning (SC-FT-XF); **X-VARS, despite exclusive XFoul training, scored only 2.91 — comparable to the untrained Q2VL at 2.58 — because it lacks a strong general pretraining foundation**; standard deviation across XFoul-trained models ≈ 2.6.
**Six-class action classification** (Ball out of play, Foul, Goal, Shots off target, Shots on target, Throw-in), mean QwQ score (§5.2.1): SoccerChat **6.80** (75th percentile 10), SC+XF 5.97, Q2VL 3.44, SC-FT-XF 3.40, X-VARS 3.26, Q2VL-XF 2.72. Table 1 weighted F1: SoccerChat **0.57**, SC+XF 0.49, Q2VL 0.17, X-VARS 0.19, SC-FT-XF 0.12, Q2VL-XF 0.05. Confusion matrix (Fig. 5): strong on corners, penalties, yellow cards; struggles direct vs indirect free-kicks and shots on vs off target.
**Sixteen-class classification** (§5.2.2): SoccerChat **6.42**, SC+XF 6.15, SC-FT-XF 4.00, Q2VL-XF 3.88, Q2VL 2.72, X-VARS 2.70. Authors interpret SC-FT-XF's drop as catastrophic forgetting from sequential foul fine-tuning.

## 6. Strengths
First soccer-specific video-instruction dataset of this scale (49,120 QA pairs) with a reproducible pipeline: clip extraction → consecutive-event pairing → caption/commentary alignment → GPT-3.5 structured descriptions → QA generation. Jersey-color anonymization is a clever generalization trick preventing name memorization. Full artifact release (dataset, all variant weights, eval code, intermediate generations). Clean ablations separating data composition (SoccerChat vs XFoul) from training strategy (joint vs sequential), with a memorable, decision-relevant result: joint training > sequential; strong pretraining > narrow fine-tuning.

## 7. Limitations / threats to validity
Evaluation rests on an **LLM-as-judge (QwQ Scorer)** rather than human raters — scores inherit the judge's biases, and no human-correlation validation is reported. ASR transcripts are noisy and sometimes reference off-screen content; the filtering heuristics are ad hoc. QwQ scores are reported as means/violins without confidence intervals or significance tests. Referee-task evaluation is confined to the XFoul validation set; no live/referee-human agreement baseline. Soccer-only: zero cross-sport evidence (compare paper 0364's generalization critique). Frame sampling fixed at 24 frames/2.4 FPS regardless of event speed.

## 8. Implementation spec (how GSE would apply it)
1. Replicate the **instruction-dataset pipeline** for NFL broadcast video: replay-aware clip extraction (±5 s windows around event labels, ≤10 s, no camera transitions), consecutive-event pairing, caption/commentary alignment — but swap jersey-color anonymization for **team-color / home-away anonymization** to keep analysis team-agnostic. 2. Fine-tune a current-generation video LLM (Qwen2-VL-7B class or newer) on the resulting instruction set; per the paper's finding, **train jointly on all task datasets from the start** — never sequentially fine-tune on a narrow task afterward. 3. Adopt the **two-type QA taxonomy** (overview-based for clip narrative, detail-based for before/after facts) as the labeling schema, since GSE's content pipeline needs both highlight narration and factual Q&A. 4. Evaluate with an LLM-judge score plus human spot-checks (the paper's judge-only evaluation is the gap to close).

## 9. Experiments / tests to run
- Reproduce the joint-vs-sequential training comparison on a small NFL clip instruction set (e.g., 500 QA pairs): does joint training win the same way? - Measure LLM-judge/human correlation before trusting QwQ-style scoring for GSE QA. - Test the 24-frames-at-2.4-FPS budget against denser sampling for fast NFL events (long-developing routes vs quick hits); the fixed budget may undersample rapid events. - Ablate jersey-color-style anonymization: does team-name anonymization actually improve generalization, or does it destroy useful team-identity signal for content? - Evaluate catastrophic forgetting when a soccer-trained video model is instruction-tuned on NFL — mirroring the XFoul sequential-fine-tune result.

## 10. Relation to existing research
The existing-research map (2026-09-21) has **no prior coverage** of SoccerChat, SoccerNet video QA, XFoul/X-VARS, or soccer video instruction tuning — this is new ground for the corpus. It connects to: the **15-area ML research brief (2026-09-18)**, whose "multimodal fusion" and "frontier-model techniques" topics anticipated exactly this class of work (results not yet in repo); papers **0359/0360** (Wave 4 — tennis annotation factory and tennis MLLM analysis), which provide the complementary annotation-taxonomy and MLLM-scoring precedent (0360's structured-CV-outputs-as-text + edit-score lesson pairs directly with 0363's QA-generation pipeline); and the **NGS replacement spec**, since broadcast-video event understanding is a candidate source for recreating tracking-adjacent metrics.

## 11. Improvement path
Replace the judge-only evaluation with human-rated calibration of the scorer; add audio-stream modeling (the current model ignores audio despite the paper's "multimodal" framing — ASR text is a workaround, not true audio integration); extend to full-match long-horizon reasoning instead of ≤10-s clips; test cross-sport transfer (train soccer, probe on NFL or vice versa) to quantify the generalization tax the survey paper 0364 warns about; and add uncertainty/confidence outputs to QA responses for downstream betting-adjacent use.

## 12. Verdict reasoning
**ADAPT.** The SoccerChat weights and soccer taxonomy don't transfer to NFL directly, but the paper's two durable, decision-grade contributions do: (1) a complete, reproducible recipe for turning broadcast sport video into a 49k-pair instruction dataset — the same "labeling factory" pattern ledger 0359 established for tennis, now with the QA-generation layer added; (2) a clean empirical rule with teeth: joint multi-dataset training beats sequential fine-tuning, and general pretraining strength beats narrow domain fine-tuning (X-VARS 2.91 vs Q2VL 2.58 is the cautionary tale). Both rules directly shape how GSE should train any video-understanding model. REJECT is wrong because the pipeline and training-strategy findings are sport-agnostic.

## 13. Risks / open questions
- LLM-judge evaluation without human correlation: are the 6.80 vs 5.97 gaps real or judge noise? - ASR/commentary noise: how much of the QA quality depends on noisy transcripts hallucinating off-screen context? - Catastrophic forgetting measured only via sequential XFoul fine-tuning — does the same happen with NFL instruction data, and can replay/mixing prevent it? - No latency or deployment numbers: a 7B video LLM with 2,688 tokens/segment is expensive for real-time broadcast use. - Rights: SoccerNet footage is research-licensed; a GSE pipeline needs licensed or owned NFL footage.
