# [1564] The Causal Effect of the Two-For-One Strategy in the National Basketball Association (arXiv:2412.08840)

**Citation:** Prateek Sasan, Daryl Swartzentruber (2024). *The Causal Effect of the Two-For-One Strategy in the National Basketball Association*. arXiv:2412.08840. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08840
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (ar5iv HTML).
**Verdict:** ADAPT

the play-by-play causal protocol (covariate-balancing propensity scores + IPW/matching for ATE/ATC + causal forest + RATE heterogeneity tests) is a reusable template for quantifying causal impact of NFL tactical decisions (go-for-it on 4th, onside kick, hurry-up) on game outcomes; the sports-causal-methods pipeline matters more to GSE than the TFO finding itself.

## 1. Research question
Does the NBA "two-for-one" (TFO) end-of-quarter strategy — shooting early with 35-43s left to secure two possessions to the opponent's one — causally improve a team's quarter-end score differential, and is the effect heterogeneous across game contexts/lineups? Secondary: a methodological demonstration of IPW + propensity matching + causal forests + RATE on sports play-by-play data.

## 2. Dataset / schema
NBA play-by-play data, regular seasons 2018-19 (last pre-COVID season) and 2021-22 (first full post-COVID season), via the `nbastatR` R package (public). Treatment: TFO opportunity (TFO-O = possession gained with 35-43s left in quarter) → TFO-A (attempt: shot or shooting foul with ≥28s left) vs TFO-NA (non-attempt: held ball past 28s mark without shot/foul). Turnovers, jump balls, violations during TFO-O excluded (uncategorizable). Counts: 2018-19: 1036 TFO-A / 529 TFO-NA; 2021-22: 950 TFO-A / 462 TFO-NA; combined 1986 A / 991 NA. Only first three quarters (4th-quarter behavior differs). Outcome: Post Opportunity Difference (POD) Yᵢ = Yᵢ,EOQ − Yᵢ,TFO-O (score-differential change from opportunity to quarter end). Covariates: quarter, time left (s), score margin, max/mean on-court player ratings for team and opposition (NBA 2K ratings via HoopsHype), betting spread and total (Sportsbook Reviews archive), starting lineups (Basketball Reference) → on-court lineups. Data sources public except NBA 2K ratings scrape (HoopsHype).

## 3. Method / model
Potential-outcomes framework; estimands: ATE (Eq. 2), ATT (Eq. 3), ATC (Eq. 4); paper estimates ATC (twice as many treated as controls, so matching ATC natural; IPW estimated ATC too for consistency). Propensity scores via covariate-balancing propensity score (Imai & Ratkovic 2013) — chosen for balance, not just fit. ATE via IPW (R: WeightIt + survey) and 1:1 optimal pair matching without replacement (R: MatchIt, marginaleffects with cluster-robust SEs via g-computation). Assumptions stated and checked: consistency, no interference (Yᵢ(Wᵢ,W₋ᵢ)=Yᵢ(Wᵢ); discussed season-long practice spillover as unlikely), conditional exchangeability ((Yᵢ(0),Yᵢ(1)) ⊥⊥ Wᵢ|Xᵢ), positivity (0<P(Wᵢ=wᵢ|Xᵢ)<1). Heterogeneity: causal forest (Athey/Wager, R grf): DML orthogonalization (residualize Y,W on X), heterogeneity-maximizing splits Δ(s)=(τ̄_L−τ̄_R)², honest estimation (τ̂(l)=|l|⁻¹Σ ỸᵢW̃ᵢ/(ê(Xᵢ)(1−ê(Xᵢ)))), B-tree ensemble averaging. Heterogeneity tests: test calibration (regress outcome on mean forest prediction + differential forest prediction; p-value on the latter) and RATE (Yadlowsky et al. 2024): TOC(q)=E[Y(1)−Y(0)|S(Xᵢ)≥F⁻¹(1−q)]−E[Y(1)−Y(0)], RATE=∫₀¹TOC(q)dq; train causal forest on 2018-19, predict on 2021-22 for the RATE prioritization rule. Also variable-importance pruning: keep covariates with cumulative 95% importance (dropped 3 variables).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
- POD: Yᵢ = Yᵢ,EOQ − Yᵢ,TFO-O (Eq. 1).
- ATE/ATT/ATC: E[Yᵢ(1)−Yᵢ(0)], E[Yᵢ(1)−Yᵢ(0)|Wᵢ=1], E[Yᵢ(1)−Yᵢ(0)|Wᵢ=0] (Eqs. 2-4).
- No interference: Yᵢ(Wᵢ,W₋ᵢ)=Yᵢ(Wᵢ) (Eq. 5). Conditional exchangeability: Yᵢ(0),Yᵢ(1) ⊥⊥ Wᵢ|Xᵢ (Eq. 6). Positivity: 0<P(Wᵢ=wᵢ|Xᵢ)<1 (Eq. 7).
- Propensity: e(X)=P(W=1|X) (Eq. 8). IPW weights: Wᵢ/ê(Xᵢ)+(1−Wᵢ)/(1−ê(Xᵢ)) (Eq. 9); ATE estimator Eq. 10; ATT Eq. 11; ATC Eq. 12.
- Matching ATT: τ̂_ATT = n₁⁻¹Σ_{i:Wᵢ=1}(Yᵢ−|M(i)|⁻¹Σ_{j∈M(i)}Yⱼ) (Eq. 13).
- CATE: τ(x)=E[Yᵢ(1)−Yᵢ(0)|X=x] (Eq. 14).
- Causal forest: residualization Ỹᵢ=Yᵢ−μ̂(Xᵢ), W̃ᵢ=Wᵢ−ê(Xᵢ); split Δ(s)=(1/|L|Στ̂(Xᵢ)−1/|R|Στ̂(Xᵢ))²; honest leaf τ̂(l)=1/|l|Σ ỸᵢW̃ᵢ/(ê(1−ê)); ensemble τ̂(Xᵢ)=B⁻¹Σ_b τ̂_b(Xᵢ).
- TOC/RATE: TOC(q)=E[Y(1)−Y(0)|S(Xᵢ)≥F_S(Xᵢ)⁻¹(1−q)]−E[Y(1)−Y(0)] (Eq. 15); RATE=∫₀¹TOC(q)dq (Eq. 16), "RATE lies between 0 and 1" per paper (true only for bounded outcomes/standardized rule).
- All four causal assumptions stated explicitly; SUTVA discussed per sub-component.

## 5. Features / target
Features for propensity model (Table 1): quarter (period), time left in quarter (s), score margin, max player rating (team), max player rating (opposition), mean player rating (team), mean player rating (opposition), betting spread, betting total. Augmented for causal forest with: max-rating difference, mean-rating difference, |score margin|. Target: Post Opportunity Difference (POD), change in score differential from TFO-O to end of quarter. Horizon: within-quarter (seconds). Treatment: TFO-A vs TFO-NA conditional on TFO-O.

## 6. Validation design
No train/test split for ATE estimation; estimand estimated per season (2018-19, 2021-22) and pooled (n≈2977). Validation: (a) covariate balance via Love plots (cobalt): standardized mean differences vs 0.1 rule-of-thumb before/after IPW and matching — 5 covariates above 0.1 pre-adjustment (time left = 0.074, not above 0.1; paper lists time left separately), IPW brings all near 0, matching leaves time left slightly above 0.1; (b) positivity via propensity-score overlap histograms (complete overlap except a couple control units); (c) IPW vs matching cross-method consistency; (d) causal-forest ATE (0.61, SE 0.11) vs IPW/matching as third check; (e) heterogeneity: test calibration + RATE with train-on-2018/predict-on-2021 split; RATE bootstrap SE with 200 bootstrap samples. Splits not time-ordered for the main estimate (pooled), but the RATE test uses a forward-season split. Baselines: naive difference-in-means would be confounded; paper compares IPW vs matching vs causal-forest ATE as mutual baselines.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
ATC estimates (Table 2, exact): IPW — 18-19: 0.55 (0.43, 0.66), p<0.001; 21-22: 0.57 (0.45, 0.69), p<0.001; both: 0.55 (0.47, 0.64), p<0.001. Matching — 18-19: 0.60 (0.35, 0.85), p<0.001; 21-22: 0.64 (0.39, 0.89), p<0.001; both: 0.63 (0.46, 0.80), p<0.001. Interpretation: each unconverted TFO-O costs slightly more than half a point of score differential. Causal forest ATE: 0.61 (SE 0.11). Test calibration (Table 3): mean forest prediction 1.00511 (SE 0.17876, p<0.001); differential forest prediction 0.63352 (SE 0.67520, p=0.1741) → no significant heterogeneity. RATE: 0.035 (SE 0.112, 200 bootstraps) → no heterogeneity. RATE by rating diffs (Table 4): max-rating diff 0.028 (0.085); mean-rating diff 0.0825 (0.087) — both non-significant. Sample: 1986 TFO-A, 991 TFO-NA combined.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
TFO-O→TFO-A labeling uses outcomes within the opportunity window (shot/foul timing) that are partly determined by unobserved intent/skill — coaches' play-calling ability is an unmeasured confounder (good coaches both attempt TFO and score). Player ratings from NBA 2K are subjective video-game ratings, not objective ability measures. No tracking/spatial data (authors' own limitation) — shot difficulty unobserved. Two seasons only (~3000 opportunities) → heterogeneity tests underpowered (authors admit). Conditioning on TFO-O (35-43s window) is conditioning on a game state partially caused by prior team behavior; turnovers excluded from both arms (could be differential — teams attempting TFO may turn it over more on rushed shots). Quarter-only exclusion of 4th quarters loses the highest-leverage situations. The RATE "0 to 1" claim is only valid under bounded outcomes. The ATE≈0.55 POD is an average over quarters 1-3; external validity to late-game/4th-quarter TFO (where it matters most) untested. R-package dependence (WeightIt, MatchIt, grf, marginaleffects, cobalt, survey) — no Python replication.

## 11. GSE implementation spec

(5) Serving: precomputed ATC tables by situation; content: "the data says going for it here is worth +X WP" — supports GSE's real-time call content and the X engagement mandate. Effort: 3-5 engineer-days per decision type; shared pipeline makes the 2nd+ cheap (~1 day each).

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: nflverse 2018-2024 play-by-play, 4th-down plays in "go territory" (4th & 1-5, between own 40 and opponent 30), quarters 1-3. Treatment: go-for-it (W=1) vs punt/FG (W=0). Outcome: ΔEP (expected points added from the decision to end of drive, using nflverse EP). Baseline to beat: raw difference-in-means and the conventional 4th-down-bot recommendation frequency. Metric: ATC with 95% CI via IPW and matching; require both methods' CIs to exclude zero and agree in sign, plus Love-plot max standardized difference <0.1 post-weighting. Window: 2018-2024 pooled; hold out 2024 season for the RATE-style train/predict heterogeneity check.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT the causal-tactics pipeline if: (a) on 4th-down go-for-it, IPW and matching ATC estimates both exclude zero with the same sign and |ΔEP| ≥ 0.3 points per decision; (b) post-weighting balance: all covariates' standardized mean differences <0.1 (matching the paper's diagnostic bar); (c) causal-forest ATE within the IPW 95% CI (third-method consistency as in the paper). Otherwise reject — tactical causal claims stay out of public content and GSE keeps descriptive win-probability framing only.

## 14. Improvement experiment
The paper stops at average effects with no significant heterogeneity — but GSE can do better with scale: pool 7+ NFL seasons (~10x the paper's n) and test heterogeneity by quarterback tier and coach identity via RATE with a train-on-2018-2022/predict-on-2023-2024 split. If a "which coaches' go-for-it decisions beat the causal estimate" ranking emerges, GSE gets a proprietary coach-aggressiveness causal leaderboard — the paper's method, but with the sample size the authors said they lacked. Second upgrade: replace POD-analog ΔEP with a market-relative outcome (did the decision beat the closing spread expectation?) to connect the causal estimate directly to betting value.
