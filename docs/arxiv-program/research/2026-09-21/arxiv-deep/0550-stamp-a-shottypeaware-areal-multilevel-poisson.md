# [0550] STAMP: A shot-type-aware areal multilevel Poisson model for league-wide comparison of basketball shot charts (arXiv:2603.24015v1)

**Citation:** Kazuhiro Yamada, Keisuke Fujii (2026). *STAMP: A shot-type-aware areal multilevel Poisson model for league-wide comparison of basketball shot charts*. arXiv:2603.24015v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24015v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 3615 lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — the areal Poisson-with-exposure-offset hierarchical template ports directly to NFL (team × field-zone × play-type rate modeling per possession), even though the basketball shot-chart application itself is not GSE-relevant.

## 1. Research question
How to fairly compare basketball teams' shot-type-specific spatial tendencies league-wide: a unified model of team-level field-goal attempt counts across predefined court regions, seasons, and shot types that separates true stylistic tendency from differences in pace/exposure, with interpretable relative-rate maps and left-right bias summaries.

## 2. Dataset / schema
B.LEAGUE (Japan's men's professional basketball league) play-by-play, 2023–24 and 2024–25 seasons: 2,352 games, 306,444 field-goal attempts total (296,768 regular season = 96.84%; 9,676 postseason). Aggregation: 13 predefined court areas (left/right symmetric zones merged), 6 shot-type categories after merging similar labels (jump_shot 156,423; step_pull 50,454; lay_up 48,550; floater 19,000; rim_finishes 17,819; fade_turn 14,198), coarse side labels (Left: zones 6,7,11,12; Center: 1,2,5,10,13; Right: 3,4,8,9). Mean FG attempts per team per season 4,032.16 (sd 251.80). Possession defined shot-terminated: a possession ends at each field-goal attempt; an offensive rebound begins a new possession. Access: proprietary — B.LEAGUE play-by-play is viewable per-game on the official website but not available as open bulk data; the study used shot-location data provided for research by Data Stadium Inc. via the Institute of Statistical Mathematics (data-availability statement, §4.4). Not replicable from public sources.

## 3. Method / model
Latent Gaussian Poisson GLMM fitted with Integrated Nested Laplace Approximation (INLA) in R-INLA. Observation model: Y_{isadk} ~ Poisson(μ_{isadk}), log μ_{isadk} = log E_{is} + η_{isadk} (eq. 1), where E_{is} = possessions of team i in season s (exposure offset). Linear predictor (eq. 2): η = fixed effects (intercept β_0, season, court side, shot type) + team i.i.d. random effect u_i + area i.i.d. random effect v_a + seasonally-correlated team×area effect w_{ias} (equicorrelation structure across seasons) + season-specific team×side effect z_{ids} + shot-type-specific team×area seasonally-correlated random slopes r^{(m)}_{ias} weighted by shot-type loadings γ_m(k), included for jump shots and step-back/pull-up shots (M = 2 key categories in the main model). Sum-to-zero constraints identify all random-effect blocks (eqs. 3–7). Penalized Complexity (PC) priors on all variance/correlation hyperparameters, calibrated by prior predictive checks over grids U_{sd}, U_{slope} ∈ {0.5,0.8,1.0,1.2,1.5}; 7 admissible (U_sd, U_slope) settings retained (eq. 19), representative config (1.5, 1.0). One INLA fit ≈ 1 minute wall-clock. Posterior inference yields per-team×area×shot-type relative rates (log-rate contrasts vs league) and left-right bias summaries (log(Right/Left) of posterior multipliers, geometric-mean aggregated over seasons, with ~95% credible intervals).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
- Likelihood (eq. 1): Y_{isadk} ~ Poisson(μ_{isadk}); log μ_{isadk} = log E_{is} + η_{isadk}.
- Linear predictor (eq. 2): η_{isadk} = β_0 + β_s^{(season)} + β_d^{(side)} + β_k^{(shot_type)} + u_i + v_a + w_{ias} + z_{ids} + Σ_{m=1}^{M} γ_m(k) r^{(m)}_{ias}.
- Constraints (eqs. 3–7): Σ_i u_i = 0; Σ_a v_a = 0; Σ_{i,a,s} w_{ias} = 0; Σ_{d∈{Left,Right,Center}} z_{ids} = 0 (∀ i,s); Σ_{i,a,s} r^{(m)}_{ias} = 0 (∀ m).
- Stated assumptions: (i) counts are Poisson conditional on the latent Gaussian field — overdispersion is assumed to be absorbed by the random effects (authors explicitly reject adding a negative-binomial dispersion parameter as an identifiability risk); (ii) seasonally-correlated effects follow an equicorrelation (compound-symmetry) structure; (iii) left-right symmetric court areas can be merged; (iv) similar shot-type labels merged (fadeaway+turnaround; layup+driving layup; dunk+tip-in+alley-oop+hook; step-back+pull-up) to ensure sufficient counts per cell; (v) shot-terminated possession definition; (vi) regular season is representative training data for postseason evaluation.

## 5. Features / target
Inputs (fixed effects): season indicator, coarse side (Left/Center/Right), shot-type category. Exposure: team-season possession count (offset, log scale). Target: Y_{isadk} = field-goal attempt count for team i, season s, area a, side d, shot type k. The interpretable outputs are posterior relative-rate maps (team-level percentiles of posterior rates vs league per area) and left-right bias log-ratios — i.e., the random-effect contrasts, not raw counts.

## 6. Validation design
Train on regular-season possessions (296,768 shots), evaluate out-of-sample on postseason possessions (9,676 shots) — a time-ordered, deployment-like split. Primary criterion: Monte Carlo estimate of out-of-sample expected log predictive density, elpd_post^{MC}, computed by drawing posterior samples of the latent Gaussian field and averaging cell-wise log-likelihood contributions on postseason data. Secondary: LPML on regular-season fitting data (LPML_reg). Compared 16 structures toggling four random-effect blocks (use_ta, use_ts, use_jump, use_step), plus a 32-configuration extended-slope experiment (extra team-level slopes for lay_up, floater, rim_finishes, fade_turn), plus prior-sensitivity averaging over the 7 admissible PC-prior settings.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
- Full STAMP model (all four components on): elpd_post^{MC} = −3,383; LPML_reg = −49,967 (Table 1).
- No-random-effects baseline: elpd_post^{MC} = −6,581; LPML_reg = −130,617. Full-model gain: Δelpd_post^{MC} ≈ 3.2×10³ (§2.2).
- Intermediate structures: team×area only → −6,422 (−115,261); team×area + jump + step slopes (use_ts=0) → −3,385 (LPML −37,835), only ~2 units worse out-of-sample than the full model while substantially better on LPML — the team×side effect is marginal.
- Prior sensitivity: for every admissible prior setting, the best elpd_post^{MC} was always one of the two richest structures (use_ta=use_jump=use_step=1, use_ts ∈ {0,1}); Kendall's τ rank correlations between model rankings across priors > 0.97.
- Extended slopes: some configurations achieved slightly higher elpd_post^{MC} (e.g., best top-10 rows ≈ −3,284 to −3,352) but with extremely poor LPML_reg on the order of −5×10⁵ (overfitting). The best trade-off (floater + fade_turn extra slopes only, use_ts=0): elpd_post^{MC} ≈ −3.35×10³ (+3.0×10¹ vs full model) and LPML_reg improved by ~1.0×10³; authors retained the original full model for parsimony.
- Case studies: Utsunomiya Brex 2024–25 maps confirm their reputation as a 3-point-oriented team with strong above-the-break off-the-dribble 3PT preference; Nagoya Diamond Dolphins step_pull charts show season-to-season spatial-tendency shifts (percentile maps, no single headline numbers stated).
All numbers are the paper's stated claims (Tables 1–2, §2.2).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Be adversarial: (a) Postseason evaluation uses the same season's teams/arenas — no opponent-adjustment in the model, so "tendencies" conflate team style with the opponents and game scripts faced; the authors note this is a scouting tool, not a matchup model. (b) Frequency-only: made/missed outcomes are not modeled, so the maps say nothing about efficiency (the authors' own stated limitation §3.2; xR/xP extension proposed but not built). (c) Predefined 13-region aggregation discards fine within-region preferences (paint spots); the LGCP comparison is acknowledged. (d) Shot-type label merging and the shot-terminated possession definition are analyst choices that shape the exposure offset. (e) Equicorrelation across seasons is a strong, rigid temporal structure with only 2 seasons observed — seasonal correlation is barely identified. (f) External validity to NFL: basketball shot charts have 300k+ attempts; NFL play-type×zone cells are far sparser (a team runs ~1,000 plays/season), so partial pooling is even more necessary but some cells may be near-empty. (g) The postseason "out-of-sample" set is only 3.16% of the data (9,676 shots) — small relative to the 3.2×10³ elpd gain claimed; elpd scale is dominated by cell count.

## 10. GSE overlap
New capability. The existing-research-map has no spatial/areal count-modeling work: no Poisson GLMM, no INLA, no shot-chart-style spatial tendency modeling. Nearest neighbors are the Poisson/Skellam/Dixon-Coles mentions (rating/outcome distributions, not spatial) and the nflverse gse-lab metrics (efficiency stats, not hierarchical spatial models). This is an extension of GSE's modeling toolkit into areal hierarchical rate models — the basketball application itself does not duplicate anything, and the paper is not in the 64-ID dedup list.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Port the template to NFL offensive tendency modeling: 1. Data: nflverse play-by-play 1999–2026; define ~10 field zones (own 1–20, 21–40, 41–midfield, opp 40–21, red zone 20–11, goal-to-go ≤10, split by hash L/C/R → collapse to ~12 zones), play-type categories (deep pass, short pass, inside run, outside run, scramble, etc.); exposure = team-season (or team-game) offensive plays/drives as offset. 2. Model: same Poisson GLMM with team, zone, team×zone (seasonally correlated), team×hash-side, and play-type-specific random slopes for the high-variance categories (deep pass, outside run); PC priors; R-INLA or the `inlabru`/`brms` stack. 3. Outputs: per-team×zone×play-type relative-rate maps vs league → "tendency" features feeding the matchup model (e.g., opponent's deep-pass rate vs league when facing top-2-high coverage) and DFS game-script reads. 4. Second stage (per the authors' proposed extension): joint binomial submodel on success/EPA>0 per cell → xR/xP surfaces. 5. Effort: 2–3 days for data prep + zone definitions, 1 day for INLA spec, 1–2 days for map outputs and feature wiring; reuse is Python/R — recommend R-INLA or PyMC equivalent.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: nflverse 2021–2024 regular seasons (train) vs 2024 postseason + 2025 regular season (test). Metric: out-of-sample expected log predictive density (elpd) on held-out team×zone×play-type counts — same MC-elpd protocol as the paper. Baseline: fixed-effects-only Poisson (season + zone + play-type + offset, no random effects). Runnable: R script with R-INLA on the 12-zone aggregation; no charting data needed.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT the areal-Poisson tendency layer (and wire its relative-rate maps into matchup features) if the full hierarchical model beats the fixed-effects baseline by Δelpd ≥ 500 nats on the §12 holdout AND the team×zone relative-rate estimates are stable across the two test windows (Spearman ρ ≥ 0.7 between 2024-postseason and 2025-regular-season team-zone percentiles). REJECT if Δelpd < 500 or instability — fall back to raw empirical rates with shrinkage.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Add the authors' unimplemented second stage in one joint model: a binomial submodel on per-cell success (EPA > 0) sharing the team×zone random-effect structure via a correlated bivariate latent field, so "how often" (xR) and "how well" (xP) are estimated jointly with partial pooling across both. Then test whether matchup-adjusted xP differentials (team xP surface minus opponent-allowed xP surface) predict ATS margin better than the current EPA-based features — this would turn a descriptive tendency tool into a predictive edge.
