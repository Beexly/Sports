# [0402] Expected Hypothetical Completion Probability (arXiv:1910.12337v1)

**Citation:** Sameer K. Deshpande, Katherine Evans (2019). *Expected Hypothetical Completion Probability*. arXiv:1910.12337v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12337v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 1,030 lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — the completion-probability model is a reproducible recipe for the NGS completion-probability metric Garrett's taxonomy inventories but cannot reproduce, and the EHCP layer (did the QB throw to the most-catchable receiver?) is a genuinely new QB-decision metric for GSE; port it to modern Big Data Bowl tracking with a conditional (not marginal) imputation model and gradient-boosted catch model instead of BART.

## 1. Research question
On a given NFL passing play, did the quarterback throw to the receiver who was most likely to catch it? The paper introduces Expected Hypothetical Completion Probability (EHCP): for every receiver on every passing play, the expected completion probability *had the ball been thrown to him at each point in his route*, averaged over the unobservable post-throw covariates (receiver speed/separation at arrival, ball flight time) that are only measurable on actually-targeted receivers. This lets the authors evaluate routes and QB decisions by process rather than outcome (e.g., the Tolzien interception vs. the Kupp touchdown from Week 1, 2017).

## 2. Dataset / schema
- **NFL Big Data Bowl 2019 dataset**: tracking + play + game data from all **91 games of the first 6 weeks of the 2017 NFL season**, tracking at **0.1-second granularity** per play. Per player/ball per frame: timestamp, x (0–120 yd), y (0–53.3 yd), speed (yd/s), distance since prior frame, direction (0–360°), tagged events (snap, pass release, catch, tackle), player ID/name/jersey/team, frame ID, game ID, play ID. Derived: pairwise distances (receiver–ball, receiver–nearest defender), cumulative distance run. Play data: quarter, clock, down, distance, yardline, scores, penalty/special-teams flags, pass length, pass result (caught/incomplete/intercepted), play result, description. Game data (temperature, humidity, wind) collected but **not used**.
- Analysis sample: **N = 4,913 passes**. Access: public via https://github.com/nfl-football-ops/Big-Data-Bowl.

## 3. Method / model
Three-part framework:
1. **Completion-probability model**: logistic form log(P(y_i=1|x_i)/P(y_i=0|x_i)) = f(x_i), i.e., P = [1+e^{−f(x)}]^{−1}. Two estimators compared: (a) Bayesian logistic regression with independent N(0,1) priors on θ after Gelman et al. (2008) rescaling (continuous covariates to mean 0/SD 0.5, binary recentered), fit by MCMC in **Stan via rstan**; (b) **Bayesian Additive Regression Trees** (Chipman et al. 2010) with Linero (2018)'s sparsity-inducing prior (fit via `lbart(sparse=TRUE)` in R's BART package), which also yields posterior splitting probabilities for variable importance. Model selection by 10 random 75%/25% train/test splits on MSE, log-loss, misclassification — **BART wins on all three** and is used for EHCP.
2. **Imputation of unobservables**: for a hypothetical pass, covariates split into x*_obs (measurable up to throw time) and x*_miss (speed/direction at catch attempt; receiver/defender/ball pairwise distances at arrival; total distance run snap→catch; ball air time; changes in speed/separation/direction/distance while ball in air). Missing values are sampled from the **empirical (marginal) distribution over all observed passes** in the dataset.
3. **EHCP**: EHCP(x*_obs) = E_{x*_miss}[F(x*_obs, x*_miss)] ≈ (1/M) Σ_{m=1}^{M} F(x*_obs, x*^(m)_miss), computed per posterior draw of f to propagate model uncertainty; report posterior mean and 95% interval. Tracked per receiver over the play to find the max-EHCP receiver-time.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
- Logistic model (equation 1): log(P(y=1|x)/P(y=0|x)) = f(x); P(y=1|x) = [1 + e^{−f(x)}]^{−1}.
- Bayesian logistic: π(θ|y) ∝ π(θ)·p(y|θ); prior π(θ_j) = N(0,1) independently after Gelman-2008 rescaling.
- EHCP (equation 2): EHCP(x*_obs) = E_{x*_miss}[F(x*_obs, x*_miss)] ≈ (1/M) Σ_m F(x*_obs, x*^(m)_miss), where F(·) = [1+e^{−f(·)}]^{−1}.
- Evaluation metrics: squared error (y_i − p̂_i)²; log-loss −(y_i log p̂_i + (1−y_i) log(1−p̂_i)); misclassification 𝟙(y_i ≠ 𝟙(p̂_i ≥ 0.5)).
- Stated assumptions: (i) the catch indicator y_i is Bernoulli given x_i; (ii) x*_miss for hypothetical passes is drawn from the **marginal empirical distribution over all passes** — the authors call this "by far the simplest approach" and flag conditional modeling of x*_miss | x*_obs as future work; (iii) their "counterfactual" terminology is explicitly *not* causal-inference counterfactuals — they impute a midstream variable (intended receiver location at arrival) plus upstream/downstream variables; (iv) BART's sum-of-trees prior is taken as an adequate function prior for f; (v) Tables 2–3 (QB/receiver rankings) are explicitly "for illustrative purposes only" — six games per player, "we do not recommend extrapolating much from these results."

## 5. Features / target
Input features (exact list as given):
- At pass-throw time: receiver speed and direction; pairwise Euclidean, horizontal, and vertical distances between receiver, nearest defender, and ball; total Euclidean distance the receiver has run in the game up to that point.
- At catch-attempt time (unobservable for hypotheticals): same measurements again, plus changes in speed, separation, direction, and total distance traveled while the ball is in the air.
- Situational: snap-to-pass time, ball air time, snap-to-catch-attempt time, seconds left in half, down, distance/yards-to-go, whether offense is leading, and score-differential category (9+ points, 1–8 points, 0 points — discretization suggested by Mike Lopez).
Target: y_i ∈ {0,1}, whether the pass was caught. Secondary derived targets: per-receiver EHCP over the play; QB-level % of throws to the max-/min-EHCP receiver; receiver-level EHCP minus fitted completion probability (credit/blame for post-throw actions).

## 6. Validation design
Model selection: 10 independent 75%/25% train/test splits; both models fit per split; posterior predictive mean p̂_i evaluated by MSE, log-loss, misclassification (Table 1). Splits are random, not time-ordered (a limitation — see §9). Variable importance: posterior mean splitting probabilities from the sparse BART prior, plus single-covariate partial-dependence curves with 95% credible intervals (Figure 2, on the Kupp touchdown). No separate validation of the imputation step (unobservable by construction); EHCP illustrated on two plays with posterior histograms and 95% intervals.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Paper's claims (10-split averages, SDs in parentheses — Table 1):
- Bayesian logistic: MSE **0.099 (0.004)**, misclassification **0.138 (0.008)**, log-loss **0.332 (0.018)**.
- BART: MSE **0.086 (0.004)**, misclassification **0.113 (0.005)**, log-loss **0.289 (0.011)** — better on all three.
- Variable importance (posterior mean splitting probabilities): receiver speed at catch attempt **20.74%**; receiver–ball Euclidean distance at catch **17.29%**; total distance receiver traveled snap→catch **10.92%**; receiver–ball distance at throw **7.35%**; separation at catch **6.74%**. Negligible: speed snap→throw **0.27%**, separation at throw **0.08%**, distance run snap→throw **0.01%**. Partial-dependence: Kupp's completion probability rises with catch-time speed up to ~8 yd/s then plateaus; rises with separation; falls with total distance run.
- Kupp touchdown vs. Tolzien interception: forecasted completion posterior means **47% vs. 37.1%** (both <50%, overlapping posteriors, Kupp shifted right). EHCP at actual arrival: **65.1% vs. 59.0%**. Kupp's EHCP ~2 s earlier: **85.1%** (95% intervals nearly disjoint from arrival-time interval). Sammy Watkins had the play's highest EHCP **92.2%** at 1.5 s after snap (location C); Kupp 91.9% (85.5%, 96.8%) at the same time.
- Tolzien interception: Hilton's EHCP at arrival (4.3 s after snap) **59.0%**; ~2 s earlier **89%**; Donte Moncrief highest EHCP at 2.4 s after snap; 95% intervals Hilton [81.4%, 95.1%] vs. Moncrief [89.9%, 99.0%] — substantially overlapping. All receivers' EHCP at Hilton's arrival time hovered **40–60%**.
- Table 2 (min 100 passes; "illustrative only"): highest % throws to max-EHCP receiver — Winston 26.8% (least-EHCP 16.8%), Siemian 26.2%/16.9%, Goff 24.8%/19.2%; lowest — Wilson 13.2%/23.5%, Carr 15.1%/27.5%, Wentz 15.3%/27.5%.
- Table 3 (min 40 targets; EHCP vs fitted completion probability): Tate 64.9%/76.7%/**+11.8pp**; McCaffrey 65.1%/75.0%/**+9.9**; Brown 60.2%/68.7%/**+8.5**; Bryant 60.9%/42.5%/**−18.4**; Hopkins 60.5%/42.6%/**−17.8**; Allen 64.8%/54.2%/**−10.6**. Positive = receiver/defense reactions while ball in air improved catch odds.

## 8. Code / data availability
**Code: https://www.github.com/skdeshpande91/ehcp** (stated in the paper). Data: NFL Big Data Bowl public repo (https://github.com/nfl-football-ops/Big-Data-Bowl). Models fit in Stan/rstan and R's BART package (`lbart`, sparse=TRUE).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- **Random (not time-ordered) train/test splits** — 10 random 75/25 splits leak future games into training; the reported MSE/log-loss are optimistic for a forward-deployed model. No time-series validation.
- **Crude imputation**: x*_miss sampled from the *marginal* distribution over all passes — a deep crosser's unobserved arrival covariates are imputed from screens and checkdowns alike. The authors admit this is "by far the simplest approach"; EHCP quality hinges on it and it is unvalidated by construction.
- **No QB/pressure features**: the authors note NGS's proprietary Completion Probability includes QB-centric features (pass-rusher speed/distance at throw); EHCP handles pressure only indirectly through imputation averaging. Throw quality (ball placement) is folded into the average rather than modeled.
- **Small, stale sample**: 6 weeks of 2017, N=4,913 passes; the QB/receiver tables rest on ~6 games per player and the authors explicitly warn against extrapolation. Defensive schemes and route distributions have evolved since 2017.
- **Selection bias in training**: the catch model trains only on *thrown* passes — the hypothetical-pass covariate space (e.g., throwing into double coverage) is out-of-distribution relative to training data.
- **BART opacity**: acknowledged — variable importance is via splitting probabilities, no coefficient interpretation; partial dependence is single-covariate only.
- **External validity**: the framework transfers to any tracking-data era; the fitted model and all numbers do not (2017 schemes, 0.1 s tracking, 6-week sample).

## 10. GSE overlap
Extension, bordering on high-priority gap-fill — not a duplicate. Per the existing-research map: the 2026-09-21 NGS taxonomy **inventories** completion probability as one of 27 NGS metric families, but NGS's model is proprietary and the map's gap list explicitly states: "nflfastR CPOE/EP feature specifications — methodology articles unfetchable in prior passes (UNVERIFIED). **Any paper pinning down current production features is high value.**" This paper pins down a complete, reproducible catch-probability feature set (exact list in §5) with a public code repo and public data — the closest thing in the sweep to a buildable CPOE/completion-probability recipe. The EHCP layer itself (QB decision quality: % of throws to the max-EHCP receiver) has no counterpart in the repo — GSE grades QBs by efficiency outcomes, not by process-vs-outcome decision quality. Burke (2019) DeepQB is cited but not in the repo's read list. So: (a) the catch-probability model fills a named gap; (b) the EHCP decision metric is a new capability for QB grading content.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Build **GSE-CPOE + QB Decision Grade** on modern tracking data:
1. Data: NFL Big Data Bowl releases (2018–2024 weekly tracking, public) — replicate the exact §5 feature set (throw-time, arrival-time, and in-flight-change covariates + situational features). Target: caught ∈ {0,1}.
2. Model: replace BART with gradient boosting (LightGBM/XGBoost) for speed and production serving; keep the paper's Bayesian framing for the content/uncertainty story by calibrating with isotonic regression and reporting Clopper-Pearson intervals per the repo's existing calibration stack. Train on 2018–2023, validate on 2024 (time-ordered — fixing the paper's random-split flaw).
3. EHCP layer: replace marginal imputation with a **conditional imputation model** — predict x_miss | x_obs (route-type-conditioned distributional regression or a small normalizing flow per route family); then EHCP = Monte-Carlo average exactly as in equation (2). Add the paper's suggested QB-pressure features (pass-rusher distance/speed at throw, available in modern tracking).
4. Products: (a) per-play QB Decision Grade (% of dropbacks targeting the max-EHCP receiver, min 100 dropbacks) — weekly X content in Garrett's voice; (b) receiver credit/blame (EHCP − fitted completion probability) as a weekly risers/fallers input; (c) feed the catch-probability model into GSE's CPOE-equivalent as a public, auditable alternative to NGS's black box.
5. Effort: ~3 weeks (feature pipeline on BDB data 1 wk; model + calibration 1 wk; conditional imputation + EHCP + QB grade 1 wk). Compute: single workstation; no GPU required for LightGBM.

## 12. Reproducible test
Replicate the paper's Table 1 on 2023 Big Data Bowl tracking: build the exact §5 feature set for all 2023 pass attempts, fit (a) L2 logistic regression and (b) LightGBM, and evaluate on a **time-ordered** holdout (train weeks 1–12, test weeks 13–18) using the paper's three metrics (MSE, log-loss, misclassification at 0.5). Baselines: the paper's BART numbers (MSE 0.086, log-loss 0.289, misclass 0.113) and a naive always-predict-league-average-catch-rate model. Then compute EHCP for all 2023 plays with the conditional imputation model and verify the sanity property the paper demonstrates: for ≥70% of interceptions, the targeted receiver's arrival-time EHCP is below the play's max receiver EHCP (i.e., the QB had a better option) — if this fails, the imputation is broken. Runnable with public BDB data on one machine.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
**Adopt** if on the 2023 weeks-13–18 holdout the LightGBM catch model beats the paper's BART benchmarks (log-loss ≤ 0.289 AND misclassification ≤ 0.113) with a time-ordered split — the stricter protocol means matching the paper's random-split numbers is a genuine improvement — AND the EHCP sanity check passes (≥70% of interceptions show a higher-EHCP alternative receiver). Then the QB Decision Grade ships as weekly content and the catch model becomes GSE's public CPOE-equivalent. **Reject** if log-loss > 0.30 or misclassification > 0.13 on the holdout (no improvement over a 2019 BART on 2017 data), or if the conditional imputation collapses (EHCP ≈ fitted probability for all receivers, meaning the hypothetical layer adds no information). Gate evaluated on 2023 data before any content or model integration.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Go beyond the paper in the exact direction its discussion proposes but does not attempt: replace the binary catch target with a **continuous value target** — expected points added of the hypothetical completion — to derive Expected Hypothetical EPA (EHEPA). Train the catch model as now, then train a second model for YAC/points conditional on catch given the same feature set, and define EHEPA(x*_obs) = E[completion × (EPA if caught)] over the imputed x_miss. This answers the question the paper leaves open ("we could model a continuous outcome like change in expected points") and fixes EHCP's blind spot visible in the Kupp example: Watkins had the highest EHCP (92.2%) on a play where Kupp's touchdown was the better *football* outcome. Test on 2024 holdout: does max-EHEPA receiver selection correlate with actual EPA/play better than max-EHCP selection (metric: Spearman ρ between the grade and team offensive EPA/play)? If ρ_EHEPA > ρ_EHCP by ≥0.05, EHEPA replaces EHCP as the decision metric.
