# [0228] ShuttleSet: A Human-Annotated Stroke-Level Singles Dataset for Badminton Tactical Analysis (arXiv:2306.04948v1)

**Citation:** Wei-Yao Wang, Yung-Chang Huang, Tsi-Ui Ik, Wen-Chih Peng. *ShuttleSet: A Human-Annotated Stroke-Level Singles Dataset for Badminton Tactical Analysis*. Proceedings of the 29th ACM SIGKDD Conference on Knowledge Discovery and Data Mining (KDD '23), August 6–10, 2023, Long Beach, CA, USA. arXiv:2306.04948v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04948. DOI: 10.1145/3580305.3599906
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (local extraction, all 1,992 lines: Sections 1–8, references, Appendix A with all 44 matches).
**Verdict:** REJECT — a badminton-only dataset release with no transferable concept to NFL prediction; the dataset, labels, models, and benchmarks are all specific to stroke-level badminton rally analysis.

## 1. Research question
Can a large, publicly available, expert-annotated stroke-level dataset for badminton singles close the gap between turn-based sports analytics research and practical coaching use? The paper introduces ShuttleSet — 44 matches (2018–2021) annotated stroke-by-stroke by domain experts with a computer-aided S²-labeling tool — and demonstrates its utility through three benchmark tasks (shot influence, stroke forecasting, movement forecasting) plus a deployed coach-facing visualisation platform.

## 2. Dataset / schema
ShuttleSet: 104 sets, 3,685 rallies, 36,492 strokes from 44 matches (2018–2021) between 27 top-ranking players (16 men's singles, 11 women's singles); all matches listed in Appendix A.1 (Table 5: tournament, round, year, sets, duration, winner, loser). Match selection: quarter-finals/semi-finals/finals of top tournaments (Super 1000 series, BWF World Tour Finals; a few BWF World Tour Finals group-stage matches); videos crawled from public sources (bwf.tv) at 30 fps, 1280×720. Per-stroke schema in four categories: Rally (current scores of both players; rally winner on last stroke; lose reason ∈ {out, touched the net, not pass over the net, opponent's ball landed, misjudged}); Temporal (hitting time, video frame); Spatial (x,y locations of both players and shuttle at hitting time, plus 9 in-court + 7 out-of-court expert grids per sub-court; binary height above/below net); Hitting (serve/return/dead-bird status; backhand flag; around-the-head flag; shot type from 18 classes). Annotation by 6 badminton-knowledgeable annotators with the S²-labeling tool (one-pass 7-step cycle: hitting time → returner location → opponent location → backhand/around-head/height → opponent return time → landing location → shot type). Consistency check on 3 re-labelled matches: temporal MAE 0.24 (serve) / 0.02 (return) / 0 (dead bird) frames; spatial MAE 0.18 (shuttle) / 0.86 (player); shot-type accuracy difference 0 (serve) / 0.05 (return) / 0.11 (dead bird). No fixed train/validation/test split by design; benchmarks define their own splits. Access: Python implementation at https://github.com/wywyWang/CoachAI-Projects; visualisation platform at https://coachai.cs.nctu.edu.tw:55000.

## 3. Method / model
The paper is a dataset paper; its methodological contributions are the annotation pipeline and three benchmark tasks with baselines, not a novel predictive method. Annotation pipeline: S²-labeling tool (Huang et al. 2022) with BLSR format (Wang et al. 2022a); match preparation (metadata + 4 court corners for homography), rally segmentation, video preprocessing (trajectory/player/skeleton detection; only trajectory used), shot-by-shot labelling with fool-proof design and random quality sampling. Benchmark 1 (shot influence, Wang et al. 2021/2022a): predict rally winner from stroke-level features → per-stroke win-probability attribution; SOTA ShuttleScorer (shot encoder + pattern extractor + Transformer rally encoder) vs Bi-GRU, ProSeNet, DeepMoji, ON-LSTM, Transformer, Reformer, TokenLearner; split = latest 10 matches as test. Benchmark 2 (stroke forecasting, Wang et al. 2022b): predict future strokes (18 shot types + landing locations) from previous strokes in a rally; SOTA ShuttleNet (player-style Transformer extractor + rally-progress Transformer extractor + position-aware gated fusion) vs Seq2Seq, CF-LSTM, TF, dNRI, DMA-Nets; first 80% rallies train / 20% test per match; 10 sequences generated per rally, closest-to-truth evaluated; conditioning lengths τ ∈ {8, 4, 2}. Benchmark 3 (movement forecasting, Chang et al. 2022): stroke forecasting plus both players' movements; SOTA DyMF (dynamic interaction-style graph extractor + hierarchical fusion) vs Seq2Seq, TF, ShuttleNet, dNRI, GCN_PM, R-GCN_PM. Deployment: Flask-based visualisation platform (match analysis + matchup summaries; position, win/lose, and shot-type views) used by national badminton teams during international high-ranking matches.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
The only equation stated is the camera-to-world homography for court-coordinate mapping: p' = Hp, where p' is the 2D real-world coordinate, p the 2D camera coordinate, and H ∈ ℝ^{3×3} the homography matrix solved from ≥4 point correspondences (court corners (0,0), (0,6.1), (13.4,0), (13.4,6.1); court is 13.4 m × 6.1 m). No modelling equations are stated for the benchmark models (architectures described by reference to prior papers; hyperparameters "identical to the original papers"). Stated assumptions: (i) badminton is a turn-based sport amenable to stroke-by-stroke sequential analysis; (ii) human expert annotation is the quality upper bound vs automatic detection; (iii) the 18 shot-type taxonomy covers all strokes; (iv) the 9+7 grid system is an adequate discretisation of court location for tactical analysis; (v) matches from quarter-finals onward of top tournaments represent "state-of-the-art and highly-organized tactics" suitable for studying and teaching. The 18 shot types: net shot, return net, smash, wrist smash, lob, defensive return lob, clear, drive, driven flight, back-court drive, drop, passive drop, push, rush, defensive return drive, cross-court net shot, short service, long service.

## 5. Features / target
Benchmark 1: input = stroke-level records (shot type, player/shuttle locations, backhand/around-head/height flags, timestamps); target = rally winner (binary), used to derive per-stroke influence scores; metrics AUC, accuracy, Brier score. Benchmark 2: input = previous τ strokes of a rally; target = future strokes' shot types (18-class, cross-entropy) and landing locations (MSE, MAE). Benchmark 3: input = previous τ strokes; target = future shot types + locations + both players' movement trajectories (CE/MSE/MAE). All features/targets are badminton-rally-specific; no transferable feature set for football.

## 6. Validation design
No fixed splits (explicit design choice). Shot influence: latest 10 matches as test set, remaining 34 as train; results averaged over 5 random seeds. Stroke/movement forecasting: per match, first 80% of rallies train, last 20% test ("to enforce the model equipped with the information of previous matches of each player"); stochastic generation: 10 sequences per rally, best-vs-ground-truth selected; conditioning lengths τ ∈ {8, 4, 2} to test robustness. Baselines: 7 models (Benchmark 1), 6 models (Benchmark 2), 7 models (Benchmark 3), all with hyperparameters from their original papers. No cross-validation, no time-ordered holdout across seasons beyond the latest-10-matches test.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Benchmark 1 (shot influence; averages of 5 seeds): ShuttleScorer AUC 0.8371, ACC 0.7869, BR 0.1574; Bi-GRU 0.6603/0.7054/0.2158 (paper claims ShuttleScorer outperforms Bi-GRU by 26.8% AUC, 11.6% ACC, 27.1% BR); ON-LSTM 0.6766/0.6808/0.2244; DeepMoji 0.6786/0.6802/0.2238; Transformer 0.6846/0.7570/0.1986; Reformer 0.7046/0.7454/0.1994; TokenLearner 0.7166/0.7698/0.1933; ProSeNet 0.5689/0.5000/0.2500. Benchmark 2 (stroke forecasting, CE/MSE/MAE): ShuttleNet τ=8: 2.4125/1.8121/1.3582; τ=4: 2.4065/1.5673/1.3777; τ=2: 2.3901/1.5034/1.3615 — consistently beats Seq2Seq (τ=8: 3.0588/1.8414/1.4840); CF-LSTM, TF, dNRI, DMA-Nets all worse; paper notes performance degraded vs the original ShuttleNet paper because ShuttleSet uses 18 shot types vs 10. Benchmark 3 (movement forecasting): DyMF τ=8: 2.3566/1.0963/1.5875; τ=4: 2.3641/1.1107/1.6107; τ=2: 2.3146/1.1371/1.6142 — beats Seq2Seq and the GCN variants. Dataset statistics: 36,492 strokes / 3,685 rallies / 104 sets / 44 matches; most common men's shot types: net shot 3,781 (15.45%), lob 3,124 (12.76%), return net 2,130 (8.70%); women's: net shot 527 (2.15% of men's+? — Table 1 gives per-gender counts and proportions), lob 470, clear 421. Case studies (Malaysia Masters 2020): Ng Ka Long Angus's right side identified as weak area; Kento Momota's net-shot proficiency quantified (outside-distribution 0.25 vs 0.37 for Axelsen's opponents).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- Adversarial points: (i) the entire artefact is badminton-specific — 18 badminton shot types, badminton court geometry, rally/serve/dead-bird structure — with zero applicability to football; (ii) the benchmark models (ShuttleScorer, ShuttleNet, DyMF) are architectures for turn-based rally sequences, not transferable to NFL play sequences in any stated way (no experiment or claim suggests cross-sport transfer); (iii) annotation consistency check covers only 3 of 44 matches and reports MAE/accuracy differences without inter-annotator agreement statistics (no κ); (iv) player coverage is highly imbalanced (matchup matrices show rare-appearance players) and the paper acknowledges this as an open challenge; (v) no fixed test split means future benchmark comparisons are not standardised; (vi) the "used by national badminton teams" deployment claim is unverifiable from the paper (no usage statistics); (vii) external validity to NFL: none — there is no concept in the paper (dataset, label taxonomy, model, or metric) that maps onto football prediction, betting, or player evaluation.

## 10. GSE overlap
