# [2203] Multi-Camera Asynchronous Ball Localization and Trajectory Prediction with Factor Graphs and Human Poses (arXiv:2401.17185)

**Citation:** Xiao, Q., Zaidi, Z. & Gombolay, M. (2024). *Multi-Camera Asynchronous Ball Localization and Trajectory Prediction with Factor Graphs and Human Poses*. arXiv:2401.17185. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17185
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-22. **Read:** full text (ar5iv HTML).
**Verdict:** ADAPT

## 1. Research question
How can a fast-moving, spinning ball (tennis) be localized in 3D and its trajectory predicted in real time when camera detections arrive asynchronously (no frame synchronization/pairing across cameras), and how can early-flight spin — which strongly affects Magnus lift and bounce dynamics but is unobservable from early detections — be estimated? The paper asks whether an incremental factor-graph formulation with physics-based aerodynamic/bounce factors plus a learned spin prior from human pose (TCN on kicker/hitter posture) beats Extended Kalman Filter baselines. (Abstract, §I–II)

## 2. Dataset / schema
Proprietary hardware setup: indoor tennis court, 6 Blackfly S cameras at 5.5 m height on back walls (3 per side, overlapping coverage), each with a Jetson Xavier; intrinsic calibration via checkerboard, extrinsic via AprilTag + court keypoints; ball detection via background subtraction + YOLO-tiny trained on tennis balls, ~140 Hz detection per camera, ~1 ms Ethernet latency to central computer. Human poses via AlphaPose; per trajectory, 100 frames of pose keypoints centered at the hip, emphasizing right arm (shoulder/elbow/wrist). Spin labels generated by the authors' iterative procedure: factor-graph inference with evolving initial spin estimates, iterated until the final-detection spin estimate converges, then used as labels (self-distillation). Dataset split 80/20 train/validation for the TCN. Not public; not downloadable. (IV-A, V-A, V-B)

## 3. Method / model
Two-part system (§III–IV):
1. **Incremental factor graph (GTSAM + ISAM2) for asynchronous localization.** Variables: camera poses X_i (priors from extrinsic calibration), ball 3D location L_t, 3D velocity V_t, 3D spin W_j (constant between bounces — spin only changes at ground contact). Factors (§III-A):
   - projection factors (2D detection − π^proj(L), K[R|T]L);
   - location factors (Euler integration L_{s+1} = L_s + V_s·Δt);
   - aerodynamic factors: F_D(V)=−½ρ_air A C_D‖V‖V, F_L(V,W)=½ρ_air A C_L‖V‖²U (U=W×V), acc=(F_D+F_L)/m+g, then V_{s+1}=V_s+acc·Δt (eqs. 7–11);
   - bounce factors: 3D extension of prior bounce work (eqs. 12–19) mapping pre-bounce (V_{t−1}, W_{j−1}) to post-bounce (V_t, W_j) with ball radius R, shell radius R_1, restitution e_z;
   - spin-prior factor on W_0 (from TCN on human pose; zero with large noise if pose unavailable).
   Graph grows with detections; each new detection triggers relinearized incremental optimization (ISAM2): argmin_η −log G(η) ≈ argmin_η ‖Jη−b‖² (eq. 20). No camera synchronization needed — every single detection updates the graph.
2. **TCN spin-prior network (§IV):** temporal convolutional network regressing ball spin from the 100-frame human-pose sequence (hip-centered, arm keypoints). Authors report TCNs beat RNNs/LSTMs for this task.
3. **Prediction (§III-C):** propagate estimated (L,V,W) with 4th-order Runge-Kutta on the flight ODE [L̇, V̇, Ẇ]=[V, acc(V,W), 0] (eq. 21); on ground contact (V_z<0, L_z<z_ground) terminate integration, apply bounce dynamics (eq. 12), restart.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
- Graph factorization (eq. 1): G(η)=∏_{i=0}^{M−1} f_i(ξ_i); potentials (eq. 2): f_i(ξ_i)=exp(−½‖err_i(ξ_i)‖²_{Σ_i}).
- Projection (eqs. 3–4): π^proj(L)=K[R T]L; err^proj=u_detect−π(L).
- Location (eqs. 5–6): π^loc(L_s,V_s)=L_s+V_s[t(s+1)−t(s)]; err^loc=L_{s+1}−π^loc.
- Aerodynamics (eqs. 7–11): drag/lift as above; acc(V,W)=(F_D+F_L)/m+g; π^aero(V_s,W_j)=V_s+acc·Δt; err^aero=V_{s+1}−π^aero.
- Bounce (eqs. 12–19): V_{x,t}=[RW_{y,j−1}+(1.5R²/R_1²)V_{x,t−1}]/[1+1.5R²/R_1²]; V_{y,t}=[−RW_{x,j−1}+(1.5R²/R_1²)V_{y,t−1}]/[1+1.5R²/R_1²]; V_{z,t}=−e_z V_{z,t−1}; W_{x,j}=−V_{y,t}/R; W_{y,j}=V_{x,t}/R; W_{z,j}=W_{z,j−1}; err^bc=[V_{s+1},W_{j+1}]ᵀ−π^bc(V_s,W_j).
- Flight ODE + RK4 prediction (eq. 21).
Stated assumptions: spin constant during flight, changes only at bounce; ball is a sphere (radius R); detections arrive with known timestamps; camera poses known from calibration; hip-centered arm poses carry spin information.

## 5. Features / target
Inputs: asynchronous 2D ball detections with timestamps from 6 cameras; per-trajectory human-pose keypoint sequences (100 frames). Intermediate targets: 3D location, 3D velocity, 3D spin (hidden states). Final targets: predicted first and second landing positions of the ball. TCN target: initial spin prior (Hz).

## 6. Validation design
No time-ordered splits (hardware-collected trajectories; 80/20 train/validation for TCN spin regression, RMSE metric). Trajectory-prediction evaluation: landing-position RMSE binned by observation stage (quartiles of detection count per trajectory — Stage 1 = first 25% of detections, etc.), mean ± std per stage, for first and second landing positions. Baselines: prior EKF system (Zaidi et al. [14]), adaptive EKF with spin as hidden state (AEKF), ablation without spin priors, and oracle labeled-spin variant. Heavy-spin evaluation on a competitive player's shots. No cross-validation reported; no blind test set described.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
- TCN spin prior: training RMSE 2.95 Hz, validation RMSE 5.27 Hz; Pearson R 0.807 (novice) / 0.815 (competitive), p<0.001 (V-B).
- Claim: "reduces the prediction error of landing positions by over 63.6% compared to a baseline method that utilized an adaptive extended Kalman filter" (Abstract); §IV: "63.6% reduction in the RMSE for the ball's landing position vs. baseline methods" via spin-prior integration.
- Stage-binned RMSE curves (Fig. 6, qualitative from text): error drops most from Stage 1→2 (first 25% of observations); TCN-spin variant (green) beats EKF (red), AEKF (purple), and no-spin (blue), approaching the labeled-spin oracle (orange); spin advantage appears mainly in second-bounce prediction (spin affects first bounce, then propagates).
- Heavy-spin pro shots (Fig. 7): TCN variant consistently beats EKF/AEKF but underperforms labeled-spin oracle — authors attribute TCN gap to limited training data and missing racket pose.

## 8. Code / data availability
GTSAM (open-source factor-graph package) and AlphaPose used; no author code or dataset link stated in the paper. Work supported by a Google grant.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- Spin labels are self-distilled from the same factor-graph inference the method evaluates — circularity risk in the TCN's training signal (labeled-spin oracle comparisons inherit this).
- Small, proprietary, single-venue dataset; no public benchmark; bounce-model coefficients required "ad hoc modifications" for high-spin ball-ground slip — authors propose a future *learnable* bounce factor (§VI), which is exactly the right next step.
- Tennis ball ≠ football: prolate spheroid with orientation dynamics; bounce equations are sphere-specific.
- No real-time latency numbers reported for the central factor-graph optimization (detection runs at 140 Hz, but ISAM2 update cost per detection is unstated).
- External validity to NFL: the pose→spin-prior idea is the gem (kicker/punter body mechanics predict ball flight); the asynchronous multi-view factor graph is overkill for NFL where broadcast is monocular, but the *factor-graph-as-learned-simulator* framing (physics factors + learned factors, incremental inference) is a portable design pattern.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: 2022–2024 NFL punts with NGS hang time + landing yard line (weak GT), plus ~150 manually labeled kick sequences with frame-level ball positions from All-22. Metric: landing-spot RMSE and early-flight (first 25% of observations) prediction error. Baseline: AEKF-style filter with no spin. Test window: held-out 2024 season second half. Must reproduce the paper's signature result: second-landing prediction error reduced ≥40% vs the no-spin baseline when spin priors come from kicker pose.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT if on held-out NFL punts: (a) landing-spot RMSE ≤ 2.0 yards, AND (b) pose-based spin priors cut early-flight prediction error ≥30% vs the no-spin factor graph, AND (c) incremental inference runs <50 ms per detection on a single GPU/CPU (real-time viable). Reject if the TCN spin prior shows no correlation (R<0.3) with fitted spin on NFL data — the pose→spin link is the load-bearing assumption.

## 14. Improvement experiment
The authors' own proposed next step: replace the analytic bounce factor with a *learned* bounce factor (small MLP factor trained on turf-impact data, capturing ball-ground slip for tumbling punts). Test learned-vs-analytic bounce factors on punts that hit and roll (vs fair catches); hypothesis: learned bounce factors cut second-landing error by another 20%+ on bouncing punts, where analytic sphere bounce models fail hardest — directly addressing §VI's admitted weakness.
