# [0538] Attention Limited Reward Learning (arXiv:2607.04590v1)

**Citation:** Wenqian Xing (Stanford MS&E, 2026). *Attention Limited Reward Learning*. arXiv:2607.04590v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04590v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 34,000+ chars; §1–§6 incl. all propositions, both empirical case studies, conclusion read; references skimmed).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — RLHF-theory paper, but it delivers the first rigorous diagnostic for a question Garrett's corpus takes for granted: when does pairwise-comparison data *reject* representation by any scalar Bradley–Terry rating at all? The cycle-consistency test + one-eighth law is directly portable to GSE's BT-based rating aggregation and to any pairwise evaluation of engine variants.

## 1. Research question
What does the Bradley–Terry model miss when pairwise comparison labels are generated by rationally inattentive evaluators? The paper models each label as the output of an attention-limited channel (ℓ_z = η_z + β_z Δ*_z) and asks: (1) when are attention-filtered log-odds representable by any scalar reward? (2) what does BT converge to when they are not? (3) what can any procedure learn, information-theoretically? (4) do the predicted signatures appear in real human comparison data?

## 2. Dataset / schema
Theory paper with two empirical case studies. (1) Chatbot Arena: 57,477 pairwise battles between 64 language models (HF dataset arena-human-preference-55k); filtered to ties dropped (31%), ≥100 decisive votes per pair, largest connected component → 32 models, 74 pairs, 15,001 votes, cycle space dim 43. (2) Perceptual comparison task [33]: 25 participants, 31,854 trials, with response times and gaze measurements. Haldane–Anscombe estimates q̂_e = (w_e + 1/2)/(n_e + 1) for edge log-odds.

## 3. Method / model
Shannon rational-inattention first-order condition → reduced-form attention-scaled channel (Definition 1): q_z = σ(ℓ_z), ℓ_z = η_z + β_z Δ*_z, β_z ≥ 0 (attention multiplier = inverse information cost), η_z = default/order/salience term. Graph-theoretic analysis on comparison graph G=(V,E) with incidence matrix B (Br)_e = r_i − r_j: cycle-consistency criterion (Prop. 1), BT pseudo-true limit as weighted projection r†(ℓ) = (BᵀWB)⁺BᵀWℓ (Prop. 2), non-identification of (reward, attention, defaults) from passive comparisons (Prop. 3), per-label information scaling ∝ β_z² and rank-one local information matrix under unknown attention (Props. 4–5), sample-complexity lower bounds via KL/Fano (Theorem 1: distinguishing Δ=δ from −δ needs ~1/(β²δ²) labels; recovery governed by total attended information, not label count), Hodge decomposition ℓ = ℓ^pot + ℓ^cyc with one-eighth law pricing the scalar-reward misfit (Prop. 7).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
- BT baseline (Eq. 1): P[y¹≻y⁰|x] = σ(r(x,y¹) − r(x,y⁰)).
- Attention channel (Eq. 2/8): ℓ_z = η_z + β_z Δ*_z; homogeneous attention = η_z=0, β_z≡β>0.
- Shannon RI (Eq. 4–5): max_{π_z} E[u_z] − κ_z I_z(ω;a); Lemma 1 generalized logit → log[π_z(1|ω)/π_z(0|ω)] = α_z + β_z{u_z(1,ω)−u_z(0,ω)}, β_z = κ_z⁻¹.
- Cycle criterion (Prop. 1, Eq. 9): scalar representation exists iff Σ_k ℓ_{i_k i_{k+1}} = 0 for every directed cycle.
- BT projection (Prop. 2, Eq. 11): r†(ℓ) = (BᵀWB)⁺BᵀWℓ + O(ε³) near low-information regime.
- One-eighth law (Prop. 7, Eq. 29): inf_r Σ_e ρ_e D_KL(Bern(σ(ℓ_e))‖Bern(σ((Br)_e))) = (1/8)‖ℓ^cyc‖²_W + O(ε³).
- Assumptions: (a) reduced-form channel (motivated by, not derived from, RI); (b) connected comparison graph, q_e ∈ (0,1); (c) results 1–3 exact on finite graphs; information bounds local/asymptotic; (d) η_z absorbs endogenous defaults + exogenous order/format effects.

## 5. Features / target
Target: population BT score r† and its relationship to deliberative reward R*; cyclic energy share ‖ℓ^cyc‖²_W/‖ℓ‖²_W; per-comparison KL misfit of best scalar fit; information content (bits) of labels vs response times about gap sign/magnitude.

## 6. Validation design
Parametric bootstrap null for cyclic energy (fit BT by weighted MLE, regenerate binomial votes at observed n_e, 2000 replicates); likelihood-ratio test on 43 df; three-item projection-reversal example (Example 1: R* = (2,1,0), ℓ_AB=ε, ℓ_AC=ε, ℓ_BC=5ε → BT ranks B above A despite A deliberatively better and every majority correct); perceptual study decomposes label vs RT vs gaze information. No train/test splits — theory + observational case studies.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
- Chatbot Arena: observed cyclic energy share 4.2% vs bootstrap null mean 2.5%, 95th percentile 3.5% → p=0.008; LR statistic 70.6 on 43 df, p=0.007 — scalar-score representation rejected at 1%.
- Misfit pricing: best scalar fit loses 0.0034 bits/comparison (weighted KL); one-eighth law predicts 0.0038 bits — ratio 0.89, despite max log-odds ≈1.8 (small-ε hypothesis violated, law still holds); noise-corrected population misfit ≈0.0013 bits/comparison.
- Projection reversal (Example 1): BT fit gives B (10ε/3) > A (8ε/3) > C (0) — ranking reversed vs deliberative (2,1,0).
- Perceptual study: psychometric slopes vary 6× across evaluators (heterogeneous β directly observed); label carries 0.33 bits about gap sign but 0.0001 bits about magnitude; response time carries 0.035 bits about magnitude (350× more than the label); 1 s relative gaze dwell → +0.91 (SE 0.03) choice log-odds, ≈ a full quality level.
- Caveats (stated): Arena votes aggregate over prompts/annotators (cyclicity can arise from aggregation, not per-query attention); analysis conditions on platform-sampled pairs; perceptual task is not RLHF annotation; gaze association observational.

## 8. Code / data availability
Arena data public: https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmarena-ai/arena-human-preference-55k. Paper states "a self-contained script reproduces all numbers" (no URL captured in extract).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Author-stated: reduced-form channel, not fully derived; cyclicity in Arena data consistent with but not unique evidence for the attention mechanism; perceptual findings are channel ingredients in a controlled task, not RLHF annotation; next steps = oversight interventions shifting attention, RT/effort proxies, partial-identification bounds. Unstated: the one-eighth law and projection results are local (small-ε) but tested at ε≈1.8; the non-identification result (Prop. 3) makes the theory hard to falsify on passive data; no prescription for estimating β_z per query from labels alone (rank-one information). Transfer limits: no sports data; NFL head-to-head graphs are too sparse (pairs meet ~once/year) for the cycle test to have power on raw game outcomes — the diagnostic needs dense comparison graphs.

## 10. GSE overlap
Methodological extension with a direct hook into the corpus. Garrett's existing-research map inventories Bradley–Terry as a core rating/prediction tool — and this paper is the first in the wave to ask whether the BT scalar-rating assumption itself is falsified by the comparison data, giving an exact test (cycle sums) and a misfit price (one-eighth law). No corpus work covers cyclic-inconsistency diagnostics, Hodge decompositions of comparison fields, or attention-heterogeneity in pairwise evaluation. Relevant wherever GSE fits BT-style ratings or consumes pairwise judgments: power-rating aggregation from multiple sources, engine-variant A/B comparisons across backtest weeks, and any future human grading of GSE write-ups (ties to the 9.2 voice-floor workflow — "weak preference may reflect hidden evaluation difficulty rather than genuine indifference" is exactly the right prior for near-tie judge scores).

## 11. GSE implementation spec
**Cycle-consistency diagnostic for GSE's pairwise rating machinery.** (a) Build dense comparison graphs where they exist: engine-variant pairwise win rates across backtest game-weeks (v5.2.7 vs v5.2.8 vs v5.2.9 — every pair compared on every week = dense graph), or multiple power-rating sources' implied pairwise edges. (b) Compute the Hodge decomposition of the empirical log-odds field; report cyclic energy share vs a parametric bootstrap null (same procedure as §5.1). (c) If cyclic share exceeds noise: identify the top circulating triangles — these are the (variant, variant, week-regime) triples where scalar ratings are provably misleading, and route them to regime-specific analysis instead of a single leaderboard number. (d) For any human pairwise grading of GSE outputs (write-ups, pick cards): log decision latency alongside the label; treat near-50/50 pairs with long latencies as "hard-because-hidden" (route to a second, better-instrumented review) rather than "indifferent." Estimated effort: 2–4 engineer-days (comparison-graph builder + bootstrap null + Hodge decomposition via sparse least squares). Risk: low — diagnostic only, changes no production numbers until it fires.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: backtest ledger of engine versions v5.2.6–v5.2.9, pairwise per-week win-rate (ROI-positive weeks) comparisons — dense comparison graph guaranteed. Baseline: current practice — scalar BT ranking of engine versions by aggregate pairwise win rate. Candidate: the cycle diagnostic; compute cyclic energy share and bootstrap p-value. Metric: (i) does the observed cyclic share exceed the 95th percentile of the BT-null bootstrap? (ii) do the top circulating triples correspond to identifiable regimes (e.g., variant A beats B in high-total weeks, B beats C in divisional games, C beats A in primetime)? Success = diagnostic fires (p<0.05) AND at least one circulating triangle maps to a named regime, proving the scalar leaderboard was hiding a real interaction. If no excess cyclicity: the BT ranking stands validated — a useful null result worth the 2–4 days.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADAPT the diagnostic into the model-evaluation pipeline if: (a) on engine-variant comparison data the cyclic energy share exceeds the bootstrap null at p<0.05, OR (b) even under the null, the one-eighth-law misfit price is material (>0.01 bits/comparison — i.e., scalar compression is discarding real information). REJECT (park) if cyclic share is within noise on all dense comparison graphs GSE can build — then the corpus's BT-rating practice is empirically vindicated and this paper remains a theoretical reference. Either outcome is informative, which is why the test is cheap and worth running.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Go beyond the paper: REGIME-CONDITIONED BT — the paper prices cyclicity but doesn't model it. When the diagnostic fires, fit a context-dependent BT where each variant's score is a function of game regime (total band, divisional, primetime): r_i(x) = θ_i + φ_iᵀx. The cyclic component that a scalar rating discards becomes the interaction term the regime model captures. Test whether regime-conditioned ratings predict holdout pairwise outcomes better than the scalar leaderboard by log-loss. The paper's framework says scalar rewards can't represent the cycles; the natural GSE move is to make the rating itself regime-aware — which is also independently valuable for matchup-specific pick logic.
