# [0227] Stop Simulating! Efficient Computation of Tournament Winning Probabilities (arXiv:2307.10411v1)

**Citation:** Ulrik Brandes, Gordana Marmulla, Ivana Smokovic (2026). *Stop Simulating! Efficient Computation of Tournament Winning Probabilities*. arXiv:2307.10411v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10411
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (local extraction, all 4,101 lines: Sections 1–6, references, Appendices A–D).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — apply the paper's "exact beats simulation" principle and bottom-up bracket DP to GSE's NFL playoff-advancement simulations (fixed-seed single elimination needs no Monte Carlo at all); the group-stage enumeration machinery itself does not transfer since the NFL has no group phase.

## 1. Research question
Published tournament winning probabilities are almost always approximated by simulating the tournament 10,000–100,000 times from single-match outcome models. The paper asks whether this is computationally efficient, and shows that exact winning probabilities — computed by enumerating group-stage outcome sequences and propagating joint advancement probabilities up the knockout bracket while exploiting independence among non-mixing subtrees — are substantially faster than simulation for the 2022 (men's) and 2023 (women's) FIFA World Cup formats, independently of the single-match model used.

## 2. Dataset / schema
No new dataset is collected. Inputs are FIFA/Coca-Cola World Ranking points prior to each tournament (rounded to nearest integer, listed in Appendix A): men's October 2022 (e.g. Brazil 1841, Belgium 1817, Argentina 1774, France 1760, England 1728; lowest Ghana 1393) and women's June 2023 (e.g. USA 2090, Germany 2062, Sweden 2050, England 2041, France 2027; lowest Zambia 1298, Morocco 1334). Tournament schedules: 32 teams, m=8 groups of 4, top-2 advance, then 16-team knockout (Figure 1; Appendix B lists all groups). The re-scaled σ for the women's model was chosen by minimising squared differences between computed winning probabilities and averaged pre-tournament betting odds. Published simulation baselines referenced for context: FiveThirtyEight 2022 WC predictions, Alan Turing Institute WorldCupPrediction repo, Joshua Bull (Oxford), DTAI Sports Analytics Lab, Zeileis FIFA Women 2023. No row-level dataset with timestamps; all "data" are the ranking points and the fixed schedules.

## 3. Method / model
Assumptions (Section 2.2): predictions made before the tournament; match outcome probabilities independent, depending only on the two teams; outcomes distinguished are win/draw/loss; group-stage ties broken uniformly at random (no scorelines); knockout draws split 50/50 (extra time/penalties treated as fair coin flips).
Demonstration single-match model (Section 3, claimed illustrative only): team strengths θ_i = 1/(1+10^{(ρ_j−ρ_i)/σ}), θ_j = 1−θ_i, with FIFA ranking points ρ and sensitivity σ (FIFA uses σ=600 men's; women's unpublished, estimated ≈400 from update magnitudes). Group stage via Bradley-Terry + Davidson-Beaver (1977) draw extension: P(i lose) = θ_j/(θ_i+√(θ_iθ_j)+θ_j), P(draw) = √(θ_iθ_j)/(θ_i+√(θ_iθ_j)+θ_j), P(i win) = θ_i/(θ_i+√(θ_iθ_j)+θ_j). Knockout: P(M'_{ij}=1) = θ_i/(θ_i+θ_j) (draw probability split evenly, i.e. P(M'_{ij}=1) = ½P(M_{ij}=1) + P(M_{ij}=2)). Re-calibration: σ=240 for women (minimiser of squared error vs betting odds; 60% of 400), σ=0.6·600=360 for men.
Tournament algorithm (Section 4): per group, enumerate all 3^6=729 outcome sequences as ternary integers s=s_5…s_0 ∈ {0,1,2}^6 (digits = loss/draw/win per match); each sequence's probability = product of its six single-match probabilities; precompute R[s] = list of possible (1st, 2nd) advancing pairs under random tie-breaking (only 1260 distinct pairs occur, vs 729×12=8748 naive) and accumulate joint advancement probabilities G_{ij} (Algorithm 1: 8×(729+1260)=15,912 elementary steps). Knockout: propagate joint-pair probabilities up the bracket — L16: 4×12²=576 cases; QF: 2×56²=6,272; semifinals: 240²=57,600 (men's, mixing) vs 2×240=480 (women's, same-subtree); final: 32×31=992 (men's) vs 16²=256 (women's). Totals: 80,452 cases (2022), 23,256 (2023) — no large hidden constants; one simulation run samples 63 match outcomes. Key structural observation: whatever happens inside two mixing subtrees is independent of all other groups, and this independence persists up the tree (Figure 4); dependencies arise only for teams on trajectories that meet, especially pairs from the same group. Byproducts: per-round exit probabilities; live updates by fixing known outcomes to probability 1.
Generalisation (Section 6): formats with t elimination rounds, 2^{t_1} advancing and 2^{t_2} exiting per group, n=2^t·(1+2^{t_2−t_1}) teams, m=2^{t−t_1} groups of 2^{t_1}+2^{t_2} teams, mixing constraint g÷2^{t_1+r−1}=h÷2^{t_1+r−1}; scales reasonably for n=40 (8 groups) and n=64 (16 groups).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Group enumeration: 4 teams per group, C(4,2)=6 matches → 3^6=729 outcome sequences; sequence probability p = ∏_{i<j∈{0,…,3}} M[4g+i, 4g+j, s_{C(j,2)+i}]; G[4g+i_1, 4g+i_2] += p·(1/|R[s]|) for (i_1,i_2) ∈ R[s]. Group-exit probability for team i: 1 − Σ_j (G_{ij} + G_{ji}).
Knockout propagation (e.g. Algorithm 2, round of 16): p = G[i,j]·G[k,ℓ]; L16[i,j] += p·M'[i,ℓ]·M'[j,k]; L16[i,k] += p·M'[i,ℓ]·M'[k,j]; L16[ℓ,j] += p·M'[ℓ,i]·M'[j,k]; L16[ℓ,k] += p·M'[ℓ,i]·M'[k,j]. Semifinal (men's): p = QF[i,j]·QF[k,ℓ], four updates of SF[i,j], SF[i,k], SF[ℓ,j], SF[ℓ,k]; (women's): SF[i] += p·M'[i,j], SF[j] += p·M'[j,i]. Final (men's): F_i += p·M'[i,j], F_j += p·M'[j,i] with p = SF[i,j]; (women's): p = SF[i]·SF[j].
Generalised format parameters: n = 2^t·(1+2^{t_2−t_1}), m = 2^{t−t_1}, n/m = 2^{t_1}+2^{t_2} teams per group, log(m·2^{t_1}) = t elimination rounds; r-th-round match between i ∈ N_g and j ∈ N_h possible only if g÷2^{t_1+r−1} = h÷2^{t_1+r−1}.
Stated assumptions: (i) pre-tournament predictions only, no in-tournament evidence; (ii) independent match outcomes depending only on the two teams (violated by e.g. strategic result-engineering); (iii) only win/draw/loss distinguished; group ties broken uniformly at random; knockout draws = fair coin flips (no extra-time/penalty modelling); (iv) all fixtures fixed in advance (unlike cup competitions with redraws); (v) match outcomes independent within and across groups.

## 5. Features / target
Input features: none beyond pairwise team identities — the single-match model consumes only FIFA ranking points (ρ_i, ρ_j) and the calibrated sensitivity σ; the tournament algorithm consumes only the precomputed 32×32 pairwise outcome-probability matrices M and M'. Target: exact per-team probabilities of winning the tournament F_i, plus byproduct probabilities of reaching/exiting in each round (last-16, quarterfinal, semifinal, final). Prediction horizon: the tournament as a whole, computed once pre-tournament (or live-updated by clamping known results to probability 1). No scorelines, no covariates, no in-tournament dynamics.

## 6. Validation design
No predictive validation against real outcomes — the paper explicitly states its objective is "only to demonstrate computational feasibility, not quality of prediction" (Section 5). Validation is computational: the same single-match model is used both for exact computation and for 100 independent tournament-simulation trials; approximation error (median and middle quartiles of three error measures vs the exact probabilities, log-log scale, Figure 8) is tracked as a function of simulation-run count. Retrospective winning probabilities for 2022/2023 are presented as plausibility checks (Morocco semifinal, Belgium/Germany group exits noted as deviations from expectation). The σ=240 women's recalibration used averaged pre-tournament betting odds as the fit target (squared differences).

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Computational (Section 5, Figure 8): exact computation finishes in the time of a few hundred simulation runs (total cases 80,452 for 2022, 23,256 for 2023; one sim run = 63 sampled matches). At the moment exact computation finishes, the maximum simulation error is ≈2.5% points — larger than most entries; ≥10,000 simulations needed to reach 1% point max error; ≥20,000 for "reasonable accuracy"; even after 100,000 simulations the maximum error is still above 0.1% points. Claim: exact is "two orders of magnitude faster than any reasonably accurate approximation through tournament simulation".

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- The demonstration single-match model is a thin wrapper on FIFA ranking points with an ad hoc recalibration (σ=240 chosen by fitting to betting odds on the same tournament being predicted — circular if used as a genuine forecast); the men's σ=360 is a heuristic 60% scaling of FIFA's 600 with no fit.
- Knockout draw = coin flip and group ties = random are acknowledged simplifications; in reality tie-breakers (goal difference, fair play) and penalty shootouts are skill-dependent.
- Match-independence assumption is violated by strategic play (teams engineering results to choose opponents), which the paper admits.
- The algorithm's efficiency hinges on limited trajectory mixing: for the planned 2026 format (48 teams, 8 groups of 6), enumeration needs 3^15 = 14,348,907 sequences per group — prohibitive; the 12-groups-of-4 format with 8 third-placed teams advancing "does not fit our generalization" (groups not a power of 2, mixing unknown in advance).
- NFL external validity: the NFL has no group phase — the 729-sequence enumeration is entirely inapplicable; what transfers is only the already-classical fixed-bracket DP (Edwards 1991; Schwertman et al. 1991), which the paper itself cites as prior art. The paper's novel contribution is specific to group+knockout formats.
- No uncertainty quantification on the reported probabilities themselves (they are exact given the model, but the model is arbitrary).

## 10. GSE overlap
The existing-research-map does not record any tournament/playoff-probability computation work in GSE — no bracket-DP, no playoff simulation methodology, no "stop simulating" lane. GSE's engine does run Monte Carlo simulations (a 40,000-run Monte Carlo was referenced and walked back in recent work per memory), so a simulation-based playoff-probability pipeline plausibly exists or is planned. This paper is a new capability, not a duplicate: the concrete import is (a) exact bottom-up bracket DP for fixed-seed single elimination (NFL playoffs), eliminating Monte Carlo error entirely; (b) the error-quantification argument (2.5% max error at exact-compute time; 20,000+ sims for reasonable accuracy) as the justification for replacing simulation; (c) the live-update property (clamp known results to probability 1) for in-season/in-game playoff-probability updates. The group-stage enumeration (Algorithms 1) has no NFL application and should not be ported.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
1. Data: GSE's existing single-game win-probability model output (any model — the paper's scheme is model-independent); current NFL standings/seeding.
2. Model: exact fixed-bracket DP for the 14-team NFL playoff bracket. Conditional on a set of first-round games with known matchups, P(team advances past round r+1) = Σ_{possible opponents o} P(o reaches round r) · P(team beats o). Byes handled by carrying teams forward with probability 1. For pre-playoff (regular season in progress): enumerate remaining-game outcome combinations only where computationally feasible is NOT the approach — instead, use the paper's Section 6 observation in reverse: run the exact bracket DP conditional on each simulated regular-season ending is unnecessary; rather compute exact advancement probabilities given current seeding probabilities by integrating the bracket DP over the (already simulated or analytically approximated) distribution of seedings. Pragmatic v1: exact bracket DP applied to the current projected seeding each week (the "most likely bracket" analogue of the paper's Figure 7), published alongside the existing Monte Carlo as a cross-check.
3. Serving: recompute weekly (and live, clamping completed games to probability 1 per the paper's Section 6); output per-team P(win division), P(make playoffs), P(first-round bye), P(reach divisional/SB), P(win Super Bowl) — exact, no simulation noise, deterministic given the single-game model.
4. Effort: small — the fixed-bracket DP is ~50 lines; the main work is wiring GSE's game probabilities and seeding logic. Estimated 2–3 days.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: 2023–2025 NFL seasons, GSE's archived single-game win probabilities (or a public baseline such as closing moneyline-implied probabilities). Protocol: at the start of each playoff round (wild card, divisional, championship, Super Bowl) for seasons 2023–2025, compute exact advancement probabilities via the bracket DP and compare against a 100,000-run Monte Carlo using identical game probabilities. Metric: max absolute difference between exact and simulated Super Bowl probabilities across the 14 playoff teams (the paper's Figure 8 max-error measure). Pass = exact DP runs in <1 second per week and Monte Carlo max error at 100k runs is >0.1% points on ≥1 team in ≥1 season (replicating the paper's finding that simulation error is material relative to compute cost). Also verify the DP's probabilities sum correctly and match brute-force enumeration on a 4-team toy bracket.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT if: the exact bracket DP reproduces brute-force enumeration on toy brackets exactly, runs in <1s on the full 14-team bracket, and on the 2023–2025 backtest the 100k-run Monte Carlo shows max team-level Super Bowl probability error >0.25% points in at least 2 of 3 seasons (proving simulation noise is worth eliminating). REJECT (keep Monte Carlo) if: GSE's playoff probabilities are already consumed only as coarse tiers where sub-1% noise is immaterial AND the Monte Carlo serves double duty producing joint distributions (e.g. correlated parlay pricing) that the marginal DP cannot supply — in that case keep simulation but use the exact DP as a nightly validation harness for it.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Beyond the paper: (i) extend the exact DP to the regular season by exact enumeration of division-race outcomes — with ≤4 relevant games per division race, 2^4=16 to 3^4=81 combinations are enumerable exactly, giving exact P(win division) without any simulation for the most common fan-facing question; (ii) generalise the paper's joint-pair propagation to compute exact joint probabilities needed for correlated markets (e.g. P(Team A AND Team B both reach the Super Bowl)), which the paper's Algorithms 2–5 already compute as intermediates (L16[i,j], QF[i,j], SF[i,j]) but never surfaces — these joint matrices are directly usable for pricing correlated playoff futures; (iii) test the paper's live-update claim under GSE's real in-season cadence: measure staleness/error of weekly-frozen exact probabilities vs daily recomputation across a full season.
