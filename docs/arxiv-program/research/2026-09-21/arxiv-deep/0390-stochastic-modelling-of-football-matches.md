# [0390] Stochastic modelling of football matches (arXiv:2312.04338v1)

**Citation:** Luiz Fernando G. N. Maia, Teemu Pennanen, Moacyr A.H.B. da Silva, Rodrigo S. Targino (2023). *Stochastic modelling of football matches*. arXiv:2312.04338v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04338v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 3672 lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — the Cox-process in-game framework with state-dependent intensities and strictly concave log-likelihood ports directly to NFL live win-probability and live spread/total surfaces; adapt the regressors from soccer state (score, red cards, stoppage time) to football state (down, distance, field position, clock, timeouts, penalty shocks).

## 1. Research question
How can the dynamics of a football (soccer) match — goals, red cards, and the random lengths of referee stoppage time — be modeled jointly so that the model produces calibrated in-game (live) forecasts of match results and exact scores? The paper builds a general Cox-process (doubly stochastic Poisson) framework where event intensities depend on all modeled events plus external factors, with the theoretical guarantee that the log-likelihood is strictly concave, making maximum-likelihood estimation a convex optimization problem. (Abstract, §1, §3)

## 2. Dataset / schema
- **Campeonato Brasileiro de Futebol Série A, seasons 2015–2022:** 3,039 matches, 7,126 goals (4,248 home), 717 red cards (434 to away players; cards to coaches/bench players excluded). Outcomes: home win 1,468 (48.3%), away win 746 (24.5%), draw 825 (27.1%). Sources: match/goal/red-card info from Flashscore; stoppage-time data from official match reports on the CBF website; starting-lineup player market values from Transfermarkt (Table 1 gives mean starting-11 market value in millions of euros, seasons played, mean league position per team, e.g., Flamengo €49.08M/8 seasons/4.00, Paraná €5.08M/1 season/20.00).
- Event times recorded at minute granularity (discrete); stoppage times in whole minutes per half (announced after the 45th minute).
- Key descriptives: 3.4% of goals in first-half stoppage time, 6.7% in second-half stoppage time; >7% of total match duration is stoppage time; goal rates higher and increasing in the second half (Figure 3); red-card rates also increase over time (Figure 4).
- Forecasting exercise uses 2,611 matches (first 4 matches of each team and season 2015 excluded from the rolling window).

## 3. Method / model
- **Event processes (§3.1):** event types J = {home goals, away goals, home red cards, away red cards} each follow a Cox (doubly stochastic Poisson) process with intensity λ^j(t); conditional on λ^j, event times follow an inhomogeneous Poisson process. Full likelihood: ℒ(λ|{t^j_l},T) = Π_j Π_l φ^j_{λ^j}(t^j_{l−1}; t^j_l) exp(−Λ^j(t^j_{L^j}, T)) = Π_j exp(−Λ^j(0,T)) Π_l λ^j(t^j_l).
- **Stoppage time (§3.2):** T = 45 + U_1 + 45 + U_2; U_k ~ Poisson(π_k), π_k may depend on events observed by the 45th minute of half k. Likelihood: ℒ(π_k|U_k) = π^{U_k} e^{−π_k}/U_k!.
- **Parameterization (§4):** log-linear intensities ln λ^j(t) = Σ_i ξ^j_i ψ^j_i(t) (Eq. 2) and ln π_k = Σ_i ξ_i φ_i (Eq. 3). Regressors must be adapted to available information (depend only on events observed by time t).
- **Theorem 1:** the log-likelihood is concave on ℝ^I, strictly concave when regressors are linearly independent → unique MLE, solvable with standard convex optimization (linear equality constraints, e.g. the attack/defence normalization Σ_k ln α_k = Σ_k ln β_k, preserve convexity).
- **Regressors (§5):** static: Maher-style attack/defence/home-advantage indicators; team market-value log-ratio (ln V^h − ln V^a); dynamic: score difference N^h(t) − N^a(t); red-card difference N^{ar}(t) − N^{hr}(t); second-half indicator 1_{t≥45+u}; stoppage-time regressors: goals and red cards in the half plus the close-game indicator 1_{|N^h − N^a| ≤ 1}; red-card intensity uses constant + ln t (power law λ^j(t) = e^{ξ} t^{ξ}, §5.4).
- **Fitting (§6):** five nested goal models G0–G4 (static Maher → +values → +half-time → +goals → +red cards; 67→71 parameters); six stoppage models S0–S5; estimation via MOSEK primal-dual interior-point solver through the R CVXR package. Fitting 78 parameters to 3,039 matches took <10 s on an Intel i7-10700F with 32 GB RAM.
- **Forecasting (§7):** for each match simulate 100,000 scenarios from minutes 0, 15, 30, 45, 60, 75: simulate the four processes, take the earliest event, update state, repeat; simulate stoppage time at minute 45. Nonhomogeneous red-card processes simulated by the inversion method. Predictions use only information available up to the forecast minute (no lookahead). Compared models: G0S5R … G4S5R on result (Home/Draw/Away) likelihood and exact-score likelihood.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
- Event-time CDF/density conditional on intensity: Φ^j_{λ^j}(s;t) = 1 − exp(−Λ^j(s,t)); φ^j_{λ^j}(s;t) = λ^j(t) exp(−Λ^j(s,t)); Λ^j(s,t) := ∫_s^t λ^j(r) dr.
- Full single-match likelihood (§3.3, Eq. 1): ℒ = Π_{k=1,2} π^{U_k} e^{−π_k}/U_k! × Π_j exp(−Λ^j(0,T)) Π_l λ^j(t^j_l), with T = 90 + U_1 + U_2.
- Log-linear intensity (Eq. 2): ln λ^j(t) = Σ_{i∈I^j} ξ^j_i ψ^j_i(t).
- Log-linear stoppage parameter (Eq. 3): ln π_k = Σ_{i∈I^k_s} ξ_i φ_i.
- Parametric log-likelihood (§4.3): ℓ(ξ) = −Σ_j[∫_0^T exp(Σ_i ξ^j_i ψ^j_i(r))dr − Σ_l(Σ_i ξ^j_i ψ^j_i(t^j_l))] + Σ_{k=1,2}[U^k Σ_i ξ_i φ_i − exp(Σ_i ξ_i φ_i) − ln(U^k!)].
- Theorem 1: ℓ is concave on ℝ^I; strictly concave under linear independence of regressors (proof: compositions of linear maps with the strictly convex ∫exp(η)dr − Σ η(t_l) and the Poisson terms).
- Stoppage likelihood: ℒ(π_k|U_k) = π^{U_k} e^{−π_k}/U_k!; red-card power law: λ^j(t) = e^{ξ^j_{i_0}} t^{ξ^j_{i_1}}.
Stated assumptions: events independent across types conditional on intensities and match length; events independent across matches conditional on intensities; regressors adapted to available information (no future dependence); red-card intensity depends only on time (constant + ln t basis).

## 5. Features / target
- Input features (regressors): team attack/defence indicators (33 teams), home-advantage constant, starting-11 market-value log ratio, current score difference, cumulative red-card difference, half indicator, elapsed time (ln t for red cards), and for stoppage time: goals/red cards in the half and the close-game indicator.
- Targets: (a) goal event times/intensities for home and away teams; (b) red-card event times; (c) stoppage-time lengths U_1, U_2. Forecast outputs: end-of-match result (Home/Draw/Away) and exact score, evaluated as predictive probabilities p^k_m(t) at minutes 0/15/30/45/60/75.

## 6. Validation design
- Nested-model comparison by in-sample log-likelihood, AIC, BIC, and likelihood-ratio tests (all nested LRT p-values ≈ 0, G0→G4). G4 preferred by both AIC and BIC despite rising parameter count (Table 4: G0 LL −38,290/AIC 76,713/BIC 77,174 vs G4 LL −38,191/AIC 76,523/BIC 77,011).
- Out-of-sample rolling-window forecasting: fit on all data available the day before the match; 2,611 matches × 100,000 simulated scenarios at 6 in-match time points; scoring by predictive log-likelihood of results and exact scores (Figures 10–11, normalized by 2,611), computed from only information available at the forecast time (no lookahead in the protocol).
- Empirical-probability calibration: simulated vs observed outcome proportions (Tables 11–13), e.g. G4S5R draws 0.2748 vs observed 0.2742; and minute-by-minute probability paths for two illustrative matches (Figures 12–13).

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
- Dynamic regressors significantly improve in-game prediction; headline effects (Abstract/§6.1): (a) a red card reduces the receiving team's goal intensity by **30.48%** (exp ξ = 1.4385 for the *opponent's* intensity increase; own intensity ×(1−0.3048)); (b) losing by one goal raises a team's goal rate by **1/0.9082 = 1.1011 (+10.11%)**, losing by two by **1/0.9082² = 1.2124 (+21.24%)**; a team leading by one has its rate ×0.9082, by two ×0.8248; (c) second-half stoppage time is on average **>1 minute longer** when the score difference ≤1 (S5 goal-difference coefficient 1.2814; tied vs 3–0 example: 1.2935 fewer extra minutes when 3–0).
- Home advantage: 1.5140 → home goal intensity +51.40% vs away, all else equal; second-half goal rate +20.62% (1.2062); team value: doubling squad value multiplies goal rate by 1.1454^{ln 2} = 1.0987; 10× value → ×1.3670.
- Stoppage-time base rates (S0): first half 2.6680 min, second half 4.8066 min expected. Goals-per-half have essentially no effect on stoppage length (coefficients ~1.0, S1/S2).
- Red cards (§6.3): home red-card intensity at minute 15 = e^{−12.6054}·15^{1.4275} = 1.6010×10^{−4}/min; at minute 75 = 1.5929×10^{−3}/min (~10× higher). Away rate at minute 75 = 2.5092×10^{−3}/min (~1.5× home). Away exceeds home after minute 8.
- Draw calibration: G4S5R predicts 27.48% draws vs 27.42% observed — far better than Maher's original static models (24.46% / 27.32% on 27.92% observed). G4S5R beats simpler models on both result and exact-score likelihood from minute 30 onward (Figures 10–11); the team-value regressor's value decays by minute 75 (starting-lineup values become stale after substitutions — paper's own interpretation).
- Team strength: Palmeiras highest attack/defence ratio (1.4872), then Flamengo (1.3420); ratio correlates with mean league placement (Figure 9).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- Event times are minute-granularity (discrete); multiple events within one minute are collapsed — intensity estimates at fine time scales are coarse.
- The team-value regressor's predictive value decays over the match (stale after substitutions) — a static pre-match feature misused in-game; the paper acknowledges it but keeps it in G4.
- Minute-45 and minute-75 forecast evaluations are conditional on the team's *future* being simulated from a model whose parameters were fit pre-match — fine protocol-wise, but the in-sample parameter estimates for G4 are fit on the same seasons used for calibration checks (Tables 11–13), so those are in-sample fits, not out-of-sample (only §7.1's likelihood comparison is rolling-window).
- 33 Brazilian Série A teams, 8 seasons — no cross-league validation; attack/defence parameters are team-fixed, so relegation/promotion churn (several teams appear for only 1–2 seasons) makes some estimates noisy (Paraná: attack 0.0454 on 1 season).
- Red-card intensity is a pure function of time with no dependence on match state (score, fouls committed, referee identity) — a simplification; referee-specific effects (a known GSE gap for NFL crew effects) are absent.
- External validity to NFL: soccer goals ≈ NFL touchdowns in rarity, but NFL has no stoppage-time concept, no sendings-off equivalent, and a discrete play structure — the event-process *framework* transfers, the soccer-specific regressors do not.
- Adversarial note: the "fitting in <10 seconds" claim is for a smooth convex problem — the speed comes from convexity (the paper's real contribution), not from anything predictive; and AIC/BIC comparisons between non-nested stoppage models (S2 vs S5) are not strictly valid, which the authors concede.

## 10. GSE overlap
Extension — partially new. The map lists Poisson, Dixon-Coles, Skellam, Harville, Stern models as inventoried, and iWinRNFL (1704.00197) as the covered in-game NFL win-probability work. What this paper adds that the map does NOT cover: (a) the **Cox-process in-game framework with dynamic state-dependent intensities** as a general engine (beyond static pre-match models); (b) the **strict-concavity/convex-optimization guarantee** (Theorem 1) — fast, globally-optimal fitting of live models; (c) explicit modeling of event-shock regressors (red cards as intensity shocks → NFL analog: ejections, key injuries, weather shifts). The map's gap list item 7 names exactly this: "In-play / live NFL spread & total modeling — iWinRNFL covers in-game WP; live spread/total probability surfaces are thin." This paper is the methodological seed for filling gap 7. Not duplicate of iWinRNFL (that is a different method; this one adds convex fitting + event-shock dynamics).

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Build an NFL live-game Cox/Hawkes-style event model as GSE's live probability engine:
1. **Events J:** scoring events {home TD, away TD, home FG, away FG} + intensity shocks {key-player injury/ejection, weather change at open-air stadiums, referee flags}. Play-by-play from nflverse gives event times (game-clock mapped to elapsed time) — a cleaner input than the paper's minute granularity.
2. **Regressors ψ:** score differential, possession state (down/distance/field position), time remaining, timeouts remaining, pregame spread-implied strength (replacing Transfermarkt values with market ratings), turnover/injury shock indicators (analog of red cards), pace (plays per minute — analog of the half-time indicator).
3. **Fitting:** log-linear intensities per Theorem 1; the log-likelihood is concave → fit with any convex solver (the paper used MOSEK/CVXR in R; GSE can use cvxpy/Python or a custom Newton method). Refit intra-week on nflverse 2020–2025 play-by-play. Constraint handling: attack/defence normalization analogous to the paper's Σln α = Σln β.
4. **Forecasting:** replicate the paper's §7 simulation: from any live game state, simulate 100k forward paths of the event processes, take the earliest event, update state, repeat → live win probability, live spread/total distributions, live derivative prices.
5. **Serving:** pre-match fit runs weekly; in-game inference is simulation-based (embarrassingly parallel — the paper's 100k paths is the template); cache intensity parameters per matchup; stream nflverse live play-by-play (or ESPN API) as the state updater.
Estimated effort: 3–4 engineer-weeks for a v1 (reproduce paper on soccer data as the scaffold, then swap in nflverse regressors), plus validation work per §12.

## 12. Reproducible test
- **Step 1 (mechanism check):** reimplement the paper's model G4S5R on the Brazilian Série A data (reconstruct from Flashscore/CBF/Transfermarkt scrapes, or request the authors' dataset) and reproduce: red-card effect ≈ −30% own intensity, losing-by-one +10% goal rate, G4S5R draw rate ≈ 27.48% vs observed 27.42% on 2,611 matches. Tolerance: each headline number within ±2 percentage points.
- **Step 2 (NFL transfer):** fit an NFL analog on nflverse play-by-play 2020–2023 (train) → predict 2024–2025 in-game outcomes: at each quarter boundary, simulate 100k forward paths from the live state and score predictive log-likelihood of the final result vs two baselines: (a) static pregame-spread-implied probabilities carried through the game, (b) GSE's current live WP if any / nfl4th-style WP. Test window: full 2024–2025 regular seasons. Success metric: mean per-game log-likelihood improvement over the pregame-spread baseline, plus draw-equivalent calibration (no draws in NFL; use exact-margin distribution calibration instead).

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADAPT into the live engine only if: (a) the mechanism check reproduces the three headline effects within ±2 pp (confirms correct implementation); AND (b) the NFL transfer test beats the pregame-spread-carry baseline on mean per-game log-likelihood over the 2024–2025 test window by a statistically significant margin (paired t-test, p < 0.05), AND (c) calibration of the simulated final-margin distribution passes a χ² goodness-of-fit test at α = 0.05. If (b) or (c) fails, reject the transfer — the Cox framework stays as a soccer/analytics reference, not a GSE live component.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Two extensions beyond the paper: (1) **Self-exciting (Hawkes) terms for momentum bursts** — the paper assumes intensities depend on state but not on recent event *rate*; add a Hawkes kernel on recent scoring events (the map's gap #14: "Hawkes processes / self-exciting models — mentioned 1×; momentum/scoring-burst modeling absent"). Test whether a decaying excitation term improves in-game likelihood beyond the pure state-dependent Cox model — this is exactly the momentum question the GSE discovery lane has been probing (Koopman/DMD momentum was REJECTED; a Hawkes test is the clean next attempt). (2) **Market-implied regressors** — replace Transfermarkt values with live market-implied team strength (de-vigged moneyline/spread) as a dynamic regressor, updated as the market moves in-game; this fuses the paper's framework with GSE's market-relative learning lane and tests whether market information adds predictive power beyond game state.
