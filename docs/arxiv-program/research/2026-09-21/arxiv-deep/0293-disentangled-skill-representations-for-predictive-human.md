# [0293] Disentangled Skill Representations for Predictive Human Modeling (arXiv:2608.23776v1)

**Citation:** Schrum, M., Gopinath, D., Srivatsa, S., Rosman, G., & Chen, T. (2026). *Disentangled Skill Representations for Predictive Human Modeling*. Toyota Research Institute. arXiv:2608.23776v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23776v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 2358 lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — SAIL's persistent-skill embedding (participant-level z_s, novice–expert basis blending, counterfactual subskill swaps) is a strong template for NFL player-level skill representations from tracking data; the coaching downstream task is the transfer path (GSE content/coaching products), not betting directly.

## 1. Research question
Human skill — persistent, compositional, behaviorally grounded — must be inferred from patterns over time, not single observations. The authors ask how to learn a skill representation satisfying three desiderata: construct validity (stable across sessions, robust to trial noise), predictive utility (predicts behavior across in-domain contexts), and interpretability (decomposes into disentangled, human-recognizable subskills). They introduce SAIL (Skill Abstraction with Interpretable Latents) and test it on high-performance racing (primary) and baseball batting (supplemental), including a downstream AI-coaching task and validation against a professional coach's ratings.

## 2. Dataset / schema
Racing: 95 participants (novice→expert) in a driving simulator, 1,545 laps total — 70 participants × ≥10 laps on one track, 25 participants × 4 laps on 4 tracks; each trajectory = vehicle pose, speed, control signals downsampled to 100 points per track segment; context c = racetrack. Behaviorally grounded skill metrics (from expert coaches): peak lateral g-force in skidpad drill (vehicle handling), gaze fixation (gaze policy), written test scores (know-how of racing lines), etc., across six subskills: vehicle handling, gaze control, know-how, control inputs, physical ability, perceptual ability. Baseball: 13 semi-pro players, 74 batting trials (tee + pitching machine), whole-body kinematics from optical motion capture, 3 subskills (kinematic chain, pelvis pausing, thigh pausing); synthetic augmentation (time warping, noise injection, scaling) for data scarcity; contexts = tee vs machine-pitch. Coaching datasets: 2 simulator coaching studies, 38 participants (Sumner et al. 2026; Schrum et al. 2026), instructor-feedback labels. Human-subjects protocol WCG IRB approved June 2023.

## 3. Method / model
SAIL: (1) Participant-specific persistent embedding z_s ∈ ℝ^d, learnable, shared across all trajectories of a participant (init N(0,0.1)); at test time model parameters (g_φ, q_θ, h_ψ) frozen, only z_s optimized. (2) Novice–expert basis blending: per context c, canonical expert bases B_exp(c) = {B_exp^(i)(c)}_{i=1}^M (strongest performer's trajectories) and novice bases B_nov(c) (PCA on novice trajectories); g_φ maps z_s to simplex weights w_exp, w_nov and elementwise interpolation α(z_s,c) ∈ [0,1]^{T×D}; predicted behavior τ̂_{z_s} = α ⊙ B̄_exp + (1−α) ⊙ B̄_nov, with B̄_• = Σ_j w_•^(j) B_•^(j) (Eq. 1) — each subskill independently modulates its own blending coefficients. (3) Counterfactual subskill swaps: partition z_s = [z_s^(1),…,z_s^(K)]; with probability (1−p) per batch, replace one slice z_orig^(k) ← z_donor^(k) (and corresponding metric), reconstruct CF trajectory without ℒ_traj (no ground truth), apply metric loss only for swapped subskill — forcing each slice to encode its designated subskill. (4) Auxiliary mutual-information network q_θ reconstructing z_s from τ̂ (variational MI lower bound, prevents collapse). Total loss ℒ = λ_traj ℒ_traj + λ_metric ℒ_metric + λ_MI ℒ_MI (Eq. 2); ℒ_MI = −E_{τ̂∼p_φ(τ̂|z_s,c)}[log q_θ(z_s|τ̂)]; h_ψ is an LSTM predicting skill metrics from τ̂_{z_s}. Expert basis from top performer; novice basis via PCA.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Eq. 1: B̄_•(z_s,c) = Σ_j w_•^(j)(z_s,c) B_•^(j)(c), • ∈ {exp,nov}; τ̂_{z_s} = α ⊙ B̄_exp(z_s,c) + (1−α) ⊙ B̄_nov(z_s,c). Eq. 2: ℒ = λ_traj ℒ_traj + λ_metric ℒ_metric + λ_MI ℒ_MI. CF swap: z̃_orig^(k) = z_donor^(k), z̃_orig^(ℓ) = z_orig^(ℓ) ∀ℓ≠k; m̃ likewise. ℒ_MI = −E_{τ̂∼p_φ(τ̂|z_s,c)}[log q_θ(z_s|τ̂)]. Assumptions: skill persistent across sessions (no coaching occurred during collection, so skill assumed constant); contexts observed at train and test; skill transferable across in-domain contexts; behavior expressible as convex novice–expert blend; predefined noisy subskill metrics anchor semantics; smooth novice–expert continuum (authors note this may miss certain strategies); discretization of skill groups for silhouette.

## 5. Features / target
Features: racing trajectories (vehicle pose, speed, control signals @ 100 pts/segment); baseball kinematics (whole-body motion capture). Subskill metric supervision: racing — skidpad peak lateral g, gaze fixation, written-test scores, etc. (6 subskills); baseball — kinematic-chain sequencing, pelvis pause, thigh pause measures (3 subskills). Targets: (a) trajectory reconstruction/prediction (RMSE); (b) predicted skill metrics; (c) downstream instructor-feedback prediction (classification); (d) coach skill ratings (validation). Horizon: per-lap/per-trial behavior; context-level generalization across tracks/conditions.

## 6. Validation design
Composite desideratum scores (min–max normalized per metric across methods, lower-is-better reversed, summed). Baselines: SimCLR (contrastive), β-VAE, AE, AE-LC (autoencoder with linear metric constraints), ablations SAIL w/o CF, SAIL w/o basis. Metrics: construct validity — silhouette score ↑, test–retest similarity ↑; predictive utility — in-context RMSE ↓, out-of-context RMSE ↓ (train one track, test another); disentanglement — Alignment Ratio ↑ (slice predicts intended vs non-target metrics), Targeted Change Index ↑ (CF swaps concentrate change in targeted features), Relative Influence Ratio ↑ (per-subskill perturbation influence). Downstream: instructor-feedback prediction over 15 participant-held-out folds vs unconditioned and trial-time baselines (paired t-tests). External: Spearman correlation of SAIL skill percentile vs professional coach ratings on 22 held-out participants. Values = mean (SE) across evaluation folds.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Racing construct validity: SAIL silhouette .72 (.08), test–retest .995 (.003), overall construct score 1.86 — best. Predictive: in-context RMSE 2.75 (.12), OOC RMSE 6.37 (3.0), predictive score 2.0 — best (w/o-basis ablation nearly doubles error: 5.05/12.77). Disentanglement: AR 3.25 (.79), TCI .93 (.06), RIR 2.11 (.46), interpretability score 3.0 — best; removing CF collapses AR to 1.24 and interpretability score to 1.37. Baseball (supplemental): test–retest 1.000; predictive competitive (in-context RMSE .161); AR 0.758. Downstream coaching (Table 2): weighted F1 — SAIL .595 (.014) vs trial-time .573 (.014) vs none .541 (.015) — 10.0% relative improvement over unconditioned, paired t(14)=2.50, p=.025; accuracy .589 vs .567 vs .538 (p=.028); macro F1 .537 vs .520 vs .504 (only SAIL improves significantly, p<.001). Coach validation: Spearman ρ = 0.81, p<.001, 95% bootstrap CI [0.56, 0.94], n=22. β-VAE and AE baselines score near 0 on several composites, showing standard disentanglement fails to yield behaviorally grounded subskills.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
(a) Baseball domain is tiny (13 players, 74 trials) and synthetically augmented — generality claims rest mostly on racing. (b) Subskill metrics are noisy, predefined, and defined by the same expert coaches whose downstream judgments validate the model (circularity risk partially mitigated: coach ratings were collected independently of drill metrics). (c) Assumes smooth novice–expert continuum; team-sport roles and non-monotonic skill (e.g., high-risk styles) may break the basis-blending inductive bias. (d) The 10% coaching F1 improvement is relative to a weak unconditioned baseline (.541) — absolute gain is 5.4 pp weighted F1. (e) No betting/prediction-market application; racing is a solo time-trial, not an adversarial team sport — transfer to NFL player modeling requires re-deriving subskills (route-running, separation, tackling form) and contexts (coverage, down/distance). (f) Requires multimodal trajectory data (pose/kinematics) unavailable at scale for NFL — NGS tracking (positions/velocities) is the closest proxy.

## 10. GSE overlap
New capability. The corpus's tracking lane is thin: only STRAIN (2305.10262) read in depth; the NGS taxonomy (27 families, 2026-09-21) is inventoried but not modeled; the 15-area ML brief lists "representation learning on play-by-play" as commissioned with no results yet. No existing work learns persistent player-level skill embeddings from NFL tracking data. This paper gives GSE the architecture for exactly that: participant-level z_s from NGS tracking trajectories (cf. NGS route classification 2.0, separation, get-off), supervised by behaviorally grounded metrics (CPOE, YAC-over-expected, separation at catch) with CF swaps across subskills (e.g., QB: pocket presence / arm talent / decision speed). Duplicate check: none — no player-embedding work in repo or Drive dossiers.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
(1) Build a SAIL-for-NFL prototype: participant = player-season; trajectories = NGS tracking snippets (route-runner: release→break→catch point positions/velocities, or pass-rush: get-off→contact arcs); context c = (coverage shell, down/distance, field zone). (2) Expert/novice bases: expert basis from top-decile players by position on domain metrics (e.g., WR: separation; EDGE: STRAIN), novice basis via PCA on bottom-quartile trajectories. (3) Subskill slices supervised by existing repo metrics — QB slices: CPOE (accuracy), time-to-throw/aggressiveness (decision), pressure-to-sack (pocket); WR slices: separation at target, YAC-over-expected, contested-catch rate. (4) CF training: swap slices between players, supervise only the swapped metric. (5) Freeze embeddings and condition GSE's prop/PFF-style projection heads on z_s — the paper's 10% coaching-F1 lift suggests player-embedding conditioning can lift prop models beyond raw stat features. Data: NGS tracking (Big Data Bowl sample or licensed feed), nflverse, FTN charting. Effort: ~2 engineer-weeks for a single-position prototype (WR), leveraging existing tracking ingestion.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: NFL NGS tracking sample (e.g., 2023–2024 WR route trajectories, public Big Data Bowl or equivalent). Metric: (a) construct validity — test–retest similarity of player embeddings across weeks (target ≥ 0.90 vs ≤ 0.75 for a per-game autoencoder baseline); (b) predictive utility — held-out route-trajectory RMSE vs β-VAE and AE baselines; (c) interpretability — Alignment Ratio of slices to (separation, YACOE, contested rate). Baselines: AE, β-VAE per the paper. Window: 2023–2024 seasons, WR position only.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT the SAIL-NFL architecture for prop-model conditioning if on the 2023–2024 WR test: test–retest ≥ 0.90, in-context RMSE beats the AE baseline by ≥15% relative, and AR ≥ 2.0 (slices align to intended metrics at 2× non-target); REJECT if embeddings are no more stable than per-game AE embeddings (test–retest gap < 0.05) or AR < 1.2 — i.e., the representation adds nothing over existing stat features.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Go beyond the paper by making the embedding *adversarial*: jointly learn team/defensive-scheme embeddings as opposing bases (offense z_s blended against defensive-scheme basis), so a WR's skill embedding is explicitly decomposed into player skill vs scheme/coverage difficulty — the paper's contexts are passive (racetracks), but NFL contexts are adversarial. This directly serves GSE's matchup modeling: conditioning props on matchup-adjusted skill rather than raw skill removes the "good stats vs bad defenses" confound the paper never addresses.
