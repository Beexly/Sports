# [1566] Z-scores-based methods and their application to biological monitoring: An extended analysis of professional soccer players and cyclists athletes (arXiv:2510.01810)

**Citation:** Geoffroy Berthelot, Brigitte Gelein, Éric Meinadier, Emmanuel Orhant, Jérôme Dedecker (2025). *Z-scores-based methods and their application to biological monitoring: An extended analysis of professional soccer players and cyclists athletes*. arXiv:2510.01810. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01810
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (ar5iv HTML).
**Verdict:** ADAPT

the paper is anti-doping biological-passport work, not injury prediction, but its intra-individual change-point Z-score machinery (Tₙ⁽²⁾ with Monte Carlo critical values, DevianLM Gaussian-linear-model extension for seasonality/trend covariates) is directly portable to GSE's longitudinal athlete monitoring: flagging fatigue/overtraining regime shifts in NGS speed, snap loads, and practice participation.

## 1. Research question
How can abnormal values be detected in longitudinal biological sequences of athletes at the individual level, accounting for intra-individual variability, biomarker correlation, seasonality, and chronological drift — with interpretable "white box" statistics whose null distributions are known (or tabulated) rather than black-box ML? Applied to anti-doping-style biological monitoring (athlete biological passport logic) in pro soccer players and cyclists.

## 2. Dataset / schema
Two datasets, ethics-approved, all male. Soccer: 3,936 players from French Ligue 1 & 2, 5 biomarkers (ferritin μmol/L, serum iron μmol/L, hemoglobin g/L, erythrocytes T/L, hematocrit %), sampled every ~6 months (July/August and January/March), 2006-2019, 27 collection waves. Cycling: 1,683 athletes (1,247 amateur + 436 professional; disciplines: road 1052, MTB XC 143, BMX 105, downhill 60, cyclo-cross 49, track sprint 38, trials 30, pursuit 40, multi 6+160), ages 13.30-43.54 (mean 24.07), 10 biomarkers (ferritin, hemoglobin, erythrocytes, hematocrit, reticulocytes G/L and %, OFF-hr score, IGF1 ng/mL, cortisol nmol/L, testosterone nmol/L, osteocalcin ng/mL), 2003-01-09 to 2014-02-12, 99.92% of samplings ≥6 days apart. Proprietary (French federations: FFF Clairefontaine, French Cycling Federation medical dept., INSEP); not publicly available. Minimum sequence lengths for analysis: n≥3/4 depending on statistic.

## 3. Method / model
Pipeline (Figure 1): (1) normality preprocessing — for each biomarker, try transformations {identity, m-th root m=2..10, log, Lambert W₀, Box-Cox with λ∈{−0.0606, 0.0202, −0.3030}}; per individual sequence with ≥4 obs run Shapiro test; collect p-values across individuals; KS-test the p-value vector against Uniform[0,1]; pick the transformation maximizing the KS p-value (Table 1 lists per-biomarker winners, e.g., ferritin: 6th-root soccer / 5th-root cycling). (2) Four Z-score statistics: Tₙ⁽⁰⁾ = (Xₙ−X̄ₙ₋₁)/(σ̂ₙ₋₁√(1+1/(n−1))) ~ Student(n−2) under H₀ (new-observation test); Tₙ⁽¹⁾ = maxᵢ leave-one-out studentized deviation (Eq. 3-4) — single abnormal observation anywhere in the sequence; Tₙ⁽²⁾ = max over all intervals I of |X̄_I − X̄_Ī|/(σ̂_{n,I}√(1/|I|+1/(n−|I|))) (Eq. 5-7) — abnormal *consecutive subsequence* (change-point style); Tₙ⁽³⁾ = (n−1)/(nd)·maxᵢ Mahalanobis (Xᵢ−X̄_{n,−i})′C_{n,−i}⁻¹(Xᵢ−X̄_{n,−i}) (Eq. 8-9) — multivariate correlated biomarkers (tuples: {ferritin, serum iron}; {erythrocytes, hemoglobin, hematocrit}). (3) DevianLM extension: Tₙ⁽⁴⁾ = maxᵢ external studentized residual |êᵢ(X)| under Gaussian linear model Xᵢ=(Mθ)ᵢ+εᵢ (Eq. 10-11); null distribution parameter-free but design-dependent → tabulated by Monte Carlo. Three designs: A: Xᵢ=β₀+εᵢ (≡Tₙ⁽¹⁾); B: Xᵢ=β₀+β₁sᵢ+εᵢ with summer/winter indicator (ANOVA, seasonality); C: Xᵢ=β₀+β₁tᵢ+εᵢ with exact sampling date (drift; cycling only). Significance α=0.05 expected false-positive rate.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
- Tₙ⁽⁰⁾=(Xₙ−X̄ₙ₋₁)/(σ̂ₙ₋₁√(1+1/(n−1))), with X̄ₙ₋₁=(n−1)⁻¹ΣXₖ, σ̂²ₙ₋₁=(n−2)⁻¹Σ(Xₖ−X̄ₙ₋₁)² (Eq. 1-2); reject H₀ if |tₙ⁽⁰⁾|>t_{α/2,n−2}.
- Tₙ⁽¹⁾=maxᵢ|Xᵢ−X̄_{n,−i}|/(σ̂_{n,−i}√(1+1/(n−1))) (Eq. 3-4).
- Tₙ⁽²⁾=max_{I∈ℐ}|X̄_I−X̄_Ī|/(σ̂_{n,I}√(1/|I|+1/(n−|I|))), ℐ = all intervals 1≤|I|<n (Eq. 5-7).
- Tₙ⁽³⁾=(n−1)/(nd)·maxᵢ(Xᵢ−X̄_{n,−i})′C_{n,−i}⁻¹(Xᵢ−X̄_{n,−i}), C_{n,−i}=(n−1−d)⁻¹Σ(Xₖ−X̄)(Xₖ−X̄)′ (Eq. 8-9).
- Gaussian linear model Xᵢ=(Mθ)ᵢ+εᵢ, εᵢ iid N(0,σ²) (Eq. 10); Tₙ⁽⁴⁾=maxᵢ|Xᵢ−X̂_{n,i}|/(σ̂_{n,i}√(1+Lᵢ(M′_{(i)}M_{(i)})⁻¹Lᵢ′))=maxᵢ|êᵢ(X)| (Eq. 11).
- Assumptions: iid Gaussian after transformation (checked via Shapiro+KS pipeline, though KS p-values all tiny — authors note true abnormalities contaminate the test); sequences treated as independent; no serial correlation (flagged as a limitation); minimum-n conditions per statistic (Tₙ⁽²⁾: n≥4; Tₙ⁽³⁾: n≥d+2; DevianLM: n>p+1).

## 5. Features / target
Features: longitudinal per-athlete biomarker sequences (after per-biomarker transformation); design covariates: season indicator (model B), exact date (model C). Target: binary flag "sequence contains an abnormal observation/subsequence" at α=0.05; no supervised injury label — the "target" is deviation from the athlete's own baseline. Horizon: retrospective detection over the full sequence (not forecasting). Multivariate tuples tested jointly.

## 6. Validation design
No train/test split and no labeled ground truth (no confirmed doping/injury labels) — validation is by false-positive-rate calibration: at α=0.05, the expected abnormal-sequence proportion under H₀ is 5%; observed proportions far exceeding 5% are interpreted as real biological signal. Results reported as proportions per biomarker × statistic × dataset (Figures 3-4), plus breakdowns by amateur/professional status and cycling discipline (Table 2, Figure 5). No baseline method comparison (e.g., vs. the ABP's own adaptive model); no time-ordered evaluation; the Monte Carlo tabulation of null distributions is the statistical-validity anchor. Baselines: the 5% nominal rate is the reference.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Exact key numbers: Tₙ⁽²⁾ (subsequence/change-point statistic) detects far above the 5% nominal rate — ferritin: 26.67% (cycling), 18.67% (soccer); IGF1: 35.97% (cycling); i.e., "more than three times the false positive rate" for ferritin and IGF1 in both datasets. Among status-switchers: ferritin Tₙ⁽²⁾ 89/158 (56.33%), IGF1 Tₙ⁽²⁾ 96/135 (71.11%). Multivariate Tₙ⁽³⁾ {erythrocytes, hemoglobin, hematocrit}: amateurs 53/604 (8.77%), professionals 27/160 (16.88%). Model A/B/C single-outlier stats mostly 3-14% (closer to nominal). Constant (malfunctioning-device) hemoglobin sequences: 4.45% soccer, 4.75% cycling. KS normality p-values for chosen transforms are all tiny (e.g., ferritin soccer 6.79×10⁻¹⁶) except osteocalcin (0.841). No injury/doping outcome prediction numbers — there are none in the paper.

## 8. Code / data availability
The DevianLM R package implements Tₙ⁽⁴⁾ (reference [27]: "Devian-lm: An R package for detecting abnormal values in the gaussian linear model"; acknowledged help from Arthur Simon). No GitHub URL printed in the text I read. Data: proprietary federation data, not shared. Methods fully specified mathematically, so reimplementation is feasible.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
No ground-truth labels: "abnormal" is defined purely statistically — the high ferritin/IGF1 detection rates are interpreted post hoc as training/inflammation effects, but could equally be model misspecification (the KS p-values show normality is rejected everywhere, so the 5% nominal rate may itself be miscalibrated). Intra-individual serial correlation ignored (authors' own limitation; samplings days apart for cycling). No training-load, nutrition, altitude, pathology, or medication covariates — all known biomarker drivers; models B/C only crudely proxy seasonality/time. Input errors in collection unaccounted. External validity to NFL: zero biomarker data exists publicly for NFL players, and teams won't share; the soccer sampling cadence (6-monthly) is nothing like weekly NFL monitoring. The multivariate statistic needs n≥d+2 — fine. The paper's framing is anti-doping, so the "abnormality" semantics (pathological/doping) don't transfer to fatigue without re-derivation.

## 10. GSE overlap
No existing GSE intra-individual anomaly-detection layer in the research map; the closest existing concepts are workload monitoring (gap #9) and NGS-derived per-player trends. Not a duplicate. The transferable asset is the statistical framework, not the biomarker application: GSE has longitudinal per-player series (NGS max speed/acceleration, snap counts, practice status, weekly fantasy output) where a Tₙ⁽²⁾-style change-point test could flag regime shifts (fatigue, undisclosed injury, role change) relative to the player's own baseline — with seasonality handled the way model B handles summer/winter (e.g., early-season vs late-season baselines). The Gaussian-linear-model extension (DevianLM) lets GSE add covariates (opponent strength, game script) into the baseline before testing for abnormality.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Build `gse-athlete-monitor`: (1) Per-player longitudinal series: NGS max speed per game, burst accelerations, snap share, practice participation score, weekly PPR output — each as its own sequence with ≥4 observations. (2) Apply the paper's transformation-selection pipeline per metric (or default log/root transforms) to approximate normality. (3) Implement Tₙ⁽²⁾ (interval change-point scan) with Monte Carlo critical values under the player's own history; flag players whose recent 2-4 game subsequence deviates at α=0.05 (fatigue/overtraining/role-change alert). (4) DevianLM-style design: baseline model with opponent-strength and home/away covariates (the analog of model B/C seasonality) so flags are opponent-adjusted, not raw. (5) Multivariate Tₙ⁽³⁾ over correlated NGS metrics {max speed, accelerations, distance} jointly. (6) Serving: weekly "workload anomaly" flags feed the injury-risk overlay and prop projections (e.g., downgrade a WR whose burst metrics just entered an abnormal low subsequence). Effort: ~1 week Python implementation (statistics are simple; Monte Carlo tabulation is the bulk), 2 weeks with backtest.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: nflverse + NGS 2021-2024, WR/RB with ≥8 games/season; series = per-game max speed (NGS) and snap share. Baseline to beat: naive z-score vs season mean (the "population baseline" the paper criticizes) and a simple 3-game rolling-mean rule. Metric: for players who subsequently missed ≥1 game to injury (nflverse injury reports) within 3 weeks, compare flag precision/recall of Tₙ⁽²⁾ vs baselines; also false-positive rate on players with no injury absence (should be ≈5% nominal). Window: train/calibrate 2021-2022, test 2023-2024. The test is retrospective detection, matching the paper's design.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT the anomaly monitor if: (a) on 2023-2024 holdout, Tₙ⁽²⁾ flags precede injury absence with recall ≥25% at ≤10% flag rate among healthy player-weeks (a real early-warning signal, not noise); (b) opponent-adjusted (DevianLM-style) flags beat raw flags by ≥5pp precision; (c) the nominal 5% false-positive rate holds within ±2pp on the healthy cohort (validating the Monte Carlo calibration on NFL data). Otherwise reject — intra-individual anomaly detection stays out of the prediction stack. Never present flags as injury predictions publicly; they are internal risk inputs only.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Extend Tₙ⁽²⁾ from retrospective to prospective: instead of scanning all intervals, test only the most recent k-game suffix (k=2,3,4) against the player's history — a sequential monitoring (CUSUM-like) variant with alpha-spending across weeks. This converts the paper's forensic tool into a live weekly alert with controlled false-alarm rate. Then correlate flagged suffixes with next-week fantasy underperformance (not just injury): if flagged players systematically underperform projections by ≥1.5 PPR points, the monitor becomes a projection adjustment, not just a risk flag — strictly beyond the paper.
