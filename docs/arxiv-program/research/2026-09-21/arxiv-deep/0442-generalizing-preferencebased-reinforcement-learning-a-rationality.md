# [0442] Generalizing Preference-based Reinforcement Learning: a Rationality Model for Incomparability (arXiv:2607.11432v1)

**Citation:** Simone Drago, Marco Mussi, Leonardo Bianconi, Alberto Maria Metelli (2026). *Generalizing Preference-based Reinforcement Learning: a Rationality Model for Incomparability*. arXiv:2607.11432v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11432v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (§§1–5, Appendices A–D, references; 11,289 text lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — the RL formalism doesn't transfer, but the core idea (learn a multi-dimensional preference model from pairwise comparisons that include an explicit "incomparable" label, recovering a Pareto frontier) adapts to GSE pick selection: edge vs cover-probability vs variance are conflicting objectives, and Garrett's historical pick approvals/abstentions are comparison feedback over that frontier.

## 1. Research question
The paper asks: how should preference-based RL be generalized when a human expert can label trajectory pairs as *incomparable* (∥) — neither alternative dominates the other — in addition to preferring (≻/≺) or being indifferent (≍)? Standard PbRL assumes a scalar utility function and discards incomparable pairs as bad data; the paper argues incomparability is rational when multiple objectives conflict (e.g., speed vs safety) and introduces Comparison-based RL (CbRL): the human-feedback counterpart of multi-objective RL, recovering a Pareto frontier of policies from comparison feedback alone.

## 2. Dataset / schema
No real datasets. Simulated evaluation only (justified by lack of human-labeled incomparability data). Environments (Appendix D.2): (a) custom 5×5 GridWorld with 3 objectives — obstacles in the topmost and bottommost cells of the middle column ((0,2), (4,2)); agent starts at (4,0); actions {U,D,L,R,S}; per-step rewards: reach target cell (4,4): +1 (−0.1 otherwise); reach top row: −0.1·x_{h+1}; avoid obstacles: −0.5 if adjacent to an obstacle, 0 otherwise; feature map φ(τ) ∈ ℝ²⁵ = state-visitation counts. (b) 1-D linear quadratic regulator with 2 costs: x_{h+1} = a x_h + b u_h + η_h, η_h∼N(0,1), a=0.9, b=1.0; cost vector c_h = (q x_h², r u_h²), q=1.5, r=2.0; φ(τ) ∈ ℝ² = (mean squared state, mean squared action); chosen because the true Pareto frontier has closed form. (c) multi-objective Hopper from the MO-Gymnasium suite (mo-hopper-2obj-v5): objectives = forward velocity (x-axis) and height (z-axis); φ(τ) = Σ_{h=1}^H s_h ∈ ℝ¹¹. Datasets are synthetic: behavioral policies generate a trajectory pool (GridWorld: one exploratory policy; MO-Hopper: a suite from sampled scalarization weights incl. near-optimal and suboptimal policies; LQR: random feedback gains); K trajectory pairs sampled (K=500–10,000); a synthetic "rational expert" (SimTeacher-based, with parametrizable irrationality ε) generates M labels per pair (M=1–10), so N=KM total comparisons (up to 100,000). The expert computes scores via eqs. 7–10 from the true per-step multi-dimensional reward vectors (hidden from the model) and samples labels from the softmax. Code "provided in supplementary material, will be made public after acceptance" — not a public URL in the paper.

## 3. Method / model
- Formalism: Markov Decision Process with Comparisons (MDPC, Def. 2.1): tuple (S,A,H,p,μ,ρ), where ρ: T²→Δ(C), C={≻,≺,≍,∥} is the comparison probability distribution. Expert rationality: ρ(τ∘τ′) ≔ f(∘|δ(τ,τ′)) with δ(τ,τ′)=u(τ)−u(τ′), u: T→R^d a multi-dimensional utility, f: R^d→Δ(C) a "rationality model."
- Desiderata (Def. 2.2): along the main diagonal δ→+∞1_d (full dominance) the modal outcome must be ≻; δ→−∞1_d → ≺; δ→0_d → indifference ≍; along every non-standard diagonal δ→+∞t, t∈{−1,1}^d\{1_d,−1_d} (conflicting objectives, non-dominance) the modal outcome must be incomparability ∥.
- Impossibility (Prop. 2.1): no f satisfies the desiderata AND has convex −log f(∘|δ) in δ for all ∘ — because the modal outcome must "change twice" along any non-standard diagonal (∥ at ±∞ → ≍ at the origin); non-convexity is intrinsic.
- MOBT (multi-objective Bradley–Terry, eqs. 6–10): f(∘|τ,τ′) = softmax over scores h_∘: h_≻ = 1_d^⊤δ/(2d); h_≺ = −1_d^⊤δ/(2d); h_≍ = α (constant); h_∥ = √d·std(δ(τ,τ′)) + β, where std is the population standard deviation of the utility-difference vector (dispersion across objectives = conflict measure); α,β∈R learnable, Lemma 3.1: α>0, α>β ⇒ desiderata hold (h_∥ grows faster than h_≻/h_≺ along non-standard diagonals). Remark 3.1: MOBT reduces to classical BT (sigmoid) when d=1 with α=β=−∞.
- Learning (eq. 11): MLE θ̂ ∈ argmin_θ L̂(θ|D) = −Σ log f(∘i|W(φ(τi)−φ(τi′))) under linear-utility Assumption 3.1 (u(τ)=Wφ(τ), W∈R^{d×k}) and boundedness Assumption 3.2 (θ*∈[−R,R]^{dk+2}, ‖φ(τ)−φ(τ′)‖₂≤Λ), optimized with ADAM. Number of objectives d assumed known (overestimation allowed, footnote).
- Appendix A (extended related works): PbRL's two approaches — reward learning (11) vs direct policy optimization (42, DPO); this work uses the former for a multi-objective reward model. The closest prior multi-objective PbRL (33) elicits preferences under a scalarization weight handed to the expert; the authors argue this burdens the expert with numerical reasoning, which MOBT avoids. Alternative rationality models catalogued: Plackett-Luce (40,32), Thurstone-Mosteller (55, Gaussian noise), rationality parameter (24), infinite preference model (22), general preference optimization (64). Incomparability-as-abstention is rational expert behavior (45, 48, 21); standard PbRL practice discards incomparables (11); recent work (2) exploits them for Pareto frontiers.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
- MDPC + rationality: ρ(τ∘τ′) ≔ f(∘|δ(τ,τ′)), δ(τ,τ′) ≔ u(τ)−u(τ′), f: R^d→Δ(C).
- Desiderata (eqs. 1–4): argmax_∘ lim_{δ→+∞1_d} f(∘|δ)={≻}; lim_{δ→−∞1_d} →{≺}; lim_{δ→0_d} →{≍}; lim_{δ→+∞t} →{∥} for all t∈{−1,1}^d\{1_d,−1_d}.
- Learning goal (eq. 5): (f̂,û) ∈ argmin_{f∈F,u∈U} L̂(f,u|D) ≔ −Σ_{(τi,τi′,∘i)∈D} log f(∘i|u(τi)−u(τi′)).
- Proposition 2.1: no f satisfies Def. 2.2 with −log f(∘|δ) convex in δ for all ∘.
- MOBT scores (eqs. 6–10): f(∘|τ,τ′) = exp(h_∘)/Σ_{∘′} exp(h_{∘′}); h_≻=1_d^⊤δ/(2d); h_≺=−1_d^⊤δ/(2d); h_≍=α; h_∥=√d·std(δ)+β, with std(x)=‖x−x̄1_n‖₂/√n.
- Sample complexity (Thm 3.2, eq. 80): with probability ≥1−δ, D_KL(P_{θ*}∥P_{θ̂}) = L(θ̂)−L(θ*) ≤ 5ν√(((dk+2)log(12RL√N/ν)+log(1/δ))/N) — global optimum, where Lemma C.1 gives Lipschitz constant L=8Λ(√d+2) and Lemma C.2 gives subgaussian parameter ν=1+10RΛ√(dk); (Thm 3.3, eq. 95): for a local minimum θ̂_loc via Lemma C.3 (f(x_loc)−f(x_glob) ≤ 2c+d²/(2λ)), the bound becomes ≤ 60(P_{θ*}(∥)+√(log(2/δ)/2N))·Λd√(k+1)·R + 2C with C=Õ(RΛdk√(log(1/δ)/N)), where P_{θ*}(∥) = marginal incomparability probability ≤ √d·E_{(τ,τ′)∼Q}[std(δ(τ,τ′))]+β (when β≥0) — "expected std of the utility-difference vector acts as an index of conflict."
- Appendix B: h_∥ derived as the L2 distance of δ(τ,τ′) from its projection on the standard diagonal, h_∥(τ,τ′)=‖δ−δ̄1_d‖₂ (δ̄ = mean of δ), plus learnable β; the projection is δ̄1_d by orthogonality (eqs. 14–20).
- Assumptions: linear feature-based utility (3.1), bounded parameters/feature differences (3.2), i.i.d. comparisons from P_{θ*} (Thm 3.2 holds under misspecification w.r.t. best-in-class), d known, non-convex optimization so no parameter-distance guarantees.

## 5. Features / target
- Features: trajectory-level feature map φ(τ)=Σ_h φ(s_h,a_h) ∈R^k (domain-engineered or unsupervised pre-trained); comparison triples (τi,τi′,∘i).
- Target: the 4-class comparison label ∘∈{≻,≺,≍,∥} generated per eq. 6; learned object is the multi-dimensional utility matrix W∈R^{d×k} plus α,β — i.e., a per-objective reward function.

## 6. Validation design
Simulated only: train/test split of synthetic comparison datasets (20% randomly held out); metric = test KL divergence between estimated and true comparison distributions over C (10 runs, mean ± 95% CI). Baselines (Fig. 4 + Appendix D.4 extended comparison): standard BT (discarding indifferences+incomparabilities), Thurstone-Mosteller, Rao-Kupper (incomparabilities discarded vs treated as indifferences), Davidson (same two variants), K=2000, M=1 on GridWorld; TV distance as the common metric since baselines' KL is +∞ under support mismatch. Policy-level evaluation on LQR: estimate Pareto-optimal policies from the recovered reward, compare induced vs true Pareto frontier via hypervolume (HV) ratio ∈[0,1]. Robustness: SimTeacher mistake probability ε∈[0,1] (random label w.p. ε), K varied, KL vs the noiseless true distribution. Implementation (Appendix D.3): Python 3.10 + PyTorch, three modules (expert, optimizer, environment), .yaml configs for the four pipeline stages, LQR as a self-contained Jupyter notebook; README + bash scripts; run on a 10-core ARM CPU with 16GB RAM. Hyperparameters (Table 3): ADAM lr 1e-3 (not tuned per environment), batch 256, max 5000 epochs, validation split 0.2, early-stopping patience 10 — early stopping serves a dual purpose (implicit regularizer + halting once settled into a local minimum). No real human data; no time-ordered splits.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
- Reward reconstruction (GridWorld, N=1000): MOBT recovers the true 3-objective reward matrices up to a translation (translation-invariance expected for BT-based models).
- Test KL (Table 2, mean ±95% CI): GridWorld — K=500/M=1: 0.17±0.01 → K=5000/M=10: 0.04±0.01; MO-Hopper — K=1000/M=1: 0.15±0.02 → K=10000/M=10: 0.05±0.01. KL decreases with N=KM; increasing pairs ≈ increasing labels per pair only if trajectories span the feature space.
- Baseline comparison (Fig. 4): BT and Rao-Kupper recover a 1-D reward matching one objective combination and cannot traverse the Pareto frontier; MOBT recovers the multi-dimensional reward natively.
- Pareto frontier recovery (LQR): HV ratio 0.86 (±0.28) at 1000 pairs → 0.96 (±0.21) at 5000 pairs.
- Robustness (Fig. 6): KL grows with ε as expected (MOBT cannot distinguish mistakes from rational samples); larger K partially compensates.
- Multiple initializations (Appendix D.4, Table 4, N=5000, 100 optimizations per ratio = 10 seeds × 10 random inits): training loss (mean ±95% CI) and relative std for incomparability ratio p ∈ {0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6}: 0.84±0.0016 (rel 0.0010), 1.02±0.0014 (0.0006), 1.01±0.0298 (0.0147), 0.91±0.0600 (0.0329). Optimization-landscape complexity (number of local optima) grows with p, but the spread stays within an acceptable range even at p=0.6.
- Extended baseline comparison (Appendix D.4, Table 5, GridWorld, N=2000 pairs, TV distance between expert's and model's comparison-label distributions, mean ±95% CI): BT 0.6113±0.0036, TM 0.6115±0.0036, RK (∥ discarded) 0.4022±0.0040, RK (∥ as ≍) 0.4613±0.0046, Davidson (∥ discarded) 0.4014±0.0040, Davidson (∥ as ≍) 0.4594±0.0046, MOBT 0.0435±0.0139 — an order of magnitude lower than all baselines. For the baselines, KL divergence on the test set (all four label types; test-time discarding would be formally incorrect) is +∞ wherever the expert assigns f(∥|τ,τ′)>0 — a support mismatch; TV underestimates the baselines' failure since it can only boundedly penalize support mismatch. Mislabeling incomparabilities as ties performs worse than discarding them (RK 0.4613 vs 0.4022; Davidson 0.4594 vs 0.4014).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- No real data of any kind; all validation is on synthetic experts in 2–3-objective simulated environments. Generalization to real human feedback, high-d problems, or any applied domain (including sports) is unverified.
- Open challenge acknowledged by authors: informativeness of comparisons decreases with d; credit-assignment ambiguity across objectives in high dimensions; tradeoff between representational power and human cognitive strain is unresolved.
- Local optima: Theorem 3.3's additive error grows with the incomparability rate; in high-conflict settings the guarantee weakens exactly where the model matters most.
- Number of objectives d assumed known (overestimation heuristic cited but not validated in the paper's experiments).
- External validity to NFL: none of the environments is a decision problem resembling sports betting; the transfer path is conceptual (multi-objective pick selection), not empirical.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Adapt, not adopt: (1) define GSE pick objectives d=3: expected edge (CLV-based), cover probability, and variance/Kelly-fraction — explicitly conflicting (a +EV longshot vs a high-probability short price); (2) construct comparison feedback from Garrett's history: for each posted/published vs considered-but-skipped pick, record (posted≻skipped); for pairs of considered picks where neither was posted (both appeared in draft pools), record ∥ (incomparable/abstained); (3) featurize picks (edge, prob, odds, Kelly fraction, sport, market liquidity); (4) fit MOBT-style softmax over the 3–4 comparison outcomes to recover a per-objective utility vector — i.e., learn Garrett's implicit weighting of edge vs safety vs variance; (5) at serving time, recover the pick Pareto frontier per slate and only post picks that are non-dominated or that the learned utility ranks above the abstain threshold. Validation on 2024–2025 pick logs. Effort: ~2 weeks for the comparison-log dataset + MOBT fit, ~1 week for the frontier-based posting filter; no RL training needed — only the preference model.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: GSE's own posted-pick logs 2024–2025 (posted picks, draft-pool picks not posted, with their engine scores). Baseline: current scalar pick-score ranking (post top-k). Metric: (a) fit quality — test-set log-likelihood of comparison outcomes (posted≻skipped, ∥) under MOBT vs under a scalar BT model fit on the same pairs; (b) policy-level — hypervolume of the posted-pick Pareto frontier in (edge, cover-prob, 1/variance) space. Time window: fit on 2024, evaluate comparison-prediction log-likelihood on 2025 H1, policy HV on 2025 H2.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT the multi-objective selection layer if on 2025 H1 the MOBT model achieves ≥0.05 nats/comparison better test log-likelihood than the scalar BT baseline (showing incomparability/abstention is informative, not noise) AND the Pareto-frontier filter on 2025 H2 yields posted-pick CLV ≥ the scalar-ranking baseline's CLV with no worse cover rate. REJECT if the ∥ labels are uninformative (MOBT ≈ BT log-likelihood), or if d must exceed 3 to fit (high-d credit-assignment problem the authors flag), or if the comparison dataset is too sparse (<500 usable pairs) to estimate W reliably.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Beyond the paper: close the loop the authors leave open — online pair selection. Implement an active-learning rule that proposes pick pairs for Garrett to compare/abstain on, choosing pairs that maximize expected information gain about the objective weights W (uncertainty sampling on the posterior of vec(W)), with a budget of ≤5 comparisons per slate. This directly attacks the paper's open challenge (minimizing human interactions while maximizing accuracy over the Pareto-optimal region) and is testable: compare weight-estimate convergence rate vs random pair selection on the 2025 comparison log.
