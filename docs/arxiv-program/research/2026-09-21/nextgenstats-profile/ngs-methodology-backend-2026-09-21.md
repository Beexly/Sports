========== HOW NGS METRICS ARE CALCULATED — PER-METRIC FINDINGS ==========

**1. Run Scheme Classification** (new for 2026 season)
- Verbatim: "Our new run scheme classification model uses a transformer architecture to interpret the spatial and temporal relationships among players throughout a play. The primary output is a label that represents the intended play design (one of 16 labels), with these concepts also being identified as man, zone and gap schemes. The model also adds secondary tags, such as read option, split zone and pitch, and identifies both the intended run gap and the gap the ball-carrier actually hits."
- Also verbatim: "Separate companion models identify play-action concepts and each defender's gap responsibility."
- Inputs: player locations captured 10 times/second. No formula, feature list, label enumeration, or accuracy figure published. Built with AWS Professional Services (AWS ProServe).
- Source: https://www.nfl.com/news/next-gen-stats-new-advanced-metrics-you-need-to-know-for-the-2026-nfl-season (The Next Gen Stats Analytics Team, Sep 7, 2026)

**2. Run Blocking Matchups & Metrics** (new for 2026)
- Verbatim: "Our new run blocking matchup model identifies each offensive player's blocking assignment, the type of block he executes -- a pull, down block or crack block, among others -- and when the engagement begins and ends."
- Derived measurements described: double-team frequency per defender, time to shed a block, defenders disrupting runs without tackle credit, blocks that spring a runner into the open field.
- No architecture, features, or equations published.
- Source: same nfl.com 2026 article as above.

**3. Route Classification 2.0** (2026)
- Verbatim: "We debuted our original Route Recognition model in 2020. This offseason, we retrained it with a more detailed route tree. We can now separate shallow and deep crossing routes, while also distinguishing fade routes that were previously grouped with go routes. We can more accurately detect what constitutes a screen by accounting for blocker movement and the receiver's route path. What's more, we've added inside and outside release classification to shine more light on a receiver's first few steps off the line."
- No architecture/features/equations published for v1 or v2.
- Source: same nfl.com 2026 article as above.

**4. Completion Probability** (debuted 2018) + CPOE
- Verbatim (nfl.com, Sep 21, 2018): "Completion Probability is measured using more than 10 different in-play factors collected by Next Gen Stats player-tracking devices. Those inputs include pass air distance (from quarterback to receiver), air yards, the distance between the receiver and the nearest defender, the distance between the quarterback and the nearest pass rusher, the speed of the quarterback at throw, among several other metrics."
- Six named factors with measured relationships to completion likelihood: Air Distance; Target Separation (distance to nearest defender); Sideline Separation; Pass Rush Separation (distance from QB to nearest rusher); Passer Speed; Time to Throw. (My inference: only ~6 of 10+ factors named publicly.)
- Model: XGBoost ML model hosted on Amazon SageMaker (per amazon.science, Feb 2026 — "blended the factors that shape a throw's outcome, from quarterback pressure to throw depth, receiver separation, and sideline proximity"). AWS 2021 article: "factors in 10 on-field measurements — including the distance of the pass, distance between the quarterback and the nearest pass rushers, and distance between the receiver and the nearest defenders — and outputs the (league-average) likelihood of completing a pass under those conditions."
- Training/validation: 36,000+ pass attempts back to 2016; validated on a random 10% holdout; Actual Completion % vs Completion Probability correlation r-squared = 0.98.
- CPOE: expected_completion_percentage = "Using a passer's Completion Probability on every play, determine what a passer's completion percentage is expected to be"; completion_percentage_above_expectation = "A passer's actual completion percentage compared to their Expected Completion Percentage" (nflreadr data dictionary).
- Sources: https://www.nfl.com/news/next-gen-stats-introduction-to-completion-probability-0ap3000000964655 ; https://www.amazon.science/blog/a-decade-of-nfl-next-gen-stats-innovation ; https://www.amazon.science/latest-news/how-aws-scientists-help-create-the-nfls-next-gen-stats ; https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/dictionary_nextgen_stats.html

**5. Expected Rushing Yards (xRY) / Rushing Yards Over Expected (RYOE)** (debuted 2020)
- Verbatim definitions (nfl.com, Jul 20, 2020): xRY — "How many rushing yards is a ball-carrier expected to gain on a given carry based on the relative location, speed and direction of blockers and defenders?" RYOE — "The difference between actual rushing yards and expected rushing yards on an individual play or series of plays." RYOE/Att — per rush attempt. ROE (Rush Pct Over Expected) — "The percentage of runs where a ball-carrier gained more yards than expected." First Down Probability — "The likelihood a ball-carrier will gain at least enough yards for a first down from the moment of handoff." Touchdown Probability — "The likelihood a ball-carrier will score a touchdown at the moment of handoff."
- Model: 2D convolutional neural network by Philipp Singer & Dmitry Gordeev ("The Zoo"), winners of the 2020 Big Data Bowl (>2,000 entrants, $75K prizes), scored with continuous rank probability score (CRPS). Inputs: "only five vector features in their final model, for all 22 players on the field: X, Y, S, A and Dir, where X and Y represent location coordinates on the field, S represents speed, A represents acceleration and Dir represents a player's direction" — evaluated at the moment of handoff. Outputs a probability distribution over rushing outcomes (not a point estimate); xRY = sum over outcomes of (outcome × probability). NGS team implemented the architecture in Amazon SageMaker. Example given: Nick Chubb 88-yd TD — xRY 7 (7.3), RYOE +81, TD probability <0.1%.
- Sources: https://www.nfl.com/news/next-gen-stats-intro-to-expected-rushing-yards ; https://www.amazon.science/blog/a-decade-of-nfl-next-gen-stats-innovation

**6. Expected Yards After Catch (XYAC)**
- Debuted 2018; original model superseded by the "same modeling structure as Singer and Gordeev's Expected Rushing Yards model" (per the 2020 xRY article). Data-dictionary definitions: avg_expected_yac = "Average expected yards after catch, based on numerous factors using tracking data such as how open the receiver is, how fast they're traveling, how many defenders/blockers are in space, etc"; avg_yac_above_expectation = "A receiver's YAC compared to their Expected YAC." No equation or full feature list published.
- Sources: https://www.nfl.com/news/next-gen-stats-intro-to-expected-rushing-yards ; nflreadr data dictionary.

**7. Pressure Probability** (2023; 2023 Big Data Bowl theme)
- Verbatim (nfl.com, Sep 21, 2023) — three ML models: (1) "Using a graphic neural network model (GNN), we can accurately discern which players are blocking (versus running a route) and which defenders are pursuing the quarterback (versus dropping into coverage) in real time." (2) "This model gauges, in tenths of a second, the likelihood of each defender creating a pressure... The output of the random forest model..." — plus "a team composite pressure probability model, two separate models were created to quantify pressure for each individual pass rusher." (3) A blocking-matchup model identifying "who blocked who." Fully automated in the NGS pipeline; history for every pass play since 2018.
- Verbatim derived-metric definitions: Pressure — "defined as a pass-rush play where the rusher affects the quarterback before the pass is thrown. A pass rush becomes a pressure when pressure probability exceeds 75 percent." Pressure rate = total pressures / total pass-rush snaps (average rusher 10.3%). Time to pressure = time from snap to first moment of pressure (avg 2.9s). Quick pressures = within first 2.5s. Pressure time = total duration with PP over the 75% threshold. Pressure probability at snap = "estimated probability of generating a pressure... based on the alignment and relative locations of each pass rusher and blocker." Average pressure probability = average over the dropback. Pressure rate over expected = "the difference between average pressure probability and probability at snap." Peak pressure probability = maximum value. Positive/negative rushes = avg PP above/below PP at snap; positive rush rate (avg 54%); net positive rushes = difference of counts. OL metrics: matchup frequency, pressures allowed, sacks allowed, pressure rate allowed, double teams.
- Old logic it replaced: "Any pass attempt or sack where a defender was within 2 yards of the QB at pass forward or within 1.5 yards of the QB at any point during the play counted as a pressure."
- Whitepaper (Keegan Abdoo co-author): "Feeling the Pressure: A Unified Framework for Automating Pass Rushing Statistics in NFL Games" — MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference (Sungmin Hong, Laura Kulowski, Dan Volk, Henry Wang, Keegan Abdoo, Conor McQuiston, Kyeong Hoon Jung, Mike Band, Diego Socolinsky). 25-page PDF downloadable from the Sloan page; abstract: estimation of defensive pressure scores throughout a play with rusher/blocker identification, matchup, and pressure-score estimation.
- Sources: https://www.nfl.com/news/next-gen-stats-introduction-to-pressure-probability ; https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/feeling-the-pressure-a-unified-framework-for-automating-pass-rushing-statistics-in-nfl-games

**8. Tackle Probability** (+ missed-tackle derivatives; 2024 Big Data Bowl theme)
- Verbatim (amazon.science, Feb 2026): "Tackle probability estimates the likelihood of a defender completing a tackle at the moment of contact, factoring in speed, angle, distance, leverage, and pursuit. That data allows NGS to identify true tackle opportunities, quantify missed tackles, and calculate the yards a defender saves or concedes."
- Pipeline (AWS blog, Sep 10, 2024, quoting Keegan Abdoo, Research Analytics Manager, and Mike Band, Senior Manager, Research & Analytics, NGS): model built on >2 million data points; computes tackle probability "for every player, during each play, every 10th of a second"; trained on 2018–2022, tested on 2023; 20 features per frame for all 11 defenders (a 10-second play ≈ 20,000 data points); 15 different ML models tested on >1M data points in 4 months; in production in <6 months; real-time inference "going through hundreds of thousands of data points every second, millions per game" — "compute all those metrics, feed them into a model, have the model compute a time series probability, output that, and create derivative metrics from that all in seconds." Play-level (frame-level) labeling. Fusing tackle probability + expected yards models yields "tackle yards saved."
- Derived metrics named: missed tackles by defender; tackle efficiency (% of attempts resulting in successful tackle); missed tackles forced by ball carrier; group tackles; number of players in group tackle; open field tackle attempts; tackled out of bounds; primary tackler; downhill/upfield/chase down tackle attempts; missed tackle yards lost; tackle yards saved; yards gained in group tackle.
- RFID tags also on officials, pylons, sticks, chains.
- Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/media/beyond-the-box-score-aws-and-nfl-ai-powered-tackle-analysis/

**9. NGS QB Passing Score** (announced Jan 13, 2022; Amazon Science methodology article Aug 26, 2022 by François-Xavier Aubet & Elena Ehrlich)
- Data: updates every 100 ms from RFID chips in shoulder pads. Features per timestep: x-position, y-position, x-speed, y-speed, x-acceleration, y-acceleration, direction, body orientation per player → matrix of 176 columns (22 players × 8 features) × variable rows (snap to QB release; e.g., 40 rows for a 4-second throw). Extra engineered features (e.g., number of defenders within a 2-yard radius of target receiver). Static play attributes: down, score, games remaining in season.
- Model: temporal convolutional network (TCN) encodes each player's time series (handles variable length, long-range temporal relations); concatenated with static features; passed to a multilayer perceptron producing a probabilistic prediction of yards gained. Output distribution = spliced binned-Pareto (SBP): discrete binned base distribution (captures asymmetry/multimodality) with lower tail below the 5th quantile and upper tail above the 95th quantile replaced by weighted generalized Pareto distributions from extreme-value theory; parameterized by the network. Play-level QB passing score = the rank (0–1) of the actual yards gained in that play's predictive CDF, standardized; aggregated over plays for game/season scores (e.g., Kyler Murray scored 87 for the 2021 season, 9th of playoff QBs; 99 under pressure on 2.5–4s throws).
- Training: ~50,000 passing plays from 2018–2020 (34,000 completions, 15,000 incompletes, 1,200 interceptions). Training: ~8 hours on a 1-GPU p3.8xlarge; preprocessing 2 hours on ml.m5.m24xlarge. Inference: 0.001 second per play. AWS released PyTorch code for the SBP model + demo notebook (linked from the article). Score correlates better with win/playoff percentage than prior QB metrics.
- Source: https://www.amazon.science/blog/the-science-behind-nfl-next-gen-stats-new-passing-metric

**10. Defensive Alerts** (broadcast metric)
- Verbatim (amazon.science, Feb 2026): "Defensive alerts assess defensive alignment and movement before the snap to predict which players are likely to rush. The model uses acceleration patterns and presnap shifts, combines them with situational context such as down, distance, and game state, and then applies generative AI to predict likely rushers, who are highlighted with red circles for viewers."
- No equation or architecture detail published.
- Source: https://www.amazon.science/blog/a-decade-of-nfl-next-gen-stats-innovation

**11. Defender "ghosting"** (defensive-back trajectory prediction; CNN-LSTM; AWS ML Solutions Lab, lead scientist Lin Lee Cheong)
- Details (amazon.science, Feb 5, 2021): training-data cleaning rules — trajectories must never leave the field, speed never exceeds 12.5 yd/s (measured top speed ~11 yd/s); tracking accurate to within six inches in 99.9% of cases. Feature selection: train a gradient-boosting model on all features, keep only the most important for the deep model. Accuracy measured by predicting next 10 positions from 3 input measurements. Composite expert-eval metric: how quickly a DB's trajectory diminishes distance to the targeted receiver, distance covered vs max at top NFL speeds, penalty for superhuman speeds (actual avg −0.1036 vs predicted −0.0825). Ghosting itself is not a public stat — a component of stats under development (e.g., evaluating QB decision-making on hypothetical throws).
- Related publication: "Prediction of defensive player trajectories in NFL games with defender CNN-LSTM model."
- Source: https://www.amazon.science/latest-news/how-aws-scientists-help-create-the-nfls-next-gen-stats

========== DATA PIPELINE ==========
- RFID chips in every set of shoulder pads + inside the football; 20+ ultrawideband receivers per stadium; launched 2015. Players sampled 10x/second, ball 25x/second; "accurate to a few inches" (2026) / "within six inches in 99.9% of cases" (2021). Club-only data until 2018; league-wide access since 2018.
- AWS official technology provider since 2017. ~300M data points/season (2021 figure). 75+ ML models running on AWS processing data in under a second; results to teams/broadcasters within seconds (2026). 500–1,000 stats produced per play.
- Named tools: Amazon SageMaker (build/train/deploy ML models), Amazon QuickSight/Quick (real-time interactive visualizations; Combine IQ / Draft IQ experiences), AWS ProServe (2024 tackle model, 2026 run models), Amazon Q Business (internal content ops).
- Optical pose tracking: 4K cameras, 16 angles → x,y,z of 29 body parts per player at 60 Hz. On-site AWS servers process within ~700 ms; cloud ML inference <100 ms; capture-to-analysis <1 second total. Current (2026) season is first with "full installation, full capture" league-wide; data stays internal while validated.
- Sources: https://www.amazon.science/blog/a-decade-of-nfl-next-gen-stats-innovation (Feb 2, 2026); https://www.amazon.science/latest-news/how-aws-scientists-help-create-the-nfls-next-gen-stats (Feb 5, 2021); https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/media/beyond-the-box-score-aws-and-nfl-ai-powered-tackle-analysis/
