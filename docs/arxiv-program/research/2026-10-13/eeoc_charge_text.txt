Mr. Garrett Baxley files this Charge of Discrimination against Turner Construction Company (“Turner Construction” or “Company”) asserting claims of disability discrimination, failure to accommodate, and retaliation.
Turner Construction discriminated against Mr. Baxley because of his disability and retaliated against him for requesting a reasonable accommodation for which it never granted.
Turner Construction’s actions violate the Americans with Disabilities Act and Chapter 21 of the Texas Labor Code.
Turner Construction hired Mr. Baxley on October 6, 2025, as a Human Resources Manager.
Mr. Baxley is a member of a protected classification and was qualified for his job.
For the duration of his employment, Mr. Baxley had no disciplinary history.
In recognition of his performance, Turner Construction awarded him a seven percent raise in or around February 2026.
Mr. Baxley gave his best efforts to the company and had every expectation of a long tenure.
Mr. Baxley has autism. In connection with his diagnosis, structure and clarity in the workplace help him perform his job more efficiently.
Before February 2026, Mr. Baxley sought to connect with his direct supervisor about workplace structure as there was no clear guidance on expectations or deliverables.
Mr. Baxley disclosed his disability to the Company on two separate occasions.
In doing so, he verbally disclosed to her that he is autistic on or around January 26, 2026 and in writing via email on February 17, 2026.
Through an email, Mr. Baxley informally asked for support regarding workplace structure as an accommodation for his disability. Turner Construction never granted this accommodation and the interactive process was not otherwise engaged in by the parties.
Beginning on or around February 9, 2026, Mr. Baxley repeatedly requested support and raised concerns about his working conditions, including his concern that the gaps in the work, caused by the delayed communication from his new supervisor, Corina Murguia.
Notably, Mr. Baxley had a prior manager, Jake Overman, who had stepped away from the role. Each time Mr. Baxley raised concerns, Turner Construction met him with performance criticisms that it never documented nor showed to Mr. Baxley.
On or around February 22, 2026, Mr. Baxley had a medical episode which necessitated a brief break from his work duties. Mr. Baxley sought and received treatment subsequently.
Moreover, at a Human Resources conference on or about March 10, 2026, Mr. Baxley requested assistance from Jeff Dolan with his requested accommodations. Mr. Baxley informed Mr. Dolan that he is neurodivergent and how it may impact his communications.
Mr. Dolan offered to check on Mr. Baxley from time to time but nothing more came from his discussion.
Despite his disclosures and requests for accommodations, the Company failed to engage in the interactive process and began retaliating against Mr. Baxley.
Following the double disclosures and requests for accommodation, Mr. Baxley noticed a sharp departure from his superiors.
Indeed, Mr. Baxley was left off upstream communication that he was normally a recipient of and performance issues were being raised for the first time by the company.
When this was brought to Mr. Baxley’s attention, he asked for the alleged performance concern to be addressed in separation from his outstanding requests for accommodations.
The Company terminated Mr. Baxley under the guise of performance related concerns.
To his surprise, on May 19, 2026, Turner Construction terminated Mr. Baxley, citing performance concerns.
Notably, aside from Mr. Baxley’s admission of an unfair comment he made in jest during a meeting, no performance concerns were raised by the company until after Mr. Baxley’s disclosure and protected activities.
Put simply, Mr. Baxley had no indication that his job was in jeopardy due to performance reasons—not once was he placed on a Performance Improvement Plan or rated as deficient.
Instead, the real reason for the termination was because of Mr. Baxley’s disability and his requests for a reasonable accommodation.
Therefore, the company’s actions are in violation of both federal and state law.
