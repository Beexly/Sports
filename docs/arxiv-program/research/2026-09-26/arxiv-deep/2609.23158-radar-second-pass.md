## 1. Unpriced physiological state — a new signal class for props (highest leverage)

**The outside-the-box read:** strip away the radar and the paper is an *existence proof* that exertion produces measurable, classifiable changes in athlete physiology. The sportsbooks price injury as a binary variable (in/out, practice participation tags). Nobody in the market prices **fatigue, recovery state, or cumulative physiological load as a continuous latent variable** — and this paper demonstrates the underlying quantity is real and state-dependent.

**GSE cannot measure it with radar. GSE can model it from observable proxies.** Candidate proxy features for a physiological-load state variable feeding prop projections:

- **Snap-count / workload trajectories:** a RB whose snaps declined three straight weeks is not merely "trending down" — he is accumulating fatigue (or nursing something undisclosed). First-half vs second-half snap splits within games.
- **Schedule-density interactions:** short week × travel distance × time-zone shifts × altitude (Denver/Mexico City). The literature treats these as independent dummies; the physiology says they compound.
- **Post-bye vs pre-bye performance splits** by position group — recovery is observable in the bye-week discontinuity.
- **Age × workload decline curves:** veteran skill-position players show steeper late-season efficiency decay under heavy early-season usage. This is workload physiology, not just "aging."
- **Practice-participation gradations** (DNP → limited → full) as a noisy observation of the latent state, not just a binary flag.

**Why this matters for props specifically:** props are the market where small, persistent, unpriced edges compound — the first-pass program already identified props as the ideal deployment surface (cf. 2607.00164 verifiable-rewards ledger). A physiological-load adjustment to rushing/receiving yardage projections attacks lines the market sets from box-score medians, not load curves.

**Status:** hypothesis, not validated. Needs: historical backtest of load-proxy features against prop CLV/outcomes before any engine integration. No hardware required — this is pure modeling on data GSE already ingests.

## 2. Wavelet Scattering Transform — a cheap, real spike test

The first pass dismissed WST because zeroth-order (S0) low-pass energy carried 67.9% of discriminative power *in this dataset*. That is a property of this dataset (a mean-amplitude shift between rested and exhausted subjects), not a verdict on the method. WST is a mathematically principled multi-scale featurizer (translation-invariant, deformation-stable, energy-preserving — Mallat 2012) available in public libraries (Kymatio). The paper demonstrates it beating raw-signal baselines with a full protocol (Q sweep, path×window reshaping, order analysis). What it never tested — and what matters for GSE — is WST vs. *engineered* features on a GSE stream.

**Spike test spec (one day of work, no new data acquisition):**

- **Streams:** pregame line-movement trajectories (full season), live win-probability paths, or play-level EPA sequences.
- **Label:** CLV outcome (beat-close vs not) or against-the-spread cover.
- **Features A (WST):** Kymatio 1D scattering, Q sweep [2,1]/[4,1]/[8,1], invariance scale ≈ half window, same path×window reshaping as the paper.
- **Features B (baseline):** total movement, max excursion, time-weighted drift, late-steam indicator.
- **Model:** feedforward NN (paper's winner) or gradient boosting; **validation:** group-out (leave-one-week-out) — never random CV on sequential data (see §4).
- **Pass gate (both required):** (a) WST beats simple features by ≥ 0.02 out-of-sample AUC; (b) the gain is concentrated in **non-zeroth-order** coefficients — i.e., multi-scale structure actually contributes, not just mean amplitude. Criterion (b) is the direct application of the paper's own interpretability finding as a falsification check.

**If it fails:** drop it; the ledger's skepticism is confirmed empirically. **If it passes:** WST becomes a candidate featurizer for the live-probability and line-movement modules.

## 3. Order-contribution interpretability as engine-wide discipline

The paper's most transferable *methodological* contribution is not WST but its interpretability analysis: decomposing total discriminative power by scattering order (|t|-statistics → S0 67.9%, second-order 24.4%, first-order 7.6%) to check whether the model learned physiology or a trivial amplitude confound.

**Generalized principle for GSE:** every engine model should answer "where does your power come from?" by scale/source decomposition:

- Is the prop model learning **player skill** or just **team strength / game script**? (Ablate team-level features; measure the drop.)
- Is the calibration ladder learning **real miscalibration structure** or just "favorites win more often"? (Decompose reliability-gap closure by favorite/dog splits.)
- Is a line-movement feature learning **sharp action** or just **time-to-kickoff**? (Decompose by hours-before-close bins.)

This is the paper's S0 lesson applied as a standing diagnostic: always test whether your features are just amplitude. Recommend adopting as a checklist item in model-review for calibration-ladder and multi-market-ensemble work.

## 4. Validation discipline: the random-CV vs LOSO gap as a cautionary exhibit

NN accuracy: 99.28% (random 10-run CV) → 91.55% (LOSO), with LOSO SD ±14.39 and a 14.6-point-wide 95% CI. The random-CV number is pure within-subject leakage — train and test chirps from the same athlete. The paper introduced LOSO precisely to fix this, and the gap quantifies the inflation.

**GSE application:** any engine evaluation where multiple rows share a latent group must use group-out validation, never random splits:

- Props from the **same game** share the game outcome → leave-one-game-out or game-clustered bootstrap.
- Line-movement states from the **same game** share the final score → same treatment (cf. 2609.07617 tennis ledger: many states, one outcome).
- Player-game rows from the **same player-season** share the player → leave-one-player-out for prop models.

The oos-split module already implements group-out splitting; this paper is a vivid exhibit of *why* — a 99%→91% mirage — worth citing in validation docs and reviewer checklists.

## 5. Watchlist: mmWave vital-sensing commoditization (not a build)

Contactless vital-sign sensing (respiration, heart-rate via micro-Doppler) on 77 GHz FMCW is commoditizing — consumer presence/vitals in Nest hardware, in-cabin automotive sensing, aging-in-place monitors. The paper's edge-deployment framing (single-chirp, ARM Cortex-M class inference, no wearables) shows the trajectory: physiological state sensing is moving from lab hardware to ambient sensors.

**GSE relevance:** none today. If ambient athlete-readiness telemetry ever becomes licensable (team facilities, training camps), the paper's pipeline template (snapshot → multi-scale features → lightweight classifier → group-out validation) is the relevant starting point, and §1's proxy model becomes ground-truth-validatable. Until then: watchlist only, revisit annually. Do not build, do not buy hardware.
