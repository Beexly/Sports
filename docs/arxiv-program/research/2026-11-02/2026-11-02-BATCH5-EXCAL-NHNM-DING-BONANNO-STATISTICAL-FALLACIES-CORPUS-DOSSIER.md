### Pillar I: `ExCal` — Optimization-Free Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection via Excess Calibration (`arXiv:2610.35568v1`)
Authors **Kadir-Kaan Özer, René Ebeling, and Markus Enzweiler** (`Oct 26, 2026`) address a foundational problem in telemetry and sequential signal monitoring: given a multivariate time series $\mathbf{X} = [\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_L]^{\mathsf{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times C}$ with an unlabeled normal training prefix $\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{tr}} = \mathbf{X}_{1:L_{\mathrm{tr}}}$, how do we combine heterogeneous anomaly experts (state retrieval, cross-channel subspace, and multi-scale volatility) whose raw score scales are incommensurable, **without gradient optimization or anomaly labels**?

1. **Prefix-Only Standardization with Zero-Variance Floor (Eq. 2):**
   $$z_{t,c} = \frac{x_{t,c} - \mu_c}{\max(\sigma_c, 10^{-3})}, \qquad \mu_c = \operatorname{mean}_{t \le L_{\mathrm{tr}}} x_{t,c}, \quad \sigma_c = \operatorname{std}_{t \le L_{\mathrm{tr}}} x_{t,c}$$
2. **FFT Dominant Period & Data-Driven Window Horizon (Eq. 3):**
   From the average inverse FFT power spectrum (autocorrelation) across channels over lags $4 \le \ell \le \min(400, L_{\mathrm{tr}}/3)$, if the peak autocorrelation exceeds $0.1$ at period $p$:
   $$W = \operatorname{clip}(\lfloor 2.5p \rfloor, 32, 512), \quad H = \operatorname{clip}(\lfloor p/2 \rfloor, 4, 64), \quad R = \operatorname{clip}(p, 16, 128)$$
3. **Expert 1 — Uncentered PCA Raw State Retrieval ($r \in \{8, R\}$, Eqs. 4–5):**
   Windows $\mathbf{u}_t^{(r)} = \operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{z}_{t-r+1:t}) \in \mathbb{R}^{rC}$ are projected onto a $95\%$-variance PCA basis $\mathbf{V}_r$ centered only by the global training-prefix window mean $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_r$ (preserving absolute operating level) and compared against a non-overlapping exemplar bank $\mathcal{E}_r$:
   $$m_{\mathrm{raw}}^{(r)}(t) = \max_{j:\, t \in [j, j+r-1]} \min_{\mathbf{e} \in \mathcal{E}_r} \left\|\mathbf{V}_r^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\mathbf{u}_j^{(r)} - \bar{\mathbf{u}}_r\right) - \mathbf{e}\right\|_2$$
4. **Expert 2 — Signal/Derivative Subspace Residual (Eqs. 6–7):**
   Augmenting each standardized state with its first difference $\Delta\mathbf{z}_t = \mathbf{z}_t - \mathbf{z}_{t-1}$ yields the phase-space vector $\mathbf{a}_t = [\mathbf{z}_t, \Delta\mathbf{z}_t] \in \mathbb{R}^{2C}$. Projecting onto the $90\%$-variance PCA subspace $\mathbf{V}_s$ ($\dim \le 32$) gives the coordinate-wise orthogonal residual:
   $$\mathbf{r}_t = \left|(\mathbf{a}_t - \bm{\mu}_a) - \mathbf{V}_s\mathbf{V}_s^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{a}_t - \bm{\mu}_a)\right|$$
   Each coordinate is standardized by its training median and IQR, and the map takes the **mean of the top-3 largest residual coordinates** so sparse 1-to-3 channel breaks are not diluted by $2C - 3$ quiet channels.
5. **Expert 3 — Multi-Scale Robust Location & Log-Volatility Deviations ($s \in \{4, 16, 64, 256\}$, Eqs. 8–9):**
   $$d_{\mathrm{mean},c}^{(s)}(t) = \frac{\left|\mu_{t,c}^{(s)} - q_{\mathrm{mean},c,0.5}^{(s)}\right|}{\max\left(q_{\mathrm{mean},c,0.75}^{(s)} - q_{\mathrm{mean},c,0.25}^{(s)}, 0.05\right)}, \qquad d_{\mathrm{scale},c}^{(s)}(t) = \frac{\left|\log(\sigma_{t,c}^{(s)} + 10^{-3}) - q_{\log\sigma,c,0.5}^{(s)}\right|}{\max\left(q_{\log\sigma,c,0.75}^{(s)} - q_{\log\sigma,c,0.25}^{(s)}, 0.05\right)}$$
6. **Excess Calibration Above $q_{i,0.995}$ Normality Floor, Prevalence Damping & Rank Fusion (Eqs. 10–13):**
   For each expert map $m_i(t)$, fit its median $q_{i,0.5}$ and $99.5\text{th}$ percentile $q_{i,0.995}$ on $\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ (excluding overlapping self-matches). Retain only positive excess above $q_{i,0.995}$:
   $$e_i(t) = \left[\frac{m_i(t) - q_{i,0.995}}{\max(q_{i,0.995} - q_{i,0.5}, 10^{-6})}\right]_+$$
   Let $f_i = \frac{1}{L}\sum_{t=1}^L \mathbb{I}(e_i(t) > 0)$ be the firing prevalence of map $i$ across the scored batch. Broadly firing maps ($f_i > \rho = 0.10$) are automatically damped via square-root prevalence attenuation:
   $$h_i = \sqrt{\frac{\rho}{\max(f_i, \rho)}}, \qquad \rho = 0.10$$
   The total decomposable anomaly score is:
   $$s(t) = \sum_i s_i(t) = \sum_i \left[h_i e_i(t) + \tau\,\operatorname{rank01}(m_i(t))\right], \qquad \tau = 0.02$$

### Pillar II: `NHNM` — Natural Hard-Negative Mining from Positives for Instruction-Following Retrieval (`arXiv:2610.36900v1`)
Authors **Chaolang Li et al.** (`Oct 2026`) identify why standard RAG/embedding retrievers fail to obey instance-specific situational constraints $I$: they are dominated by unconditioned topical similarity $\operatorname{sim}(f_\theta(q), f_\theta(d))$ because synthetic training pipelines keep the original positive $d^+$ fixed and synthesize artificial negative passages around it.

`NHNM` reverses the polarity:
1. **Stage I (Candidate Cleaning):** Partition retrieved negatives $\mathcal{D}_q^-$ into False Negatives (`FN`), **Convertible Negatives (`CN`)** (passages that do not satisfy default query $q$ best, but become strictly preferable to $d^+$ under a valid situational refinement $I_i^0$), and Other Negatives (`ON`).
2. **Stage II (Reversal Construction, Eq. 3):** Select the highest-contrast pair $(d_j^-, I_j)$, promote the Convertible Negative $d_j^-$ to the **new instruction-conditioned positive** $d_{\text{new}}^+ = d_j^-$, and demote the **original query-positive $d_{\text{orig}}^+$ into the primary Natural Instruction Hard Negative $d_0^-$**:
   $$\mathcal{D}_{I_j, q}^- = \{d_0^-\} \cup \bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_q^- \setminus \{d_j^-\} \setminus \mathcal{E}_{I_j}\bigr)$$
3. **Stage III (Pretrained Geometry Filtering, Eqs. 4–5):** Retain the reversal tuple only if the frozen pretrained encoder $f_0$ exhibits positive directional alignment under instruction concatenation:
   $$\Delta^+(I_j, q, d_{\text{new}}^+) = \operatorname{sim}\!\bigl(f_0(I_j \oplus q), f_0(d_{\text{new}}^+)\bigr) - \operatorname{sim}\!\bigl(f_0(q), f_0(d_{\text{new}}^+)\bigr) > \gamma \quad (\gamma = 0)$$

### Pillar III: Peng Ding's *Linear Model and Extensions* (`arXiv:2401.00649v2`, UC Berkeley, 433 pp.)
Peng Ding provides the exact finite-sample and asymptotic matrix machinery connecting OLS geometry, causal mediation/confounding, outlier leverage, and model misspecification:
1. **Frisch-Waugh-Lovell (`FWL`) Theorem (Theorem 7.1, p. 94):**
   For long regression $Y = \mathbf{X}_1\hat{\beta}_1 + \mathbf{X}_2\hat{\beta}_2 + \hat{\varepsilon}$ with projection matrix $\mathbf{H}_1 = \mathbf{X}_1(\mathbf{X}_1^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{X}_1)^{-1}\mathbf{X}_1^{\mathsf{T}}$ and residual maker $\mathbf{M}_1 = \mathbf{I}_n - \mathbf{H}_1$:
   $$\hat{\beta}_2 = \left(\mathbf{X}_2^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}_1\mathbf{X}_2\right)^{-1}\mathbf{X}_2^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}_1 Y = \left(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_2^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_2\right)^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_2^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{Y}$$
2. **Cochran's Formula & Omitted-Variable Bias (`OVB`, Theorem 9.1, p. 117):**
   Comparing short regression $Y = \mathbf{X}_2\tilde{\beta}_2 + \tilde{\varepsilon}$ against long regression $Y = \mathbf{X}_1\hat{\beta}_1 + \mathbf{X}_2\hat{\beta}_2 + \hat{\varepsilon}$ where $\mathbf{X}_1 = \mathbf{X}_2\hat{\bm{\delta}} + \hat{\mathbf{U}}$ ($\mathbf{X}_2^{\mathsf{T}}\hat{\mathbf{U}} = \mathbf{0}$):
   $$\tilde{\beta}_2 = \hat{\beta}_2 + \hat{\bm{\delta}}\hat{\beta}_1, \qquad \hat{\bm{\delta}} = (\mathbf{X}_2^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{X}_2)^{-1}\mathbf{X}_2^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{X}_1$$
3. **Exact Identity Linking Leverage $h_{ii}$ and Mahalanobis Distance $D_i^2$ (Theorem 11.1, p. 133):**
   With an intercept included in $\mathbf{X} = [\mathbf{1}_n, \mathbf{X}_2]$ and sample covariance $\mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{n-1}\mathbf{X}_2^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{I}_n - \frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}_n\mathbf{1}_n^{\mathsf{T}})\mathbf{X}_2$:

### Pillar IV: Giacomo Bonanno (`arXiv:1512.06808v1` & `GT_book.pdf` 3rd Ed. 2024) + Christopher Griffin (`DOI: 10.1142/13962`) — Game Theory & Interactive Epistemology
1. **Strategic-Form Mixed-Strategy Indifference & Minimax Duality (Bonanno Ch. 6; Griffin 2024):**
   In any finite strategic game $G = \langle N, (S_i)_{i\in N}, (u_i)_{i\in N} \rangle$, a mixed strategy profile $\sigma^* = (\sigma_1^*, \dots, \sigma_n^*)$ is a Nash equilibrium iff for every player $i$ and every pure action $s_i \in \operatorname{supp}(\sigma_i^*)$:
   $$U_i(s_i, \sigma_{-i}^*) = \max_{s_i' \in S_i} U_i(s_i', \sigma_{-i}^*) = v_i^*$$
   In a zero-sum trench/coverage game between Offensive Coordinator (`OC`) and Defensive Coordinator (`DC`) with payoff matrix $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ (expected EPA), von Neumann's Minimax Theorem via LP duality guarantees:
   $$\max_{\mathbf{p} \in \Delta_m} \min_{\mathbf{q} \in \Delta_n} \mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{q} = \min_{\mathbf{q} \in \Delta_n} \max_{\mathbf{p} \in \Delta_m} \mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{q} = v^*$$
2. **Dynamic Imperfect-Information Games: Sequential Equilibrium & Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium (`PBE`, Bonanno Ch. 11–13):**
   An assessment $(\sigma, \mu)$ (behavioral strategy profile $\sigma$ + system of beliefs $\mu(x)$ at every decision node $x \in h$ in information set $h$) is **sequentially rational** if at every information set $h$ of player $i$:
   $$\sum_{x \in h} \mu(x) U_i(\sigma \mid x) \ge \sum_{x \in h} \mu(x) U_i((\sigma_i', \sigma_{-i}) \mid x) \quad \forall \sigma_i'$$
   and **Bayesian consistent** if at every reached information set ($\mathbb{P}_\sigma(h) > 0$):
   $$\mu(x) = \frac{\mathbb{P}_\sigma(x)}{\sum_{x' \in h} \mathbb{P}_\sigma(x')}$$
3. **Interactive Epistemology, Harsanyi Type Spaces & Aumann's Agreement Theorem (Bonanno Ch. 8–9 & Ch. 16):**
   Given state space $\Omega$, information partitions $\mathcal{P}_i$, knowledge operator $K_i E = \{\omega \in \Omega : \mathcal{P}_i(\omega) \subseteq E\}$, mutual knowledge $E_G = \bigcap_{i \in G} K_i$, and **Common Knowledge** $C_G E = \bigcap_{k=1}^\infty E_G^k(E)$:
   - **Aumann's Agreement Theorem (Bonanno Section 9.6–9.7, pp. 335–345):** If two agents share a **common prior** $P$ on $\Omega$ (Harsanyi consistency / like-mindedness) and it is **common knowledge** at state $\omega^*$ that Agent 1's posterior probability of event $A$ is $q_1 = P(A \mid \mathcal{P}_1(\omega^*))$ and Agent 2's posterior probability is $q_2 = P(A \mid \mathcal{P}_2(\omega^*))$, then **$q_1 = q_2$** (they *cannot* agree to disagree!).
   - **Implication for Sports Betting Markets (`THE STACK`):** Whenever the GSE Engine's posterior $p_{\text{GSE}}(A)$ differs from the de-vigged market posterior $p_{\text{mkt}}(A)$, Aumann's theorem proves that the divergence **must** come from either:
     1. **Private/Asymmetric Information Partition ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{GSE}}(\omega^*) \ne \mathcal{P}_{\text{mkt}}(\omega^*)$ not yet reflected in price)** — e.g., early beat-desk injury/snap-count news or unpriced 6-family situational interactions; OR
     2. **Prior / Model Misspecification ($P_{\text{GSE}} \ne P_{\text{mkt}}$)** — e.g., a broken heuristic or uncalibrated solo model!
