## 1. `Det-MASO`: Diversity-Regularized Multi-Agent Sequential Optimization for Factor Generation (`arXiv:2610.34936v1`)

**Authors:** Tiantian Wu, Peng Yang (Southern University of Science and Technology, Oct 26, 2026)  
**Source Artifacts:** `uploaded_media_0_1793652267215.png` (Abstract), `uploaded_media_1_1793652267215.jpg` (Figure 2 CSI300 Cumulative Return Backtest), `scratch/2610.34936v1.pdf`.

### 1.1 Mathematical Formulation & Linear-Scaling Diversity Barrier
When $n$ factor-generation agents $\mathcal{A}_1, \dots, \mathcal{A}_n$ are trained under a shared objective over an asset (or player/prop) universe of size $N$, they suffer from **multi-agent homogenization**: agent outputs become collinear ($\det\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta) \to 0$). Existing behavioral diversity metrics (pairwise MMD, System Neural Diversity) require $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ pairwise comparisons across the universe.

`Det-MASO` constructs a differentiable **Soft-Rank Standardized Factor Matrix** $\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_t(\theta) = [\tilde{\alpha}_{1,t}, \dots, \tilde{\alpha}_{n,t}] \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times n}$:
$$\operatorname{SoftRank}_\kappa(\alpha)_j = 1 + \sum_{\ell \neq j} \sigma\big(\kappa(\alpha_j - \alpha_\ell)\big), \qquad \tilde{\alpha}_{i,t} = \frac{\operatorname{SoftRank}_\kappa(\alpha_{i,t}) - \mu_{i,t}}{\sigma_{i,t} + \epsilon}$$
and defines the $n\times n$ **Inter-Agent Correlation Matrix** (Eq. 3):
$$\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta) = \frac{1}{N} \tilde{\mathbf{F}}_t(\theta)^\top \tilde{\mathbf{F}}_t(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$$
The **Log-Determinant Diversity Penalty** (Eq. 11) is:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{div}}(\theta) = -\log\det\big(\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta) + \delta \mathbf{I}_n\big)$$
with joint objective (Eq. 20):
$$\mathcal{J}_t(\Theta) = c_{\text{IC}}\sum_{i=1}^n w_{i,t} m_{i,t}\operatorname{IC}_i(\theta_i) + c_{\text{SR}}\operatorname{SR}(\Theta) - c_{\text{div}}\big[-\log\det\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta)\big] + c_{\text{ent}}H(\mathbf{w}_t)$$

### 1.2 Key Theoretical Guarantees
1. **Linear Computational Complexity in Asset Universe Size $N$:**
   Forming $\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta)$ costs $\mathcal{O}(N n^2)$ and inverting/Cholesky-factoring $\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ costs $\mathcal{O}(n^3)$:
   $$\frac{\partial}{\partial \theta_k}\big[-\log\det\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta)\big] = -\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta)^{-1} \frac{\partial \mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta)}{\partial \theta_k}\right), \qquad \text{Cost} = \mathcal{O}(N n^2 + n^3)$$
2. **Proposition 1 (Strict Repulsion from Homogeneous Equilibria, Eq. 15–17):**
   At any homogeneous configuration $\bar{\theta}$ where $\theta_i = \theta_j$ for some $i \neq j$, columns $i$ and $j$ of $\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_t(\bar{\theta})$ are identical, so $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\bar{\theta})) \le n - 1$, $\det\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\bar{\theta}) = 0$, and $\mathcal{J}_t(\bar{\theta}) = -\infty$. Furthermore, for any perturbation $\theta(\eta) = \bar{\theta} + \eta$ moving off the homogeneous manifold, the smallest eigenvalue satisfies $\lambda_{\min}(\eta) = \Theta(\|\eta\|^2)$ and the repulsive gradient diverges as:
   $$\big\|\nabla_{\theta_i}\varphi(\mathbf{\Sigma}(\theta))\big\| = \Omega\!\left(\frac{1}{\|\eta\|}\right)$$
3. **Proposition 2 (Smallest Eigenvalue & Effective Rank Lower Bound, Eq. 18):**
   Since $\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta)$ has unit diagonal, $\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta)) = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = n$, which implies $\lambda_{\max} \le n$. If $\det\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta) \ge \varepsilon > 0$, then:
   $$\varepsilon \le \prod_{i=1}^n \lambda_i \le \lambda_{\min}\cdot n^{n-1} \implies \lambda_{\min}\big(\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta)\big) \ge \frac{\varepsilon}{n^{n-1}} > 0 \implies \operatorname{rank}\big(\mathbf{\Sigma}_t(\theta)\big) = n$$
4. **Regime-Aware Gradient Mask (Eq. 4, 14) & Shapley Interaction Index (Eq. 22–24):**
   Market regimes $K^\star = \arg\max_{2\le K \le K_{\max}} \bar{s}(K)$ are discovered via Silhouette-optimal $K$-means over rolling return, volatility, dispersion, and skewness. Non-active regime specialists receive damped gradients via $m_{i,t} = \mathbf{1}[\text{regime}(t)=i] + \lambda_{\text{mask}}\mathbf{1}[\text{regime}(t)\neq i]$. Post-training cooperation between agents $i$ and $j$ is quantified by the exact **Shapley Interaction Index**:
   $$I(i,j) = \sum_{S\subseteq [n]\setminus\{i,j\}} \frac{|S|!(n-|S|-2)!}{(n-1)!}\Big(v(S\cup\{i,j\}) - v(S\cup\{i\}) - v(S\cup\{j\}) + v(S)\Big)$$
   where $I(i,j) > 0$ certifies superadditive synergy and $I(i,j) < 0$ flags redundancy.

## 2. `Admissible Portfolio Optimization`: Information Constraints, Conditional Efficient Frontiers, and the Price of Causal Identification (`arXiv:2610.00147v2`)

**Author:** Alejandro Rodríguez Domínguez (Miralta Finance Bank / Henley Business School, Univ. of Reading, Oct 30, 2026, 71 pp.)  
**Source Artifacts:** `uploaded_media_2_1793652267215.png` (Abstract), `uploaded_media_3_1793652267215.png` (Figure 1 Classical MPT vs Admissible Portfolio Optimization), `scratch/2610.00147v2.pdf`.

### 2.1 The Two-Stage Lexicographic Principle (Why Conditioning Information Must Precede Portfolio Optimization)
Classical Markowitz mean-variance optimization takes conditioning information $H$ as fixed and optimizes over weights $w$, allowing two fatal errors to pass into the portfolio unseen:
1. **Chronological / Look-Ahead Contamination:** Using variables not strictly in the pre-decision filtration $\mathcal{F}_{t^-}$.
2. **Diagonal-Residual Misallocation:** Treating common variation omitted by $H$ as idiosyncratic noise ($\mathbf{\Omega}_H^{\text{off}} \neq \mathbf{0}$).

Rodríguez Domínguez makes the conditioning representation $H \in \mathcal{U}_t$ a Stage I decision variable governed by a **lexicographic admissibility order** $\prec_{\mathcal{A}}$ on the defect vector:
$$\Phi_t(H) = \big(\varepsilon_t(H),\; \delta_t(H),\; \kappa_t(H),\; \iota_t(H)\big)$$
where:
- $\varepsilon_t(H) = \frac{\|\mathbf{\Omega}_H - \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{\Omega}_H)\|_F}{\|\mathbf{\Omega}_H\|_F}$ is the **statistical separation (screening) defect** (residual off-diagonal Frobenius ratio),
- $\delta_t(H)$ is the **interventional / regime-invariance defect** across declared environments $e \in \mathcal{E}_t$,
- $\kappa_t(H) = \dim(H)$ is representation complexity (parsimony), and
- $\iota_t(H)$ is implementation cost.

**Crucial Design Invariant:** *No risk-return or decision-loss quantity enters Stage I.* Stage I selects the minimal admissible equivalence class $\mathcal{A}_t^\star = \operatorname{Min}_{\prec_{\mathcal{A}}}\mathcal{A}_t$; Stage II solves classical Markowitz portfolio optimization inside $H_t^\star \in \mathcal{A}_t^\star$:
$$w_t^\star(H_t^\star) \in \arg\min_{w\in \mathcal{W}} \left\{ \frac{\gamma}{2} w^\top \mathbf{\Sigma}_{H_t^\star} w - w^\top \mu_{H_t^\star} \right\}$$

### 2.2 Fundamental Theorems Extracted
1. **Corollary 3.11 (Failure of Decision-Loss Selection Under Look-Ahead Contamination):**
   Suppose an enlarged candidate pool $\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_t \supset \mathcal{U}_t$ contains a look-ahead representation $H^\sharp \notin \mathcal{F}_{t^-}$ with strictly lower conditional residual variance than any chronologically valid representation ($V(H^\sharp) < \inf_{H\in\mathcal{A}_t} V(H)$). Then **any** rule that selects $H$ by minimizing decision loss $V(H)$ selects $H^\sharp$ with probability 1, whereas the lexicographic two-stage program rejects $H^\sharp$ at the domain gate (`A1`).
2. **Proposition 3.12(d) (Exact Three-Way Decomposition of Diagonal-Residual Variance Misallocation, Eq. 12):**
   Let $\mathbf{\Sigma}_H = \mathbf{B}_H\mathbf{V}_H\mathbf{B}_H^\top + \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{\Omega}_H) + \mathbf{\Omega}_H^{\text{off}}$, let $s_i = \sqrt{(\mathbf{\Omega}_H)_{ii}}$, $v_i = w_i s_i$, and $c_{ij} = (\mathbf{\Omega}_H)_{ij}/(s_i s_j)$. When a model drops $\mathbf{\Omega}_H^{\text{off}}$, the fraction of true portfolio variance discarded is:
   $$\frac{w^\top \mathbf{\Omega}_H^{\text{off}} w}{w^\top \mathbf{\Sigma}_H w} = \underbrace{\frac{\|v\|_2^2}{w^\top \mathbf{\Sigma}_H w}}_{\text{residual share}} \;\times\; \underbrace{\left(\frac{(\sum_{i=1}^p v_i)^2}{\|v\|_2^2} - 1\right)}_{\text{effective breadth } - 1} \;\times\; \underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{(\sum_i v_i)^2 - \|v\|_2^2}\sum_{i\neq j} v_i v_j c_{ij}\right)}_{\bar{c}_w \text{ (weighted avg residual correlation)}}$$
   On pre-diversified portfolios ($p=30$, Fama-French 30 industries), `effective breadth - 1` approaches $p - 1 = 29$, causing diagonal-residual models to discard **82% to 90% of true portfolio variance** (`1 - VR = 0.897`)!
3. **Minimax Robust Equicorrelated Covariance Completion (p. 22):**
   When residual correlations are bounded by $|c_{ij}| \le \bar{\varepsilon}$ on the nonnegative orthant $w \ge 0$, worst-case portfolio variance is solved in closed form by the **Equicorrelated Completion**:
   $$\max_{C:\, |c_{ij}|\le \bar{\varepsilon}} w^\top \Big(\mathbf{B}_H\mathbf{V}_H\mathbf{B}_H^\top + \operatorname{diag}(s)\mathbf{C}\operatorname{diag}(s)\Big) w = w^\top \mathbf{B}_H\mathbf{V}_H\mathbf{B}_H^\top w + (1 - \bar{\varepsilon})\|v\|_2^2 + \bar{\varepsilon}\|v\|_1^2$$
   corresponding to the robust covariance matrix:
   $$\widetilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_H = \mathbf{B}_H\mathbf{V}_H\mathbf{B}_H^\top + (1 - \bar{\varepsilon})\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{\Omega}_H) + \bar{\varepsilon}\, s s^\top$$
   which eliminates the 90% variance understatement without estimating $\binom{p}{2}$ noisy off-diagonal parameters (`VR = 1.073`, zero tuning parameters).
4. **Theorem 3.15 (Distance Bound for the Price of Causal Identification, Eq. 14–16):**
   Requiring interventional/regime invariance ($\delta_t(H) \le \bar{\delta}$) restricts the feasible set from observational $\mathcal{A}_t(o)$ to causal $\mathcal{A}_t(i) \subseteq \mathcal{A}_t(o)$. The **Price of Causal Identification** $\pi_{\text{ci}} := V_i^\star - V_c^\star$ satisfies:
   $$0 \le \pi_{\text{ci}} \le L \cdot d\big(H_0^\star, \mathcal{A}_t(i)\big), \qquad d(H,G) := \|\mu_H - \mu_G\|_2 + \frac{\gamma}{2}\bar{r}\|\mathbf{\Sigma}_H - \mathbf{\Sigma}_G\|_{\text{op}}$$
   with finite-sample oracle inequality (Theorem 3.21):
   $$V(\widehat{H}_{t,n}(i)) - V_o^\star \le \pi_{\text{ci}} + 2L\bar{\Delta}_n\big(\mathcal{U}_t(i)\big)$$
   showing a direct bias-variance trade-off: causal restriction pays $\pi_{\text{ci}} \ge 0$ in population but strictly shrinks the complexity radius $\bar{\Delta}_n(\mathcal{U}_t(i)) \le \bar{\Delta}_n(\mathcal{U}_t(o))$ by pruning spurious drivers!

## 3. `Optimal Size in Market Making under State-Dependent Hawkes Process` (`arXiv:2610.31903v1`)

**Authors:** Jonathan Chávez Casillas, Li Gan (University of Rhode Island, Oct 22, 2026, 40 pp.)  
**Source Artifacts:** `uploaded_media_4_1793652267215.png` (Abstract), `uploaded_media_5_1793652267215.jpg` (Figure 12 Cumulative Inventory Paths under Hard Barrier $I_{\max} = 10,000$), `scratch/2610.31903v1.pdf`.

### 3.1 State-Dependent Marked Hawkes Intensity & Queue Imbalance
Order book events $e \in \{1=\text{limit ask},\, 2=\text{market buy},\, 3=\text{limit bid},\, 4=\text{market sell}\}$ arrive with mark (volume) $z$ and state-dependent intensity (Eq. 3–5):
$$\lambda_e(t, z) = g(z; \eta_e)\left(\nu_e + \sum_{e'=1}^4 \int_{[0,t)} z' \alpha_{e' X(s^-) e} e^{-\beta_{e' X(s^-) e}(t-s)}\,dN_{e'}(s, z')\right)$$
where the book state $X(t) \in \{1,2,3\}$ is partitioned by **Queue Imbalance**:
$$\operatorname{QI}(t_k) = \frac{D_b(t_k) - D_a(t_k)}{D_b(t_k) + D_a(t_k)} \in [-1, 1]$$

### 3.2 Closed-Form Prony-Series Optimal Size & Inventory Barrier Control
Executed volume $Q_{t_{k+1}}^\pm$ as a function of quote placement depth $L_{t_k}^\pm$, market-order count $N_{t_k}^\pm$, and Hawkes intensity $\lambda_{t_k}^\pm$ is represented by the **Multiplicative Prony-Series Demand Law** (Eq. 7–9):
$$Q_{t_{k+1}}^\pm = q_{\text{avg}}^\pm \left[\sum_{i=1}^{M^\pm} w_i^\pm \exp\!\big(\beta_{i1}^\pm L_{t_k}^\pm + \beta_{i2}^\pm N_{t_k}^\pm + \beta_{i3}^\pm \lambda_{t_k}^\pm\big)\right]\left(A_N^\pm e^{-B_N^\pm L_{t_k}^\pm} + C_N^\pm\right)$$
- **Stone–Weierstrass Uniform Density Theorem:** Finite Prony sums of exponentials are uniformly dense in $C(\Omega)$ on the compact Hausdorff state domain $\Omega = \mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{N}\times\Lambda$.
- **Hard Inventory Barrier Control (`Figure 12`):** Under backward dynamic programming with terminal CARA utility $-\exp(-\gamma W_T)$ and hard inventory constraint $|I_{t_k}| \le I_{\max} = 10,000$, unconstrained/naive linear-demand baselines (`Linear-2`, `Exponential-2`) accumulate runaway one-sided inventory ($\pm 2\times 10^5$ shares), whereas the state-dependent Hawkes Prony policy dynamically widens/skews placement levels $L_{t_k}^\pm$ during self-exciting bursts to keep inventory strictly inside $[-10,000, +10,000]$ while achieving superior terminal wealth.

## 4. `Understanding the Inner Workings of a State-Space Foundation Model for Limit Order Books` (`arXiv:2610.28753v1`)

**Authors:** Valentin Mohl, Alexandre Bismuth, Aramis Marti-Shahandeh, George Nigmatulin, Kang Li, Stefan Zohren, Jakob Foerster, Mihai Cucuringu, Anisoara Calinescu (Oxford BOLD Lab / Harvard / UCLA / Oxford-Man Institute, ICAIF '26, Oct 20, 2026, 9 pp.)  
**Source Artifacts:** `uploaded_media_6_1793652267215.png` (Abstract), `uploaded_media_7_1793652267215.jpg` (Figure 1 Mamba-3 LOB Architecture & Probing Pipeline), `scratch/2610.28753v1.pdf`.

### 4.1 Mamba-3 State-Space Recurrence vs. Residual Stream
The 24-layer Mamba-3 foundation model processes windows of 500 L3 NASDAQ messages (22 tokens per message = 11,000 tokens/window) via the MIMO complex-rotary state equation (`Figure 1`):
$$\mathbf{h}_t = \alpha_t \mathbf{h}_{t-1} + \beta_t \mathbf{B}_{t-1}\mathbf{x}_{t-1} + \gamma_t \mathbf{B}_t\mathbf{x}_t, \qquad \mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{C}_t^\top \mathbf{h}_t$$
where $\alpha_t = \exp(\Delta_t A) \in (0,1)$ controls exponential memory retention with head half-life $\tau_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{-\ln \bar{\alpha}_t}$ (ranging from `<5` messages in fast heads to `28–66` messages in slow integrative heads).

### 4.2 Four Mechanistic Discoveries
1. **Recurrent State Carries Integrative Dynamics; Residual Stream Does Not:**
   Ridge-regression linear probes on the **residual stream** fail to recover cumulative features (`OFI`, signed volume, return, volatility) with $R^2 \le 0.06$, whereas probes on the **recurrent state $\mathbf{h}_t^{(\ell)}$** recover `OFI` at **$R^2 = 0.59$** (`5x` higher than a raw 10-level LOB snapshot at $R^2 = 0.12$).
2. **Head Timescale Specialization:**
   Mid-network layers (Layers 5–18) split into slow integrative heads (retaining multi-event order flow pressure across 30–66 events) and fast reset heads ($\alpha_t \to 0$ on large trade shocks).
3. **Heavy-Tail Burst Degradation & Rank/Winsorized Universality:**
   Zero-shot cross-stock transfer on standard Pearson $R^2$ appears to degrade on high-kurtosis tickers strictly because the **top 1% tail events account for up to 59% of target variance** ($r = -0.89$ between top-1% variance share and standard $R^2$). Under tail-robust **Winsorized $R^2$ and Spearman Rank $R^2$**, zero-shot probing exceeds **$0.40\text{–}0.50$ across all 20 unseen equities**.
4. **Causal Activation Steering:**
   Adding $+\lambda \mathbf{v}_{\text{OFI}}^{(\ell)}$ (the difference between top-tercile and bottom-tercile recurrent state centroids) at Layer 11 causally shifts the model's generated next-50-message mid-price trajectory upward/downward in a monotonic dose-response curve.

## 5. `Amortized Neural Filtering for Calibrated Posteriors in Statistical Arbitrage` (`arXiv:2610.25802v2`)

**Author:** Minkey Chang (Seoul National University, ICAIF '26, Oct 2026, 9 pp.)  
**Source Artifacts:** `uploaded_media_8_1793652267215.png` (Abstract), `uploaded_media_9_1793652267215.jpg` (Figure 1 Amortized Neural Filter Architecture & Winner's Curse Shrinkage), `scratch/2610.25802v2.pdf`.

### 5.1 Diagnosis of the Winner's Curse in Multi-Candidate Selection
When a portfolio or betting engine ranks hundreds of candidate spreads/props by point-estimated expected return $\hat{\mu}_i$ and trades the top $K$, it systematically selects candidates with positive parameter estimation error ($\hat{\kappa}_i > \kappa_i$). Across 2005–2025 US equity pairs, point-estimate selection (`MLE-Point`, `KFS-Point`) **over-forecasts realized return by `1,084 bps` per holding period**.

### 5.2 Posterior-Aware Position Sizing via the Law of Total Variance (Eq. 7)
Under an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck spread $S_{i,t+1} - S_{i,t} = \kappa(z_{i,t})(m_{i,t} - S_{i,t}) + \sigma_{i,t}\zeta_{i,t}$ with half-life $\tau = \ln 2 / \kappa$, an amortized neural filter maps a lookback window $w_{i,t}$ to a variational posterior $q_\psi(\tau \mid w_{i,t})$ and structural-break tail probability:
$$P(z_{i,t} = D \mid w_{i,t}) = \int_{\tau^\star}^\infty q_\psi(\tau \mid w_{i,t})\,d\tau$$
Applying the **Law of Total Variance** to the $H$-step cumulative return $\Delta S_H$ given standardized deviation $d = (S_{i,t} - m_{i,t})/\sigma_{\text{eq}}$:
$$\mathbb{E}[\Delta S_H \mid w_{i,t}] = -d\,\sigma_{\text{eq}}\,\mathbb{E}_{q_\psi}\!\big[1 - e^{-\kappa H}\big], \qquad \operatorname{Var}[\Delta S_H \mid w_{i,t}] = \sigma_{\text{eq}}^2\Big(\mathbb{E}_{q_\psi}\!\big[1 - e^{-2\kappa H}\big] + d^2 \operatorname{Var}_{q_\psi}\!\big(e^{-\kappa H}\big)\Big)$$
The mean-variance optimal position size is therefore (Eq. 7):
$$\omega_{i,t} \propto \frac{\mathbb{E}[\Delta S_H \mid w_{i,t}]}{\operatorname{Var}[\Delta S_H \mid w_{i,t}]} = \frac{-d\,\mathbb{E}_{q_\psi}\!\big[1 - e^{-\kappa H}\big]}{\sigma_{\text{eq}}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{q_\psi}\!\big[1 - e^{-2\kappa H}\big] + d^2 \operatorname{Var}_{q_\psi}\!\big(e^{-\kappa H}\big)\Big)}$$
- **Why This Cures the Winner's Curse:** For any point estimate, $\operatorname{Var}(e^{-\kappa H}) = 0$, so position size scales linearly with $|d|$ and explodes on noisy extremes. Under posterior uncertainty, the quadratic denominator term $+d^2 \operatorname{Var}_{q_\psi}(e^{-\kappa H})$ automatically attenuates sizing whenever parameter uncertainty is high or the apparent edge $d$ is extreme, reducing the forecast-realized gap from **`1,084 bps` to `137 bps` (an 8x reduction)** and lifting out-of-sample Sharpe from `0.81` to `1.56` (`2.16` during the 2008–09 crisis)!

## 6. Proof by OpenAI of the Existence of 4D Quantum Yang-Mills Theory (`KITP Shepherding Committee Statement`, Oct 2026)

**Authors:** Michael R. Douglas, David J. Gross (Chair), Arthur Jaffe, Igor R. Klebanov, Nikita Nekrasov (Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics, UCSB)

### 7.1 Entangled State Space Scaling & Riemann–Silberstein–Weber (RSW) Unitary Mapping
1. **Exponential Dimension of Entangled Qubits (Sec. 0.4.3, p. 43):**
   An $n$-qubit pure state $|\psi\rangle = \sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1} z_j |j\rangle$ ($z_j \in \mathbb{C}$, $\sum |z_j|^2 = 1$, modulo global phase) has $2^{n+1}-1$ real degrees of freedom:
   $$\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\mathcal{H}_{\text{reducible}}^{\otimes n}\big) = 3n \quad \text{vs.} \quad \dim_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\mathcal{H}_{\text{entangled}}^{\otimes n}\big) = 2^{n+1} - 1$$
2. **Dyson Map & RSW Vector for Maxwell's Equations (Eq. 0.194–0.202):**
   Classical Maxwell's equations in a lossless inhomogeneous dielectric $\epsilon(\mathbf{r}), \mu(\mathbf{r})$ are mapped into a strictly unitary Schrödinger/Dirac equation using the **Riemann–Silberstein–Weber (RSW)** complex field vector:
   $$\mathbf{F}^\pm(\mathbf{r},t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\sqrt{\epsilon(\mathbf{r})}\,\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r},t) \pm \frac{i}{\sqrt{\mu(\mathbf{r})}}\,\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r},t)\right)$$
   Defining the 4-component quantum state vector:
   $$|\psi\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} -F_x^+ + iF_y^+ \\ F_z^+ \\ F_z^+ \\ F_x^+ + iF_y^+ \end{pmatrix}$$
   Maxwell's curl equations become the exact Dirac-form PDE (Eq. 0.202):
   $$\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\psi\rangle = -\left(\gamma^x \frac{\partial}{\partial x} + \gamma^y \frac{\partial}{\partial y} + \gamma^z \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)|\psi\rangle + \mathcal{M}(\nabla\epsilon, \nabla\mu)|\psi\rangle$$
3. **Quantum Lattice Algorithm (QLA, Eq. 0.205–0.213):**
   Rather than approximating $\exp(-i H \Delta t)$ via global Trotterization, QLA decomposes each time step $\Delta t = \varepsilon^2$ into an interleaved sequence of local unitary **collision (entanglement) operators** $C_x(\theta) = \exp(\varepsilon \mathcal{C}_x)$ and **lattice streaming (shift) operators** $S_{\pm x}^{01}, S_{\pm x}^{23}$:
   $$|\psi(t + \Delta t)\rangle = \widetilde{U}_y U_y \widetilde{U}_x U_x |\psi(t)\rangle + \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^4)$$
   preserving exact unitarity ($\|U\| = 1$, unconditional numerical stability) at every lattice step.
