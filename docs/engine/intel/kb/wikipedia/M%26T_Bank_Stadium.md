<p id="mwKw"><b id="mwLA">M&amp;T Bank Stadium</b> is a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-purpose_stadium" title="Multi-purpose stadium" id="mwLQ">multi-purpose stadium</a> in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore" title="Baltimore" id="mwLg">Baltimore</a>, Maryland, U.S. It has been the home of the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Ravens" title="Baltimore Ravens" id="mwLw">Baltimore Ravens</a> of the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League" title="National Football League" id="mwMA">National Football League</a> (NFL) since its opening in 1998. The stadium is immediately adjacent to <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriole_Park_at_Camden_Yards" title="Oriole Park at Camden Yards" id="mwMQ">Oriole Park at Camden Yards</a>,  home of the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Orioles" title="Baltimore Orioles" id="mwMg">Baltimore Orioles</a>.  Often referred to as "Ravens Stadium" or "The Bank",<sup about="#mwt57" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-9" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-9"}}'><a href="#cite_note-9" id="mwMw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwNA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwNQ">[</span>9<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwNg">]</span></span></a></sup> the stadium has a listed capacity of 70,745 and has been praised for its fan amenities, ease of access, concessions and other facilities.<sup about="#mwt60" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-10" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-10"}}'><a href="#cite_note-10" id="mwNw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwOA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwOQ">[</span>10<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwOg">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwOw">The stadium was originally known as <b id="mwPA">Ravens Stadium at Camden Yards</b>, until <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSINet" title="PSINet" id="mwPQ">PSINet</a> acquired the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_rights" title="Naming rights" id="mwPg">naming rights</a> in 1999, naming it <b id="mwPw">PSINet Stadium</b>. It then reverted to <b id="mwQA">Ravens Stadium</b> in 2002 when PSINet filed for bankruptcy. <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M&amp;T_Bank" title="M&amp;T Bank" id="mwQQ">M&amp;T Bank</a> bought the naming rights in 2003 and signed a 15-year, $75 million contract with the Ravens, which was brokered by <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Services,_LLC" title="Team Services, LLC" id="mwQg">Team Services, LLC</a>. The naming rights deal for M&amp;T Bank Stadium was renewed in 2023, extending the name through 2037.<sup about="#mwt61" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-11" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-11"}}'><a href="#cite_note-11" id="mwQw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwRA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwRQ">[</span>11<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwRg">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" id="mwRw" data-mw='{"autoGenerated":true}'/></section><section data-mw-section-id="1" id="mwSA" aria-labelledby="History"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="History">History</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%26T_Bank_Stadium&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwSQ">Ground was broken for the new stadium in mid-1996, shortly after the arrival of the Ravens. The team played its first two years at <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Stadium_(Baltimore)" title="Memorial Stadium (Baltimore)" class="mw-redirect" id="mwSg">Memorial Stadium</a>. Although there was some sentiment from Baltimore residents in having the Ravens stay there permanently, it was deemed too old to host an NFL team. The Orioles moved away from Memorial Stadium after the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Major_League_Baseball_season" title="1991 Major League Baseball season" id="mwSw">1991 season</a>.</p>

<p id="mwTA">The stadium site was previously the site of the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wm._Knabe_&amp;_Co." title="Wm. Knabe &amp; Co." id="mwTQ">Wm. Knabe &amp; Co.</a> piano factory, which closed during <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Depression" title="The Great Depression" class="mw-redirect" id="mwTg">the Great Depression</a>. A sidewalk keyboard mosaic on the southwest corner of the stadium honors the company's legacy.</p>

<p id="mwTw">In <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_NFL_season" title="2003 NFL season" id="mwUA">2003</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M&amp;T_Bank" title="M&amp;T Bank" id="mwUQ">M&amp;T Bank</a> acquired naming rights to the stadium. The bank had recently entered the Baltimore market with its purchase of Allfirst Bank. Two other companies were in the running to be granted naming rights to the stadium. They were reportedly <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nextel" title="Nextel" id="mwUg">Nextel</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CarMax" title="CarMax" id="mwUw">CarMax</a>. Following the September 2002 death of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Baltimore_Colts" title="History of the Baltimore Colts" class="mw-redirect" id="mwVA">Baltimore Colts</a> <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarterback" title="Quarterback" id="mwVQ">quarterback</a> <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Unitas" title="Johnny Unitas" id="mwVg">Johnny Unitas</a>, public sentiment leaned toward renaming the then-sponsorless stadium after the Baltimore icon. The Ravens and the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland_Stadium_Authority" title="Maryland Stadium Authority" id="mwVw">Maryland Stadium Authority</a> held firm in their right to negotiate naming rights fees.</p>

<p id="mwWA">In the end, the plaza in front of the main entrance to the Ravens' stadium was named "Unitas Plaza", complete with a bronze statue of the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame" title="Pro Football Hall of Fame" id="mwWQ">Hall of Famer</a>. Many Ravens fans, as they enter the stadium, will rub the foot of Unitas' statue as they walk by it. The plaza formerly featured large banners, each containing a picture of Unitas in his playing days, flanking the stadium entrance. After 10 years, these were replaced by large metal 19s (Unitas' number) for the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_NFL_season" title="2012 NFL season" id="mwWg">2012 season</a>.</p>

<p id="mwWw">In 2014, the Ravens unveiled a new statue of long-time Raven <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Lewis" title="Ray Lewis" id="mwXA">Ray Lewis</a> next to Unitas' statue. The bronze figure depicts Lewis in the final pose of his iconic "squirrel dance", which he performed before every Ravens home game upon coming on to the field.  Since 2020, the letters "MO" of Baltimore Ravens in the Stadium's endzone are painted either gold or white in honor of Baltimore sports icon <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_Gaba" title="Mo Gaba" id="mwXQ">Mo Gaba</a>.</p>

<p id="mwXg">In the 2020s, M&amp;T Bank Stadium underwent several renovations and upgrades that were completed in 2026. The $489 million project was a joint venture between the Ravens and Maryland Stadium Authority and includes a new team store, entertainment venue, bars, field-level seats, premium suites, and revamped North Plaza.<sup about="#mwt62" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-12" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-12"}}'><a href="#cite_note-12" id="mwXw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwYA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwYQ">[</span>12<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwYg">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwaQ">The stadium contains five levels, being the lower bowl, club level, 300 suites level, 400 suites level, and the upper bowl. The lower bowl contains 42 rows of seats, split into two sections. The seats below the tunnel entrances are labeled from 1 to 18, while the seats above the tunnels are labeled from 19 to 42, except in sections 123–130, which contain rows 19–35, due to the press box taking up sideline space.</p>

<p id="mwag">On the club level, the rows are labeled from 1 to 13 on the sidelines, and 1 to 17 in the corners where no suites are located. In the upper bowl, the sideline seats are labeled from 1 to 32, while in the upper bowl end zones, the rows range from 1 to 26. Seat widths for the lower and upper bowls of the stadium vary from 19 to 21 inches, due to the curve design of the stadium, while the padded club seats range from 21 to 23 inches respectively.</p>

<p id="mwaw">The venue is served by the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium/Federal_Hill_station" title="Stadium/Federal Hill station" id="mwbA">Stadium/Federal Hill station</a> of the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Light_Rail" title="Baltimore Light Rail" class="mw-redirect" id="mwbQ">Baltimore Light Rail</a>.</p>

<section data-mw-section-id="3" id="mwbg" aria-labelledby="Playing_surface"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Playing_surface">Playing surface</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%26T_Bank_Stadium&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Playing surface"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>

<p id="mwbw">The stadium originally featured a natural grass surface. An artificial surface, Sportexe Momentum Turf, was installed for the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_NFL_season" title="2003 NFL season" id="mwcA">2003</a> season, which in turn was replaced by a new-generation Sportexe Momentum 51 in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_NFL_season" title="2010 NFL season" id="mwcQ">2010</a>. In December 2015, the Ravens announced that in 2016 the team would go back to natural grass playing surface, which is currently Tifway 419 <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermudagrass" title="Bermudagrass" class="mw-redirect" id="mwcg">Bermudagrass</a>.<sup about="#mwt65" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-13" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-13"}}'><a href="#cite_note-13" id="mwcw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwdA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwdQ">[</span>13<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwdg">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

On December 7, 2008, an M&amp;T Bank Stadium then-record crowd of 71,438 watched the Baltimore Ravens defeat the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Washington_Redskins_season" title="2008 Washington Redskins season" id="mweQ">Washington Redskins</a> 24–10 on <i id="mweg"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sunday_Night_Football" title="NBC Sunday Night Football" id="mwew">Sunday Night Football</a></i>, only to be surpassed the next week when the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Pittsburgh_Steelers_season" title="2008 Pittsburgh Steelers season" id="mwfA">Pittsburgh Steelers</a> defeated the Ravens 13–9 in front of 71,502.<sup about="#mwt68" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-14" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-14"}}'><a href="#cite_note-14" id="mwfQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwfg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwfw">[</span>14<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwgA">]</span></span></a></sup> On January 15, 2012, a record crowd of 71,547, the largest in Ravens history,<sup about="#mwt71" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-15" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-15"}}'><a href="#cite_note-15" id="mwgQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwgg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwgw">[</span>15<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwhA">]</span></span></a></sup> was in attendance at the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011–12_NFL_playoffs" title="2011–12 NFL playoffs" id="mwhQ">2011 Divisional Playoff Game</a> in Baltimore against the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Houston_Texans_season" title="2011 Houston Texans season" id="mwhg">Houston Texans</a>, which the Ravens won 20–13.</p>

<p id="mwwg">The <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland_Public_Secondary_Schools_Athletic_Association" title="Maryland Public Secondary Schools Athletic Association" id="mwww">Maryland Public Secondary Schools Athletic Association</a> (MPSSAA) held the four state football championships for Maryland's public high schools at M&amp;T Bank Stadium until 2016. Two Baltimore <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_football_in_North_America" title="High school football in North America" class="mw-redirect" id="mwxA">high school football</a> rivalry games have been held at the stadium in November. <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_City_College_Football" title="Baltimore City College Football" class="mw-redirect" id="mwxQ">Baltimore City College</a> used to play <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Polytechnic_Institute" title="Baltimore Polytechnic Institute" id="mwxg">Baltimore Polytechnic Institute</a> <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City–Poly_football_rivalry" title="City–Poly football rivalry" id="mwxw">every November</a>, in one of the oldest <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_school_football_rivalries_(100_years+)" title="List of high school football rivalries (100 years+)" class="mw-redirect" id="mwyA">high school football rivalries in the United States</a>. Every Thanksgiving, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyola_Blakefield" title="Loyola Blakefield" id="mwyQ">Loyola Blakefield</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvert_Hall_College_High_School" title="Calvert Hall College High School" id="mwyg">Calvert Hall College</a> also square off in what has now been called for many years as the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football_on_Thanksgiving#High_school_football" title="American football on Thanksgiving" id="mwyw">Turkey Bowl</a>, usually reaching up to 13,000 people in the audience.<sup about="#mwt90" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-21" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-21"}}'><a href="#cite_note-21" id="mwzA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwzQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwzg">[</span>21<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwzw">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mw0A">Both games were once played back-to-back on Thanksgiving Day at <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Stadium_(Baltimore)" title="Memorial Stadium (Baltimore)" class="mw-redirect" id="mw0Q">Memorial Stadium</a>. When City College and Polytechnic joined the MPSSAA before 1994 season, the game was forced to be played in early November, due to MPSSAA rules and playoff schedule.</p>
