<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We are a mere two weeks away from the mid-way point of the NFL season because time is a make-believe construct placed upon us by the man, which I believe is one of the reasons why the people of France are protesting.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The trade deadline is the Tuesday after Week 9, and there will be players on the move within the next handful of weeks. But rather than just throw names and teams into a random generator and tell you why they would be a fit, how about we make actual connections &#8211; both in terms of fit and previous relationships that could lure teams to players currently on the trade market.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">WR Brian Thomas Jr to the New York Giants</h2>
<img src="https://platform.sbnation.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/10/gettyimages-2245058019.jpg?quality=90&amp;strip=all&amp;crop=0,0,100,100" alt="" title="" data-has-syndication-rights="1" data-caption="&lt;p&gt; (Photo by Ian Maule/Getty Images)&lt;/p&gt;&lt;br&gt; | Getty Images" data-portal-copyright="Getty Images" />
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Thomas has as many catches as you and I over his last two games, and it’s clear that he is at the bottom of the totem pole. This stems from last season when Thomas missed time due to injury. Jacksonville traded for Jakobi Meyers, and gave him a three-year, $60 million extension after a hot start with his new team all while Parker Washington was blooming into a star receiver. Washington has remained the team’s top target with Meyers as the No. 2. Thomas isn’t even the No. 3 option, though. Or the No. 4. Or the No. 5. Tight end Brenton Strange is tied for the team lead with 26 catches, and Josh Cameron has out-snapped Thomas over the last two games. Plus, Travis Hunter had a potential breakout game on both offense and defense in Week 6 against the Texans. It’s a matter of when Thomas gets traded, not if he will.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So why the Giants, you may ask. Well, they have a big need at receiver. Outside of Malik Nabers, their next-leading wide receiver is Malachi Fields with 12 catches. Thomas would instantly become the No. 2 receiver on the depth chart. To continue, the New York Giants’ wide receivers coach is Chad Hall, who was Thomas’ position coach in 2024 with the Jaguars when he put up nearly 1,300 yards and was a Pro Bowler as a rookie. And I’m sure that Nabers and Thomas would love to run back their LSU days playing side-by-side. </p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Not to ramble on about this one trade in particular, but it would also help the Giants win a wide open division, but also evaluate J.J. McCarthy and Jaxson Dart. Everyone loves Dart, but he spends more time in the blue tend then under center. If Thomas comes in and helps the Giants’ offense look good and win games under McCarthy, the Giants enter next spring with a quarterback battle on their hands. Having a pair of weapons like Nabers and Thomas would make it much easier to see who the best of the two quarterbacks is, and elevate whomever the starter ends up being.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">WR Stefon Diggs to the Pittsburgh Steelers</h2>
<img src="https://platform.sbnation.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/10/gettyimages-2301046391.jpg?quality=90&amp;strip=all&amp;crop=0,0,100,100" alt="" title="" data-has-syndication-rights="1" data-caption="&lt;p&gt;(Photo by Thearon W. Henderson/Getty Images)&lt;/p&gt;&lt;br&gt; | Getty Images" data-portal-copyright="Getty Images" />
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The writing is on the wall for the Commanders. They’re 1-4-1, although Dan Quinn probably thinks they are 2-3-1. Jayden Daniels’ health, and overall demeanor are becoming real issues that have real question marks.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Steelers fans would likely prefer the younger Thomas, but assuming he ends up in New York, Diggs could be a name to whom they shift their attention.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Yes, he will turn 33 at the end of November, but Diggs is coming off a 1,000-yard season and playing good football for Washington. Through six weeks, he has 24 receptions and four touchdowns. 62.3 percent of his snaps have come from the slot this season, which could lead to the Steelers going with DK Metcalf and Germie Bernard on the outside with Diggs inside as their top three receivers, while also rotating in Michael Pittman and Roman Wilson.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">To make a similar connection to that of Thomas and Chad Hall, Steelers wide receivers coach Adam Henry was with Diggs in Buffalo in 2023 &#8211; a year in which Diggs caught 107 passes.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">EDGE Josh Sweat to Green Bay Packers</h2>
<img src="https://platform.sbnation.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/10/gettyimages-2295512554.jpg?quality=90&amp;strip=all&amp;crop=0,0,100,100" alt="" title="" data-has-syndication-rights="1" data-caption="&lt;p&gt;(Photo by Cooper Neill/Getty Images)&lt;/p&gt;&lt;br&gt; | Getty Images" data-portal-copyright="Getty Images" />
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This one is pretty cut and dry. Packers defensive coordinator Jonathan Gannon coached Sweat when he was the head coach in Arizona. When Micah Parsons returns, a pairing of he and Sweat would give the Packers a tremendous duo off the edge that would help supplement the loss of Edgerrin Cooper.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">S Derwin James to Dallas Cowboys</h2>
<img src="https://platform.sbnation.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/10/gettyimages-2296312616.jpg?quality=90&amp;strip=all&amp;crop=0,0,100,100" alt="" title="" data-has-syndication-rights="1" data-caption="&lt;p&gt;(Photo by Brooke Sutton/Getty Images)&lt;/p&gt;&lt;br&gt; | Getty Images" data-portal-copyright="Getty Images" />
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Chargers are a tire fire and should immediately begin a fire sale with James at the top of the list. </p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As fun as it would be to see James reunite with Jesse Minter in Baltimore to play alongside Kyle Hamilton, the two safeties share a lot of similar responsibilities defensively, so I’ll go with the Cowboys, whose defense continues to give up big play after big play. Dallas has already traded for cornerback Joey Porter Jr, and if there is one thing Jerry Jones isn’t afraid to do, it’s make moves if it gives Dallas a better chance at winning.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The connection for James to Dallas is Derrick Ansley, who is the Cowboys’ defensive backs coach and served as the defensive backs coach with the Chargers from 2021-22 and their defensive coordinator in 2023. This is a Cowboys team that has started very slow due to inconsistencies in large part on defense, and a proven All-Pro like James could help clean up some of those issues.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">EDGE Khalil Mack to Buffalo Bills</h2>
<img src="https://platform.sbnation.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/10/gettyimages-2299588733.jpg?quality=90&amp;strip=all&amp;crop=0,0,100,100" alt="" title="" data-has-syndication-rights="1" data-caption="&lt;p&gt;(Photo by Brooke Sutton/Getty Images)&lt;/p&gt;&lt;br&gt; | Getty Images" data-portal-copyright="Getty Images" />
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The connection to Mack isn’t on the coaching staff, but in the city itself. He is a Buffalo native and went to the University of Buffalo. Considering the nine-time Pro Bowler has yet to appear in a Super Bowl, going back home and playing alongside Greg Rousseau and Bradley Chubb could help both he and the Bills get over the hump.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">RB Jonathan Taylor to Chicago Bears</h2>
<img src="https://platform.sbnation.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/10/gettyimages-2298049875.jpg?quality=90&amp;strip=all&amp;crop=0,0,100,100" alt="" title="" data-has-syndication-rights="1" data-caption="&lt;p&gt;(Photo by Vince Mignott/DeFodi Images/DeFodi via Getty Images)&lt;/p&gt;&lt;br&gt; | DeFodi Images via Getty Images" data-portal-copyright="DeFodi Images via Getty Images" />
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This would be a case of the rich getting richer. The Bears’ offense is already incredibly dynamic, and the Colts are floundering after a loss to the Titans. They fired GM Chris Ballard, and head coach Shane Steichen could be next sooner than later.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Bears making a play for Jonathan Taylor would remind me a lot of the 49ers trading for Christian McCaffrey. Granted, Chicago has yet to reach a Super Bowl, but they also have a better quarterback than the 49ers did at the time of the McCaffrey trade. Putting Taylor behind Caleb Williams would give the Bears a bump in the run game, which they currently rank 16th in success rate.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The connection is a bit of a reach, admittedly, but current offensive coordinator Press Taylor was an offensive assistant with the Colts in 2021, the year Taylor ran for over 1,800 yards and 18 touchdowns. Seeing what he and Ben Johnson would do in the run game with Taylor is something we all deserve to see, and they could be a sneaky team that makes a big move for the All-Pro running back.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Welcome to Week 7 of the fantasy football season! Last week gave us big games from some players we’ve grown accustomed to seeing big games from. Christian McCaffrey, Malik Nabers, Kenneth Walker, and Jaxon Smith-Njigba all did what they usually do. But some studs were just not their usual selves, especially running backs. Bijan Robinson for some reason had just 11 touches against the Bears, which is malpractice by any offensive play caller. Derrick Henry had the touches, with 18, but the Browns defense shut him down for just 60 yards and no touchdowns. Kyren Williams split work with Blake Corum in their surprising loss to the Cardinals, and ended up with just 13 touches for 67 yards and no touchdowns. After the game he was as upset as his fantasy backers, so maybe we can look at a squeaky wheel narrative for Mr. Williams this week? </p>

<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Bye Weeks</h4>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Buffalo Bills, Jacksonville Jaguars. Los Angeles Chargers, and Washington Commanders will take a week off. We’ll be without big names like Josh Allen, James Cook, Trevor Lawrence, Parker Washington, Bayshul Tuten, and Jayden Daniels, but you will get a chance to not worry about Chargers’ players bringing your score down!</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Below, we’ll take a look at some starts and, you guessed it, sits! As usual, your mileage may vary as to who you MUST start, even if they’re in a bad matchup or haven’t played well. It’s always a tight-rope act when deciding who to start, so don’t feel compelled to sit a guy if you do not have a better option. </p>

<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Quarterbacks</h4>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Start</h5>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Jordan Love, Packers vs. Lions</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is a no-brainer, as the Lions defense has been the worst in the league, especially at allowing fantasy points to quarterbacks. Love has cracked the Top 10 fantasy QBs for the first time this season in the last two weeks against the Cowboys and Bears. He has thrown exactly two touchdowns in all but one game, but he added a rushing touchdown in that one, giving him good consistency, but not a bunch of upside. The good news is that the Lions have helped all quarterbacks play better than usual. Jacoby Brissett, Tyler Shough, Geno Smith, and Josh Allen all had their best fantasy games of the year against the Lions. Bryce Young is the only QB not to have peaked against them, but he still threw for 329 yards and two touchdowns. You’re starting Love unless he falls in a pit of melted cheese on the way to the game.</p>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Kyler Murray, Vikings vs. Colts</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Murray played his best game before last week’s bye against the Saints. He didn’t blow the doors off the competition, but his connection with a now healthy Justin Jefferson was strong and he started running more (10-51). He also will get Jordan Addison back from his hamstring injury and the Colts will be without cornerback Sauce Gardner. Also, the Colts aren’t great against the pass, allowing the eighth-most yards per attempt and the eighth-most rushing yards to opposing quarterbacks. </p>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Sit</h5>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Patrick Mahomes, Chiefs at Seahawks</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Mahomes is currently QB12 in fantasy. He’s played well, throwing two or more touchdown passes in all but one game, but this Chiefs offense is balanced and Mahomes doesn’t always need to throw the ball that often, which cuts down on his counting stats. He’s usually a start, especially during a week with four QBs on bye, but playing the Seahaawks in Seattle is going to be difficult. Seattle’s pass defense has given up the fewest yards per pass attempt and the fewest passing yards by a wide margin. Mahomes has been efficient, and getting a couple touchdown passes out of him isn’t out of the question, but I don’t believe that’s a given, and as no QB has topped 189 passing yards against the Seahawks, I’m skeptical of his upside.</p>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Tyler Shough, Steelers vs. Saints (Paris)</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shough has been really good this season, with four Top 8 fantasy finishes in six games. He’s also had two duds against the Falcons and Vikings. The question this week is if he can play across the ocean in Paris against T.J. Watt and company? So far the Steelers have allowed the fourth-fewest fantasy points per game to quarterbacks. The only QB to throw more than one touchdown against them was Joe Burrow. </p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I like Shough and believe he has a chance to perform well for fantasy in tough matchups, but the travel and the matchup make this a hard start this week. </p>

<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Running backs</h4>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Start</h5>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Ollie Gordon, Dolphins vs. Jets</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Gordon has looked much better as the lead back in Miami than I expected. He’s shown explosive ability, and it shows up in the analytics, as he is Top 10 among all running back in explosive run rate. Of course, his team isn’t good and he doesn’t get a bell-cow-like snap percentage. But, this week he gets a good matchup, as the Jets rank 11th-worst in fantasy points allowed to running backs. The Dolphins are also coming off their bye, which I am hoping means they have evaluated their first five games and realized that Gordon should be on the field more. </p>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Jaylen Warren, Steelers</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Warren has shown he’s easily the best running back on the Steelers, but the team wants to give him rest by bringing in Rico Dowdle. The good news is that Warren saw 74% of the snaps last week against the Buccaneers. He still split work, with 14 rushing attempts and four targets to Dowdle’s seven attempts and four targets. This week they get a much better matchup though, as the Saints have allowed the most fantasy points to running backs this season. Sure, the Falcons scored five rushing touchdowns against them to skew those numbers, but the underlying metrics are bad, as they’ve allowed the highest rushing success rate in the league.</p>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Sit</h5>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Chase Brown, Bengals at Ravens</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Brown has not been good this season, but he has remained the lead back. Samaje Perine outplayed him going into the Bengals’ Week 6 bye. Perine isn’t some up and coming back that we’ll see take over as lead back, but I do expect he continues to split time with Brown moving forward. </p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This week they get a tough matchup with a Ravens run defense that’s allowed the seventh-fewest fantasy points and ranks fifth best in stopping explosive runs. They’ve also allowed the fewest receiving yards per game to opposing backs, which is where Brown gets a good percentage of his fantasy value. </p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In the end, Brown is still the starter for a team with a good to great offense at times. He’s worth a flex in most leagues, but I don’t expect big numbers anytime soon. </p>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Emanuel Wilson/Zach Charbonnet, Seahawks vs. Chiefs</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Charbonnet returns this week to face the Chiefs, which will push Wilson’s work down. But, we also don’t know how much work Charbonnet will get. He practiced in full all week, so I expect he’ll be ready to go, but there is a chance they ease him back. The problem for the Seahawks’ running game isn’t who will be running the ball, but who they are facing. Kansas City’s run defense has been one of the best in the league this season. They’ve allowed just 3.5 yards per carry to running backs so far, while giving up the sixth-fewest fantasy points to the position. Wilson isn’t startable, but Charbonnet has enough upside to throw in a flex spot if desperate. </p>

<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Wide receivers</h4>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Start</h5>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Omar Cooper Jr., Jets at Dolphins</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Cooper broke out in his first full game last week against the Patriots. The first-round rookie injured his ankle in Week 1, so it wasn’t until Week 5 that we got to really see him play. Cooper now has 28 routes under his belt and has seen eight targets, catching seven for 133 yards and a touchdown. And yes, he’s a rookie and Adonai Mitchell may return this week from his finger injury, but I see no reason the Jets don’t make their first-rounder the No. 2 receiver behind Garrett Wilson and see if he can continue his hot start. And against a bad Dolphins pass defense, he has a good chance. </p>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Germie Bernard, Stelers vs. Saints (Paris)</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Bernard has stepped up as a PPR stud in the wake of Michael Pittman and Roman Wilson’s injuries. Over the last two weeks he has accumulated 17 receptions for 167 yards and one touchdown. The Saints play a lot of zone, and Bernard has been great against zone so far this season. Aaron Rodgers should be able to find Bernard for plenty of short receptions underneath and I expect at some point we’re going to see the rookie break one of these off for a big gainer. But, even if he doesn’t put up an explosive play, he fits Aaron Rodgers old-man style well. </p>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Sit</h5>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Josh Downs/Keenan Allen, Colts vs. Vikings</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Vikings pass defense and consistent blitzing will likely turn Daniel Jones inside out. Minnesota has only allowed three passing touchdowns and are third best in passing yardage allowed. Downs is pretty hard to start at this point, but Allen has been paying off in PPR leagues. That might not be the case this week though. </p>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Xavier Worthy, Chiefs at Seahawks</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Chiefs tried to get Worthy going last week, as Patrick Mahomes targeted him a team high seven times, of which he caught all seven for 73 yards. He was used mostly as an underneath target, with just 31 total air yards against the Chargers for the speedster. This week he gets a tougher matchup on the road in Seattle. He’ll likely see a lot of CB Josh Jobe, which is bad for Worthy. Overall, the Seahawks have given up the third-fewest fantasy points to wide receivers and if Worthy isn’t getting open deep, I don’t like his chances to consistently put up good numbers.</p>

<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Tight Ends</h4>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Start</h5>

<h5 class="wp-block-heading">Tyler Kraft, Packers vs. Lions</h5>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">You’re going to want to start your Packers players against the Lions this week, but the best matchup of the lot is at tight end. The Lions have been absolutely awful against the position, allowing 51 receptions for 545<br> yards and 9 touchdowns in just five games. On average, opposing tight ends are catching 10 passes for over 100 yards and almost two full touchdowns per game.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The preseason hype surrounding football in Los Angeles was real. The Rams made bold, splashy moves to tweak an already stellar team and presumably turn them into a monster. The Chargers retooled their coaching staff, adding vaunted offensive coordinator Mike McDaniel in a move assumed would <em>finally</em> get the hapless franchise over the hump and into contention.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Fast-forward six weeks and both organizations are a dumpster fire. The Rams (2-4) have drastically underperformed their potential, losing to the Cardinals over the weekend and finding themselves at the bottom of the NFC West with no clear way to catch up. The Chargers (0-6) are even worse, boasting one of the worst offenses in the NFL, hurtling towards a No. 1 overall pick, with a coaching staff that seems incapable of turning the ship around.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The idea of an all-Los Angeles, Chargers vs. Rams Super Bowl at SoFi Stadium didn’t feel like it was outside the realm of possibility at one point. Now the notion of putting these teams together on football’s brightest stage is a hilarious joke. Neither team is likely to make the playoffs at all, and everything that could go wrong, has.</p>

<h4 class="wp-block-heading">What the hell happened to the Los Angeles Rams?</h4>
<img src="https://platform.sbnation.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/10/gettyimages-2300395674.jpg?quality=90&amp;strip=all&amp;crop=0,0,100,100" alt="" title="" data-has-syndication-rights="1" data-caption="&lt;p&gt;(Eric Thayer / Los Angeles Times via Getty Images)&lt;/p&gt;&lt;br&gt;" data-portal-copyright="" />
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We all know that icky feeling when you wake up on the wrong side of the bed. From the second your feet hit the floor you know it’s going to be a rough day, and even the most minor of inconveniences feel like an all-consuming plot to ruin your life. That’s life for the Rams after Sean McVay made the ill-advised move to send his team to Australia late for the season opener, have them play on little rest, and get stomped by the San Francisco 49ers to kick off the season. </p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Rams are sleepwalking through this season. McVay is known for his razor-sharp football mind, routinely coming up with creative ways to problem-solve — but this season there’s nothing he can do to right the ship. Rams players seem like they’re just going through the motions, as if they bought into the preseason hype about their team’s invulnerability and sauntered into the NFL season without putting the work in.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It’s certainly fair to note that the team has been battling injuries, as Puka Nacua missed games, and the defense hasn’t gotten to benefit from Myles Garrett yet, who is still out following offseason surgery. Regardless, it’s difficult to imagine this team being drastically better simply with those additions. This is not a one or two player problem, but a fundamental, holistic breakdown of everything we know the Rams to be when it comes to discipline, focus, and determination.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Special Teams are indicative of how far this team has fallen. Football’s middle child, the Rams have actively avoided spending and strengthening special teams over the past five years under the assumption they would be wasted resources. That has come back to bite them in 2026, as the Rams are allowing 28.86 yards-per-return, 13.50 yards-per-punt-return, and languishing as the worst kicking team in football, going just 6-for-10 on field goals.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The hits just keep coming for the Rams:</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Matthew Stafford is on pace to throw 15 fewer touchdowns than 2025, and double the interceptions</li>



<li>Kyren Williams is poised to have the fewest carries of his career since he was a rookie</li>



<li>Puka Nacua has the worst catch rate and success rate of his career</li>



<li>Davante Adams has been lost for up to 10 weeks due to injury</li>



<li>Poona Ford will miss the rest of the season</li>
</ul>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This isn’t a case of lightning striking out of nowhere, but rather <em>everyone</em> failing on the Rams at the exact same time. Rubbing salt in the wound is the fact that the 49ers are undefeated and pulling away in the NFC West, which makes it incredibly difficult to envision a way Los Angeles can catch up. Instead of eyeing the Super Bowl by Week 13, the Rams could very well be starting Ty Simpson and preparing to make big changes for their future.</p>

<h4 class="wp-block-heading">What the hell happened to the Los Angeles Chargers?</h4>
<img src="https://platform.sbnation.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/10/gettyimages-2301199204.jpg?quality=90&amp;strip=all&amp;crop=0,0,100,100" alt="" title="" data-has-syndication-rights="1" data-caption="(Photo by David Eulitt/Getty Images) | Getty Images" data-portal-copyright="Getty Images" />
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The shared DNA between the Chargers and Rams was expectation, but it came from very different places. With the Rams we’d seen the potential before, and the idea of adding both Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie to the mix felt like a home run. The Chargers on the other hand had all these pieces on paper that were easy to like, but it was clear Jim Harbaugh wasn’t the offensive guy to unlock their potential — which is where Mike McDaniel entered the picture.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This experiment has been a total mess. The Chargers have injury issues along their offensive line, but that doesn’t excuse how horrific Justin Herbert and Co. are at moving the ball. Numerous other teams have equal, if not worse, injuries on the OL but are performing much better than the Chargers are.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Herbert’s precipitous fall is fascinating, because on paper it just doesn’t make any sense. The Chargers’ QB is seeing roughly the same pressure rate as he’s faced over the last three years, and he has a pocket time within 0.2 seconds of his career high, but 17.9% of his passes grade as bad throws, he’s trending towards a career-high 23 interceptions, and this is all happening despite the length of the offense being dropped to 7.5 intended air yards per pass attempt, which would be the lowest since his rookie season.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In short: The offense is easier, Herbert is worse. This extends to the receivers as well, who are dropping 18.1% of passes thrown their way, and unable to move the chains on critical downs. This has led to Herbert forcing the ball too much, which has led to the team gaining just 24.2 yards per drive, down from 32.4 a year ago.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There’s a significant issue with the Chargers offense, but discerning the larger problem is tricky. Offensively, they feel like a team at odds with itself, which was always the risk of trying to combine the football sensibilities of Jim Harbaugh with Mike McDaniel. Harbaugh is an old-school football head who believes in establishing the run, developing the pass off play action, and meticulously breaking down a defense over time. McDaniel, meanwhile, is a new-school coordinator who believes in winning through explosive plays, chunk gains, and unpredictable sets. What we’re left with is a team that is trying to run both systems at the same time, and it’s blowing up in their face.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If we go back to the play action scenario it really highlights the issue this team is having. In both 2022 and 2024, when Justin Herbert was one of the best quarterbacks in the NFL, he threw out of play action 165.5 times a season, for an average gain of 8.7 yards per pass. This year he’s on pace to throw 111 passes out of play action, for an average gain of 6.74 yards. </p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Chargers aren’t getting results on play action because they can’t establish the run, and they can’t establish the run because Mike McDaniel has <em>never</em> been about “establishing the run.” He’s a coordinator who likes to use running backs as home-run explosive threats, not methodical chain movers. As a result the whole vibe around the Chargers is that there are too many cooks in the kitchen on offense, and all of them are burning their soup to hell.</p>

<h4 class="wp-block-heading">What’s next for NFL football in Los Angeles?</h4>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Sadness. With each passing day it feels more and more like these organizations are hurtling towards rebuilds. The Rams were poised to go on this rapid fire, win-at-all-costs run that was designed to bring a trophy or two to the organization before Matthew Stafford retired and they had to retool. Now this team is likely going to be looking at reshaping its roster on an expedited timeline, and they lack the draft picks to get it done — owning their immediate selections to the Browns as part of the Garrett trade.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As for the Chargers, well, they’re the Chargers. It’s difficult to imagine a scenario where Jim Harbaugh is worthy of retaining his job, but it’s even more difficult to imagine the Chargers having the guts to <em>fire</em> someone with Jim Harbaugh’s resume. Instead, it seems the team is more content to scapegoat Mike McDaniel and Omarion Hampton for the offensive issues, because it’s easier than admitting that Justin Herbert has been butt. We’re back to the same old Chargers we’ve known and loved for decades. A team that looks great on paper, but can’t get the job done.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">One thing is certain: These two teams <em>will</em> be going to the Super Bowl — only because both team owners have a vested interest in SoFi Stadium and will get to use their suites to watch two successful teams play football.</p>
