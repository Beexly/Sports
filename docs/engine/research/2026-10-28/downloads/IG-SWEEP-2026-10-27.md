## Item 48 — LEANN: the vector index that stores 60M chunks in 6 GB

- **Creator/Date/URL:** @simplifyinai (Simplifying AI) · shared 2026-10-27 · https://www.instagram.com/p/De-eRt1knz7/
- **What it is:** A carousel about LEANN, an open-source (MIT) vector index from the paper "LEANN: A Low-Storage Vector Index" (arXiv 2506.08276, Wang et al.) — repo yichuan-w/LEANN. Note the carousel never names it; the identification comes from the numbers, which are unique to this project. It is also not new: the paper is from June 2025 and the carousel is recirculating it.
- **Verified (with sources):** The mechanism is the interesting part and it's real: a normal ANN index stores every embedding (768-dim float32 ≈ 3 KB per chunk, forever). LEANN instead builds the proximity graph, then prunes the embeddings of low-degree nodes, keeping stored vectors only for high-degree "hub" nodes that traversal visits constantly. At query time, when graph search steps onto a pruned node, its embedding is recomputed on the fly by re-encoding that chunk's raw text through a persistent embedding server (ZMQ-connected), and the graph itself is stored in compressed sparse row format. Storage-for-compute arbitrage: you keep the raw text + the embedding model, and pay milliseconds of recompute instead of gigabytes of vectors. The headline numbers trace to the paper/repo: 60M chunks (RPJ-Wiki, 256-token chunks, Contriever 768-d) — the paper's table puts raw embeddings at ~171 GB for a flat index; the repo's 201 GB comparison figure is the full traditional stack; LEANN's index is ~6 GB, the "97% less" claim. Sources: https://deepwiki.com/yichuan-w/LEANN ; paper, https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08276v1
- **Unverified/gated:** "No accuracy drop" is the authors' own test result, not an independent replication: the paper reports retrieval and downstream RAG accuracy (NQ, TriviaQA, GPQA, HotpotQA, plus FinanceBench, Enron email, LAION) holding up on their benchmarks, with the cost showing up as latency — retrieval overhead of roughly 3–20% depending on dataset. "Runs on a standard laptop, no GPU" is the project's positioning and is plausible for the index itself, but the recomputation path needs the embedding model running locally at query time, which is where the real resource cost hides.
- **Relevance to Garrett:** Personal + fleet AI infra. - **Disposition:** Candidate for a lane — local AI infra / personal knowledge base. Catalog the latency trade-off with it.
