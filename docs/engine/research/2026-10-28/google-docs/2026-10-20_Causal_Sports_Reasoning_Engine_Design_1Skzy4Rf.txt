Frontier Continuous Causal Inference Engine for Galaxy Sports Edge (Beexly/Sports)
1. Executive Architectural Paradigm: What Is Fundamentally New
Conventional sports prediction architectures—including gradient-boosted decision trees (nflfastR expected points, win probability, and completion probability baselines), Bayesian logistic team-rating systems, and standard retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines—operate exclusively on Rung 1 of Judea Pearl’s Ladder of Causation: observational association P(Y \mid X). When a conventional system observes a star quarterback’s injury tag, a sudden movement in a betting line, a shift to a two-tight-end (2TE) offensive personnel grouping, or an elevated acute-to-chronic workload ratio (ACWR), it computes conditional expectations over historical feature correlations. This associational paradigm suffers from four fatal structural pathologies in adversarial sports markets.
The first pathology is confounded regime shifts and Simpson’s paradox. Observational models cannot distinguish whether an observed metric shift is an autonomous tactical intervention or a symptom of an unmeasured common cause (latent confounder U). For example, teams trailing by multiple scores pass at high rates and accumulate high raw passing yardage; a naive correlational learner associates pass-heavy play-calling with losing, whereas the interventional effect P(\text{Win} \mid \text{do}(\text{PassRate} = p)) on early downs is strongly positive. Similarly, elevated ACW[span_1](start_span)[span_1](end_span)R values appear confounded by game schedule congestion unless explicitly conditioned on current session load.
The second pathology is unsigned context collapsing and decorative citation attachment. Audits of legacy Beexly/Sports codepaths revealed that while 46 to 240+ signal families produced contextual scalars (such as expected points added, wind velocity, rest differential, and roof state), these signals lacked declared directional semantics (homeSign), sample counts, and interventional mechanisms. Worse, legacy literature integration (mind-reasoning.ts v5.3.0) relied on hardcoded constants (0.015, 0.024, 0.012) and static prose templates (+395 vs +485 fair odds, 18.5% parlay correlation), using keyword regular expressions over MIND_CORPUS_3000.jsonl merely to decorate pre-authored heuristics with academic citations—a flaw where papers on the No Free Lunch optimization theorem or magnetic resonance physics were misclassified under NFL situational labels.
The third pathology is the entity-to-relation deficit in multimodal perception. Benchmarks of state-of-the-art vision-language models on physical and situational world modeling (CausalPhys) demonstrate a severe structural decoupling: models achieve high Entity Faithfulness (\text{EF} \approx 0.697) in recognizing players, formations, and attributes, but collapse on Relation Awareness (\text{RA} \approx 0.286 on interventions and 0.305 overall), failing to compose perceived entities into valid directed causal mechanisms.
The fourth pathology is static uncertainty under non-exchangeable distribution shift. Sports data streams violate the independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) and exchangeability assumptions required by classical split conformal prediction. Roster turnover, mid-game injuries, weather fronts, and market-maker pricing adaptations induce continuous covariate and concept drift. Simultaneously, applying rapidly adapting conformal intervals directly into fractional Kelly position sizing degrades compound bankroll growth by 1.4 to 3.3 percentage points per year due to nonlinear variance amplification in the Kelly denominator.
The Galaxy Sports Edge (GSE) Continuous Causal Inference Engine is an end-to-end architecture that replaces weighted-feature blending and associative RAG with an online, self-correcting Structural Causal Model (SCM) and Living Literature Graph.

In Stage 5 (Interventional Calculus and Automated Adversary Review via DoCalculusIDEngine and AdversaryRedTeam), for every actionable shock X=x—such as \text{do}(\text{LeftTackle}=\text{Inactive}), \text{do}(\text{Scheme}=\text{Cover6}), or \text{do}(\text{MarketSteam}=2.5\text{ pts/hr})—the engine executes graph mutilation \mathcal{G}_{\overline{X}} (severing incoming arrows to the intervened node) and runs the complete Shpitser-Pearl ID algorithm over Confounded Components (C-components). 3.1 In-Writing Constrained Decoding and Four-Layer Verification Gates
Forcing an LLM to emit JSON schema tokens from decoding step t=0 imposes a severe constraint tax: because both the latent deliberation sequence R and the structured extraction A are conditioned on the grammar automaton F, P(R \mid q, F) = \frac{1}{Z} P(R \mid q)\mathbf{1}_{R \in F}, the model must commit to principle_name before localizing the verbatim evidence_quote and mechanism_quote. GSE eliminates this distortion via the In-Writing Decoupled Extraction Protocol implemented in XGrammar-2 using StructuralTag and TagDispatch:
P(R \mid q, F) = P(R \mid q) \implies P(A \mid q, F) = \sum_{R} P(A \mid R, q, F) P(R \mid q)
During the unconstrained <|GSE_ANALYSIS|> channel (excluding <|GSE_JSON|> to prevent premature triggering on mathematical set notation like [span_52](start_span)[span_52](end_span)[span_55](start_span)[span_55](end_span)S = \{x_1, x_2\}), the model verifies domain relevance (committing to abstained: true, extracted_principle: null for non-sports domains such as No Free Lunch optimization or MRI physics), localizes the exact \le 240-character evidence_quote containing the numeric_claim, and localizes the \le 300-character mechanism_quote. Upon emitting <|GSE_JSON|>, XGrammar-2 activates token-level logit masking \tilde{\mathbf{z}} = \mathbf{z} + \mathbf{M}^{(q_t)} with Repetition State Compression, collapsing the 240 and 300 character-counting states into a 28.4\text{ KB} (0.028\text{ MB}) adaptive cache that fits entirely inside the CPU L1 data cache. To prevent CPU–GPU synchronization stalls during batch ingestion, the worker pool enforces the non-stalling batch threshold:
B_{\max} = \left\lfloor \frac{T_{\text{GPU}}(B) - T_{\text{sync}}}{\frac{t_{\text{mask}}}{N_{\text{cores}}} + t_{\text{xfer}}} \right\rfloor
Using 16-core C++ batched bitset packing (4.63\ \mu\text{s}/sequence), the pipeline scales to B = 128\text{--}256 concurrent paper extractions with zero GPU wait penalty.
Because free-string grammar states (q_{\text{str}}) permit closing quotes (", ID 1 or ",", ID 3093) at any character index L \in [15, 240], token-level masking alone cannot enforce semantic grounding. Every extracted candidate must therefore pass a Four-Layer Deterministic Verification Gate before insertion into the canonical index. Layer L1 (Verbatim Quote Provenance) verifies that both evidence_quote and mechanism_quote exist as verbatim substrings in the source abstract or dataset documentation. Layer L2 (Literal Numeric Token Containment) enforces that the exact numeric_claim string is a literal substring of evidence_quote (numeric_claim in evidence_quote), and deterministically rejects any residual hardcoded fallback magnitude (\{0.22, 0.16, 0.18, 0.15, 0.238\}) that is absent from the claim string. Layer L3 (SAV Character-Offset Anchoring with MSVH Numeric Invariance) computes the exact CharacterSpan (start_char, end_char) under tier MSVH_S1_EXACT, MSVH_S2_NORMALIZED, or MSVH_S3_LEVENSHTEIN, requiring 100\% numeric token invariance between the extracted quote and the raw source span. Finally, Layer L4 (Mechanism Entailment and Lexical Exclusion Gate) runs a cross-encoder Natural Language Inference (NLI) scorer combined with a domain lexical exclusion filter to verify that mechanism_quote entails the causal or statistical relationship for the specific applicable_context_fields enum with a score \ge 0.80, rejecting cross-domain false positives (L4_MECHANISM_ENTAILMENT_FAIL).
3.2 Canonical pgvector HNSW Schema and Parameter Optimization
Verified principles from arXiv, OpenAlex, Kaggle, and MIND_CORPUS_3000.jsonl are stored in Neon PostgreSQL (neondb) with the pgvector extension. Based on the Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) formalism of Malkov and Yashunin (2016, 2020), each premise vector \mathbf{v}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{1024} is inserted into a multi-layer proximity graph where its maximum layer l_i is drawn from an exponentially decaying distribution l_i = \lfloor -\ln(\text{unif}(0, 1)) \cdot m_L \rfloor with optimal scale multiplier m_L = 1/\ln(M) to minimize inter-layer neighbor overlap while preserving logarithmic zoom-in routing.

3.3 Dual-Frequency Spectral Routing (BRIDGE) and Content-Driven Delta Equations
Standard vector retrieval suffers from an alignment-ranking paradox identified in BRIDGE (Li et al., CIKM 2026): over-aligning multimodal and textual representations suppresses private, high-frequency ranking variation and blurs distinctions between genuinely applicable situational mechanisms and superficial lookalikes. GSE integrates the BRIDGE Dual-Frequency Graph Evidence (DFGE) and Candidate Residual Integrator (CRI) directly into arxiv-reasoning-bridge.ts and mind-reasoning.ts.
In the Spectral Band Decomposition stage, let \mathbf{Z}^c \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d} denote the graph-smoothed representation matrix over the premise-signal bipartite interaction graph for channel c \in \{\text{id}, \text{context}, \text{text}\} after L=2 rounds of symmetric normalized message passing \mathbf{H}^{c,(\ell+1)} = \hat{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{H}^{c,(\ell)} where \hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{D}^{-1/2}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{D}^{-1/2}. Computing the singular value decomposition \mathbf{Z}^c = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^\top, the right singular directions are partitioned in descending singular-value order into M=3 contiguous spectral bands \mathbf{V} = [\mathbf{V}_1, \mathbf{V}_2, \mathbf{V}_3] (splitting 64 dimensions into 22/21/21 low/mid/high bands):
\mathbf{H}^{c,m} = \mathbf{Z}^c \mathbf{V}_m \mathbf{V}_m^\top, \qquad \mathbf{e}_n = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \omega_m \alpha_n^m \mathbf{h}_n^m, \qquad \alpha_n^m = 1 + \rho \cdot \sigma[span_59](start_span)[span_59](end_span)(\mathbf{W}_g \mathbf{z}_n + \mathbf{b}_g)_m, \qquad \omega_m = \sigma(a_m)
Here, the leading low-frequency band (m=1) captures shared cross-view domain consensus, while the high-frequency band (m=3) preserves fine-grained situational discrimination.
In the Candidate-Scoped Behavior Residual Calibration stage, for an incoming game context event u triggering context primitive c \in \mathcal{C} with observed value x_c, the HNSW index retrieves the base top-K_c candidate set (K_c = 50):
\mathcal{C}_u = \text{TopK}\left(s_{\text{base}}(u, \cdot), K_c\right), \qquad \text{where} \quad s_{\text{base}}(u, i) = \langle \mathbf{e}_u, \mathbf{e}_i \rangle
From historical training settlements \mathcal{D}_{\text{tr}} (strictly excluding validation and 2025/2026 holdout partitions), the Behavior Evidence Normalizer (BEN) computes co-occurring premise success overlap B_{ij} = \frac{|\mathcal{U}_i^{\text{tr}} \cap \mathcal{U}_j^{\text{tr}}|}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{U}_i^{\text{tr}}| \cdot |\mathcal{U}_j^{\text{tr}}|}}, aggregates historical support \tilde{b}_{ui} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_u^{\text{tr}}|}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{H}_u^{\text{tr}}} B_{ij}, and z-score normalizes it into b_{ui} = \frac{\tilde{b}_{ui} - \mu_u}{\sigma_u + \epsilon}. The Candidate Residual Integrator (CRI) then calibrates the retrieval activation score strictly inside the candidate scope \mathcal{C}_u, combined with the dynamic settlement promotion score \pi_i^{(t)}:
s_{\text{f[span_175](start_span)[span_175](end_span)inal}}(u, i) = s_{\text{base}}(u, i) + \mathbb{I}[i \in \mathcal{C}_u] \cdot \lambda_b b_{ui} + \lambda_\pi \ln \pi_i^{(t)}
In the Pure Content-Driven Delta and Opposing-Claim Deadband stage, let \mathcal{M}_c \subseteq \mathcal{C}_u be the retrieved active premises matching primitive c with s_{\text{final}}(u, i) \ge \tau_{\text{ret}}. For each premise i \in \mathcal{M}_c, the normalized context displacement from baseline anchor x_{c,0} is \delta x_{c,i} = \frac{x_c - x_{c,0}}{u_i}, where u_i is unit_scale_denominator. The epistemic confidence weight w_i^{\text{eff}} integrates the study design class w_i \in \{1.0, 0.5, 0.3\} (causal, correlational, theoretical) with the settlement-learned promotion score \pi_i^{(t)}:
w_i^{\text{eff}} = w_i \cdot \pi_i^{(t)}, \qquad \text{where} \quad w_i = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{if } \text{claim\_type}_i = \text{"causal"} \\ 0.5 & \text{if } \text{claim\_type}_i = \text{"correlational"} \land \text{confound\_conditioned}_i = \text{True} \\ 0.3 & \text{if } \text{claim\_type}_i = \text{"theoretical"} \lor \text{confound\_conditioned}_i = \text{False} \end{cases}
The directional sign operator s_i(x_c) \in \{-1, 0, +1\} evaluates influence_direction and any conditional_threshold predicate \mathcal{T}_i(x_c) \in \{0, 1\}, yielding the individual proposed delta \delta_{c,i} = s_i(x_c) \cdot |\beta_i| \cdot \delta x_{c,i}, where |\beta_i| is parsed_float_magnitude. Over the active subset \mathcal{M}_c^{\text{active}} = \{i \in \mathcal{M}_c : s_i(x_c) \neq 0\}, the engine computes the confidence-weighted aggregate delta \bar{\Delta}_c and directional consensus ratio \eta_c \in [0, 1]:
\bar{\Delta}_c = \frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{M}_c^{\text{active}}} w_i^{\text{eff}} \cdot \delta_{c,i}}{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{M}_c^{\text{active}}} w_i^{\text{eff}}}, \qquad \eta_c = \frac{\left| \sum_{i \in \mathcal{M}_c^{\text{active}}} w_i^{\text{eff}} \cdot \delta_{c,i} \right|}{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{M}_c^{\text{active}}} w_i^{\text{eff}} \cdot |\delta_{c,i}|}
Applying the intermediate field clamp \Delta_{\max} = 0.10 (FIELD_DELTA_CLAMP) and the opposing-claim deadband threshold \eta_{\min} = 0.20 yields the final literature adjustment:
\Delta_c^{\text{final}} = \begin{cases} \text{clip}\left(\bar{\Delta}_c, -\Delta_{\max}, +\Delta_{\max}\right) & \text{if } \eta_c \ge 0.20 \land |\bar{\Delta}_c| \ge 10^{-4} \quad (\text{status: APPLIED}) \\ 0.0 & \text{if } \eta_c < 0.20 \lor |\bar{\Delta}_c| < 10^{-4} \quad (\text{status: CONFLICT\_NEUTRALIZED}) \end{cases}
4. Causal Inference as the Core Product: SCMs, do-Calculus, and Adversary Review
Rather than treating \Delta_c^{\text{final}} and bridge-model.ts outputs as flat additive features in a linear tilt, the GSE engine constructs an explicit Structural Causal Model (SCM) \mathcal{M} = \langle \mathcal{U}, \mathcal{V}, \mathcal{F}, P(\mathcal{U}) \rangle for every game and player prop. Here, \mathcal{V} = \{V_1, \dots, V_d\} are endogenous observable game variables, \mathcal{U} are exogenous background and unmeasured confounding variables with joint distribution P(\mathcal{U}), and \mathcal{F} = \{f_1, \dots, f_d\} are deterministic structural equations v_j = f_j(\text{pa}_j, u_j). Whenever exogenous terms U_j, U_k \in \mathcal{U} are correlated (representing unmeasured common causes such as undisclosed injury severity or private syndicate information), the induced graph is a semi-Markovian Acyclic Directed Mixed Graph (ADMG) \mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}_d, \mathcal{E}_b) containing both directed causal arrows V_i \to V_j \in \mathcal{E}_d and bidirected confounding arcs V_i \leftrightarrow V_j \in \mathcal{E}_b.

	4.2 Hybrid Causal Discovery: Remediating the NOTEARS Scale Trap with Interventional Constraints
To discover edges among the 240+ signal families without relying solely on manual domain graphs, GSE combines continuous DAG optimization (NOTEARS / DYNOTEARS; Zheng et al. 2018, 2020; Pamfil et al. 2020) with constraint-based latent discovery (FCI). Standard NOTEARS estimates a weighted adjacency matrix \mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d} by minimizing regularized least-squares loss subject to the smooth acyclicity characterization h(\mathbf{W}) = \text{tr}(e^{\mathbf{W} \circ \mathbf{W}}) - d = 0 via the Augmented Lagrangian method.
However, as demonstrated by Kaiser and Sipos (2021), naive NOTEARS is not scale-invariant: in a two-node system X_0, X_1, minimizing residual variance pushes the directed edge from the lower-variance node to the higher-variance node (X_0 \to X_1 when \text{Var}(X_1) > \text{Var}(X_0)), meaning arbitrary unit rescaling (such as measuring altitude in feet versus kilometers, or wind in mph versus m/s) flips causal directionality during the first L-BFGS-B inner update step. GSE eliminates this pathology through three mathematical modifications. First, it enforces a Precision-Preconditioned Non-Equal Variance Log-Likelihood, standardizing all continuous context variables against their conditional prior precision and replacing the least-squares objective with the scale-invariant Gaussian log-likelihood loss with an explicit log-determinant penalty:
\ell_{\text{NV}}(\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{X}) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{j=1}^{d} \ln \left( \left\| (\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W})_{:,j} \right\|_2^2 \right) - \ln \left| \det(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{W}) \right|
Second, it incorporates Bitemporal Time-Series Decomposition (DYNOTEARS), modeling contemporaneous (intra-slice) edges \mathbf{W}_0 and time-lagged (inter-slice) edges \mathbf{A}_1, \dots, \mathbf{A}_p across plays and weeks jointly via \mathbf{X}_t = \mathbf{X}_t \mathbf{W}_0 + \sum_{\tau=1}^p \mathbf{X}_{t-\tau} \mathbf{A}_\tau + \mathbf{E}_t, where temporal precedence strictly forbids future-to-past edges and acyclicity is enforced solely on \mathbf{W}_0. Third, it applies Literature-Anchored Interventional Path Constraints. Verified causal premises from literature_premises impose hard structural support masks \mathcal{S}_{\text{lit}}^{[span_62](start_span)[span_62](end_span)[span_66](start_span)[span_66](end_span)+} (known active mechanisms where W_{ij} \neq 0) and temporal/physical tier masks \mathcal{S}_{\text{forbid}}^{0} (such as forbidding game score from causally altering pre-game wind speed: W_{\text{score} \to \text{wind}} = 0). Denoting the total causal effect matrix across all directed paths by \mathbf{T}(\mathbf{W}) = \sum_{k=1}^{d-1} \mathbf{W}^k = (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{[span_63](start_span)[span_63](end_span)[span_67](start_span)[span_67](end_span)W})^{-1} - \mathbf{I}, the constrained Augmented Lagrangian objective solved by GSE is:
\min_{\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}} \mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\mathbf{W}) = \ell_{\text{NV}}(\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{X}) + \lambda_1 \|\mathbf{W}\|_1 + \frac{\rho}{2} |h(\mathbf{W})|^2 + \alpha h(\mathbf{W}) + \frac{\mu_{\text{lit}}}{2} \sum_{(j,i) \in \mathcal{S}_{\text{lit}}^{+}} \left( T_{ij}(\mathbf{W}) - \Delta_{j \to i}^{\text{lit}} \right)^2 \quad \text{s.t.} \quad W_{uv} = 0 \ \forall (u,v) \in \mathcal{S}_{\text{forbid}}^{0}
where h(\mathbf{W}) = \text{tr}(e^{\mathbf{W} \circ \mathbf{W}}) - d = 0. After convergence (h(\mathbf{W}) < 10^{-6}), Fast Causal Inference (FCI) conditional independence tests run over the residual vectors \hat{\mathbf{E}} = \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{X}\hat{\mathbf{W}}; any statistically significant residual partial correlation between nodes V_i and V_j that cannot be directed without violating acyclicity or temporal tiering is instantiated as a bidirected latent confounding arc V_i \leftrightarrow V_j \in \mathcal{E}_b.
4.3 Pearl's do-Calculus, Graph Mutilation, and the Shpitser-Pearl ID Algorithm
When a live shock arrives—such as a left tackle ruled Inactive, a defensive coordinator shifting to Cover 2 Man, public betting tickets reaching 82\% on a favorite while the line moves the opposite way (RLM), or a pre-snap formation shift to Empty—the engine must compute the interventional distribution P(\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{y} \mid \text{do}(\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{x})), denoted P_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}), rather than the observational conditional P(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{x}).
Let \mathcal{G} be the causal ADMG, and let \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}, \mathbf{Z}, \mathbf{W} \subseteq \mathcal{V} be disjoint subsets of vertices. Graph mutilation operates through three canonical topological transformations: \mathcal{G}_{\overline{\mathbf{X}}} denotes the incoming-mutilated graph obtained by deleting from \mathcal{G} all directed and bidirected edges pointing into nodes in \mathbf{X}, physically severing the natural causes that normally govern \mathbf{X}; \mathcal{G}_{\underline{\mathbf{Z}}} denotes the outgoing-mutilated graph obtained by deleting from \mathcal{G} all directed edges emerging from nodes in \mathbf{Z}, blocking direct causal transmission out of \mathbf{Z}; and \mathcal{G}_{\overline{\mathbf{X}}\under[span_179](start_span)[span_179](end_span)[span_181](start_span)[span_181](end_span)[span_183](start_span)[span_183](end_span)line{\mathbf{Z}}} denotes the graph with both incoming arrows to \mathbf{X} and outgoing arrows from \mathbf{Z} removed. Under the positivity condition P(\mathbf{x} \mid \text{Pa}(\mathbf{X})_{\mathcal{G}} \setminus \mathbf{X}) > 0, Pearl’s three rules of do-calculus (Pearl 1995, 2000, 2009) govern all valid algebraic transformations of interventional distributions via d-separation (\perp\!\!\!\perp_d) in mutilated graphs. Rule 1 (Insertion/Deletion of Observations) states that:
P_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{z}, \mathbf{w}) = P_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{w}) \qquad \text{if } (\mathbf{Y} \perp\!\!\!\perp_d \mathbf{Z} \mid \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{W})_{\mathcal{G}_{\overline{\mathbf{X}}}}
Rule 2 (Action/Observation Exchange) states that:
P_{\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{w}) = P_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{z}, \mathbf{w}) \qquad \text{if } (\mathbf{Y} \perp\!\!\!\perp_d \mathbf{Z} \mid \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{W})_{\mathcal{G}_{\overline{\mathbf{X}}\underline{\mathbf{Z}}}}
Rule 3 (Insertion/Deletion of Actions) states that:
P_{\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{w}) = P_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{w}) \qquad \text{if } (\mathbf{Y} \perp\!\!\!\perp_d \mathbf{Z} \mid \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{W})_{\mathcal{G}_{\overline{\mathbf{X}}\overline{\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{W})}}}
where \mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{W}) = \mathbf{Z} \setminus \text{An}(\mathbf{W})_{\mathcal{G}_{\overline{\mathbf{X}}}} is the subset of \mathbf{Z} nodes that are not ancestors of any node in \mathbf{W} in the mutilated graph \mathcal{G}_{\overline{\mathbf{X}}}.
Rather than searching heuristically over Rules 1–3 at runtime, GSE implements the sound and complete Shpitser-Pearl ID algorithm (Shpitser & Pearl 2006, 2008; Tian & Pearl 2002) based on Confounded Component (C-component) factorization. A subset S \subseteq \mathcal{V} is a C-component of \mathcal{G} if every pair of vertices in S is connected by a path consisting entirely of bidirected confounding arcs \leftrightarrow in \mathcal{G}. Every ADMG \mathcal{G} uniquely partitions its observable vertices \mathcal{V} into maximal C-components \mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G}) = \{S_1, \dots, S_k\}. By Tian and Pearl’s Q-decomposition lemma, the interventional distribution of any set S_j when all other variables are intervened upon, Q[S_j](\mathbf{v}) := P_{\mathbf{v} \setminus \mathbf{s}_j}(\mathbf{s}_j), is identifiable directly from the observational distribution P(\mathbf{v}) under topological ordering \pi = (V_1 \prec \dots \prec V_d):
P(\mathbf{v}) = \prod_{j=1}^{k} Q[S_j](\mathbf{v}), \qquad \text{where} \quad Q[S_j](\mathbf{v}) = \prod_{V_i \in S_j} P\left(v_i \ \middle|\ v_{\pi^{(i-1)}}\right)
and the truncated factorization (g-formula) over \mathcal{V} \setminus \mathbf{X} decomposes as:
P_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{v} \setminus \mathbf{x}) = \prod_{S_j \in \mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G}[\mathcal{V} \setminus \mathbf{X}])} Q_{\mathcal{G}[\mathcal{V} \setminus \mathbf{X}]}[S_j]
To establish the necessary and sufficient graphical condition for non-identifiability, an ADMG \mathcal{F} that is a single C-component where every node has at most one directed child (forming a directed forest pointing toward root set \mathbf{R}) is defined as an \mathbf{R}-rooted C-forest. For disjoint sets \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y} \subseteq \mathcal{V}, two \mathbf{R}-rooted C-forests \mathcal{F}, \mathcal{F}' in \mathcal{G} form a hedge for P_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}) if \mathcal{F}' \subset \mathcal{F}, \mathbf{X} \cap \mathcal{V}(\mathcal{F}') = \emptyset, \mathbf{X} \cap (\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{F}) \setminus \mathcal{V}(\mathcal{F}')) \neq \emptyset, and \mathbf{R} \subseteq \text{An}(\mathbf{Y})_{\mathcal{G}_{\overline{\mathbf{X}}}}. By Shpitser and Pearl’s Hedge Theorem, P_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}) is identifiable from P(\mathbf{v}) in \mathcal{G} if and only if no hedge exists in \mathcal{G} for any P_{\mathbf{x}'}(\mathbf{y}') with \mathbf{X}' \subseteq \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}' \subseteq \mathbf{Y}.

4.4 Automated Adversary Review: E-Values, Counterfactual Refutation, and Chain Demotion
Even when P_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}) is graphically identifiable in \mathcal{G}, the edge could be fragile to an unmodeled latent confounder U_{\text{missing}} \notin \mathcal{E}_b or to lexical/entity overfitting. The first test is VanderWeele and Ding (2017) E-Value Sensitivity Analysis. For every active causal path X \to Y yielding an interventional risk ratio RR = \frac{P(Y=1 \mid \text{do}(X=1))}{P(Y=1 \mid \text{do}(X=0))} (where RR is inverted as RR^* = 1/RR if RR < 1 so that RR^* \ge 1), let RR_{EU} denote the maximum risk ratio relating an unmeasured confounder U to the intervention X, and RR_{UD} denote the maximum risk ratio relating U to outcome Y, conditional on measured covariates \mathbf{Z}. The E-value quantifies the minimum strength of association on the risk-ratio scale that an unmeasured confounder would need to have with both X and Y to explain away the estimated causal effect entirely (RR_{\text{true}} = 1.0):
\text{E-value}(\hat{RR}^*) = \hat{RR}^* + \sqrt{\hat{RR}^* \left(\hat{RR}^* - 1\right)}
Simultaneously, the engine computes the E-value for the lower limit of the 95\% confidence interval LL (if LL \le 1, \text{E-value}(LL) = 1.0; if LL > 1, \text{E-value}(LL) = LL + \sqrt{LL(LL - 1)}). For continuous outcomes (such as player rushing yards or point spread margin with standardized effect size d = \frac{\hat{\Delta}_Y}{\sigma_Y} and standard error s_d), the risk ratio approximation RR \approx \exp(0.91 \cdot d) yields:
\text{E-va[span_306](start_span)[span_306](end_span)[span_312](start_span)[span_312](end_span)lue}(d) = \exp(0.91 |d|) + \sqrt{\exp(0.91 |d|) \left(\exp(0.91 |d|) - 1\right)}
If \text{E-value}(\hat{RR}^*) < \tau_{E,\text{point}} = 1.35 or \text{E-value}(LL) \le \tau_{E,\text{CI}} = 1.10, an unmeasured confounder of trivial magnitude could nullify the causal claim; the Adversary Review module demotes the chain to FRAGILE_CONFOUND_RISK and clamps its interventional contribution to zero.
The second test is Pearl Rung 3 Counterfactual Refutation (Abduction–Action–Prediction). 5.1 Murphy (1973) Brier Score Vector Partition and Calibration Diagnostics
Given a rolling window of N settled predictions \{p_t, y_t\}_{t=1}^N partitioned into K probability bins \mathcal{B}_1, \dots, \mathcal{B}_K of size N_k = |\mathcal{B}_k|, with bin mean forecast \bar{p}_k = \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{t \in \mathcal{B}_k} p_t, bin realized frequency \bar{y}_k = \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{t \in \mathcal{B}_k} y_t, and overall base rate \bar{y} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N y_t, calibration-tracker.ts computes the exact Murphy (1973) Vector Partition of the Brier Score:
\text{BS} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^{N} (p_t - y_t)^2 = \underbrace{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^{K} N_k (\bar{p}_k - \bar{y}_k)^2}_{\text{REL[span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span) (Reliability / Calibration Error)} \downarrow} - \underbrace{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^{K} N_k (\bar{y}_k - \bar{y})^2}_{\text{RES (Resolution / Sharpness)} \uparrow} + \underbrace{\bar{y}(1 - \bar{y})}_{\text{UNC (Climatological Uncertainty)}}
Alongside \text{BS}, the tracker computes the Expected Calibration Error \text{ECE} = \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{N_k}{N} |\bar{y}_k - \bar{p}_k| and the strictly proper Log Loss \mathcal{L}_{\text{log}} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \left[ y_t \ln p_t + (1-y_t)\ln(1-p_t) \right] against the coin-flip baseline \ln 2 \approx 0.6931. Whenever the trailing 30-game reliability error \text{REL}_{30} or \text{ECE}_{30} exceeds the trailing 250-game baseline \text{ECE}_{250} by > 0.02, a regime-shift flag CALIBRATION_DRIFT_ALERT is raised.
