# NFL Defensive Prop Edges: TFL, QB Hits, Pressures, PDs, INT Splits

*Research compiled from PFF, Pro Football Reference (2025 regular season), PropsBot.AI, ESPN, Gridiron Decoded, FantasyLife, and Establish The Run. Last updated: August 2026.*

---

## Edge 1: TFL OVER for Edge Rushers vs. Run-First Opponents

**Metric:** Tackles for Loss (TFL) — every TFL is behind the line of scrimmage, but *not* every TFL is a sack. TFLs clear on run plays, screens, and backside pursuit — exactly the situations where books misprice the market.

**The Edge:** Sportsbooks price TFL props like they're sack-adjacent (since sacks ⊂ TFL). But edge rushers who excel at backside pursuit, chase down screens, and set the edge generate TFLs independently of passing situations. Against run-first offenses, the TFL OVER is the most consistently mispriced market on the board.

**Key Data (2025):**
- **T.J. Watt** — PFR: 7.0 sacks, but TFL prop over 0.5 sits at **-130 to -180** week-to-week. PropsBot identifies this as "*the most mispriced market on his board*" because books treat it like a sack prop when it clears on run plays, screens, and pursuit.
- **Myles Garrett** — 23.0 sacks, **33 TFL**, 39 QB hits, 60 combined tackles (Gridiron Decoded). Garrett's TFL total exceeds his sack count by 10, showing massive non-sack disruption.
- **Maxx Crosby** — 10.0 sacks but **28 TFL** (Gridiron Decoded), a +18 spread that reveals constant backfield presence without the sack stat line.

**Betting Angle:** Target TFL OVER against run-first opponents (AFC North, physical matchups). Avoid the correlated sack+TFL parlay in run-heavy scripts — the two markets want *opposite* opponent profiles.

**Source:** [PropsBot — T.J. Watt Props](https://propsbot.ai/players/tj-watt/), [Gridiron Decoded](https://gridirondecoded.com/nfl-stats/defense-stats-decoded/)

## Edge 2: QB Hits OVER for High-Pressure-Rate, Low-Sack Rushers

**Metric:** QB Hits — credited when a defender reaches the QB *after* he's released the ball. This metric captures disruption that sacks cannot, and it's far more accessible than pressures (which require PFF charting).

**The Edge:** Players who consistently pressure the QB without converting to sacks are systematically undervalued on their sack props but *overlooked* on the QB-hits market. Their high QB-hit rate signals real disruptive impact that bookmakers don't fully price into the OVER.

**Key Data (2025):**
- **Zach Allen, DEN** — 7.0 sacks, **47 QB hits** (led the NFL), first-team All-Pro. Gridiron Decoded: "Forty-seven hits means he was reaching the quarterback constantly and someone else was finishing the play."
- **Aidan Hutchinson, DET** — 14.5 sacks, 20 QB hits, **leads the NFL in total pressures** and 4th-quarter pressure rate (Instagram/PFF).
- **Will Anderson Jr., HOU** — 12.0 sacks, career-highs in TFL and QB hits in his first fully healthy campaign (FantasyLife).
- **Trey Hendrickson, CIN** — 5th in pressure rate at **16.8%** among edge defenders (137 qualifiers), 64 QB hits since 2021 (4th at position), 346 total pressures. Still available at +800 DPOY odds — name recognition lags behind production.

**Betting Angle:** Fade the sack prop. Target **QB Hits OVER** for rushers whose pressure rate exceeds their sack rate. Players on teams that generate pressure via 4-man rushes (Broncos 39.3%, Browns 38.2%) benefit because the QB is under constant duress, increasing hit opportunities even without sacks.

**Source:** [Gridiron Decoded](https://gridirondecoded.com/nfl-stats/defense-stats-decoded/), [PFF Free Agency](https://www.pff.com/news/nfl-free-agency-2026-early-look-at-the-top-graded-players-set-to-hit-the-market), [PFF Pressure Article](https://www.pff.com/news/nfl-qb-pressure-teams-with-highest-pressure-rates-from-four-man-rushes-blitzes-stunts)

## Edge 3: Interior DT Sack UNDER Despite Elite Pressure Metrics

**Metric:** Pass rush win rate at DT — the rate at which an interior rusher beats his blocker within 2.5 seconds. Elite interior pressure doesn't translate to sacks at the same rate as edge pressure because of scheme (quick throws, hot routes, QB rollouts).

**The Edge:** Interior defensive tackles with elite pressure win rates are *underpriced* on the SACK UNDER because books assume pressure = sacks. But interior rushers rarely convert pressure to sacks, especially in neutral or negative game scripts. The ESPN model regularly prices these UTs at -300+ when the market line is -210 or shorter.

**Key Data (2025):**
- **Byron Murphy II, MIN** — 7.0 sacks, but a **9.2% pass rush win rate at DT** (17th-best among qualifiers) and **6.7% pressure rate** (9th-best). ESPN's model priced his **UNDER 0.25 sacks at -216**, but the model's fair value was **-309**.
- **Milton Williams, PHI** — 5.5 sacks, **13% pass rush win rate at DT** (would rank 6th if he qualified — ESPN), key cog in the Eagles' 35.5% standard-pressure-rate defense.
- **Jalen Carter, PHI** — **18.8% pressure rate** (4th-highest at DT) per CBS Sports, the centerpiece of Philly's interior disruption. High pressure, modest sack volume.

**Betting Angle:** When an interior DT has a sack line of 0.25–0.5 and elite pressure metrics, **bet the UNDER**. The losing-team game script (fewer passing downs) and position-based sack limitations compound. A DT sack OVER is only playable if the opposing QB is immobile AND the DT's team is favored.

**Source:** [ESPN Super Bowl Betting Analysis](https://www.espn.com/espn/betting/story/_/id/47756900/super-bowl-60-sports-betting-odds-seahawks-patriots-offense-defense-value), [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/news/eagles-jalen-carter-highest-paid-defensive-tackle-nfl-history/)

## Edge 4: Elite CB INT UNDER (Low Target Share Suppresses Opportunities)

**Metric:** Interception rate per target — INTs are opportunity-driven. A corner nobody throws at cannot intercept anything.

**The Edge:** Shutdown corners are typically targeted at a *lower* rate than average CBs. If a CB allows a lower target share, they have fewer opportunities for INTs. Books often price elite CBs' INT OVERs aggressively because of reputation, when the target-volume reality makes the UNDER the value.

**Key Data (2025):**
- **Kevin Byard, CHI** — Led the NFL with **7 interceptions** on only **52 targets** (13.5% INT rate — unsustainable, but shows opportunity matters). Most elite CBs see 75-100+ targets.
- **Devon Witherspoon, SEA** — 1 INT on 59 targets, 72.4 passer rating. Targeted less than volume CBs, so even his INTs are opportunity-constrained.
- **Cooper DeJean, PHA** — 2 INTs on 81 targets, but only 1 TD allowed and 75.4 rating. High PD volume, low INT — classic "there time after time, didn't finish" profile.

**The Trap:** Per Gridiron Decoded, "A cornerback nobody throws at cannot intercept anything." Some of the best coverage players post modest INT totals *because* quarterbacks avoid them. The OVER on INTs for shutdown corners is a reputation-based trap.

**Betting Angle:** Cross-reference INT prop with projected targets. If a CB is projected for <5 targets (elite coverage, low target share), **bet the UNDER** on INTs regardless of reputation. Even at +200 odds, the opportunity set is too small.

**Source:** [Gridiron Decoded](https://gridirondecoded.com/nfl-stats/defense-stats-decoded/), [Pro Football Reference 2025](https://www.pro-football-reference.com/years/2025/defense_advanced.htm)

## Edge 5: PD (Passes Defensed) as a More Reliable Coverage Prop Than INT

**Metric:** Passes Defensed (PD) vs. Interceptions — PD counts passes a defender knocked away *without* catching them. There are far more PDs than INTs, and far less luck involved.

**The Edge:** INTs are highly volatile — a tipped ball landing in your hands counts the same as a brilliant read. PDs are a "more honest measure of coverage" (Gridiron Decoded) because the volume is higher and the luck component is lower. For prop betting, PD OVERs on slot corners and high-target CBs are more predictable than INT OVERs.

**Key Data (2025 PFR):**
- **Cameron Dantzler** — Not in top 200 table, but PD leaders show slot CBs dominate PD volume (natural edge for OVER).
- **Nahshon Wright** — 1 INT, 98 targets, 813 yards allowed. Low PD because he's targeted heavily — PD UNDER is the trap here.
- **Marlon Humphrey** — 4 INTs, 99 targets, 920 yards, 2 TDs. High volume, high INT, but PD is moderate.
- **Jalen Ramsey** — 1 INT, 7 TDs allowed, 118.1 passer rating against. PD was low relative to targets — coverage declining, but INT UNDER is dangerous (high TD rate suggests balls in play).

**The Read:** High PD + low INT = "in position but can't finish." High INT + high PD = "ballhawk + reliable." High targets + low PD/INT = coverage liability. The PD prop is steadier week-to-week.

**Source:** [Gridiron Decoded](https://gridirondecoded.com/nfl-stats/defense-stats-decoded/), [Pro Football Reference 2025](https://www.pro-football-reference.com/years/2025/defense_advanced.htm)

## Edge 6: CB Tackle Prop Base-Rate UNDER (Even High-Rate CBs Rarely Hit)

**Metric:** CB tackle rate (% of snaps with a tackle/assist) vs. base-rate frequency of high-tackle games.

**The Edge:** Books price CB tackle props based on tackle rate per snap, but ignore the *base rate* — the frequency with which outside CBs reach high tackle counts. Even the highest tackle-rate CBs only hit high lines ~12% of games because they play limited snaps in the box and face limited running plays.

**Key Data (2025):**
- **Devon Witherspoon, SEA** — 10.6% tackle rate (5th-highest among outside CBs with 300+ snaps). ESPN's model: a **6.5 tackle line** for any CB is "very high." Only **12% of games** by outside CB starters resulted in 7+ tackles. Model priced Witherspoon UNDER 6.5 at **-137**, while the market line was higher.
- **Cooper DeJean, PHI** — 13.4% missed tackle rate? No — 13 PD, low tackle count despite versatility.
- **Trent McDuffie, KC** — 63 combined, 43 total pressures... wait, that's pressure data.

**The Math:** At 10.6% tackle rate over ~65 defensive snaps, even elite CBs average ~7 tackles. But the *distribution* is skewed — most games are 3-5 tackles, with occasional 8-10 tackle outliers. The base rate says the OVER hits ~12% of the time.

**Betting Angle:** When a CB's tackle line is set above 5.5, cross-check with the team's run-defense script. If the opposing team is pass-heavy (or the CB is a boundary/slot specialist), **bet the UNDER** even if the tackle rate looks high.

**Source:** [ESPN Super Bowl Betting Analysis](https://www.espn.com/espn/betting/story/_/id/47756900/super-bowl-60-sports-betting-odds-seahawks-patriots-offense-defense-value)

## Edge 7: Team Pressure Rate Predicts Opposing QB Pressure Props

**Metric:** Team-level pressure rate (4-man rush, blitz, stunts) → correlates with opposing QB hurry/completion/sack props.

**The Edge:** When a defense generates elite pressure with a standard 4-man rush, the opposing QB's hurry and sack props should trend UNDER — pressure speeds up the QB's decision-making, leading to quick releases and *fewer* sacks. But QB hurry props can go EITHER way depending on whether "pressure" is defined as QB hurries (statistical) or pressures (PFF charted).

**Key Data (PFF, 2024 season):**
- **Broncos** — 39.3% pressure rate on 4-man rushes (1st). Four different linemen logged 50+ pressures.
- **Browns** — 38.2% (2nd) — Myles Garrett-centric.
- **Seahawks** — 36.6% (3rd).
- **Eagles** — 35.5% (4th) — kept 2 safeties deep, powered by Jalen Carter (18.8% pressure rate at DT), Milton Williams, Moro Ojomo.
- **Lions** — 35.2% (5th) — Aidan Hutchinson.

**Stunt-based pressure (PFF):**
- **Rams** — 51.4% pressure rate on stunts (1st).
- **Browns** — 48.6% (2nd, tied).
- **Vikings** — 48.6% (2nd, tied).

**Betting Angle:** When a defense ranks in the top 5 in standard pressure rate and faces a QB with a high sack rate or slow time-to-throw, **the QB sack UNDER becomes risky** (pressure forces quick throws = fewer sacks but more hurries). Instead, target **QB hurry/completion OVER** — the QB is under constant duress but releases before the sack.

**Source:** [PFF: The numbers behind QB pressure](https://www.pff.com/news/nfl-qb-pressure-teams-with-highest-pressure-rates-from-four-man-rushes-blitzes-stunts)

## Edge 8: Divisional/Physical Game Scripts Split TFL OVER vs. Sack UNDER

**Metric:** AFC North-style physical, run-heavy games → TFL environment, but mobile QBs → sack suppression.

**The Edge:** Divisional matchups in the AFC North are "physical, run-heavy knife fights" (PropsBot). This creates a systematic split:
- **TFL OVER** — backside pursuit, edge-setting, screen disruption, and tackle-for-loss opportunities multiply.
- **Sack UNDER** — QBs like Lamar Jackson, Joe Burrow, Justin Herbert escape pressure, turning elite pass-rushing performances into goose eggs on the stat sheet.

**Key Data:**
- **T.J. Watt** — PFR 2025: 10.6% tackle rate on limited snaps. PropsBot: "AFC North games are the systematic UNDER spot for his sack prop, because escapes turn into scrambles and the pressure stats don't pay your ticket." Sharp play = **TFL OVER**, because these games are "physical, run-heavy, and played in phone booths."
- **AFC North QBs (2025):** All mobile — Jackson, Burrow, Wilson, Rudolph. Their average sack rate is below the league average against elite edge rush.
- **Contrast with NFC:** NFC North QBs (Goff, Cousins, Love) are more pocket-oriented → TFL OVER still applies, but sack OVER becomes viable.

**Betting Angle:** In AFC North-style physical, run-heavy games:
- **Edge rushers:** TFL OVER + Sack UNDER (correlated but opposite).
- **Interior DTs:** Sack UNDER (reinforced by Edge 3).
- **CBs:** Tackle OVER (more run snaps in physical games increase box presence).

**Source:** [PropsBot — T.J. Watt Props](https://propsbot.ai/players/tj-watt/), [ESPN NFL Analysis](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/48894110/2026-nfl-season-100-days-nuggets-mvp-predictions-fantasy-rookies)

## Bonus: Stat Source Discrepancy — NFLGSIS vs. PFF vs. PFR

**Metric:** Same stats, different numbers depending on the source.

**The Edge:** The NFL's official stat provider (NFLGSIS) credits tackles, TFLs, and QB hits differently than PFF's charted data. This creates price discrepancies across sportsbooks:

- **NFLGSIS TFLs** historically group all TFLs together (run + pass), while some PFF metrics separate "pressure TFLs" from "run TFLs."
- **QB hits** are an NFLGSIS official stat, but **pressures and QB hurries** are PFF-only.
- **Tackles** are credited by human stat-spotters and vary by crew — PFR notes counts can differ noticeably between crews.

**Betting Angle:** When the prop specifies "tackles for loss" (NFLGSIS), the volume is driven by run defense and screen stops. When a model projects pressure-based TFLs, it may be using PFF data and overestimating. Cross-check the stat source before betting.
