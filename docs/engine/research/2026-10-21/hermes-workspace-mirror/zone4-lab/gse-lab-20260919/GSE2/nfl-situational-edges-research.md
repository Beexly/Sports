# NFL Game-Script & Situational Edges — Research Notes

Sources: NFL.com, PFF, Sharp Football Analysis, NFelo, SumerSports, StatRankings, SportsInfoSolutions (via Mike Tanier), Yahoo Sports, Football Study Hall. 2024–2025 data unless noted.

## EDGE 1 — Red Zone Efficiency & Target Share

**Hook:** 77.2% of all NFL touchdowns originate inside the red zone; 67.1% of passing TDs (69.1% in 2025) come from inside the 20. Red-zone work is a disproportionate share of scoring, so small efficiency gaps compound.

**Numbers (2024 Red-Zone Plus/Minus — TD=6, FG=3, stops turnover/sack = 0):**
| Rank | Team | Off | Def | +/- |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ravens | 327 | 201 | +126 |
| 2 | Lions | 339 | 216 | +123 |
| 3 | Eagles | 297 | 189 | +108 |
| 4 | Buccaneers | 333 | 240 | +93 |
| 5 | Packers | 309 | 216 | +93 |
| … | Cowboys | 180 | 285 | -105 |
| 32 | Panthers | 225 | 357 | -132 |

**PFF 2024 red-zone QB grades (TD-INT, rating):**
- Lamar Jackson: 50/73 for 447 yds, **36 TDs (league-most)**, 2 INT, 112.8 rating (2nd)
- Jayden Daniels: 54/90, 22 TDs, 2 TWP — Commanders rose 28th→2nd in red-zone offense (57.0→75.5)
- Baker Mayfield: 63/86, 31 TDs, 113.7 rating (NFL-high)
- Josh Jacobs / Derrick Henry: **15 red-zone TDs each** (led RBs), both forced 16 missed tackles

**TE position leverage:** TEs command ~20–30% of red-zone targets despite ~15-17% overall share.
- Mark Andrews: 32.6% of BAL red-zone targets → 13/16 for 11 TDs (only TE with double-digit RZ TDs)
- Trey McBride: 33.3% of ARI red-zone targets (2025)
- Dallas Goedert / Hunter Henry: ~23-34% RZ target share vs ~15% outside it
- Tight end red-zone target share is the market inefficiency fantasy & game-plan edges exploit

**Edge application:** A team with an elite TE room + mobile QB (BAL, WAS) is the model. The corollary — *avoid defenses that bleed red-zone TDs to TEs* — is exploitable: Carolina (allowed 75 drives, 50 TDs, 6 shutouts) and Dallas (13 failed RZ drives) leaked here.

## EDGE 2 — 3rd Down Package & Distance Archetypes

**Hook:** Aggregate 3rd-down % hides stratification. Short/medium/long require different personnel; teams win or lose on which package they trot out.

**League baseline (2025, via StatMuse/TruMedia):**
- Avg 3rd-down conversion: ~39%. Top of pack sits at ~50%.
- 49ers led 2025 at **49.8% (106/213)**; Packers 48.8%; Chargers 45.8%.
- Bottom: Patriots-style offenses cluster ~40–41%.

**By distance stratum (PFF 2024), conversion %:**
| Distance | Top performer | Rate | Tendency |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3rd-and-short (0-3 yds) | Ravens | **79.7%** | 77.6% run — Henry/Jackson power game |
|  | Cardinals | 70.7% | 61.1% run, varied concepts |
|  | Cowboys | 69.5% | 57.4% run — 44% inside zone |
| 3rd-and-medium (4-6 yds) | Buccaneers | **62.5%** | Mayfield 70% comp, 10.4 ypa, spread targets |
|  | Bills | 60.0% | Allen 75.9% comp; Shakir 90.3% grade; 84% use shifts/motion |
| 3rd-and-long (7-10+ yds) | Buccaneers | **36.4%** | Best in the league; rest cluster 28–33% |

**Distance decay (Princeton Sports Analytics, Football Study Hall):** Conversion drops steeply past 3rd-and-6 — ~44% at 3-and-6 sliding to ~36% at 3-and-7+. The inflection is real: long-yardage 3rd downs are a pass-heavy, coverage-dependent coin flip.

**Edge application:** Identify teams whose *short-yardage personnel* differs from their *medium-yardage personnel*. Run-heavy 3rd-and-short (BAL/CAR/DAL) is reliable but telegraphed; counter it by stacking box + aggressive A-gap (forces Jackson/Henry laterals or passes behind LOS). Medium-yardage is where route-gen and QB calm separate (TB/BUF model: motion + checkdown authority). Long-yardage is a *defensive* edge — pressure rates spike and coverage busts multiply; betting on *under* for teams that reach 3rd-and-long often and lack intermediate accuracy.

## EDGE 3 — Tempo / No-Huddle Volume & Execution

**Hook:** Up-tempo is not uniformly applied. The Commanders weaponized it as a *base* philosophy in 2024 (not just 2-min drill), while most teams use it <15% and only to kill clock or avoid substitutions.

**2025 tempo ranks (Sharp Football Analysis — seconds per play / off plays per game):**
| Fastest tempo (sec/play) | | Slowest |
|---|---|---|
| Cardinals 28.3 / 63.2 | | Ravens 30.6 / 57.3 |
| Chiefs 29.0 / 62.4 | | Bills 31.2 / 63.6 |
| Bengals 29.1 / 62.2 | | Dolphins 31.1 / 55.8 |
| Chargers 30.5 / 64.4 | | Vikings 30.3 / 56.1 |

**No-huddle dropbacks 2024 (Sports Info Solutions via Mike Tanier) — top since 2015 DB:**
1. **2024 Commanders — 429 dropbacks**, 95.9 rating (most in DB; ~2x the #2 team)
2. 2015 Eagles — 493 (Chip Kelly)
3. 2016 Giants — 411
4. 2024 Eagles — 153 dropbacks (2nd *in 2024 alone*)
- Most teams: 60-75 no-huddle dropbacks *per season*.

**No-huddle rush attempts 2015-24 leaders:**
- **2024 Commanders — 305 rushes at 5.3 ypc**
- 2024 Eagles — 159 rushes at 5.7 ypc
- Commanders also led 2024 (via Tanier): Jayden Daniels 433 scramble yds (8.5 ypc) exploiting chaos

**2025 no-huddle rate (StatRankings):** Commanders 61.6%, Saints 22.7%, Giants 21.4%, Eagles 19.3%, Broncos 12.9%. League avg 2025 = **9.91%** (down from 12.06% in 2024 — cyclical cooldown).

**Execution caveat:** Up-tempo raises both ceiling *and* floor — it produces more 3-and-outs if fundamentals break down (Kelly's decline). The Commanders succeeded because they simplified to ~25 concepts and had a QB comfortable processing pre-snap. The Eagles' 2024 no-huddle rush (5.7 ypc) was a *change-up*, not a base.

**Edge application:** Tempo as a *base* (Commanders) compresses halves — defenses rotate tired, vanilla looks, and miss substitutions. Tempo as a *tactic* (#2-3 per game) only shifts late-clock dynamics. Bet/edge *under* pace for teams with low no-huddle rate *and* thin benches (fatigue late); favor *over* on total plays when a true tempo-base offense (WAS) faces a defense that subs slowly. Watch 3-and-out rate — if a fast-pace team also ranks bottom-half on success rate, the tempo is a mirage.

## EDGE 4 — Close-Game / Late-4Q Behavior & Win Probability Asymmetry

**Hook:** "Clutch" is mostly luck, but the *decision-making under leverage* — 4th down, 2-pt, clock management — has persistent edges that separate coaches/teams.

**Close-game frequency & volatility:**
- 23% of NFL games decided by ≤3 points (league-wide baseline).
- Correlation between a team's win% in close games vs. games decided by ≥4 points is **near zero** — i.e., "clutch" wins regress to mean. Good teams avoid close games; once in one, it's a coin flip amplified by variance (penalties, officiating, one-possession bounces).

**Win-probability model calibration (per-team models):**
- At a modeled 70% WP, teams actually win 72%; at 80%, they win 82%.
- i.e., models are *slightly conservative* — when a team is flagged as a "sure thing," it's *even more* likely to close. Edges compound in the 60–80% WP band, not the extremes.

**Late-game comeback inflection:**
- Teams trailing by ≥15 entering the 4th quarter lost **135 consecutive games** (through 2024). 2025: *first 4 instances* of a 15+ point deficit being overcome (per r/nfl highlight thread) — a regime-breaker, signaling offenses can build leads faster and defenses fatigue differently now.

**4th-down / 2-pt decision asymmetry (ESPN Next Gen Guide, TruMedia):**
- 2025: coaches went for it on **~34% of 4th downs** (up sharply from historical ~~22%). Teams above 55% win-rate attempt 25% *more* 4th-down conversions than sub-.450 teams (who punt 30% more).
- 2-pt conversions: only +1.3% WP lift *on average* — but for teams strong in short-yardage (Eagles, Ravens, Lions), the edge is larger and consistent. The Eagles lead the league in 2-pt attempts (aggressive short-yardage identity).
- The Lions (Dan Campbell) are the most aggressive — a coaching-style edge that creates game-script volatility (higher-floor, higher-ceiling outcomes).

**Edge application:** When a team is 60-80% modeled WP but *hasn't yet closed* and shows conservative clock management (e.g., 3 timeouts, 4:30 left, leading by 1 possession), they're *slightly* more likely to let it slip than models imply. Conversely, when a sub-.500 offensive line faces a 4th-and-short near the goal line late, assume conservative (FG) unless the coaching staff historically goes for it >40% — the market often overprices the FG.

## EDGE 5 — Personnel Game-Scripts & PROE Stratification

**Hook:** 11 personnel (1RB/1TE/3WR) is now the *default* (60-80% of neutral downs league-wide; 90%+ of snaps are 11/12/21 combined). The edges now live in *departures* from the norm — when teams go heavier, lighter, or shift their pass-rate *over expected* (PROE).

**PROE (Pass Rate Over Expected) — 2025 extremes:**
- *Pass-happy (above expected):* Cardinals **+4.0%**, Chiefs **+4.0%**, Chargers **+0.6%**, Jaguars **+1.6%**
- *Run-heavy (below expected):* Ravens **-8.8%**, Jets **-8.8%**, Falcons **-6.2%**, Seahawks **-6.8%**, Commanders **-4.3%**

**Personnel tell mapping:**
- 11 personnel = modern default; deviation signals *game script intent*:
  - 12 personnel (2TE) usage ↑ = run-heavy/play-action identity (49ers, Titans, Broncos).
  - 21 personnel (2RB) ↑ = power-run/PA ground game (49ers, Dolphins).
- No-huddle rate (from Edge 3): Commanders 61.6% vs. rest of league <25%. When a team *doesn't* match this with personnel, the tempo is a tempo-only disguise.

**Formation & aDOT signal shifts:**
- Teams with aDOT ≥ 9.0 (deep-shot profile): Patriots 9.57, Rams 9.51, Packers 9.31, Eagles 9.1 — *when these teams fall behind script*, aDOT collapses (<7) but conversion rates hold; when they *lead*, they dial up deep → exploitable for over/under on longest completion.
- "Vs Sticks" (yards vs. first-down marker): positive values = aggressive downfield; Commanders -1.0, Saints +1.2 (only team throwing *past* sticks consistently).

**Close-game behavior shift:**
- Good teams (55%+ win %) cut their run rate by less than 10% in close games (stay true to identity); *bad* teams swing heavily pass-heavy when behind (>60% pass), which spikes turnover rate.
- Personnel: in the 4th quarter of 1-score games, 12/21 personnel usage drops 15–20% as teams empty personnel to pass; run-dependent offenses lose efficiency precisely here (Ravens/Bills hybrid exceptions).

**Edge application:** The exploitable signal is *personnel-game-script mismatch*. A run-first team (12 personnel heavy, low PROE) facing a prevent/2-deep shell late because they're behind will force low-percentage throws. DraftKings/QB props spike on these QBs in Q4 of close games. Conversely, teams with *both* high no-huddle rate AND high 11-personnel usage (pure tempo-base) are structurally built to *control* clock and script — they're the "fastest first downs" engines (target ~25 first downs; Denver hit 27.2 in 2014).

## Quick Reference — Where the Edges Compound
- **Red zone + 3rd down:** TE target-share arbitrage (TEs 20–33% RZ vs. ~15% overall) is the single biggest cross-situational market inefficiency.
- **Tempo + game script:** True tempo-base teams (WAS) create *volume + first-down* edges; tactical tempo users (PHI/Pittsburgh subsets) only shift late-clock dynamics.
- **Close games + personnel:** Late-game no-huddle + 11-personnel + high aDOT teams *extend* drives; run-first + low-PROE teams *fragment* when behind → clock-management / over-under edges.
