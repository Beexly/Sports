## Challenges (weak claims, method problems, INFERENCE-marked speculation)

1. **FRANS gate size vs NFL sample (d19).** The ledger's own adversarial read (leakage-contaminated windows; several 8/10 wins within noise) is honest, but the proposed adopt gate — ≥0.004 log-loss on 2024–2025 walk-forward — is weak for NFL scale: ~544 games over two seasons means a 0.004 log-loss point improvement is likely inside the bootstrap noise band. INFERENCE: the gate should require a clustered bootstrap CI excluding zero, not a point threshold; the paper's small-margin wins (<1% relative sMAPE) do not survive translation to a 32-team, 17-game-season discriminative task where each "class" has tiny window counts (the ledger itself notes this, `2187-…md:51`).
2. **ECDD on the win-probability error stream assumes i.i.d. Bernoulli drift (d18).** NFL game outcomes are clustered (upset streaks, QB-injury regimes, schedule pockets) — the "pre-change rate" p̂_{0,t} will be polluted by schedule-strength correlation, and the detector can alarm on correlated-but-non-structural losses. INFERENCE: the error stream should be stratified or schedule-adjusted (e.g., residuals from a baseline, not raw win/loss) before ECDD sees it; otherwise the 272-game ARL_0 knob is miscalibrated because the effective sample is ~4,101 games → 2,291 independent-play equivalents (map #14) — the same clustering problem one level up.
3. **CRAFTER residual correction on calibrated probabilities risks noise-fitting (d18).** The paper's domains (electricity, retail) have rich exogenous covariates and unsaturated cells; mature NFL spread markets are the saturated case the paper says "collapses onto RAW." The ledger's headroom-gating experiment (`1851-…md:75`) is the right guard, but the proposed acceptance gate (≥0.003 held-out 2025 log-loss) needs cluster-corrected standard errors — the NONE floor protects deployment but not the false-discovery rate of the search loop itself. INFERENCE: run the headroom proxy (COV corrector lift) on game-clustered CV before spending LLM queries.
4. **1868→NFL is a bigger transfer than the brief's gate admits (d18).** Limitations the ledger states: single-player motions only (no ball, teammates, opponents, game context); scale claims rest on proprietary ProSoccer. The brief's acceptance gate (≥10% motion-error reduction at 1.0s, ≥5pp route-classification gain) is fine, but the real NFL use is the multi-agent setting — which is DIMA's (2205) territory, not 1868's. INFERENCE: sequence the two — 1868's per-frame player-node reps become DIMA's state encoder; don't evaluate 1868's transfer in isolation from the 22-player transition model.
5. **2205's NFL leap is honest but large (d19).** Authors: untested beyond ~6 agents / 52-DoF hands; NFL needs 22+ball with permutation-invariant dynamics over role-structured (not homogeneous) entities. The ledger's improvement experiment (football-meaningful reveal ordering: ball carrier → blockers → defenders) is the key adaptation — random permutation sampling over 23 entities will waste capacity on irrelevant orderings. INFERENCE: the adopt gates (position error ≤0.8 yd at +0.5s, EPA reward head ±0.75 on ≥70% of held-out 2024 plays) should be evaluated against a structured-order baseline, not just the paper's random-order protocol.
6. **GNG + coresets: toy-scale evidence for a production objective (d18).** Permuted/split MNIST, mean-field BNNs, accuracy-only metrics; "no calibration/uncertainty metrics"; GBMs have no posterior so a bootstrap-distribution surrogate is required (`1894-…md:31` context). The EWC+Adam warning is valuable and verified, but the certainty-weighted weekly update (Δ_i ∝ σ_i²·gradient_i) inherits the caveat the ledger flags: NFL weeks are more "split" than "permuted" — GNG helps most where tasks conflict, which is exactly where NFL weeks do NOT conflict.
