## 2. Syntheses — DFS/props features that compose with QB-behavioral profiles

**The core thesis:** the DFS research in this partition already *is* a QB-behavioral feature spec — it was built for slate plays, but its features (first-read/TPRR/air-yard-share, pressure response, scramble/aggressiveness profiles) are engine-grade once they're computed from play-by-play (FTN charting, claim 8) instead of transcribed from X charts.

### S2.1 Trust-target trinity → QB trust-target profile
- **Features:** first-read share (Jefferson 52.9%, Puka 50.0%, Purdy 27.0% distributor vs Love 78.6% one-read), TPRR (JSN 42.3%/44% league-leading, Kittle 0.32, Addison 8% flag), air-yard share (Jefferson 46.5%, Kittle 24.3%) — sources: StatRankings CSV dump 2026-09-18, FantasyPtsData.
- **Join:** per-QB → per-receiver distribution `P(target=t | QB, situation)` stored as a versioned profile table. Conditional variant: recompute under each pass-catcher's absence — the Pitts-with/without-London split (claim 1) is the template; generalize to `trust_target_share(qb, receiver | absent_set)`.
- **Composition with TD decomposition (claim 10):** the trust-target distribution is exactly the `P(targeted=t)` factor; the `P(TD|targeted=t)` factor comes from coverage/separation features (HB cover grades, PFF man-coverage ratings with the McDuffie dispute caveat). This turns anytime-TD/receiving props into a marginalization problem over the QB profile — the cleanest compositional link in the partition.

### S2.2 Pressure-response features → the L3 pressure chain
- **Features:** pressure EPA/db (Lawrence +0.81, Purdy league-avg 0.00 when blitzed per SumerSports), sack-vs-blitz (Stroud 7.1%, Rush 80% pressure-to-sack), scramble % (Caleb 34.2%, Maye 27.5%, Daniels 20.6%), pressure-to-sack trait with the <2.5s quick-pressure split, time-to-throw.
- **Join:** HB-style OL pressure-rate grade (claim 3) sets *expected pressure*; the QB's pressure-to-sack/scramble traits convert pressure to outcomes. FTN `is_qb_fault_sack`, `n_blitzers`, `n_pass_rushers`, `qb_location`, `is_qb_out_of_pocket` (claim 8) are the ground-truth columns to compute both sides from play-by-play — replacing the X-chart transcriptions.
- **Veto boundary (claim 6):** pressure→sack R²<0.005 vetoes *individual sack props*; it does not veto the QB trait at season scale (Challenge 1).

### S2.3 Per-QB efficiency → team engine (highest-measured gain)
- Per-QB rolling EPA/dropback keyed by `passer_player_id` (claim 4) is the bridge from QB-behavioral profiles to game prediction: it *follows trades/midseason takeovers*, which team-level efficiency cannot. This is the single highest-confidence build in the partition (see §4, item 1).

### S2.4 Situational/matchup layer
- **Features:** defensive tendencies (base/press/blitz %, men in box — league avg 31.4/35.2/31.3/6.95), motion-at-snap pass EPA allowed (PIT −0.85 … CLE +0.66), DC-tendency deltas (PIT 96.2% zone despite aggressive brand; GB blitz spike 48.4% W1 vs 26.3% in 2025; MIN blitz solved in-game 83%→63%), zone/gap scheme mismatches (Bijan 57% zone vs CAR 124.9), coverage-shell splits (Concepcion +50% TPRR vs single-high — flagged as a betting-recommendation post, treat as hypothesis not fact).
- **Join:** team-level priors feeding matchup adjustment; man/zone EPA splits per QB remain queued (NGS/charting gap — internal-only doctrine applies to NGS, but FTN charting has coverage fields).

### S2.5 Red-zone tendency priors
- Goal-line rush-share monopolies (Taylor 100% inside-10/inside-5, Henry 85.7% inside-5, Javonte 100% inside-10), RZ receiving leaders, RZ share (Likely 57.1%, Andrews 25%). Join as red-zone tendency priors for the TD-decomposition head and game-total pricing.

### S2.6 Calibration governance on the QB program
- No per-QB uncertainty bands from the fantasy signal (claim 9, r=+0.06) — profile point estimates are fine; bands = positional prior. Any per-QB interval product passes the band claim-gate. Purdy-style early-season percentile rows are display-bait, not engine inputs (two-week sample).

## 3. Challenges — findings that don't hold up, biases, unbuildable specs

### C3.1 The R²<0.005 veto vs the pressure-to-sack trait (apparent contradiction — resolved)
The metric-bible veto (claim 6) is at the **team/season level**: pressure→sack conversion doesn't explain sack *counts* beyond pressure volume. The QB trait (claims 2, 11, map finding 1) moves year-over-year *for individual QBs* against an ~18% league baseline, and the <2.5s split separates coverage sacks from rush wins. **Both can hold.** The engine consequence: pressure-to-sack is a legitimate per-QB behavioral feature (INFERENCE: with ≥100 pressures of sample), but it must never be used to price an individual sack prop — the veto stands there.

### C3.2 Tiny samples everywhere in the X sweeps
- Purdy 99th-percentile CPOE/EPA on n=2 weeks; pressure-to-sack leaderboards on denominators of 13–24 (claim 11); QB feature rows from a single Week-1 game (qb-phase2); "generational prospects" cohort 77 players with n=1–2 games of 2026 data mixed in. Percentiles and leaderboards on these n's are marketing, not measurements. (INFERENCE: any engine feature derived from the sweeps needs a sample-size floor + shrinkage — the HB 150-rep / 80-attempt-shrinkage conventions are the template.)

### C3.3 X-sweep selection and survivorship bias
- Charts are posted *because they're extreme*: "Worst aDOT", "highest pressure rate allowed", tails-only leaderboards. The sweeps therefore over-sample tail values — a feature table built from transcriptions inherits this bias.
- Data-quality flags in-file: 31 of 32 rows (one cropped, not reconstructed); Keenum/HOU logo misread; McDuffie 0.0 man-coverage passer-rating claim publicly disputed in replies ("Fake News"); post title "Through Week 2" vs chart "Week 3"; Concepcion single-high split is a betting recommendation, not research.
- OneWeekSeason salary extract is heavily corrupted (garbled CJK cells, Derek Carr as NO starter, Mike Evans on SF — claim-adjacent caveat). Usable only as a historical ownership *calibration point* after filtering, never as player/team intel.

### C3.4 The trust-target magnitudes are case studies, not parameters
The Pitts 0.18→0.28 split is a single WR-absence case from one account's implied data (claim 1). It *templates the computation* (conditional trust-target shares) but the number itself should not be hardcoded as a prior for all absences — (INFERENCE) concentration shifts are receiver-role-specific (WR1-out ≠ TE-out), and need multi-case estimation before they become priors.

### C3.5 The x-intake-registry as a named spec does not exist in this slice
**Finding:** no file named `x-intake-registry` (or variant) exists in the local `~/workspace/vendor/Sports` checkout or in any c01 brief (verified by repo-wide filename and content search; grep of all briefs for "x-intake" returns zero hits). The map's gap #4 ("spec'd but no automation") references something outside this slice or not yet written — **do not treat it as an existing artifact**.
- **What does exist:** the X dossiers (25-account follow list with methods/lanes — `data-sources/research/2026-09-17/dossiers/nfl-analytics-x-dossier.md`), competitive-intel intake memo (`reasoning/competitive-intake-2026-09-26.md`), manual X sweeps with harvest methodology (21 primary + 6 secondary accounts, keyword searches, read-only browser tasks), and the signal-staleness gate's queued personnel/news source.
- **What breaks if the registry is spec'd as an automated feature feed:**
  1. **Source fragility** — sweeps run on read-only browser tasks with no API; @FTNData is protected, two timelines never render; X scraping at intake cadence is ToS-risky and CAPTCHA/rate-limit exposed. An automated feed will rot.
  2. **Fidelity** — the intake material is text-only transcriptions and visual approximations (flagged throughout). A registry that doesn't preserve approximation-flags launders low-precision inputs into engine features.
  3. **Trust scoring is subjective** — the dossier's engagement posture ("reply frequency not established" for most accounts); ClevTA's 54% ATS is self-published, not audited. A numeric trust score per account would be arbitrary without a track-record harness.
  4. **The missing-news problem** (square-3 rule, claim 4 brief): a registry records what's posted; absence of a post ≠ absence of news. Big model-market disagreements must still be flagged `missingNewsSuspected`, not auto-ingested as edges.
- **Implementable version:** a *ledger*, not a feed — account registry (handle, methods, lanes, credentialing tier), harvest log (post URL, timestamp, metric, value, approximation flag, transcriber), trust tiers (audited record / credentialed analyst / self-published / betting-recommendation), and analyst-in-the-loop promotion to the feature catalog. The registry feeds the *catalog*, never features directly.

### C3.6 Competitive-intel ingestion discipline (verified working)
`reasoning/competitive-intel-intake-2026-09-26.md` demonstrates the correct posture and it's already practiced: opponent-adjusted EPA residual method adopted (80/40-attempt shrinkage, 55/15/15/10/5 blend), duplicate CPOE/EPA/OL/scheme pastes *rejected*, non-commercial data (Big Data Bowl, SiriusXM audio, NGS summaries >0.15 weight) kept research-only. The HB-grade rebuild (§4 item 4) must follow this rule.

### C3.7 Partition noise
`data-sources/research/2026-09-28/neon-max-leverage-round2/round2-laneC.md` (Neon infra pricing), `research/2026-09-28/orchestration/coding-agent-briefs.md`, `docs/props/research/2026-09-18/notes/sfdata9ers.md` (partially — mock-draft matrices), and the Vercel/R2 infra briefs matched my directory pattern but are infra/ops, not DFS/props/trust content. The invisible-insiderstats dossier (2026-10-01) is competitive *packaging* intel — its only engine-relevant kernel is the situational-trends-database concept (buildable internally from play-by-play).
