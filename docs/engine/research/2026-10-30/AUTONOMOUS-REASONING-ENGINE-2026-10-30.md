Multi-Marginal Kantorovich Relaxation, the Birkhoff Polytope, and the Friesecke Gap
Given N Polish probability spaces (X_k, \mu_k)_{k=1}^N and a lower semi-continuous cost functional c: X_1 \times \dots \times X_N \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}, the multi-marginal Kantorovich problem seeks a joint coupling \gamma \in \Pi(\mu_1, \dots, \mu_N) that minimizes expected interaction cost:
\mathcal{K}(\mu_1, \dots, \mu_N) = \min_{\gamma \in \Pi(\mu_1, \dots, \mu_N)} \int_{X_1 \times \dots \times X_N} c(x_1, \dots, x_N) \, d\gamma(x_1, \dots, x_N) \quad \text{subject to} \quad (\pi_k)_\# \gamma = \mu_k \;\; \forall k \in \{1, \dots, N\}
In contrast, the deterministic Monge ansatz restricts the minimization to couplings concentrated on the graph of measurable transport maps T_k: X_1 \to X_k pushing \mu_1 forward to \mu_k (T_{k\#}\mu_1 = \mu_k):
\mathcal{M}(\mu_1, \dots, \mu_N) = \inf_{T_{k\#}\mu_1 = \mu_k} \int_{X_1} c\big(x_1, T_2(x_1), \dots, T_N(x_1)\big) \, d\mu_1(x_1)
Because every tuple of Monge maps induces an admissible Kantorovich plan \gamma_T = (\text{id}, T_2, \dots, T_N)_\# \mu_1, the relaxation gap \Delta_{\text{MMOT}} = \mathcal{M} - \mathcal{K} is non-negative (\Delta_{\text{MMOT}} \ge 0). In the classical two-marginal setting (N = 2) with uniform discrete marginals \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \frac{1}{\ell} \sum_{i=1}^\ell \delta_{a_i}, the Kantorovich polytope \Pi(\mu_1, \mu_2) is isomorphic (up to scaling by 1/\ell) to the Birkhoff polytope \mathcal{B}_\ell of \ell \times \ell doubly stochastic matrices. By the Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem, every extreme point of \mathcal{B}_\ell is a permutation matrix, which corresponds bijectively to a deterministic Monge map. Consequently, linear programming over \Pi(\mu_1, \mu_2) always attains its minimum at a permutation vertex, forcing the two-marginal relaxation gap to collapse identically to zero (\Delta_{\text{MMOT}}^{(N=2)} = 0).
When the number of marginals increases to N \ge 3, the Birkhoff–von Neumann property fails catastrophically. As proved by Friesecke (2019), the convex polytope \mathcal{P}_{\text{sym}}(X^N) of symmetric Kantorovich plans for N = 3 particles on \ell = 3 discrete sites possesses exactly 22 extreme points, of which only 7 are of Monge form, while the remaining 15 extreme points exhibit irreducible fractional mass-splitting across multiple graphs. When projected via the two-body marginal operator M_2: \mathcal{P}_{\text{sym}}(X^3) \to \mathcal{P}_{3\text{-rep}}(X^2) for pairwise interaction costs, the reduced polytope exposes 8 of these 22 extreme points, 3 of which remain non-Monge. Even on a binary state space X = \{0, 1\} with uniform marginals \mu_k = (0.5, 0.5) for N = 3 under the parity-repulsive / Frenkel–Kontorova interaction cost c(x_1, x_2, x_3) = (x_1 \oplus x_2 \oplus x_3), the Kantorovich relaxation attains \mathcal{K} = 0.0 via the non-Monge fractional vertex \gamma_{000} = \gamma_{011} = \gamma_{101} = \gamma_{110} = 0.25, whereas every deterministic pair of permutation maps (T_2, T_3) \in S_2 \times S_2 incurs a cost of at least \mathcal{M} = 0.5, yielding the exact discrete certificate \Delta_{\text{MMOT}} = 0.5.
By the Ambrosio–Lisini–Friesecke superposition principle, this discrete relaxation gap lifts directly to continuous state spaces. Embedding piecewise-constant or smooth block densities over X \subset \mathbb{R}^d onto the discrete sites causes the failure of the Monge ansatz to manifest as nonattainment of the Monge infimum and the spontaneous formation of fine-scale spatial "microstructure"—generalized Young-measure flows where fluid elements or decision agents split probability mass across bifurcating trajectories. This phenomenon is further cemented by OpenAI Math Corpus Result #373 (Nonattainment of the three-marginal Coulomb Monge problem), which proves that for N = 3 repelling particles in \mathbb{R}^3 with a smooth, compactly supported density \rho whose square root \sqrt{\rho} \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^3) is also smooth, the Monge and Kantorovich infima coincide (\inf \mathcal{M} = \min \mathcal{K}), yet no pair of Borel-measurable, measure-preserving maps (T_2, T_3) attains the minimum for the Coulomb cost \sum_{1 \le i < j \le 3} |x_i - x_j|^{-1} or any inverse-power Riesz interaction \sum_{i < j} |x_i - x_j|^{-s} (s > 0, d \ge 2). Sharp One-Third Stability of Brenier Maps (OpenAI Corpus Result #374)
In continuous two-marginal quadratic optimal transport (c(x, y) = \frac{1}{2}\|x - y\|^2), Brenier's theorem guarantees that for an absolutely continuous source measure \rho \in \mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d) and any target measure \mu \in \mathcal{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^d) with bounded second moments, there exists a unique optimal transport map T_\mu = \nabla \phi_\mu given by the gradient of a convex Brenier potential \phi_\mu pushing \rho forward to \mu (T_{\mu\#}\rho = \mu). For decision systems operating under empirical distribution shift, the fundamental question is quantitative stability: how much does the optimal allocation map T_\mu fluctuate in L^2(\rho) when the target distribution \mu is perturbed to \nu in the 2-Wasserstein metric W_2(\mu, \nu)?
While one-dimensional transport (d = 1) is an exact isometry (\|T_\mu - T_\nu\|_{L^2(\rho)} = W_2(\mu, \nu), exponent \beta = 1), higher dimensions exhibit geometric sensitivity near singularities of the Brenier potential. OpenAI Math Corpus Result #374 (Sharp one-third stability of Brenier maps, formalized in Brenier.lean) establishes the universal, dimension-independent sharp bound for any uniform source measure \rho on a compact convex body \Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d (d \ge 2) with nonempty interior and any target measures \mu, \nu supported in a fixed compact set K \subset \mathbb{R}^d:
\|T_\mu - T_\nu\|_{L^2(\rho)} = \|\nabla \phi_\mu - \nabla \phi_\nu\|_{L^2(\rho)} \le C(\Omega, K) \, W_2(\mu, \nu)^{1/3}

Entropic Regularization, Sinkhorn Dual Ascent, and Madelung Geometric Hydrodynamics
To compute optimal couplings at scale and connect static linear programming to continuous-time physical dynamics, the engine regularizes the Kantorovich objective with the relative entropy (Kullback–Leibler divergence) of the coupling \gamma with respect to the reference measure \mu_0 \otimes \mu_1 at temperature \varepsilon > 0:
\text{OT}_\varepsilon(\mu, \nu) = \min_{\gamma \in \Pi(\mu, \nu)} \sum_{i,j} c_{ij} \gamma_{ij} + \varepsilon \sum_{i,j} \gamma_{ij} \left( \log \frac{\gamma_{ij}}{\mu_i \nu_j} - 1 \right)
By Fenchel–Rockafellar duality, the unconstrained dual maximization problem over Kantorovich potentials (u, v) \in \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^m reads:
\max_{u, v} \; \mathcal{D}_\varepsilon(u, v) = \sum_{i=1}^n u_i \mu_i + \sum_{j=1}^m v_j \nu_j - \varepsilon \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^m \exp\left( \frac{u_i + v_j - c_{ij}}{\varepsilon} \right)
Standard matrix-scaling Sinkhorn iterations (a^{(k+1)} = \mu \oslash (K b^{(k)}), b^{(k+1)} = \nu \oslash (K^\top a^{(k+1)}) with Gibbs kernel K_{ij} = e^{-c_{ij}/\varepsilon}) suffer from numerical underflow as \varepsilon \to 0. The live engine executes exact coordinate ascent in the log-stabilized dual domain:
u_i^{(k+1)} = \varepsilon \log \mu_i - \varepsilon \, \text{LSE}_{j=1}^m \left( \frac{v_j^{(k)} - c_{ij}}{\varepsilon} \right), \qquad v_j^{(k+1)} = \varepsilon \log \nu_j - \varepsilon \, \text{LSE}_{i=1}^n \left( \frac{u_i^{(k+1)} - c_{ij}}{\varepsilon} \right)
where \text{LSE}_j(z_j) = z_{\max} + \log \sum_j e^{z_j - z_{\max}}. Each alternating half-step monotonically increases the concave dual objective \mathcal{D}_\varepsilon(u, v) and decreases the Kullback–Leibler projection residual V_{\text{Lyap}}^{(k)} = \| \gamma^{(k)} \mathbf{1} - \mu \|_1 + \| (\gamma^{(k)})^\top \mathbf{1} - \nu \|_1, providing a strict runtime Lyapunov certificate (V_{\text{Lyap}}^{(k+1)} \le V_{\text{Lyap}}^{(k)}).
Beyond discrete algebra, Modin (2024) demonstrated that the Sinkhorn algorithm on a smooth Riemannian manifold M is an exact space-and-time discretization of a continuous nonlinear integral flow governed by geometric hydrodynamics and the Schrödinger bridge problem. Specifically, for smooth strictly positive densities \rho_0, \rho_1 \in \text{Dens}(M) and heat semigroup e^{\varepsilon \Delta} f(x) = \int_M K_\varepsilon(x, y) f(y) \, dy, the potentials (f, g) evolve in continuous algorithmic time s \in [0, \infty) according to:
\frac{\partial f}{\partial s} = -f - \log\left( e^{\varepsilon \Delta} e^g \right) + \log \rho_0, \qquad \frac{\partial g}{\partial s} = -g - \log\left( e^{\varepsilon \Delta} e^f \right) + \log \rho_1
In multiplicative coordinates a = e^f, b = e^g, this system forms the autonomous ODE \partial_s a = -a \log\left(\frac{a \, e^{\varepsilon \Delta} b}{\rho_0}\right) and \partial_s b = -b \log\left(\frac{b \, e^{\varepsilon \Delta} a}{\rho_1}\right). Applying a Trotter splitting discretization with step size h > 0 yields the generalized over-relaxed Sinkhorn flow:
a^{(k+1)} = \left(a^{(k)}\right)^{1-h} \left( \frac{\rho_0}{K_\varepsilon b^{(k)}} \right)^h, \qquad b^{(k+1)} = \left(b^{(k)}\right)^{1-h} \left( \frac{\rho_1}{K_\varepsilon^\top a^{(k+1)}} \right)^h
Standard Sinkhorn corresponds to h = 1, while linear stability holds for h \in (0, 2) with optimal spectral eigenvalue coalescence occurring at h \approx 1.75, where asymptotic convergence accelerates from geometric to near-linear decay. Crucially, the bridge between this entropic flow and classical Benamou–Brenier fluid dynamics is given by the imaginary Madelung transform:
\Psi_{\text{Madelung}}: T\text{Dens}(M) \cong \text{Dens}(M) \times \Omega^0(M) \ni (\rho, S) \longmapsto \left( a, \bar{b} \right) = \left( \sqrt{\rho} \, e^{S/\varepsilon}, \; \sqrt{\rho} \, e^{-S/\varepsilon} \right)
with inverse \rho = a \bar{b} (fluid density) and S = \frac{\varepsilon}{2} \log(a / \bar{b}) (hydrodynamic velocity potential satisfying \int_M a \bar{b} \, dx = 1). While the classical quantum Madelung transform uses imaginary \varepsilon \in i\mathbb{R} (yielding oscillatory Schrödinger wave mechanics), real \varepsilon > 0 transforms the Hamiltonian H(\rho, S) = \frac{1}{2}\int \langle \nabla S, \nabla S \rangle \rho - \frac{\varepsilon^2}{8}\int I(\rho) into decoupled hyperbolic modes \dot{x} = x/2, \dot{y} = -y/2 via the Hopf–Cole variables, driving deterministic mass-conserving bridge trajectories between \rho_0 and \rho_1.
