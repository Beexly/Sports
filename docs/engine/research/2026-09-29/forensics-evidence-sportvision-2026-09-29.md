# PATENT FORENSICS — Sportvision/FOX Telestration Family (expired)

Research date: 2026-09-29. All facts verified on Google Patents and contemporaneous press.
Bottom line up front: **all six patents expired by normal 20-year term completion (2017–2019), not by fee lapse or abandonment.** The yellow-line technology was a commercial success, was acquired (not killed) by SMT in 2016, and is still in active use on NFL broadcasts. The patenting line stopped because the 20-year clock ran out *and* the company behind the patents was absorbed — not because the tech failed.

---

## 1. US7075556B1 — "Telestrator system"
- **Link:** https://patents.google.com/patent/US7075556B1/en
- **Assignee:** Sportvision Inc → assigned to Sportsmedia Technology Corp (2017-03-17, post-acquisition)
- **Filed:** 1999-10-21 · **Granted:** 2006-07-11 · **Status:** Expired – Lifetime (2019-10-21, normal 20-year term)
- **Inventors:** Kevin R. Meier, Walter Hsiao, James R. Gloudemans, Marvin S. White, Richard H. Cavallaro, Stanley K. Honey
- **What the claims actually cover (plain English):** A broadcaster draws on live video during a game; the system smooths the drawing, converts it to 3D field coordinates so it appears *painted on the field* even when the camera moves or switches (claims 1–5, 12–15); blends it without covering players (claim 3); and does the compositing with a color-map keyer plus a flicker filter (claims 9–11, 24–32). Dependent claims add tessellating the drawing when zoomed past a threshold (claim 2) and a specific Bezier-spline stroke-smoothing algorithm (claim 23).
- **What the claims do NOT cover:** Pre-drawn graphics that are *not* manually created by a human operator during the live event (claim 1 is human-drawing-triggered); graphics drawn in pure 2D screen space with no 3D↔2D conversion; compositing via non-color-map methods (e.g., depth buffers, segmentation masks, neural segmentation); automatic drawing *generation* from tracking data without a human touch-screen input.
- **Admitted limitations:** Background section concedes prior-art telestrators' arrows floated on screen instead of sticking to the field when the camera panned; hand-drawn marks were sloppy and obscured players. The invention depends on **camera sensors** (optical shaft encoders, inclinometers, fiber-optic gyros, lens/zoom/2x-extender telemetry) and requires **camera attitude calibration** — with pattern recognition/image-beacon alternatives only sketched, not enabled. Any re-implementer inherits the calibration burden.

## 2. US5953077A — "System for displaying an object that is not visible to a camera"
- **Link:** https://patents.google.com/patent/US5953077A/en
- **Assignee:** Fox Sports Productions LLC (original; this is the FoxTrax glowing-hockey-puck lineage) → Sportsmedia Technology Corp
- **Filed:** 1997-01-17 · **Granted:** 1999-09-14 · **Status:** Expired – Lifetime (2017-01-17, normal 20-year term)
- **Inventors:** Stanley K. Honey, Richard H. Cavallaro, Terence J. O'Brien, Timothy P. Heidmann, Patrick W. Olsen, James R. Gloudemans
- **What the claims actually cover (plain English):** If the camera's view of a target (e.g., a hockey puck) is blocked by a "barrier" (e.g., the boards), automatically detect the obstruction and edit the live video to show where the target is — either by keying in a second image of the target or adding a highlight (claims 1, 6–8). Obstruction detection can be computed symbolically: build a line-of-position from camera to target and check whether it passes through a barrier surface (claims 28–29). Target position comes from a separate sensor (not the camera) plus field-of-view sensors (claims 20–23).
- **What the claims do NOT cover:** Enhancing an object that is already visible (claim 1 requires obstruction); position determination without a barrier/geometry check; non-video presentation of obstruction (claims are video-edit methods); any system where the camera itself is the only sensor.
- **Admitted limitations:** Background admits the puck is 3″×1″, moves at high speed, appears as "a small dot or a smear," and is frequently blocked by the boards. The design requires **co-located IR/detection sensors separate from the broadcast cameras** plus camera field-of-view sensors — i.e., per-arena hardware installs. This patent covers the *glowing puck* approach that TV audiences rejected as a commercial product; the core mechanic (target invisible → show it) was sound, but the product wrapped around it was not.

## 3. US6133946A — "System for determining the position of an object"
- **Link:** https://patents.google.com/patent/US6133946A/en
- **Assignee:** Sportvision Inc → Sportsmedia Technology Corp
- **Filed:** 1998-03-11 (priority 1998-01-06) · **Granted:** 2000-10-17 · **Status:** Expired – Lifetime (2018-03-11, normal 20-year term)
- **Inventors:** Richard H. Cavallaro, Jerry N. Gepner, James R. Gloudemans, Stanley K. Honey, William F. Squadron, Marvin S. White
- **What the claims actually cover (plain English):** Determine an object's vertical position (headline use: how high a basketball player jumped) by having an operator **point at the object with a pointing device** in images from two cameras, constructing a ray from each camera through the pointed pixel, transforming both rays to a common coordinate system, and finding the point of closest approach between them (claim 1). Variants: single camera with a pre-calibrated pixel→height lookup table (claim 37); subtract player height to get jump height (claims 2, 9); report via graphic overlaid on replay video (claim 5).
- **What the claims do NOT cover:** *Automatic* detection — the independent claims are operator-in-the-loop (manual pointing); an autonomous tracking system with no human click does not read on the core claims. Also not covered: single-camera triangulation without the lookup table, or anything not measuring *vertical* position.
- **Admitted limitations:** The specification includes full camera-mapping/registration flowcharts (FIGs. 7–9) and a dedicated FIG. 10 for **"minimizing registration error"** — i.e., the patent's own math only converges after calibration to suppress registration error. Practical accuracy is bounded by: camera registration quality, operator pointing precision, and frame synchronization between the two cameras (time-code readers in the apparatus claims). This is a *post-hoc replay-tool* patent, not a real-time tracking patent.

## 4. US6292130B1 — "System for determining the speed and/or timing of an object"
- **Link:** https://patents.google.com/patent/US6292130B1/en
- **Assignee:** Sportvision Inc → Sportsmedia Technology Corp
- **Filed:** 1999-04-09 · **Granted:** 2001-09-18 · **Status:** Expired – Lifetime (2019-04-09, normal 20-year term)
- **Inventors:** Richard H. Cavallaro, Fred Judson Heinzmann, Stanley K. Honey, Kenneth A. Milnes, Marvin S. White
- **What the claims actually cover (plain English):** Measure bat-swing speed and pitch timing using **multiple radar units** (apparatus claim 40: five at batter level behind home plate plus one above) with frequency-time resolution of the radar data: FFT the returns, detect pitch start from peak intensities meeting pitch criteria, find the end of the pitch by predicting end-of-flight and locating "end of line" points in a window, and identify "bat flashes" in the radar returns to compute max bat speed (claims 1–8, 19–22). 3D velocity vectors and trajectory estimation are claimed (claims 13, 27–28).
- **What the claims do NOT cover:** Radar-free speed measurement (optical/computer-vision tracking, RFID, wearables); speed detection using a single radar without the multi-unit flash-identification scheme; speed displayed in true real time.
- **Admitted limitations:** Claim 9 states detection + determination happen **"within 5 seconds"** — the system was near-real-time at best, designed for between-pitch broadcast insertion, not in-play use. It demands a **six-radar behind-the-plate installation** — heavy fixed infrastructure per stadium. The hardest sub-problem (bat flash identification in noisy Doppler returns) is heuristic-based (predefined pitch criteria, predicted windows), which is where accuracy and false-positive risk live.

## 5. US6229550B1 — "System for blending a graphic"
- **Link:** https://patents.google.com/patent/US6229550B1/en
- **Assignee:** Sportvision Inc → Sportsmedia Technology Corp
- **Filed:** 1998-09-24 · **Granted:** 2001-05-08 · **Status:** Expired – Lifetime (2018-09-24, normal 20-year term)
- **Inventors:** James R. Gloudemans, Richard H. Cavallaro, Stanley K. Honey, Marvin S. White
- **What the claims actually cover (plain English):** This is the compositing engine behind the yellow line. An operator picks a region of video; the system records its colors; then for each live frame it compares pixel colors to the stored colors and blends the graphic accordingly, using **"inclusions"** (colors to draw over) and **"exclusions"** (colors never to draw over, e.g., uniforms) with taper zones and per-pixel or per-polygon alpha blending coefficients (claims 1, 16–26). The sampled region can differ from the target region (claims 3–4) and the color table can be re-sampled mid-broadcast (claim 15).
- **What the claims do NOT cover:** Blending that never samples a reference color region from video (pre-computed or learned blending without live color comparison); segmentation not based on color comparison (semantic masks, depth maps); any single hard-edged binary chroma key — the claims require *degrees* of blending via coefficients.
- **Admitted limitations:** The background is explicit: standard chroma-keying can't run in real time **and** can't distinguish "green of grass" (blend) from "green of uniform" (don't blend); changing weather/light/uncontrolled stadium colors force operator re-sampling during the event. The whole method is colorimetry — it breaks under snow, glare, team-color collisions, or uniforms with grass-like colors. **Family has litigation:** Sportvision/Fox sued PVI (Oct 1999, C.D. Cal.) over virtual-insertion patents; PVI counterclaimed (Aug 2001); the parties **settled with cross-licensing in 2002** (SportsBusiness Journal) — so PVI's L-VIS approach (image-recognition, no stadium hardware) was never suppressed.

## 6. US6466275B1 — "System for enhancing a video presentation of an event with event information"
- **Link:** https://patents.google.com/patent/US6466275B1/en
- **Assignee:** Sportvision Inc → Sportsmedia Technology Corp
- **Filed:** 1999-04-16 · **Granted:** 2002-10-15 · **Status:** Expired – Lifetime (2019-04-16, normal 20-year term)
- **Inventors:** Stanley K. Honey, Richard H. Cavallaro, Jerry N. Gepner, James R. Gloudemans, Marvin S. White
- **What the claims actually cover (plain English):** Split the graphics pipeline in two: put only sensors at the stadium and do the heavy processing **at a remote studio**. Camera-view data (pan/tilt/zoom/roll/focus/extender sensors, 40,000-count optical encoders) is time-code-synced at the venue, embedded in the vertical blanking interval or sent over phone/cable/satellite lines, and matched frame-by-frame to program video at the remote location, which computes where the graphic goes and inserts it (claims 1–8, 13–14, 25–31). One central facility can serve many simultaneous events (claim 13).
- **What the claims do NOT cover:** All-at-the-venue processing (the claims' remote-location limitation is their defining feature — do everything in one truck and you don't read on them); enhancement without camera-view sensor data (pure image-based tracking without encoders); non-video event data use.
- **Admitted limitations:** The background's whole premise is that per-event equipment is expensive ("four sets of equipment… trucks to house/transport"); the remote model only pays off if the camera data link and registration info stay synchronized. The claims repeatedly require **receiving registration information multiple times / periodically** (claims 5, 10, 30) — i.e., camera-sensor drift is a known, recurring failure mode that must be continuously corrected, and any latency in the backhaul breaks the frame→sensor-data match.

## WHY THEY STOPPED — Sportvision verdict

**Expiry mechanism: normal 20-year term completion on all six.** Google Patents shows "Expired – Lifetime" for each, with anticipated-expiration dates exactly 20 years post-filing (2017–2019). No fee lapse, no abandonment. The patent assignments were diligently maintained — Sportvision repeatedly pledged the portfolio as collateral to lenders (Comerica, Hercules Technology Growth Capital, Escalate Capital, Velocity Venture Holdings, Multiplier Capital, 2004–2015), which reads as a company under financing pressure, not one abandoning its IP.

**What actually happened to the company (evidence):**
- **The yellow first-down line won commercially.** Emmy-winning, adopted across NFL broadcasts, and — per SMT's own Super Bowl LII coverage (Sports Video Group, 2018) — SMT was still running the 1st & Ten line on NBC's Sunday Night Football, Thursday Night Football, SkyCams, goal-line cameras, and the in-stadium videoboard in 2018, *plus* adding new real-space graphics (backwards-pass line, route painting, Next Gen Stats overlays). This is active development, not maintenance mode.
- **Acquisition absorption: SMT (SportsMEDIA Technology), Durham NC, acquired 100% of Sportvision (Chicago), deal closed October 4, 2016** (unanimously approved; financed via minority investments by Eldridge Industries, Vicente Capital Partners, and CEO Gerard J. Hall). Sportvision was rebranded as SMT; all six patents were assigned to Sportsmedia Technology Corp in March 2017. Reported price >$25M (Bleacher Report). SMT held ~70 Sportvision patents, including PITCHf/x, RACEf/x, and the 1st & Ten line.
- **Continuations show R&D actually ran until ~2010, not 1999.** US7075556 spawned continuations US7492363B2 (filed 2005), US7750901B2 (2009), US7928976B2 (2010) — the telestrator line kept filing for a decade, then stopped, consistent with a company in financial restructuring (the lender-security-agreement years) heading toward sale.
- **The litigation was settled, not lost:** Sportvision/Fox vs. PVI (1999–2002) ended in cross-licensing (SBJ, 2002). PVI's L-VIS remained a competing, sensorless approach — which validates that sensor-free (image-recognition) implementations were never blocked by this portfolio.
- **Supersession, lane by lane:** PITCHf/x was replaced by Statcast (TrackMan + ChyronHego) in 2017 — SMT later sued MLB Advanced Media over alleged misappropriation and infringement of U.S. Patent 7,341,530 in the PITCHcast/Statcast buildout (Harvard JOLT digest). The glowing puck (FoxTrax) was a viewer-rejected product dead-end. Radar bat-speed and jump-height tools were always secondary products.
- **Who sells/enhances broadcast graphics now:** SMT remains the incumbent — live 1st & Ten lines, Next Gen Stats data integration, ISO Track image-based tracking (Indy 500, Kentucky Derby, Tour de France). Competitors include ChyronHego (tracking/integration, e.g., Statcast), and the leagues' own data arms (NFL Next Gen Stats via RFID chips — a sensor paradigm this patent family never covered).

**Verdict:** (a) **commercial success with normal term expiry** is the dominant story for the yellow-line core; (c) **acquisition absorption** explains why new filing stopped (Sportvision was consolidated into SMT, which develops the products under its own banner and newer methods); (d) **technical dead ends** apply to the puck and, arguably, the six-radar bat-speed rig; (e) **supersession** applies to PITCHf/x (Statcast) and to RFID-based player tracking generally.

**The single most important technical limitation a re-implementer must design around:** **calibration/registration fragility.** Across the family, the linchpin is keeping the virtual graphic registered to the real camera: Sportvision's approach requires instrumented cameras (pan/tilt/zoom encoders, inclinometers, gyros) with *repeated, continuous re-registration* (US6466275 claims demand periodic registration updates; US6133946 needs a dedicated registration-error minimization routine), and its blending engine is colorimetry-based, which degrades under weather, lighting shifts, and uniform/field color collisions (US6229550 background). Modern re-implementations avoid both weaknesses with image-based match-moving/segmentation that needs no stadium hardware — the very approach PVI shipped in the 1990s and SMT's ISO Track uses today.
