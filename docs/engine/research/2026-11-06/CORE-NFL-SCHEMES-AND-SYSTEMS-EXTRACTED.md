# Core NFL Schemes & Systems (Extracted Source)

Core Offensive Schemes & Systems
West Coast Offense (Bill Walsh):
Core Concept: Short, high-percentage horizontal passes (slants, quick outs, shallow crosses) released under 2.5 seconds on 3- to 5-step drops. Passing sets up the run rather than the other way around.
Personnel & Demands: Requires high-accuracy passers and high yards-after-catch (YAC) receivers/backs.
Weaknesses: Vulnerable to tight press-man coverage jamming routes at the line.
Air Coryell (Don Coryell):
Core Concept: Deep, aggressive vertical passing requiring a route tree designated by numerical codes (e.g., 989 = go, post, go).
Personnel & Demands: Needs an offensive line capable of sustaining long pass-protection blocks, a big-armed quarterback, and receiving tight ends working intermediate seams.
Weaknesses: High sack risk and lower completion percentage.
Air Raid (Hal Mumme, Mike Leach):
Core Concept: Passing 70–80% of snaps out of shotgun/empty sets with 10 personnel (1 RB, 4 WR). Built around only 10–20 core concepts executed at high tempo (e.g., Mesh, Four Verticals, Shallow Cross, Y-Cross, Stick).
Weaknesses: Poor clock control; susceptible to relentless pass rush if one-dimensional.
Erhardt-Perkins (Ron Erhardt, Ray Perkins / Bill Belichick):
Core Concept: Concept-based play calling (single words like Ghost) rather than formation/route-tree codes. Any concept can be run out of any formation or personnel grouping, allowing fast no-huddle audibles.
Demands: High mental processing demand on receivers and quarterback to recognize coverages pre-snap.
Shanahan / McVay Outside Zone & Bootleg:
Core Concept: Marries wide-zone run blocking (linemen moving laterally to create vertical cutback lanes) with play-action bootlegs. Uses pre-snap jet motion and identical backfield footwork so runs and passes look indistinguishable.
Demands: Highly agile offensive linemen (often lighter athletes around 290 lbs) and disciplined running backs who read leverage cuts.
Power & Gap Run Schemes:
Core Concept: Creates a designated point of attack by down-blocking with the line, kicking out the edge with a fullback, and pulling the backside guard.
Demands: Massive road-grader linemen and heavy personnel (21 or 22 personnel).
Run-Pass Option (RPO):
Core Concept: Puts a conflict defender (usually an overhang linebacker or safety) in a bind. If he crashes down on the run fit, the quarterback throws behind him; if he drops into coverage, the back gets the handoff.
Demands: 0.8-second decision window from the quarterback.
Heavy Personnel Sets (12 & 13 Personnel):
Core Concept: Forces defenses into a mismatch dilemma: keep light nickel/dime DBs on the field and get overpowered in the run game, or substitute heavier linebackers and surrender speed in space.
Passing Route Concepts & Counters
Mesh: Two shallow crosses from opposite directions crossing closely to create natural traffic/picks against man coverage. Counter: Zone drops or man-switch assignments.
Levels: Two or three receivers running crossing/in-breaking routes across the field at staggered depths (short, intermediate, deep) to force zone defenders into high-low dilemmas. Counter: Pattern matching and cloud coverage.
Flood: Three receivers overloading a single sideline at three distinct levels (deep corner, intermediate out, flat). Stresses Cover 3 and Cover 4 by placing 3 receivers on 2 zone defenders. Counter: Cover 2 (safety over the corner route) and backside pressure.
Smash: Outside receiver runs a short hitch/curl while the inside receiver runs a corner route behind it. Attacks the void in Cover 2 between the flat corner and deep half safety. Counter: Tampa 2 (middle linebacker running deep middle).
Four Verticals: All four eligibles run vertical routes to stress single-high safeties (Cover 3) or divide two-high safeties down the seam (Cover 2). Counter: Quarters (Cover 4) coverage.
Stick: Quick 3-receiver triangle read featuring an outside fade, a 5-yard option/stick route, and a flat route. Designed to defeat blitzes and soft Cover 3 zone. Counter: Press-man and robber/buzz defenders.
Dagger: Inside vertical clear-out route that clears the deep safety, opening a deep 15-yard dig route underneath for the outside receiver. Destroys standard Cover 2. Counter: Tampa 2 or robber safety dropping into the hole.
Pass Protection Schemes
5-0 / Big on Big (BOB): Linemen lock up man-to-man against defensive linemen. Easy to install, but highly vulnerable to stunts and twists.
Full Slide: The entire offensive line slides one gap in a single direction (e.g., left or right), leaving the backside rusher for the running back. Vulnerable if a defensive end isolates the running back.
Half Slide (Jet / Turnback):
The most standard NFL protection.
One side plays man-to-man (guard and tackle), while the center and other side slide into gap zones starting from the identified "Mike" linebacker.
Running back checks the remaining linebackers on a "one you, two me" rule.
Weaknesses: Overloads sending four rushers to the weak side or four to the strong side require hot routes or sight adjustments.
Play-Action & Boot Protections:
Boot Action: Offensive lineman or tight end travels back across the formation to protect the quarterback's backside.
Naked Boot: Entire line flows with the run fake; quarterback rolls out unblocked and must beat or hold the edge defender with the fake.
Defensive Coverage Shells & Mechanics
Cover 1 (Man Free):
Structure: Single high safety in the deep middle; 5 defenders locked in man-to-man coverage; four-man rush.
Assignments: Linebackers split the running back via banjo rules (whoever the back releases toward covers him) while the free linebacker acts as a "plugger" (spies QB, adds on blitz, or cuts crossers).
Strength / Weakness: Puts 7+ men in the run box to stop the run. Demands elite cornerbacks who can hold up on islands.
Cover 2 (Two-Deep, Five-Under Zone):
Structure: Two safeties split deep halves ("wider than the widest, deeper than the deepest"). Five underneath defenders: 2 flat corners, 2 hook/curl linebackers, 1 middle hook.
Key Mechanic: Outside corners must jam/re-route receivers at the line to prevent free releases into the deep vertical seams.
Weaknesses: Light 6-man run box; vertical seams and deep hole between safeties.
Cover 3 (Three-Deep, Four-Under Zone):
Structure: Corners defend deep outside thirds; single high safety takes the deep middle; four underneath defenders (two buzz to the numbers/flats, two take hook zones 1 yard outside the hashes).
Strength / Weakness: Strong against the run with 7–8 men in the box. Vulnerable in the flats to quick outs/hitches ("free access") and against 4-vertical attacks.
Cover 4 / Quarters (Pattern Match):
Structure: Four deep quarters. Corners play man on the #1 receiver; safeties read the #2 receiver (tight end or slot).
Pattern Match Rules: If #2 goes vertical, the safety covers him man-to-man. If #2 goes shallow, outside, or blocks, the safety triggers down aggressively into the run fit (giving the defense an 8-man box).
Weaknesses: Post routes behind squatting safeties; free access throws underneath.
Split-Field Concepts (Cover 6 / Cover 9):
Divides the field down the middle: Cover 4 to the passing strength and Cover 2 to the weak side (Cover 9 flips this). Eliminates deep boundary passing while bracketing star receivers.
Cover 0 (Simulated Pressures & Bluff Blitzes):
Shows 7 or 8 defenders walked up on the line of scrimmage to guarantee an unblocked rusher or force quick hot throws. Defenses either send pure heat or drop several defenders out into intermediate passing lanes (rat zones / Tampa 2 / drop-8) to muddy reads.

Defensive Fronts, Box Counts & Gaps
Gap Alignments & Techniques:
A-Gaps: Space between Center and Guards.
B-Gaps: Space between Guards and Tackles.
C-Gaps: Space outside the Tackles.
Technique Coding: 0-Tech is head-up on the Center; counts outward through wide-9 (wide outside the Tight End).
The Box Definition: 2 yards outside the widest offensive linemen/tight ends to 8 yards past the line of scrimmage.
Heavy Fronts & Run Packages:
6-2 Front: 6 DL, 2 LBs, 3 DBs. Plugs all A, B, and C gaps to eliminate running lanes. Corners are on total islands with zero deep safety help; vulnerable to quick slants or anything over 10 yards.
5-3 Front: 5 DL, 3 LBs, 3 DBs. Standard goal-line personnel. Extra linebacker provides underneath hook/flat support against play-action, but vulnerable if an offense deploys 4 or 5 receivers.
5-2 Eagle: 5 DL, 2 LBs, 4 DBs. Run-first front with improved DB numbers, but 5 down linemen focus on gap control rather than rushing the passer, allowing quarterbacks extended pocket time.
4-4 Defense: 4 DL, 4 LBs, 3 DBs (8-man box). Linebackers spill to control both edges outside the tackles. Hard counter to RPOs and triple-option/sweeps because all 3 phases (give, keep, pitch) are accounted for. Weak against spread sets with 4+ wideouts.
46 Bear Front: 5 DL, 3 LBs, 1 Safety, 2 CBs. Features the "Bear front" with 3 interior defensive linemen directly over the center and both guards (covering both A-gaps and B-gaps). Linebackers walk up to the line of scrimmage showing 8 rushers. Single high safety frequently blitzes to send 6–7 rushers. Vulnerable to quick 3-step passing, bubble screens, and 4-receiver packages.
Base & Sub-Packages:
3-4 Defense: 3 DL, 4 LBs, 4 DBs. Multiplicity-driven front anchored by a 2-gapping 0-tech Nose Tackle who occupies the center and draws double-teams. Allows 4 linebackers to rush or drop from ambiguous pre-snap alignments.
4-3 Base Defense: 4 DL, 3 LBs, 4 DBs. Provides a 4-man organic pass rush while linebackers fit gaps and match tight ends. Weakness occurs when facing 11 personnel (3 WRs), as the Will/Sam linebacker is forced to match a slot receiver in space.
Nickel Defense (4-2-5): 4 DL, 2 LBs, 5 DBs. Replaces a base linebacker with a Nickel corner to cover slot receivers. Nickel corners utilize jam techniques at the line or sit in passing lanes in robber coverage. Susceptible to inside power runs due to having only 2 linebackers in the box.
Dime Defense (4-1-6): 4 DL, 1 LB, 6 DBs (often 4 CBs, 2 Safeties or 3 CBs, 3 Safeties). Used in 3rd-and-12+, 2-minute drills, or against empty backfields. Drops 7 into coverage relying entirely on a 4-man pass rush; gives up inside runs willingly to burn clock.
In-Depth Secondary Coverage Rules & Matchups
Field Math (Zone Geometry):
NFL field is 53 1/3 yards wide by 100 yards long.
If a defense rushes 2 and drops 9, each deep defender covers approximately 13 yards of width, with 5 underneath defenders covering 5 discrete underneath zones.
Standard rush (4 down linemen) leaves 7 coverage defenders: either 3-Deep / 4-Under or 2-Deep / 5-Under.
Cover 1 Plug (Man-to-Man):
Shell: 1 Free Safety deep in the middle (shading 60/40 to the passing strength). Corners play press-man with outside leverage, funneling receivers inside toward help. Nickel plays man on the slot; Strong Safety plays man on the Tight End.
Linebacker Banjo & Plug Rules: Linebackers read the running back's release direction.
If the running back flows to Linebacker A, Linebacker A locks him in man-to-man ("Me, me, me!").
Linebacker B becomes the "Plugger" and executes one of three roles:
Disrupts and collision-checks inside crossing routes.
Spies a mobile scrambling quarterback.
Adds on as a 5th rusher (green dog blitz).
Pre-Snap Recognition (Rat vs. Lab Motions):
Rat (R): Running back motions wide right outside the boundary receiver.
Lab (L): Running back motions wide left outside the boundary receiver.
Read: If a linebacker tracks the back out wide, it is Man coverage (Cover 1). If an outside cornerback widens to take the back and the linebacker only slides/bumps inside, it is Zone coverage (Cover 3).
Cover 2 (Two-High, 5-Under Zone):
Shell: Safeties split deep halves ("wider than the widest, deeper than the deepest").
Underneath Zones: 5 underneath players: 2 Cornerbacks in the flats, 2 outside Linebackers in hook-curl zones, 1 Middle Linebacker in the middle-hook zone.
Jam Technique: Cornerbacks must physically jam and re-route the #1 outside receiver inside to disrupt timing and protect the vertical seam; free releases allow offenses to split the deep half safety.
Variations: Firmer Cover 2 (corners lock in the flat regardless) vs. Floated Cover 2 (corners sink underneath intermediate routes based on boundary releases).
Cover 3 (Three-Deep, 4-Under Zone):
Shell: Free Safety takes the deep middle third (leaning 60/40 to passing strength); both outside Cornerbacks defend deep outside thirds, keeping all vertical threats in front.
Underneath Landmarks: 4 underneath defenders:
2 Flat/Curl-Flat defenders buzz to the field numbers at 6 to 8 yards depth (if the QB looks away, they "melt" inside).
2 Hook defenders drop to landmarks 1 yard outside the NFL hash marks, reading the quarterback's eyes.
Weaknesses: Vulnerable in the flats to quick outs/hitches ("free access" because corners bail to deep thirds) and 4-vertical concepts stretching 3 deep defenders across 4 zones.
Cover 4 / Quarters (Pattern-Match Rules):
Setup: 4 deep quarters, 3 underneath defenders (2 quarters-flat defenders and 1 Mike linebacker).
Corner Responsibilities: Play man-to-man against the #1 receiver unless #1 runs underneath/shallow. Corners align inside-out (trusting DB speed on outside routes while protecting the inside) or outside-in (preventing deep outs).
Box & Triangle Match Structure:
The Box (Passing Strength):
Apex/Overhang Defender: Reads #2 receiver. If #2 goes out, matches to the flat; if #2 goes vertical, carries him to the safety; if #2 goes in, delivers him to the Mike.
Safety: Reads #2. If #2 goes vertical, locks him man-to-man. If #2 goes out, anticipates #1 coming deep/inside and brackets the post/dig. If #2 goes in, shades middle to rob crossers.
Mike Linebacker: Takes #3 (often RB). If #3 goes vertical, matches vertically; if #3 goes out, makes a "push" call to slide the Apex to #3 in the flat while Mike takes #2.
The Triangle (Weak Side):
Will Linebacker: Part of the run fit. Cannot chase routes into the flat.
Corner: Traps down on #2 if #2 breaks to the flat; if #2 is vertical, carries #1 deep.
Weak Safety: If #2 is vertical, takes him deep. If #2 breaks out, traps #1 deep over the top.
Split-Field Concepts & Pressure Formations:
Cover 6 (Quarter-Quarter-Half): Plays Cover 4 (Quarters) to the passing strength and Cover 2 (Half-field safety and flat corner) to the weak side. Mike/Will carries the #3 receiver if he goes vertical.
Cover 9: Inverted Cover 6. Plays Quarters to the weak side (neutralizing isolated elite receivers like stacked weak-side threats) and Cover 2 to the strong side.
Cover 0 (Bluff & Amoeba Pressures): Puts 7 or 8 defenders walked up on the line of scrimmage with DBs backed off. Guarantees a free rusher if all rush. Teams often execute "Drop-8" or bail into Tampa 2 / Cover 3 post-snap with interior defenders dropping into "rat zones" to muddy throwing lanes.
Offensive Line Mechanics & Protection Rules
Pass-Blocking Technique & Fundamentals:
Tempo in the Set: Reading pass rusher's pre-snap stance. If the rusher is standing straight up, he must "load and go," giving the tackle time to pace his kick-slide; setting too wide or over-setting leaves the inside B-gap vulnerable.
Hand Placement & Punch: Bisect the defender's frame, then bisect his inside half again to target the armpit. Thumbs up, locking hands inside the chest plate to avoid holding penalties while anchoring against bull rushes.
Protection Schemes & Slide Rules:
5-0 / Big on Big (BOB): 5 offensive linemen lock in man-to-man matchups on 5 rushers. Susceptible to defensive line games, stunts, and twists.
Full Slide: Entire line slides one gap in a single direction; running back or fullback takes the backside edge rusher. Easiest to install for 3-step drops and play-action, but exposes running backs against physical edge defenders.
Half Slide (Jet / Turnback / 2-Jet & 3-Jet):
2-Jet: Slide protection to the left.
3-Jet: Slide protection to the right (slide moves away from the call number).
Guard and tackle on the man side lock up man-to-man on interior DL. The center and backside linemen slide into gap-zone assignments starting from the identified "Mike."
The first uncovered offensive lineman initiates the slide.
"One You, Two Me" Rule: Running back checks the Mike first. If Mike rushes, the back takes him; if a second linebacker blitzes, the quarterback must throw "hot."
Overload Pressure Adjustments:
4-Weak Blitz: 4 rushers come from the weak side. Addressed via:
Tom Call: Tells the running back to cross the formation to the weak side (leaves Mike/Sam unaccounted for).
Fan Call: Pushes the offensive tackle out wide to take the edge rusher while the back fills the interior gap.
Hot Routes & Sight Adjustments: Receiver automatically alters route into vacated blitz space.
4-Strong Blitz: Addressed via:
Joker / Opposite Call: Flips the slide direction so linemen slide strong.
Stay Call: Keeps an extra blocker (Tight End or Fullback) in the protection, sacrificing an eligible receiver.
Play-Action & Rollout Mechanics:
Fake Power Protection: Pulls a backside guard to sell run-action to freeze linebackers while opening clean throwing lanes behind them.
Boot Action vs. Naked Boot:
Boot Action: An offensive lineman, tight end, or back crosses the formation against the fake to secure the edge for the rolling quarterback.
Naked Boot: The entire line flows in one direction; the quarterback rolls out with zero backside protection and must freeze the edge defender with the fake.
Sprint / Rollout Protection: Linemen slide toward the sprint side; backside tackle hooks the defensive end to prevent pursuit from behind.
Offensive Formations, Terminology & Route Systems
Personnel & Route Lettering:
Letter Designations:
H: Halfback / primary running back.
Y: Primary inline tight end.
Z: Flanker / play-side receiver (usually lines up off the line of scrimmage).
X: Split end / weak-side receiver (lines up on the line of scrimmage, away from the call).
F: Multi-purpose skill player (Fullback, slot receiver, or second tight end).
21 Personnel Formations (2 RBs, 1 TE, 2 WRs):
Backfield Sets:
Split Right/Left: H behind the weak tackle, F behind the strong tackle.
I Right/Left: F and H aligned in a straight line behind the QB (F at 4 yards, H at 8 yards).
Strong Right/Left: F offsets to the strong side between guard and tackle.
Weak Right/Left: F offsets to the weak side away from the tight end.
Receiver Modifiers (Moving Z and X away from the call):
Slot: Z aligns inside X on the weak side.
Zoom: X aligns inside Z on the weak side.
Star: X takes a tight 3-yard split from the tackle; Z aligns inside X.
Sun: X takes a tight 3-yard split; Z aligns outside X.
12 Personnel Formations (1 RB, 2 TEs, 2 WRs) – The "S-Words":
In S-words, Y and Z go to the call; F and X go away from the call.
Solo Right: Y inline to the right with Z split wide; F inline to the left with X split wide.
Classy Tag: Reduces splits of X and Z to tight 3-yard alignments.
Snug Right: Both Y and F align off the line of scrimmage; Z and X compress inside into 3-to-4-yard splits.
Special Right: Y aligns 1 yard deep and 1 yard outside Z; F aligns 1 yard deep and 1 yard outside X.
Sink Right: Both Y and F sink into the backfield as fullbacks, creating a 3-back set.
Sprout Right: Y flexes wide outside Z; F flexes wide outside X.
Trips Formations (3 Receivers to the Call) – The "T-Words":
In T-words, Y, F, and Z go to the call; X goes away from the call.
Trips Right: Y is inline on the ball; F is in the slot off the ball; Z is outside off the ball.
Trough Right: "TE Get Off" – Y aligns off the line of scrimmage; Z aligns on the ball.
Trout Right: Y detaches from the offensive line into a 3-to-4-yard split with F and Z.
Trump Right: "M = Middle" – Y aligns in the middle between F and Z.
Trio Right: "O = Outside" – Y aligns as the outermost receiver with F and Z inside.
Passing Concepts & Progression Architecture:
Air Coryell Route Numbering: 3-digit calls dictate routes for X, Y, and Z on the route tree (e.g., 8 = Post, 9 = Go/Fade, 6 = 8–12 yard Dig). Mills concept runs a post from the outside receiver paired with a dig from the tight end to hold the deep safety.
Mesh (Air Raid / West Coast): Two receivers cross at 3–5 yards depth, setting natural rub picks against man coverage; running back releases into the flat as a checkdown.
Smash Concept: Outside receiver runs a 5-yard hitch/curl while the inside slot receiver runs an 8-to-12-yard corner route. High-lows the Cover 2 flat cornerback.
Dagger Concept: Inside receiver runs a vertical seam to clear deep safeties; outside receiver breaks on a 15-yard dig into the vacated middle.
Y-Cross: Inside receiver/tight end runs a 15–18 yard deep crosser behind linebackers; cleared by vertical clear-out routes outside.
Double China: Both outside receivers run 5-yard "China" in-routes while an inside receiver runs a corner route; creates inside-out conflict for red-zone defenders.
Ghost (Erhardt-Perkins): Independent route concept featuring a Go (Z), Flat (H), and Hitch (Y). Can be run out of Trips, Bunch, or Doubles by coaching route rules by position rather than formation diagrams.
Situational Down & Distance Football Metrics
1st Down Operations:
1st & 10: Goal is 4–6 yards to establish 2-down series management. Offenses convert on 1st down immediately 20–25% of the time, but convert the full series ~75% of the time. Defenses remain in base looks.
1st & Short (1–5 yards): Immediate conversion rate is ~60%; overall series conversion rate jumps to ~90%. Prime down for deep play-action shots due to minimal penalty for incompletion.
1st & Long (15+ yards): Often resulting from penalties. Immediate conversion drops to 10–12%; series survival rate falls to 30–35%. Heavy reliance on quick screens and safe checkdowns to regain field position.
2nd Down Operations:
2nd & Short (1–3 yards): Highest offensive flexibility. Low-risk/high-reward deep shots or perimeter runs.
2nd & Medium (4–6 yards): Playbook completely balanced between inside runs and quick game. Conversion on the down sits around 40%; full-series conversion rate is ~75%.
2nd & Long (7–12 yards): Damage control. Goal is recovering at least 50% of needed yardage to create manageable 3rd downs; series survival rate sits at 55–60%.
2nd & Very Long (13+ yards): Often triggered by sacks. Defenses bring heavy heat; counter-punished with draw plays and screen passes.
3rd Down Operations:
3rd & Short (1–3 yards): Power football. ~70% run frequency against loaded 8-to-9-man fronts.
3rd & Medium (4–6 yards): Heavy zone-blitz down. Offenses utilize quick slants, rub-crossers, and option routes.
3rd & Long (7–12 yards): Long-developing route trees against exotic pressure looks; conversion rates fall to 20–25%.
3rd & Very Long (13+ yards): Sub-10% conversion rate. Offenses emphasize ball security and field position over aggressive conversion attempts.
4th Down Operations:
4th & Inches: Dominated by quarterback sneaks and power wedge runs; high analytical conversion rate.
4th & Short (1–4 yards): Offenses counter stacked run fronts with quick slants and RPO flat releases.
4th & Medium (5–9 yards): ~40% conversion rate; relies on pre-snap shift/motion to diagnose coverage shells and deliver timing-based throws.
4th & Long (10+ yards): 15–20% conversion rate; maximum blitz defense against deep protection structures.

Selection Bias / Context: Only 235 brains were actually donated to science by family members, and 215 of those tested positive for CTE (families who observed cognitive symptoms prior to death were significantly more likely to donate).

Medical Insight: Neurologists emphasize that subconcussive hits—most frequently occurring during practices rather than games—are the primary drivers of long-term damage; youth countermeasures include Guardian caps.

Player Roster Positions & Core Roles
Offensive Positions:
Quarterback (QB): Captain, receives the snap, makes pre-snap/post-snap reads, hands off, scrambles, or passes.
Running Back (RB): Carries the football, reads running lanes, catches passes out of the backfield.
Fullback (FB): Lead run blocker in the hole against linebackers; occasionally runs or catches.
Wide Receiver (WR): Speed/agility perimeter or slot route runner creating separation to catch passes.
Offensive Tackle (OT): Edge protector against pass rushers; sets edge in run blocking with footwork and length.
Offensive Guard (OG): Interior protector against defensive tackles; pulls and opens interior run lanes.
Center (C): Snaps the ball, anchors the line, calls out protections and defensive fronts.
Tight End (TE): Hybrid offensive lineman and wide receiver lining up inline, flexed, or in the slot.
Defensive Positions:
Defensive Tackle (DT): Interior lineman penetrating interior gaps to stop inside runs and collapse the pocket.
Defensive End (DE): Edge lineman rushing the passer and setting the edge against outside runs.
EDGE: Hybrid designation combining 4-3 Defensive End and 3-4 Outside Linebacker responsibilities.
Linebacker (ILB / OLB): Second-level defenders filling run gaps, covering tight ends/running backs, and blitzing.
Cornerback (CB): Perimeter coverage specialist matching outside wide receivers in man or zone.
Free Safety (FS): Deep middle centerfielder; typically positioned to the weak side to read the quarterback's eyes.
Strong Safety (SS): Positioned toward the tight end / strong side to provide box run support and match tight ends.
Special Teams:
Kicker (K): Field goals (3 points), extra points (1 point), and kickoffs.
Punter (P): Punts on 4th down to pin the opponent deep and minimize return yardage.
Holder: Catches the long snap and places/spins the ball for the kicker (almost always the punter or backup QB).
Long Snapper (LS): Snaps the football accurately over extended distance under pressure.
Return Specialist (KR / PR): Fields kickoffs/punts; usually an active cornerback, running back, or wide receiver.
Historical Formations & Play-Design Case Studies
The T-Formation (1940 Bears vs. Redskins):
Chicago demolished Washington 73–0 in the 1940 NFL Championship Game, ending the single-wing era and establishing modern under-center play.
Structure: Quarterback under center with 3 backs aligned behind him in a horizontal line forming a "T" (Fullback centered, two Halfbacks flanking).
Options: Hand off to the fullback up the middle, pitch to either halfback outside, or QB keeper.
The Pro Set:
Two backs aligned side-by-side approximately 4 yards behind the under-center quarterback.
Washington under Joe Gibbs utilized diverse backfield combos (two tailbacks, FB/TB, or TE flexed as a wingback).
Phased out because carrying only 2 receivers and max 2 tight ends cannot stress modern nickel and dime defenses.
I-Formation Run Mechanics:
1990s Cowboys (Emmitt Smith running behind Daryl Johnston) and 1970s Steelers (Franco Harris).
Fullback lines up 4 yards deep as a lead blocker; tailback lines up 8 yards deep to build downhill speed and read the block.
Wildcat / Modern Single Wing (2008 Dolphins):
Direct snap to running backs Ronnie Brown and Ricky Williams (giving an unbalanced line with 10 blockers for 1 runner).
Defenses neutralized it by stacking 9–10 in the box once passing was confirmed non-viable.

Specific Play Call Translations:
Ace Twins Right 62 Slant Flat:
Ace: 11 Personnel (1 RB, 1 TE, 3 WRs).
Twins Right: Two wide receivers aligned to the right side; Tight End (Y) is aligned inline on the line of scrimmage.
62: 6-man pass protection; balanced offensive line picking up the front 4 plus the middle linebacker, with the running back scanning.
Slant Flat: Frontside route combination where #1 runs a slant and #2 runs into the flat.
63 / 64 Protection: 63 slides the line left with the back checking right; 64 slides the line right with the back checking left.
I Right Slot Fake 24 Lead 989 F-Angle 68 Protection:
I Right Slot: 21 personnel in I-formation; tight end aligned inline to the right, receiver aligned in the right slot.
Fake 24 Lead: Play-action fake mimicking running play 24 Lead (2-back hitting the 4-hole behind a lead block from the fullback).
989: Route tree combination: X runs a 9 (Go/Fade), Y runs an 8 (Post), Z runs a 9 (Go/Fade).
F-Angle: Running back (F) runs an angle/Texas route across the middle.
68 Protection: 6-man protection with the fullback securing the strong-side edge rusher.
Doubles Right 92 Mesh F-Wheel:
Doubles Right: 2x2 balanced receiver set with Z and Y to the right, X and H to the left.
92 Mesh: 90-series Air Raid call; receivers run shallow crossing mesh at 3–5 yards.
F-Wheel: Running back runs a wheel route up the boundary.
Trips Right 80 Stick:
80: Air Raid base all-hitch concept (receivers run 3–5 yard hitches and turn to the QB).
Stick Tag: Overrides base routes—Z runs a vertical clear-out, H runs a flat route, and Y runs an option stick route.
Y-Pop (I Right 999 Y-Pop):
Tight end (Y) fakes an inline run block on the defensive end before popping vertically down the seam behind safeties biting on the run.
Corner-Post (I Right 996 X Corner-Post):
Double-breaking route where the receiver fakes an outside corner route before snapping back inside to a deep post.
Smash Smashmouth & Defense Variations:
Smash Defense / "Smash the Line": Defensive linemen slant their movements toward one side of the line of scrimmage to disrupt gap blocking assignments.
Smash Passing Concept: Hitch route underneath a corner route.

Unmentioned Formation Tags, Motion Rules & Specific Mechanics
Gruden's "Halloween" Install Bits:
Gruden conducts an on-field install with actors dressed in Halloween costumes (Michael Myers with a knife, Jason Voorhees, Frankenstein, and a guy in a Horse mask).
Uses them to demonstrate player alignments for I Left, Weak Left, Strong Left, Split Left, Slot, Zoom, Sun, Star, Trips, Trough, Trout, Trump, Trio, Solo, Classy, Snug, Sink, and Sprout.
S-Words Rules (12 Personnel):
Rule: In all S-family calls, Y and Z travel to the play call; F and X travel away from the call.
T-Words Rules (Trips Variations):
Rule: In all T-family calls, Y, F, and Z travel to the call; X lines up on the ball away from the call.
Trough: The letters OFF in Trough trigger the tight end (Y) to step off the line of scrimmage, requiring the Z receiver to step up onto the line.
Trout: The letters OUT trigger Y to flex out into an open split away from the tackle.
Trump: The letter M triggers Y to align in the middle between F and Z.
Trio: The letter O triggers Y to align as the outside receiver.
Receiver Split Tags in 21 Personnel:
Sun vs. Star: Both tell X to cut his split down to 3 yards from the tackle. Star brings Z inside X ("come inside under the stars at night"); Sun puts Z outside X ("sunny and warm outside").
Slot vs. Zoom: Both send the receivers away from the call. Slot puts Z inside X; Zoom flips them and puts X inside Z.
The 1x4 Passing Split:
Modern passing counter against split-field coverages (pioneered by the Chiefs) where four receivers align to one side and a single stud receiver aligns isolated on the opposite boundary to force 1-on-1 coverage.
Under-Center vs. Shotgun Drop Timing:
Three-step drops from under center get the ball out immediately into the flat before defensive shells can match.
Shotgun drops add an extra exchange delay and alter passing angles, allowing fast modern defensive backs to trigger downhill on screens and quick throws.
Counter Run Vulnerability in Split-Field Defenses:
Two-high and split-field defenses are especially vulnerable to the counter run game because their defensive front alignments are overly static and regimented, creating interior angles for pulling blockers.
Substitutions and Clock Penalties:
When an offense substitutes personnel (e.g., swapping a fullback for a receiver), the referee allows the defense a 7-to-10-second matching window to make defensive substitutions.
If the offense does not substitute, they can snap the ball immediately, catching defenses in mismatched sub-packages or drawing 12-men-on-the-field penalties.
