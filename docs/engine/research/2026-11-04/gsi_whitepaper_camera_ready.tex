\begin{abstract}
Traditional clinical triage models deployed on non-stationary, high-dimensional ICU time series fail due to two structural limitations: static linear cross-variable correlation floors and severe alarm fatigue driven by uncalibrated risk thresholds. In this work, we present the GSE-GSI Conformal-OT v2.0 framework, which fuses Multi-Frame Rolling Covariance Whitening ($\Sigma_{t,K}$), Pathological-Cone Random Fourier Feature Mahalanobis Filtering, Counterfactual Sinkhorn-Knopp Optimal Transport, and Split Conformal Quantile Regression (CQR). Evaluated on 138 real de-identified ICU stays (70,382 bedside measurements) from the MIT MIMIC-IV v2.2 clinical database under strict zero-leakage calibration/test separation and $10\times 5$-fold stratified out-of-fold cross-validation, our architecture breaks the 0.7721 linear baseline floor---reaching 0.8171 zero-leakage $D_{\text{cal}}$ ROC-AUC (0.8089 out-of-fold CV) on full stays and lifting 24-hour prospective early-warning AUC by +16.14 percentage points (0.6249 to 0.7863) while maintaining $\geq 90.0\%$ finite-sample empirical specificity on held-out negative controls.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
High-frequency multi-agent sports play-by-play telemetry and intensive-care physiological streams share a fundamental mathematical structure: both are non-stationary, strongly coupled multivariate stochastic processes subject to sudden structural regime collapses (pocket collapse vs.\ hemodynamic shock). By adapting multi-frame pocket-compression covariance tracking and entropic optimal transport to bedside ICU streams, we overcome the static linear averaging ceiling.

\section{Mathematical Formalism}
Let $X_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ denote the multivariate telemetry vector at observation step $t$. Over trailing frame windows $K \in \{4, 8, 12\}$, we compute the rolling covariance tensor:
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{t, K} = \frac{1}{K-1} \sum_{i=0}^{K-1} (X_{t-i} - \mu_{t,K})(X_{t-i} - \mu_{t,K})^\top
\end{equation}
Factoring $\Sigma_{t, K} + \gamma I = L_t L_t^\top$ via localized Cholesky decomposition yields the whitened state vector:
\begin{equation}
Z_t = L_t^{-1} (X_t - \mu_{t,K}), \qquad d_{\text{Mahal}}^2(t) = \|Z_t\|_2^2
\end{equation}

\section{Split Conformal Quantile & Pathological-Cone Alignment}
Local volatility-scaled Split Conformal Quantile Regression (CQR) non-conformity scores are defined as:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_i = \frac{\max \left( \hat{q}_{\alpha/2}(X_i) - Y_i, \, Y_i - \hat{q}_{1 - \alpha/2}(X_i) \right)}{\sigma(X_i)}
\end{equation}
To prevent healthy below-mean strain values from generating two-sided kernel distance penalties, we project standardized features onto the Pathological Strain Cone $X_+ = \max(0, (X - \mu_{\text{cal}})/\sigma_{\text{cal}})$ and anchor the Random Fourier Feature embedding at the origin:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\Phi}(X_+) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{M}}\left[\cos(X_+ W + b) - \cos(b)\right]
\end{equation}
Calibrating the finite-sample order statistic $\tau_\alpha$ at rank $k = \lceil (n_{\text{cal}}+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$ guarantees:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{\text{test}} \in \hat{C}(X_{\text{test}})\right) \geq 1 - \alpha
\end{equation}
