H.R. 9721 fundamentally alters the risk calculus for hybrid for-profit/nonprofit structures. The bill explicitly defines a "fiscal sponsorship arrangement" as an agreement between a 501(c)(3) organization and a non-exempt person or entity—targeting Model C pre-approved grant relationships and Model B contractor relationships—while carving out Donor Advised Funds (DAFs) and private foundations and completely nullifying and eliminating the Model L (Single-Member Disregarded LLC) fiscal sponsorship structure. Exact IRS Statutory Tests and Compliance Guardrails

To prevent the IRS from revoking the Institute's tax-exempt status or assessing punitive excise taxes against the founder, the operational boundary between Galaxy Decision Sciences LLC and Galaxy Science Institute, Inc. must strictly satisfy five statutory and regulatory tests.

First, the Institute must satisfy the Scientific Research in the Public Interest Test (Treas. Reg. § 1.501(c)(3)-1(d)(5)). Under Treasury Regulations, an organization qualifies as "scientific" under IRC § 501(c)(3) only if its research is carried on in the public interest. Pursuant to Treas. Reg. § 1.501(c)(3)-1(d)(5)(iii)(a), scientific research is carried on in the public interest if the results of such research—including any patents, copyrights, processes, or formulas resulting from such research—are made available to the public on a nondiscriminatory basis. Conversely, under Treas. Reg. § 1.501(c)(3)-1(d)(5)(iv)(b), an organization is not organized and operated in the public interest if it retains (directly or indirectly) the ownership or control of more than an insubstantial portion of the patents, copyrights, processes, or formulas resulting from its research and does not make them available to the public, or performs research only for its creators which are not exempt under IRC § 501(c)(3). Consequently, every line of code, mathematical proof, and benchmark dataset funded through Galaxy Science Institute must be published contemporaneously in a public GitHub repository under a permissive open-source license (BSD-3-Clause or Apache 2.0, matching the CS&S/OpenRefine compliance standard) and posted to arXiv under a CC-BY 4.0 license. The LLC may use the Institute's open-source packages on the exact same terms as any member of the public, but can never receive an exclusive license, a time-delayed embargo advantage, or proprietary weight files from the Institute.

Second, the entities must enforce a strict firewall against Private Inurement (IRC § 501(c)(3) & Treas. Reg. § 1.501(c)(3)-1(c)(2)) and Excess Benefit Transactions (IRC § 4958). Because the founder is a "disqualified person" (IRC § 4958(f)(1)) with respect to the Institute, and the founder's LLC is a 35%-controlled entity (IRC § 4958(f)(3)), any payment of money or transfer of property or services from the Institute to the LLC or the founder in excess of Fair Market Value (FMV) triggers Intermediate Sanctions under IRC § 4958: a 25% First-Tier Excise Tax on the disqualified person (IRC § 4958(a)(1)), a 200% Second-Tier Excise Tax if not corrected within the taxable period (IRC § 4958(b)), and a 10% tax on organization managers (up to $20,000) who knowingly participate (IRC § 4958(a)(2)). To prevent any violation, the founder should execute a unilateral or below-market Resource & Compute Sharing Agreement flowing from the LLC to the Institute (whereby the LLC donates or provides GPU cluster hours to the Institute at $0.00 cost), and prohibit any cash payments, consulting fees, or subcontracts flowing from the Institute to the LLC unless approved in advance solely by the two independent directors using documented third-party comparability data under the Rebuttable Presumption of Reasonableness (Treas. Reg. § 53.4958-6).

Third, the Institute must comply with the Private Benefit Doctrine (Treas. Reg. § 1.501(c)(3)-1(d)(1)(ii) & American Campaign Academy v. Commissioner, 92 T.C. 1053 (1989)). Even in the absence of financial inurement to insiders, a 501(c)(3) violates the operational test if its activities confer a substantial non-incidental private benefit on a particular commercial enterprise. If the Institute's research agenda consists solely of debugging features for the LLC's commercial product, it fails the Private Benefit test. Accordingly, the Institute's research agenda must be approved by its independent Board and Scientific Advisory Committee and must test its algorithms across multiple public-interest domains, including clinical trial survival monitoring, environmental NO2 and grid load forecasting, and public benchmark kinematics.

Fourth, if the founder utilizes a temporary Model C fiscal sponsor (such as Players Philanthropy Fund or Realize Impact) prior to receiving an IRS determination letter, the arrangement must satisfy the Model C "Discretion and Control" Mandate (IRS Revenue Ruling 68-489, Revenue Ruling 66-79, and H.R. 9721). Under Revenue Ruling 68-489, a 501(c)(3) may distribute funds to a non-exempt organization only if the 501(c)(3) retains full control and discretion as to the use of the funds and maintains records establishing that the funds were used exclusively for 501(c)(3) purposes. Under Adler & Colvin and NEO Law Group compliance protocols, the fiscal sponsor cannot act as a mere conduit: all donation checks and grant wires must be written directly to the fiscal sponsor, project staff must never be given direct check-signing authority on the sponsor's bank accounts, all fundraising materials must explicitly disclose the Model C sponsored status, and the grantee must submit periodic written financial and narrative reports verifying that grant funds were spent strictly within the project's charitable purpose.

Fifth, the Institute should structure its revenue-generating contracts under the Unrelated Business Taxable Income (UBTI) Exclusion for Fundamental Research (IRC §§ 511–514 & IRC § 512(b)(7)–(9)). Under IRC § 512(b)(7)–(9), all income derived from research performed for the United States or any federal agency, or by any organization operated primarily for the purpose of carrying on fundamental research the results of which are freely available to the general public, is statutorily excluded from UBTI.

6. The Narrative That Reframes the Testbed Without Hiding It

Framing sports competition and prediction markets through the familiar trope that "DeepMind used Atari and Go as testbeds for artificial general intelligence" fails to persuade federal program managers or statistical peer reviewers in 2026 because board games are deterministic, stationary, finite-action environments with exact simulator rules. Professional sports competition coupled with continuous event markets is scientifically valuable for the exact opposite reason: it is a non-stationary, partially observable, continuous-time multi-agent physical system coupled to a real-time decentralized belief-aggregation market.

To present the testbed truthfully without hiding the data source—and without sounding like a retail wagering syndicate—all external communications should be anchored in three established academic and federal research traditions.
