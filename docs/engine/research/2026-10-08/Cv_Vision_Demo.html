How the machine sees the game

The GSE computer-vision pipeline, stage by stage: what the camera captures, how pixels become tracklets, how tracklets become speed, routes and separation — and how those become film features the engine learns from.

demo uses simulated data pipeline code is real — merged to main film signals: uncalibrated · weight 0 · shadow only

01The pipeline

One frame's journey from broadcast pixels to a row in the film shadow ledger. Every stage below maps to a real module in packages/prediction-engine.

STAGE 1

Capture

watcher · OpenCV

The Windows watcher grabs broadcast frames on a schedule armed around game windows. Each frame is timestamped and queued with its game context.

inLive TV signal → frame grabber
outTimestamped JPEG frames + game id
STAGE 2

Detect

tracking/cv-detector-contract · yolo-adapter

A YOLO detector finds every player in each frame and draws a bounding box. Output is just boxes + confidence — no identities yet. The detector is a swappable boundary: any trained detector can drop in behind the same contract.

inOne frame
out~22 detections: box (x, y, w, h) + confidence
STAGE 3

Track

tracking/cv-tracklet-association

Association links this frame's boxes to last frame's boxes (IoU overlap, motion-aware gating). A box followed across frames becomes a tracklet — e.g. T-07 — a persistent ID with a position history. This is the moment "a player" exists to the machine.

inPer-frame detections
outTracklets: id, team side, frame-by-frame positions
STAGE 4

Stabilize

tracking/cv-movement-primitive · cv-camera-compensation

The broadcast camera pans and zooms. Optical flow estimates that camera motion every frame pair and subtracts it, so a player's movement is real movement, not camera movement. Toggle it off in the demo below and watch the speed readings go insane — that's what this stage prevents.

inRaw tracklet pixel positions
outCamera-compensated positions + camera motion field (dx, dy)
STAGE 5

Map to the field

tracking/cv-homography · cv-field-lines · cv-field-landmarks

Yard lines are detected in the frame, a homography is fit, and every pixel position is projected into real-world meters on a goal-line-origin field model. From here on, everything is measured in yards and meters, not pixels.

inCompensated pixel positions
outField positions (xM, yM) per frame per tracklet
STAGE 6

Measure movement

tracking/cv-movement-primitive · deriveMovementMetrics

From the meter-positions, per tracklet: distance traveled, top speed, average speed — plus the full per-frame speed series, which yields sprint curves like time-to-top-speed and directional velocity.

inField positions over time
outdistanceM · topSpeedMs · avgSpeedMs · per-frame speed
STAGE 7

Understand the play

perception/cv-play-segmentation · cv-formation-classify · cv-route-extract · cv-separation-metrics · cv-jersey-ocr

The stream is cut into plays (pre-snap → snap → live → whistle). Pre-snap alignment is classified into a formation. Each receiver's path is matched against 14 route templates (go, slant, out, dig, post, corner, curl, comeback, flat, wheel, screen, drag, seam, hitch) with a confidence score, break point and depth. At the break and catch, separation to the nearest defender is measured in yards. Jersey-number OCR maps tracklets to roster players.

inTracklets + field positions for one play
outRoute name + confidence · break angle° · sep@break yd · sep@catch yd · formation · player id
STAGE 8

Learn (shadow)

film-features/* · film-feature-adapter · film_shadow_ledger

Per-play measurements roll up into film features: a player's route mix, target share by formation, average separation; a team's run/pass tendency by down-and-distance, formation frequencies, play-action rate. These flow into the engine shadow-only, weight 0 — logged to the shadow ledger for calibration, never touching a published number until validated.

inPer-play perception outputs
outPlayer/team film features → shadow ledger rows (weight 0)

03Every metric it pulls

The complete inventory, straight from the code — real field names, real units, grouped by the stage that produces them. This is the full answer to "what metrics are involved."

Movement — per tracklet, every play

From deriveMovementMetrics + the per-frame speed series. The raw material for everything speed-related.

m
distanceM

Total ground covered by the player during the play. Sprint distance, route distance, pursuit distance — all the same measurement.

m/s
topSpeedMs

The single fastest frame-to-frame speed — the player's sprint speed on that play.

m/s
avgSpeedMs

Mean speed across the play. Separates the burners from the guys who jog between bursts.

m/s @ 60fps
Per-frame speed series

Speed at every frame — the full sprint curve. From this: time from snap to top speed, acceleration phases, and the dip-and-reburst at a route break.

vector
Directional velocity

Speed plus heading per frame: how fast, and exactly which direction. This is what the arrows in the demo are.

px
cameraMotion dx/dy

How far the broadcast camera itself moved between frames — estimated by optical flow, then subtracted. A measurement of the measurement.

Routes — per receiver, per play

From classifyRoute: the receiver's path matched against 14 route templates.

14 classes
Route name

go · slant · out · dig · post · corner · curl · comeback · flat · wheel · screen · drag · seam · hitch — or unknown when nothing fits.

0–1
confidence

Margin between the best and second-best template match. Low confidence means the route was improvised or messy.

yards
depthYards

How far downfield the route went — the number behind "he's their deep threat" vs "he lives underneath."

field point
breakPoint

The exact spot of maximum curvature — where the cut happened. Null on straight routes (go/seam have no break).

seconds
releaseT

When the receiver left the line relative to the snap — slow releases vs instant burners.

Separation — per route, per play

From cv-separation-metrics: the man-coverage truth serum. Nearest defender = closest opposing tracklet by field position.

degrees
breakAngleDeg

Angle between the incoming stem and the outgoing break — e.g. ~90° on a dig. Sharpness of the cut, measured, not eyeballed.

yards
sepAtBreakYd

Distance to the nearest defender at the break — did the cut actually create space?

yards
sepAtCatchYd

Distance to the nearest defender at the catch point — the contested-catch meter.

tracklet id
nearestDefenderAtBreak

Which defender was closest at the break — feeds matchup analysis (who got cooked, by whom).

Player film features — per player, rolling

From extractPlayerFilmFeatures: per-play measurements aggregated into a player profile. Needs the tracklet→player identity map — without it, these are skipped, never guessed.

shares
routeMix

Route name → share of the player's classified routes. His actual route tree, from film.

shares
targetShareByFormation

Where he lines up vs how often he's involved — formation tells you the plan.

yards
avgSepAtBreakYd / avgSepAtCatchYd

Does he separate consistently, or was one play a fluke?

deg / yards
avgBreakAngleDeg / avgDepthYards

How sharp his cuts are on average, how deep his average route goes.

share
redZoneRouteShare

Share of his routes run with the ball inside the opponent's 20 — red-zone role, measured.

Team film features + tendencies — per team, rolling

From extractTeamFilmFeatures and cv-tendencies: what the film says about the scheme.

matrix
Down × distance run/pass

Full 4-downs × short/medium/long run-pass tendency matrix. "They pass 78% on 2nd-and-long" — from film, not vibes.

shares
formationFreq

Personnel + distribution → share of plays. How often they live in 11 personnel, bunch, empty…

yards
yardsPerPlayByFormation

Which formations actually produce — scheme efficiency, measured per formation family.

rates
playActionRate / screenRate / hurryUpRate

How often they use play-action, screens, and tempo — the tendency fingerprints.

ranked list
routeCombinationFrequency

Most common route combos from each formation family — what the defense is actually defending.

label
Formation classification

Pre-snap alignment → personnel + distribution + play-action/screen/hurry-up flags, from cv-formation-classify.

04One play, end to end

Trace a single snap through the whole machine — the same dig route from the demo above.

Capture
Watcher grabs the broadcast at 60fps. Frame 1,142 lands with timestamp t=0.80s — the snap.
Detect
YOLO returns 22 boxes. One of them: (x 612, y 388, w 26, h 40), conf 0.93 — a player, no name yet.
Track
IoU association links it to the same box 59 frames running. It becomes tracklet T-07, offense side.
Stabilize
Camera panned 34px following the play. Optical flow measured it; every T-07 position is shifted back by the camera field. Without this, his speed would read 11.2 m/s. With it: 8.6.
Map
Yard lines detected, homography fit. T-07's pixels become (36.6m, 11.0m) on the goal-line-origin field.
Measure
distanceM 20.2 · topSpeedMs 8.6 · avgSpeedMs ~6.5 — plus the per-frame curve showing 0.75s from release to top speed and the gather-dip at the break.
Understand
Segmented as one play, snap→whistle. Formation: 11 personnel, 2x2. T-07's path matches the dig template at 0.87 confidence, depth 7.5 yd, break at (43.4m, 11.0m). Break angle 90.0°. Separation at break 2.3 yd (nearest: T-24). Separation at catch 1.1 yd. Jersey OCR reads #11 → roster identity attached.
Learn
Rolled into the player's film profile (dig share up, avg separation updated) and the team's tendency matrix. Written to film_shadow_ledger as a weight-0 shadow row — visible to calibration, invisible to every published number.

Honest status — what this page is and isn't

  • The pipeline stages, module names and metric definitions are real — pulled from the merged code in packages/prediction-engine/src/{tracking,perception,film-features} today.
  • The animation is simulated — synthetic players on a synthetic field. It demonstrates the pipeline's logic (the speed math, break-angle math and separation math mirror the real functions); it is not real game footage and not real measurements.
  • Detector boundary: the repo holds the detector contract, not YOLO weights — a trained detector drops in behind it.
  • Identity gap: player-level film features need the tracklet→player map (jersey OCR + roster join). Until that lands, they are skipped, not guessed.
  • Calibration: every film signal is UNCALIBRATED, weight 0, shadow-only until the validation spec (PR #1003) is satisfied with real graded film. Nothing on this page touches a published projection.
  • Pipeline tests: 196/196 green on synthetic fixtures. Real-film validation is the next mountain.
