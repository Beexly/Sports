## Per-family leverage

Sorted by paper count. `impact score` is a crude 0-6 strength score over the paper's own stated impact/priority/accuracy wording (quantified claim +3, priority/production/edge wording +2, explicit GSE action +1). It is the paper's claim, not a measured result.

**Read the impact score with care.** Three caveats, all observed in this run:

1. It is **sentence-level vocabulary matching, not an assessment of leverage.** A paper that writes "improves accuracy" scores the same as one that writes "improves accuracy by 0.02 log-loss over 8 seasons of NFL data, worth wiring into the spread model first."
2. When the GSE-application section yields no matching sentence the extractor **falls back to the verdict line**, which is usually an ADAPT/REJECT restatement. Top-of-table entries beginning `ADAPT —` or `REJECT —` are verdict text, not section prose, and a high score does **not** mean the paper is valuable — one score-5 entry is a REJECT.
3. The distribution is bottom-heavy (most papers score 0 or 2), so the score mostly separates "stated a claim" from "stated nothing", not strong from weak. Use the family counts for ranking; treat the score as a pointer and open the paper before believing it.

The index itself reports 585/750 verified-valuable for the 2026-09-26 program state; the count here is this extraction's own and will differ where notes are absent from the index or carry no verdict.
- Impact quotes are verbatim slices of the note's prose. They are the note author's claim about a paper, not a verified result.
- No paper text was read beyond the GSE-application section, the heading and the verdict line.
