Tell me about yourself
I am system -minded, high-context, and intensely driven toward clarity and growth.
Professionally, I am steady. Structure regulates me. Defined standards, explicit expectations, and role clarity allow me to operate at a high level almost autonomously. I think in patterns. I see incentives, second-order effects, tone shifts, and themes quickly. I often see people before they see themselves.
Personally, I am wired intensely. We discussed that I am on the spectrum, (which only a handful of friends even know). For me, that does not look like social incompetence - it looks like internal overload when ambiguity replaces structure.
I read micro-expressions, emotional tone, and subtle dynamics rapidly … but at the same time, I can hyper-analyze myself inside those moments. That internal loop can be exhausting. When expectations are unclear, my nervous system spikes. That spike can become anxiety; anxiety, if prolonged, can become panic; and panic, if sustained, can collapse into depression.
I grew up without stability and have been on my own since I was 15. That built resilience, independence, and work ethic - but it also wired me to anticipate loss. Even after significant therapy and growth, there is still a part of me that expects position, security, or belonging to disappear.
I operate at a high intensity.
When that intensity exists without clear direction, it can tip into overload.
When it’s anchored in clarity and defined expectations, it becomes focus and performance.
I take responsibility for managing my nervous system. I have therapy, tools, faith, and structure that support me. What I’m sharing isn’t justification - it’s context for how to position me where I operate at my highest level.
What are my greatest strengths to a team?
Pattern recognition and systems thinking.
Calm, decisive thinking under pressure.
High integrity, even when it costs me.
Deep empathy - I genuinely want people to feel seen and respected.
Relentless drive to improve myself and those around me.
A bility to turn chaos into process.
How do I learn new information?
Give me the architecture first - the “why,” the constraints, the standards, and what success looks like. Once I have context, I move fast. Ambiguity slows me. Clarity accelerates me.
Describe your communication styl e
How do you like people to interact with you?
Be explicit.
Be consistent.
Be direct.
I respond exceptionally well to rigorous coaching. Challenge me. Correct me. Raise the bar. I grow quickly when standards are clear and stable.
What destabilizes me is not correction - its ambiguity compounded over time. When expectations shift without recalibration, I can begin to internalize adjustment as identity. In other words, I start to interpret feedback about performance as commentary on who I am, rather than what needs refinement.
Clear benchmarks prevent that. Defined standards allow me to separate behavior from identity and improve without unnecessary internal friction
How do you prefer to interact with others?
I try to balance warmth with high standards. My tone may not always be perfect, but my intent is rarely malicious. There is not a person I’ve met who cares more deeply about whether others feel respected and understood. Nothing affects me more than realizing I made someone feel unseen.
Are you a low context or high context communicator? (how much history or information do you need)
High context - across the board. Once I understand expectations, rules, authority structures, and boundaries, I thrive. That becomes my playbook. Ambiguity is my primary destabilizer.
Describe your comfort with using information and data:
Data, defined standards, and measurable expectations calm me. I operate well in complexity if assumptions and decision rights are explicit.
Describe your conflict style
Externally calm. Internally intense. I narrow and become precise. If I escalate, it is because I see a pattern. If I withdraw, it is usually because something feels unstable and I am regulating before responding.
How do you prefer to get your point across?
By defining the problem, clarifying stakes, presenting options, and making principled recommendations .
How do we know if you are frustrated/angry/hurt?
Frustrated: I become more transactional and focused on facts and deadlines.
Angry: I get quieter and more exact. My tone cools. I focus on boundaries.
Hurt: I withdraw relational energy but remain professional.
How do you prefer someone approaches you with constructive feedback?
Directly. Privately. Specifically.
Name the behavior. Name the impact. Define the standard moving forward.
How do team members get “un-sideways” with you? (bounce back from conflict):
Talk to me like a human. Name what happened. Clarify intent. Define the new standard. If I hurt someone, tell me. I will own it and repair it. Consistency rebuilds trust quickly with me.
How long / short is your fuse?
Long for complexity. Short for unpredictability without explanation. Stacked inconsistency can trigger an uncertainty response.
What are your 2-3 “hot buttons”?
I struggle in environments where standards are fluid rather than principled.
When expectations shift without clear recalibration, or when authority and decision rights are implied rather than defined, it increases cognitive load for me. I operate best when the rules of engagement are stable, consistently applied, and transparent.
Describe your leadership style:
How do you influence others?
By making invisible patterns visible. By leading with integrity when it costs something. By showing up consistently in difficult moments. People follow consistency.
What do I look like and sound like under pressure?
Focused. Minimal. Direct. I prioritize decisions and sequencing. I may reduce warmth unintentionally when intensity increases. That is something I actively monitor.
What is the most impactful leadership decision I have ever made?
Choosing integrity over short-term security at a previous company. Speaking up when it cost me stability forced me to confront both organizational politics and my own nervous system. It matured me as a leader.

Anything else you think the team should know about you?
I’m not interested in comfort. I’m interested in disciplined growth. Lowered expectations don’t serve me. Clear and consistent expectations do.
When standards are principled and stable, I channel intensity into execution. When they are ambiguous, I spend energy regulating uncertainty instead of advancing outcomes. I am loyal to the mission and to the people I trust. I self - audit constantly. I hold myself to a high bar, sometimes higher than necessary.
What makes that sustainable is awareness - the ability to examine my reactions, separate old wiring from present reality, and adjust deliberately rather than defensively. If I understand the standard and trust its consistency, I will rise to it.
