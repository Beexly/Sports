2. How to read the tree
CONFIRMED Supported by direct family knowledge and/or a strong 
record such as an obituary, official memorial, certificate, 
or multiple independent sources.
PROBABLE The names, dates, family cluster, and geography align, 
but one decisive record is still missing.
HYPOTHESIS A useful lead from a family tree, collaborative profile, 
surname match, or indirect source. It is not accepted as 
fact.
CONFLICT The proposed identity or relationship contains 
contradictory dates, names, or family structures and is 
isolated until resolved.
OPEN A missing identity or relationship that should be 
researched before moving further backward.
Proof standard used in this project
1.  Search beyond one website and across independent record systems.
2.  Cite where every claim came from, including family testimony.
3.  Build a complete timeline for each person and test whether geography and ages make sense.
4.  Resolve conflicts rather than selecting the version that produces the longest or most impressive tree.
5.  Write a reasoned conclusion and retain rejected alternatives so the same mistake is not repeated.
Living-person privacy
The dossier uses names and birth years already supplied by the family but excludes addresses, phone 
numbers, and sensitive personal details. A public website should further abbreviate living people.

5. Paternal branch: Daughtrey-Biddy
Figure 3. Daughtrey-Biddy branch, separating the documented twentieth-century core from older working leads.
Documented core. Janet Faynelle Daughtrey was a daughter of Cecil Carroll Daughtrey and Adeline Geneva Biddy. 
Family obituaries establish that Janet belonged to a sibling group of eleven children.
Why the sibling group matters
Large sibling groups create more records: obituaries, marriages, military files, death certificates, and descendants’ 
family Bibles. A missing parent or maiden name may appear in a sibling’s record even when it is absent from the 
direct ancestor’s record.
Older research lines
 Daughtrey: Wilton T. Daughtrey -> William Edward Daughtry -> William Thomas Daughtrey -> James Daughtrey is 
a coherent working path, but spelling and parentage must be proved at every step.
 Biddy: James Henry Biddy -> Isaac James Monroe Biddy -> George Henry Biddy -> James Monroe Biddy -> 
Abednego Biddy -> possibly Edmond Biddy is a substantial lead, but the pre-1900 generations presently rely 
heavily on collaborative trees.

9. Where the family appears to come from
Geographic layers
Texas and Florida: The recent Baxley, Daughtrey, and Biddy story centers heavily in Texas, with Jim Baxley’s long 
residence in Florida.
Wisconsin and the Great Lakes: The Schumacher-Apfel-Alf cluster points toward Wisconsin and a likely German-
speaking immigrant network.
Ohio and Pennsylvania: The Moore, Lawrence, Lance, Halliwell, Bennett, Ingmire, and Julien branches cluster in 
Ohio/Pennsylvania and may connect to colonial-era settlement and German/English church communities.
The American South: Older Daughtrey, Biddy, Baxley, Trevathan, and related leads point through Virginia, North 
Carolina, South Carolina, Louisiana, Alabama, and Texas.
Europe: The strongest current ethnic directions are German and English/British, but the exact overseas ancestors 
require a proven U.S. chain before European records can be trusted.
What ethnicity can and cannot mean here
 A surname is a clue, not a genetic percentage. Schumacher can be German-language, but only records can identify 
the correct family’s origin.
 A family tree describes genealogical ancestry; a DNA ethnicity estimate measures inherited segments and may not 
show every genealogical ancestor.
 At eight generations, a person has up to 256 genealogical ancestor positions, but pedigree collapse means some 
people may occupy more than one position.
 Cultural identity, tribal citizenship, nationality, language, and genetic ancestry are different categories and should 
not be collapsed into one claim.
Current summary
The responsible description today is: predominantly documented American family history with strong 
English/British and probable German ancestry; deep Southern, Midwestern, and colonial roots; Native 
ancestry unconfirmed.

11. Grandma interview and document checklist
Questions to ask
1.  Which people did you personally know, and what did they tell you about their parents?
2.  Which names came from family documents, and which came from Ancestry hints or another person’s tree?
3.  What was Hullinger Baxley’s full name, maiden name, birthplace, and spouse?
4.  What were Glenn Schumacher’s parents’ full names?
5.  What were Lois Apfel’s parents’ full names, and did she die in 1980 or 1990?
6.  Did anyone in the Schumacher, Apfel, Alf, Blässer, Coolman, or Lance families speak German or attend a German-
language church?
7.  What record or relative established Emanuel Moore and Elizabeth Jane Lance as James Buchanan Moore’s parents?
8.  Who was the man entered as Henry Lance 1, and why is he connected to two Lance branches?
9.  What was Catherine Ethel Ingmire’s complete name, and where did the Ingmire and Julien families live?
10.  Which relatives have family Bibles, funeral cards, certificates, military papers, letters, or labeled photographs?
11.  Which cemetery, church, county, or town is associated with each grandparent and great-grandparent?
12.  Who is the oldest living person on each branch who can still be interviewed?
Documents to photograph or scan
 Birth, marriage, divorce, and death certificates
 Obituaries and funeral programs
 Family Bible pages and handwritten family sheets
 Fronts and backs of labeled photographs
 Military discharge papers, draft cards, and service photographs
 Naturalization papers, passports, and passenger records
 Deeds, wills, probate papers, and land grants
 Church baptism, confirmation, marriage, and burial records
 School yearbooks, city directories, and address books
 Letters, diaries, recipes, newspaper clippings, and oral-history recordings
Capture provenance
For every item, record who owns it, who created it, approximately when, and how the family knows the 
people named or pictured. A document without provenance can be difficult to interpret later.
