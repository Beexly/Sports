# LUMERA BRAND OPERATING SYSTEM
## Version 1.0 — Signal Commerce System

**Core position:** Lumera is the live signal layer for rare commerce.

**Core line:** The signal found you.

**Strategic standard:** Lumera should not feel like a premium marketplace with nice colors. It should feel like a private broadcast field where rare goods surface, move, close, and disappear.

---

## 0. Why this exists

The previous Lumera brand direction was clean, restrained, and premium, but it was not ownable enough. It leaned on generic luxury cues: black, muted gold, lowercase wordmark, a simple ring mark, and soft abstract copy. Those choices can create trust, but they do not create cultural magnetism.

This brand operating system exists to prevent drift back into generic taste. It gives builders, designers, writers, and agents a clear system for how Lumera should look, speak, move, and sell.

The model is not to copy any reference brand visually. The model is to copy the discipline of mature brand systems: attributes first, usage rules second, application examples third, product behavior last. Lumera must have a brand kit that governs decisions, not a moodboard that decorates them.

---

## 1. Brand thesis

Lumera is the live signal layer for rare commerce.

Not a store.
Not a luxury catalog.
Not another AI shopping assistant.
Not fake urgency.
Not drop-culture noise.

Lumera is a personalized broadcast field where rare goods surface, move, close, and disappear.

The customer is not simply browsing. They are tuning into a signal.

The product is not simply listed. It has surfaced.

The account is not simply administrative. It is a signal profile.

The cart is not simply a cart. It is a hold.

Search is not simply search. It tunes the field.

A sold-out product is not a dead item. It is an archived broadcast.

## 2. Brand attributes

All five attributes matter. In each context, one or two should lead.

### 2.1 Signal-led

**Meaning:** Lumera feels live, responsive, detected, and intentionally surfaced.

**What it creates:** Urgency without manipulation. Movement without chaos. A reason to return.

**Copy rules:**
- Use words like signal, surface, tune, broadcast, live, match, window, hold, archive.
- Avoid generic words like browse, shop now, discover more, curated, elevated.

**Design rules:**
- Use signal fields, orbital paths, radial lines, soft pulses, and live state badges.
- Avoid decorative motion that does not communicate product state.

**Experience rules:**
- Give customers a sense that something is happening now.
- Show real states: live, closing, held, archived, matched.

---

### 2.2 Rare

**Meaning:** Lumera handles scarcity with restraint and honesty.

**What it creates:** Desire without cheap pressure.

**Copy rules:**
- Use “Not guaranteed to return” only when true.
- Use “Signal closing” only when there is a real window.
- Use “Low window” only when time or inventory is actually constrained.

**Design rules:**
- Rarity should feel calm, not flashing-red panic.
- Use sparse accents, fine borders, controlled contrast, and restrained badge language.

**Experience rules:**
- Never fake timers.
- Never fake inventory.
- Never reset urgency.
- Always distinguish sold out, archived, hidden, and coming soon.

---

### 2.3 Intelligent

**Meaning:** Lumera explains why something appeared.

**What it creates:** Trust, agency, and repeat behavior.

**Copy rules:**
- Use “Why this surfaced.”
- Explain matches plainly.
- Keep reasoning short and concrete.

**Design rules:**
- Product cards should expose signal reason without clutter.
- Account should make preferences visible and editable.

**Experience rules:**
- Let users shape their signal.
- Do not make AI feel magical in a dishonest way.
- Intelligence should feel useful, not performative.

---

### 2.4 Cinematic

**Meaning:** Lumera has atmosphere, depth, and memory.

**What it creates:** Screenshot-worthiness and emotional distinction.

**Copy rules:**
- Use short lines that feel like a transmission.
- Avoid fluffy luxury language.

**Design rules:**
- Use eclipse, light rupture, orbital motion, spatial layering, and product signals.
- Let the first viewport feel like entering a system, not reading a hero banner.

**Experience rules:**
- Create one unmistakable signature interaction.
- Motion should clarify state, not distract.

---

### 2.5 Trustworthy

**Meaning:** Lumera is commercially clear, accessible, and honest.

**What it creates:** Conversion and long-term credibility.

**Copy rules:**
- Use plain language in cart, checkout, returns, shipping, and account.
- Keep trust details visible.

**Design rules:**
- Search, account, cart, product details, checkout, and support must be obvious.
- Contrast and readability outrank atmosphere.

**Experience rules:**
- No hidden costs.
- No unclear checkout states.
- No ambiguity around what can be bought, held, returned, or archived.

## 4. Voice system

### 4.1 Primary voice

Lumera speaks with calm intensity.

It is precise, short, sensory, and useful.

It should feel like a private system coming online, not a brand trying to sound expensive.

### 4.2 Core line

**The signal found you.**

This is the central brand line. It should be used strategically, not everywhere.

Use it for:
- Homepage hero
- Social share card
- Founder deck opener
- Launch campaign
- High-impact drop moments

Do not use it on every button, every product card, or every email.

### 4.3 Approved phrases

- The signal found you.
- Enter the broadcast.
- Tune your signal.
- View live drops.
- Live signal open.
- Signal closing.
- Hold available.
- Matched to you.
- Why this surfaced.
- The window is open.
- The window closed.
- Archived broadcast.
- Not guaranteed to return.
- Rare goods. Live signal.
- Personalized drops, broadcast live.
- Everything worth catching moves once.

### 4.4 Avoided phrases

- Elevated marketplace.
- Curated luxury.
- Premium experience.
- Everything worth having.
- Discover more.
- Shop now, unless in checkout-critical contexts.
- Exclusive access, unless access is actually restricted.
- Limited time only, unless the limit is real.
- Hurry, act fast, don’t miss out.
- AI-powered shopping magic.
- Reimagining commerce.

### 4.5 CTA rules

Primary CTAs should use action language from the product world.

Best:
- Enter the broadcast
- Tune your signal
- View live drops
- Hold it
- Open product
- Create signal profile

Use normal commerce CTAs where clarity matters:
- Add to cart
- Checkout
- Sign in
- View order
- Return policy

Do not sacrifice clarity at checkout for brand cleverness.

## 5. Logo system

### 5.1 Mark concept: Signal Eclipse

The Lumera mark must not be a perfect decorative ring. It should be a proprietary signal eclipse.

Required qualities:
- Circular enough to imply eclipse/lens/orbit.
- Broken or asymmetric enough to become ownable.
- One first-light rupture or signal flare.
- Works as mark-only at favicon scale.
- Works as a hero-scale object.
- Can animate from dormant to active.

Possible geometry:
- Ring with asymmetric rupture at 1–2 o’clock.
- First-light point escaping the ring.
- Hidden “L” path inside the negative space.
- One missing broadcast segment.
- Slight aperture logic, not camera cliché.

### 5.2 Lockups

Lumera needs three lockups:

1. **Full lockup:** Signal Eclipse + LUMERA wordmark.
   - Use on external materials, OG cards, pitch decks, and first impressions.

2. **Wordmark lockup:** LUMERA only.
   - Use when the mark is already present in the same layout.

3. **Mark-only:** Signal Eclipse only.
   - Use for favicon, app icon, loading state, product signal badges, and established brand environments.

### 5.3 Clear space

- Mark-only clear space: minimum 25% of mark diameter on all sides.
- Full lockup clear space: minimum 50% of wordmark height on all sides.
- Never crop the mark unless intentionally used as a large background graphic.

### 5.4 Incorrect usage

Do not:
- Use a perfect plain ring with a dot as the final mark.
- Add drop shadows or glow to the core logo lockup.
- Stretch, rotate, or warp the mark.
- Place the logo over complex imagery without contrast control.
- Use neon effects on the wordmark.
- Apply gradients directly to the wordmark.
- Use the mark as random decoration unrelated to signal state.

## 6. Color system

### 6.1 Strategic color role

Color should support signal, rarity, and trust. It should not look like generic black-and-gold luxury.

### 6.2 Core palette

**Eclipse Black**
- Role: primary background.
- Feeling: depth, restraint, cinematic base.

**First Light**
- Role: primary text on dark.
- Feeling: clarity, contrast, warmth.

**Signal Green or Signal Cyan**
- Role: live state, active field, search/tuning cue.
- Feeling: detection, presence, system activity.

**Rare Violet**
- Role: rare drops, matched products, high-emotion moments.
- Feeling: mystery, cultural value, depth.

**Archive Grey**
- Role: closed windows, disabled states, past broadcasts.
- Feeling: still accessible, no longer active.

**Hold Amber**
- Role: cart hold, limited reservation, caution without panic.
- Feeling: warm urgency, not alarm.

### 6.3 Rules

- The live accent should be distinctive but not cheap neon.
- Rarity and urgency should not use the same color.
- Checkout and error states should follow conventional UX clarity.
- Contrast must pass accessibility standards.
- Product imagery should carry color when possible; UI should not become a carnival.

## 7. Graphic devices

Lumera needs repeatable visual devices that can be recognized without the logo.

### 7.1 Broadcast Field

A spatial grid or radial field that suggests live detection.

Use on:
- Homepage hero
- Search background
- OG card
- Drop pages
- Launch deck

Avoid:
- Generic starfield
- Crypto grid
- Overloaded sci-fi HUD

### 7.2 Eclipse Rupture

The signature first-light break in the mark.

Use on:
- Hero animation
- Loading states
- Social graphics
- Product signal badges

Avoid:
- Random rings everywhere
- Decorative circles with no state meaning

### 7.3 Signal Coordinates

Fine-line coordinates or product-location markers.

Use on:
- Product card hover
- Drop map
- Search results
- Share cards

Avoid:
- Making the interface look like a flight dashboard or military tool.

### 7.4 Archive Static

A quiet visual language for closed broadcasts.

Use on:
- Sold-out products
- Past drops
- Archived pages

Avoid:
- Making sold-out pages feel broken or abandoned.

## 8. Product UI system

### 8.1 Product states

Every product should have a state. States are not decorative labels. They tell the customer what is happening.

Approved states:

**LIVE SIGNAL**
- Product/drop is active now.

**MATCHED TO YOU**
- Product is shown based on user preference or behavior.

**LOW WINDOW**
- Real time or inventory window is low.

**HOLD AVAILABLE**
- Product can be reserved or cart-held.

**SIGNAL CLOSING**
- Real close event is approaching.

**ARCHIVED**
- Product/drop is no longer active.

**RETURNING UNKNOWN**
- No restock confirmed.

### 8.2 Product card anatomy

Each card should include:
- Product image.
- Product name.
- Price.
- Signal state.
- Match reason if available.
- Availability/hold clarity.
- Clear path to product page.

Optional:
- Drop window.
- Inventory threshold, only if real.
- Archive status.

### 8.3 “Why this surfaced”

Use this to make personalization trustworthy.

Examples:
- Matched from your saved material preferences.
- Surfaced from today’s live broadcast.
- Similar to a product you held.
- Low-volume release. No restock confirmed.
- Matched to your size and category preferences.

Do not use vague lines like:
- Chosen just for you.
- Our AI thinks you’ll love this.
- Curated by Lumera.

## 9. Page system

### 9.1 Homepage

Lead attributes: Cinematic + Signal-led.

Goal: make the first viewport unmistakable.

Required elements:
- Living broadcast field.
- Signal Eclipse mark as active system object.
- Hero line: The signal found you.
- Clear CTAs: Enter the broadcast, Tune your signal, View live drops.
- Visible commerce cues: search, account, cart.
- Live/drop preview section above or near fold.

### 9.2 Search

Lead attributes: Intelligent + Trustworthy.

Search should feel like tuning the field.

Required elements:
- Clear input.
- Useful filters.
- Empty state that teaches.
- Product results with signal states.

Empty state copy:
- Tune the field.
- Try “outerwear,” “limited run,” “black leather,” or “archived drops.”

### 9.3 Product page

Lead attributes: Rare + Trustworthy.

Required elements:
- Product story.
- Product details.
- Price.
- Availability.
- Signal state.
- Why this surfaced, if applicable.
- Shipping/returns clarity.
- Add to cart / hold CTA.

### 9.4 Account

Lead attributes: Intelligent + Trustworthy.

Account should be called **Signal Profile** in branded contexts.

Required sections:
- Active holds.
- Order history.
- Saved preferences.
- Drop alerts.
- Past broadcasts.
- Profile settings.

### 9.5 Cart

Lead attributes: Trustworthy + Rare.

Cart can say hold, but must remain clear.

Required elements:
- Items.
- Price.
- Taxes/shipping clarity when available.
- Hold window only if real.
- Checkout CTA.

### 9.6 Checkout

Lead attribute: Trustworthy.

Checkout should be plain. This is not where Lumera gets clever.

Required elements:
- Clear steps.
- Payment clarity.
- Address clarity.
- Return/support links.
- Error messages in normal language.

## 12. Motion system

Motion must communicate state.

Approved motion:
- Signal pulse when a product is live.
- Eclipse rupture animation on load.
- Product card field response on hover/focus.
- Search field tuning transition.
- Cart hold countdown, only if real.

Avoid:
- Constant ambient noise.
- Heavy parallax that hurts usability.
- Motion that makes product details harder to read.
- Uncontrolled glow.

Accessibility:
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
- Essential state must also be shown without motion.

## 13. Builder rules

Every new Lumera surface must pass these checks:

1. Is it clearly Lumera without needing the logo?
2. Is it still obviously shoppable?
3. Is the scarcity honest?
4. Does the customer know what action to take?
5. Does the design carry one of the five attributes intentionally?
6. Does the copy avoid generic premium language?
7. Would someone screenshot this?
8. Would someone trust this with money?

If the answer to 7 is yes but 8 is no, it is art, not commerce.

If the answer to 8 is yes but 7 is no, it is functional but forgettable.

Lumera needs both.

## 15. Launch-readiness standard

Lumera is not launch-ready because it is beautiful.

Lumera is launch-ready when:
- A stranger immediately understands what to do.
- A buyer trusts checkout.
- A product state is never misleading.
- A returning user has a reason to come back.
- A screenshot makes the brand look distinct.
- The share card creates curiosity.
- The site still works without animation.
- Cloud config, Stripe, catalog, email, domain, and keys are live.

Brand can create attention.
Operations keep the promise.
