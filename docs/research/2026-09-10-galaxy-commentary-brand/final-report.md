# Galaxy Sports Edge — NFL commentary brand: evidence-grade research report
**Account:** @GalaxySportsHQ ("Galaxy Sports Edge") | **Researched:** September 10, 2026
**Standard:** Every finding carries an exact source URL. Operative policy language is quoted verbatim where it matters. "Paper policy" (what written terms say) is separated from "observed practice" (what enforcement actually looks like). Unconfirmable items are marked **UNVERIFIED**.

**Scope note:** This research was done entirely via public web search and page fetch. It is not legal advice. The account's stated posture is transformative, commentary-led, seconds-long telestrated edits — never standalone rips or compilations.

## TOPIC 1 — How 3–5 top NFL film-breakdown creators actually operate

### 1.1 The NFL Access Pass changes the reading of every "surviving" creator
**Paper policy + observed roster.**

- In 2023 the NFL opened Access Pass to a "select group" of creators, granting access to "officially approved footage" with monetization allowed on YouTube; NFL executive Ian Trombetta noted "tight parameters" on what footage is approved.
  - https://digiday.com/marketing/the-nfl-gives-creators-access-to-its-archives-for-content-creation-with-pre-approved-footage/
  - https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Articles/2023/09/14/nfl-content-creators/
- As of January 30, 2025, the NFL/YouTube Access Pass roster expressly included **Brett Kollmann**, while **J.T. O'Sullivan** and **Kurt Benkert** were named for Access Pass for Legends. Year 1 covered 24 creators and 200M+ views.
  - https://www.sportsvideo.org/2025/01/30/nfl-and-youtube-debut-access-pass-for-legends-opening-doors-for-new-wave-of-athlete-media/
- The Legends program lets former players use official highlights, Super Bowl, NFL Draft, and other footage while retaining a share of advertising revenue.
  - https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/nfl-lets-former-players-tap-its-video-archive-for-youtube-posts

**Key conclusion:** Kollmann's, O'Sullivan's, and Benkert's current footage is at least partly **expressly licensed**. Their survival is not proof that an unaffiliated one-follower account will face the same enforcement.
**How we exploit this:** Copy their editorial structure (segmenting, telestration, slow-mo loops), not their apparent footage tolerance. Track and apply for Access Pass when eligible.

### 1.2 Concrete workflow and cadence: Tim Jenkins / All Things QB
**Observed practice (Front Office Sports, 2026).**

- Long-form videos frequently run **30+ minutes**; creators "loop through the same play at different angles and use a telestrator."
- Jenkins posts **two public videos weekly** and **two members-only clips**; a 30-minute episode takes roughly **2–3 hours**, mostly identifying plays.
- Toolchain: **Hudl for telestration**, **OBS Studio to record his screen**, light editing after.
- The same report expressly notes creators outside Access Pass are "taking a risk."
  - https://frontofficesports.com/newsletter/from-1-nfl-pass-to-youtube-hit/

**How we exploit this:** Standardize a repeatable screen-record → telestrate → loop-angles workflow (free: OBS + any telestration tool; Hudl is the paid reference). Keep a footage-light public version; paywalling does not cure copyright risk.
**UNVERIFIED:** whether Jenkins is licensed, his exact footage source, per-play seconds, and any claims history.

### 1.3 How analysts historically sourced All-22
**Observed practice, not permission.** Defector reported Game Pass historically offered broadcasts and All-22; analysts described accessing international Game Pass, QuickTime screen-recording All-22, and sharing files privately.
- https://defector.com/why-does-nfl-game-pass-barely-function-now

**How we exploit this:** Use legitimate viewing access (e.g., NFL+ NFL Pro's All-22 offering — see 3.6) to *study* plays, then rebuild the teaching point as original diagrams whenever footage rights are uncertain.

### 1.4 Enforcement history: even 20 seconds can be challenged
**Observed enforcement.**

- In 2007 the NFL objected to **20 seconds of game footage** in law professor Wendy Seltzer's copyright-warning video; NFL counsel: "We do not see how the use of such game content has any educational purposes or character." The NFL lifted its objection to the warning but maintained its objection to the game footage.
  - https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Daily/Issues/2007/04/12/Sports-Media/NFL-Lifts-Objections-To-Portion-Of-Copyright-Youtube-Clip
  - https://deadspin.Com/the-nfl-chases-down-itself-245527/
- A legal article reports the NFL requested removal of **more than 3,000 YouTube clips in 2006**.
  - https://gould.usc.edu/why/students/orgs/ilj/assets/docs/17-1%20Mellow.pdf

**How we exploit this:** No safe "under X seconds" rule exists. Make every second necessary to active criticism — pause, annotate, narrate; avoid uninterrupted highlight value.

### 1.5 The field's closest thing to a charter: fair use for illustration in online video
**Credible guidance, not a safe harbor.** The Center for Media & Social Impact's Code of Best Practices in Fair Use for Online Video, Principle TWO — "Using copyrighted material for illustration or example":
- **Principle:** "This sort of quotation generally should be considered fair use... It is fair when it is important to the larger purpose of the work but also subordinate to it. It is fair when video makers are not presenting the quoted material for its original purpose but to harness it for a new one. This kind of use is, thus, creating new value."
- **Limitations:** illustrative quotations should "be drawn from a range of different sources" where possible; "each quotation... should be no longer than is necessary to achieve the intended effect. Properly attributing material... will often reduce the likelihood of complaints or legal action and may bolster a maker's fair use claim."
- https://cmsimpact.org/code/code-best-practices-fair-use-online-video/

**How we exploit this:** This is the argumentative frame for telestrated breakdowns — the footage is subordinate to the analysis, used for a new purpose (teaching), kept to the minimum needed, and attributed.

### 1.6 Reconstruction/animation alternatives (substitution evidence)
- **Kurt Benkert** produces Madden-based teaching content as an alternative to game footage — a documented real-creator recreation model.
  - https://frontofficesports.com/newsletter/from-1-nfl-pass-to-youtube-hit/
- **Samuel Gold** published film-room articles with named concepts and original play diagrams (e.g., on Field Gulls), showing the diagram-first teaching model.
  - https://www.fieldgulls.com/2016/12/14/13950780/18887897777

**How we exploit this:** When footage rights are uncertain, rebuild the teaching point with Madden captures or original overhead dot/route diagrams. **UNVERIFIED:** whether any named creator's channel uses animated dots/diagrams as a *systematic* footage substitute; build that ourselves.

## TOPIC 2 — Beat reporters/outlets: reuse permissions; facts, quotations, X reposts

### 2.1 Facts and game results are not protected expression
**Court authority / paper law.** *National Basketball Association v. Motorola, Inc.*, 105 F.3d 841 (2d Cir. 1997):
- "Motorola and STATS did not infringe NBA's copyright because they reproduced only facts from the broadcasts, not the expression or description of the game that constitutes the broadcast."
- "No author may copyright facts or ideas. The copyright is limited to those aspects of the work—termed 'expression'—that display the stamp of the author's originality."
  - https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/5914bc7cadd7b0493479cc0f
  - https://www.legalsportsreport.com/22073/sports-betting-data-fees-case-6/

**How we exploit this:** Independently write posts from scores, down/distance, formations, play outcomes, and statistics. Do not copy a reporter's phrasing, graphic design, database, or footage just because the underlying facts are free.

### 2.2 PFF — unusually explicit creator permission (updated September 4, 2026)
**Paper policy.** https://www.pff.com/terms
- §1.9 permits sharing, quoting, or screenshotting **individual PFF grades, statistics, or excerpts of free articles** on personal, noncommercial social accounts with attribution/linking and without bulk substitution.
- §1.10 expressly permits monetized creator content — videos, streams, podcasts, newsletters, articles, and social content — to "reference, quote, or display individual items of Free Content," provided each item credits PFF, links where possible, is not bulk/database-like, does not substitute for PFF, and is not presented as endorsed by PFF.
- Paid content, API data, and AI output are excluded.

**How we exploit this:** One public PFF grade/stat per post, labeled "Source: PFF," with a link and original analysis on top. Never publish paid-only tables or bulk rankings.

### 2.3 The Athletic (NYT) — no sharing clause, no CC license
**Paper policy.** The Athletic's Terms of Use contains no Creative Commons license and no "share with credit" provision; it grants only "a limited, revocable, non-transferable, non-sublicensable license to access and use the Services." Sharing is permitted only "with our prior written permission... or as expressly permitted by these Terms."
- https://www.nytimes.com/content/help/rights/terms-of-service.html

The NYT Co. fair-use policy (covering The Athletic) states: "we reserve all rights to deny licenses to third parties to use our content." Its "fair use" page is in fact a contact page for *requesting licenses* to NYT content — the operative posture is request-permission-first.
- https://www.nytimes.com/content/help/rights/terms/terms-of-service.html
- https://www.nytco.com/press/fair-use-policy/

**How we exploit this:** Link to Athletic articles; restate facts independently; use any short quotation only under a separate fair-use analysis (see 2.6). Never rely on "credit" as permission.

### 2.4 ESPN/Disney — restrictive terms
**Paper policy.** Disney's terms cover ESPN-branded products: personal, noncommercial consumer license only, with "no right to reproduce, distribute, communicate to the public, make available to the public, or transform any Disney Product," and a prohibition on "use the Disney Products for any commercial or business-related use or build a business utilizing the Disney Products," except where applicable law overrides.
- https://disneytermsofuse.com/english/

**How we exploit this:** Link out; use platform-native sanctioned sharing; extract independently restated facts and short quotations under fair-use analysis only.

### 2.5 X reposts and quote posts
**Paper policy + observed practice.**

- X's 2026 terms: users retain ownership but grant X a worldwide license authorizing X to make content available and "let others do the same," including "syndication, broadcast, distribution, repost, promotion or publication," subject to X's terms. This licenses **X and its sublicensees** — it does not by itself authorize a user to download and re-upload someone else's video.
  - https://kapqcb2yzifuyqcd.lex003.online/content/dam/legal-twitter/site-assets/x-terms-of-service-2026-04-10/en/x-terms-of-service-2026-04-10.pdf
- Quote post mechanics (2026 guide): click the repost icon → select **Quote** → "Write your take above the embedded original" → post. "Quote posting wraps the original in your own commentary... The commentary sits above the embedded post, so your text gets read first."
  - https://www.recurpost.com/blog/how-to-post-on-x-twitter/
- **UNVERIFIED:** No current official X help-center article documenting the repost/quote feature was locatable; the ToS-based reading above is the strongest available basis.

**How we exploit this:** Quote-post official @NFL and team posts — the original stays embedded and attributed, and the commentary lives in *our* post. Never download and re-upload their video files.

### 2.6 Quotations: no bright-line rule, and the 1–2-sentence quote is not automatically safe
**Authoritative guidance.** The U.S. Copyright Office states: "there is no formula to ensure that a predetermined percentage or amount of a work—or specific number of words, lines, pages, copies—may be used without permission." Even a small amount can weigh against fair use if it is the "heart" of the work (citing *Harper & Row v. Nation*).
- http://copyright.gov/fair-use/more-info.html

**How we exploit this:** Quote only what is needed to identify and critique the source; surrounding original analysis should dominate. Never promise that "1–2 sentences" is automatically fair.

### 2.7 NFL press releases vs. NFL footage — separate the two
**Observed practice.** NFL.com/news press-release content (signings, rule changes, statements) is routinely and freely quotable/redistributable as news — standard editorial practice, and quoting press releases with attribution is the news norm. But that practice concerns **text**, not game footage; nothing in it waives rights over broadcast/game video.
- Example press-release hub: https://www.nfl.com/news/

**How we exploit this:** Quote press releases and official statements freely with attribution; treat footage under an entirely separate analysis.

## TOPIC 3 — Underused zero-cost opportunities

### 3.1 YouTube embeds are expressly licensed through the Service
**Paper policy.**
- YouTube's terms: "You may also show YouTube videos through the embeddable YouTube player."
- Uploaders grant users a license to use content "only as enabled by a feature of the Service (such as video playback or embeds)" — it does not authorize use independent of YouTube.
  - https://www.youtube.com/t/terms?gl=JP
  - https://yt-terms.static.usercontent.goog/pdf/terms/20231215/en_us_20231215.pdf

**How we exploit this:** Embed official NFL YouTube videos on a website (player intact). This does **not** authorize cropping footage into an independently uploaded X video.

### 3.2 Getty Embed — a real zero-dollar option with hard limits
**Paper policy / current help page.** Getty's Embed feature allows use of 70M+ photos on a **noncommercial website or blog** — not for selling a product, raising money, promotion, or endorsement.
- https://www.gettyimages.ca/faq/working-files
- Stated conditions: editorial/newsworthy/public-interest purposes only; no advertising, promotions, merchandising, implied endorsement, unlawful use, or display outside the Embedded Viewer; Getty may remove content or monetize the viewer.
  - https://searchenginewatch.com/2014/03/11/free-getty-images-what-is-getty-giving-getting-in-return/?amp=1

**How we exploit this:** Use Getty's own embed player in a non-promotional editorial site article. Do not download Getty images or place them in X graphics, thumbnails, ads, or sales pages.

### 3.3 GIPHY embed vs. GIPHY reuse — know the line
**Platform feature + paper policy.**
- GIPHY's support page instructs: select "Embed," choose iframe or HTML5, paste the supplied code, publish.
  - https://support.giphy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360020330711-How-to-Embed-a-GIF
- GIPHY's terms (as quoted): "we grant each user of the Services a worldwide, non-exclusive, non-sublicensable and non-transferable license to use Content solely for purposes of using the Services. Use, reproduction, modification, distribution or storage of any Content for other than purposes of using the Services is expressly prohibited without prior written permission."
  - https://www.sitepoint.com/community/t/free-gif-or-animations-for-my-website/270005
- Historical context: a 2016 NFL–GIPHY "test agreement" made Giphy "the source of high quality and authentic NFL GIFs of ancillary game and historical/iconic content" — **not gameplay GIFs**.
  - https://techcrunch.com/2016/12/02/the-nfl-will-reportedly-relax-their-social-media-rules-so-teams-can-post-more-gifs-and-videos/100

**How we exploit this:** Use GIPHY's hosted embed/player where an official NFL GIF exists; never download and re-upload GIFs to X. **UNVERIFIED:** whether an official @NFL GIPHY channel is currently active with current uploads.

### 3.4 Wikimedia Commons — genuinely free NFL game photos (not video)
**Verified presence.** Commons hosts NFL game photos (e.g., All-Pro Reels transfers from Flickr via #flickr2commons, 2021 Washington at Falcons from Mercedes-Benz Stadium). Commons accepts only freely licensed media; the #flickr2commons pipeline requires free licenses.
- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NFL_2021_-_Washington_at_Falcons_76_(51556155451).jpg
- Category examples: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NFL_2021_-_Washington_at_Falcons_88_(51555365507).jpg

**How we exploit this:** Use as thumbnails, post headers, and site imagery with the required attribution. **Caution (UNVERIFIED per file):** confirm each file's license field (All-Pro Reels Flickr uploads are typically CC BY-SA) before commercial/monetized use.

### 3.5 AP Images — priced, not free; public a la carte exists
**Reported pricing (dated).** AP Images allows the general public to sign up for non-subscriber, a la carte accounts; prices vary by use and duration. A 2014 report: editorial use on the web for two years of one photo carried ~$95.
- https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2014/reuters-weighs-into-photo-licensing-with-new-e-commerce-site/

**How we exploit this:** A real option for a high-value photo on a budget — not zero-dollar. **Price UNVERIFIED as current** (2014 data). USA Today Sports Images is similarly licensed stock; no zero-dollar editorial embed tier was located.

### 3.6 No current official fan clip-sharing tool was found
**UNVERIFIED — feature not located.** No current NFL app / NFL+ "make your own clip and share to social" feature was found in public documentation. What exists:
- NFL+ "NFL Pro" offers **All-22 Coaches Film for every game dating back to the 2022 season**, with playlist saving/sharing — viewing/playlist features, not re-upload rights.
  - https://www.sportsvideo.org/2024/08/21/launch-of-nfl-pro-highlights-third-season-of-nfl/
- The 2008-era NFL.com "Replay Re-Cutter" fan remix tool is discontinued (historical, not current).
  - https://www.zdnet.com/article/nfl-uses-reality-digital-to-turn-fans-into-video-producers/
- Notably, even **teams** historically could not create their own gameplay GIFs and had to rely on the NFL's social team (with large violation fines), which suggests the league does not take a permissive posture on third-party gameplay-video redistribution.
  - https://techcrunch.com/2016/12/02/the-nfl-will-reportedly-relax-their-social-media-rules-so-teams-can-post-more-gifs-and-videos/100

### 3.7 The pre-1964 public-domain avenue exists but is item-specific
**Authority.** The Library of Congress confirms: "Published works registered for copyright in the U.S. through Dec. 31, 1963 are now in the public domain **unless the copyright was renewed**" — and the only way to determine renewal status is a copyright search (Copyright Office records, card catalog, Stanford renewal database; the Office does not maintain a public-domain list).
- https://guides.loc.gov/p-and-p-rights-and-restrictions/risk-assessment
- https://www.loc.gov/legal/understanding-copyright/

**How we exploit this:** A genuine path for historical material (e.g., 1945/1956/1959 Archive.org batches), but it requires a **per-item renewal search** — archive.org hosting alone proves nothing. Default assumption for any NFL footage: still under copyright unless an item-specific search says otherwise.

### 3.8 Public-domain NFL footage is not established (general rule)
Internet Archive hosts batches labeled as 1945/1956/1959 NFL seasons, but the preservation project itself says some material cannot be uploaded due to copyright constraints. The NFL has affirmatively claimed copyright over historic footage such as Super Bowl I.
- https://archive.org/details/afpp-nfl-1945-season
- https://reason.com/2016/02/04/theres-just-one-known-recording-of-the-f
- https://abovethelaw.com/2014/09/only-surviving-recording-of-the-very-first-superbowl-is-because-a-fan-recorded-it-but-you-cant-see-it-because-copyright/

**How we exploit this:** Do not use "old NFL footage" merely because it is old or archived. Use only items with an item-specific rights statement, renewal search, or written permission. Build original diagrams from historical facts instead.
