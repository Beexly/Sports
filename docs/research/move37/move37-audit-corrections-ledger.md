## B. Overconfidence corrections (findings that are directionally right but were stated with more certainty than the evidence supports)

1. The CARA-fixes-the-blocker claim is true as a narrow domain-math fact, but "flatly settled" overstates it — sendback-02 is actually asking for a *different*, new normalized-scale proof, not re-litigating the already-closed infinite-domain question. Both were being conflated.
2. "No realistic outcome could clear the T9 margin" is numerically accurate against the one pilot estimate available (0.0158 < 0.02), but that pilot estimate is itself a confounded OLS association the lab's own report explicitly downgrades from a causal estimate — the honest phrasing is "the only estimate so far falls short," not "no realistic outcome could clear it."
3. The T3 K∈{1..5}→{1..4} narrowing is real and undocumented by name, but calling it wholly "unexamined" overstates it — repair-03 shows real methodological engagement with HMM overfitting risk (AIC+BIC, simulation-based recovery, shuffle gates), it just never explicitly says "we are dropping K=5."
4. Citation misattribution findings (DeCaro/Beilock, N-41/N-42) are correctly CONFIRMED, but grouping them at the same "high significance" tier as the T9 margin problem risks flattening an important distinction: in every misattribution case found across the whole corpus, the underlying scientific *substance* checked out — these are provenance/attribution defects, not fabricated science. They are still worth fixing (7+ instances is a systemic QC gap), but they are a different class of risk than a wrong number driving a live decision.
