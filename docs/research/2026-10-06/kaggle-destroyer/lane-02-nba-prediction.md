## franciscomrato/nba_prediction_model
- METHOD: Two-stage player-props pipeline: a LightGBM "minutes hurdle" predicts playing time, per-prop LightGBMs predict points/rebounds/assists conditional on minutes, then per-prop isotonic maps calibrate outputs. Everything is scored against sportsbook lines — season-avg, 5-game rolling, and the line are baselines — with a 2021-22 holdout P&L backtest. This is a props/trading pipeline, not a game-winner model.
- MATH: Hurdle decomposition: E[stat | x] = E[stat | minutes, x] evaluated at M̂(x) — correct, because exposure (minutes) dominates box-score variance. LightGBM (histogram-binned, leaf-wise GBDT, L1/L2 objectives) for both stages. Isotonic calibration = monotonic regression via pool-adjacent-violators: min Σ(yᵢ − f(sᵢ))² s.t. f non-decreasing; maps raw score → calibrated level/prob. Diagnostics: ECE (Σw_b|acc_b − conf_b|), Brier (p−y)², log loss, MAE vs line, over-rate, star-vs-role stratification.
- DATA: Kaggle NBA Games CSVs → 45 features + per-prop datasets; optional injury proxies and historical odds; backtest vs book lines on 2021-22 holdout.
- LEAKAGE: Three disqualifying-until-audited points: (1) Actual-vs-predicted minutes skew — if prop models train on *actual* minutes but backtest feeds predicted minutes (or worse, actual minutes at "inference"), MAE is understated and the P&L is fiction. Must confirm out-of-fold minutes throughout. (2) Calibration placement — if `calibration.pkl` is fit on the 2021-22 holdout and the before/after ECE/Brier are reported on the same holdout, all "after calibration" numbers are in-sample and optimistic. Isotonic must be fit on pre-holdout data, applied forward. (3) Box-score-derived injury proxies are same-game leakage by construction (you learn "limited minutes" from the thing you're predicting). Also verify shift(1) on all 45 rolling features; if closing odds are both feature and settlement price, the edge is circular (see Project 2).
- GSE APPLICATION: This is the props vertical: exposure-model → conditional-prop → isotonic, keyed by (sport, market, player tier), with P&L-vs-line as the acceptance gate for every GSE market module. The exposure variable generalizes: NBA minutes, NHL TOI/goalie starts, MLB PA/pitches/IP, NFL snap share/targets, soccer minutes.
- IMPLEMENTATION SPEC: New `gse/props/`: `exposure.py` (hurdle with OOF minutes via purged CV), `conditional.py` (per-prop LGBM; Poisson objective for count props), `calibrate.py` (isotonic store keyed (sport, market, season), fit-on-past/apply-forward enforced). Replace CSV loaders with the GSE feature store. Add `backtest/line_backtest.py` replicating their `benchmark_results.csv` schema as the standard props report. Add a residual-quantile head so output is P(over line), not just a point estimate.
- TRANSFER: Transfers to all five sports; MLB and NHL props are *more* exposure-driven (pitch counts, TOI) than NBA. Transfers: hurdle decomposition, isotonic-per-market, line-relative acceptance. Doesn't transfer: feature importances; Gaussian residual assumptions break on sparse soccer goals — use Poisson heads.
- VERDICT: ADAPT. The hurdle + calibration + vs-line skeleton is right and rare. Not ADOPT: single-league, and the minutes-skew and in-sample-calibration risks are unresolved. Not REJECT: nothing else in this set covers props.

## dh-dz/nba-stats-prediction
- METHOD: Moneyline betting framework: team-vs-team differentials (recent form, rest/fatigue, H2H, home court, pre-game odds) feed NGBoost, which outputs the full probability distribution of the game spread; win prob = P(spread > 0); compare to book implied prob; bet A / bet B / no-bet on positive EV. Rolling-window train/test preserves time order.
- MATH: NGBoost: each boosting stage fits the natural gradient of parametric NLL — for Normal output, ∇NLL = ((y−μ)/σ², −1 + (y−μ)²/σ²), premultiplied by the inverse Fisher information, so trees update (μ, σ) directly; final p(spread|X) = N(μ̂(X), σ̂²(X)). Win prob = Φ(μ̂/σ̂). EV per unit vs decimal odds d: p·d − 1; bet if p·d − 1 > ε with de-vigged (Shin/power) implied probs. The killer property: one distribution prices ML, spread, alt spreads, and totals simultaneously.
- DATA: Kaggle NBA Games (games.csv, teams.csv) + Kaggle NBA Odds (oddsData.csv); rolling-window evaluation.
- LEAKAGE: Structurally sound (rolling windows), but one fatal flaw: **odds as a feature, then EV computed against those same odds**. Not point-in-time leakage — lines are pre-game — but self-referential circularity: p_model = market + noise, so every "edge" is miscalibration noise dressed as signal. The shipped P&L logic is untrustworthy as-is. Secondary: Kaggle odds files have sloppy timestamps and merge-alignment bugs — verify lines are true pre-game snapshots; NGBoost early stopping/hyperparameters tuned across all rolling folds is mild leakage; form/fatigue/H2H need shift(1) verification (undocumented).
- GSE APPLICATION: The pricing core. One parametric margin distribution per game → derive every market from a single object; divergence between derived prices and market *is* the signal. The A/B/no-bet EV gate becomes GSE's betting front-end with de-vigging, edge thresholds, and Kelly sizing.
- IMPLEMENTATION SPEC: `gse/dists/ngboost_margin.py` with pluggable families: Normal (NFL, NBA margin), Normal + continuity correction (NHL puck line), negative-binomial or bivariate Poisson (MLB run diff), Dixon-Coles bivariate Poisson (soccer — Normal on goal diff is wrong for 0-0/1-0 mass). `gse/betting/ev_gate.py` (de-vig, threshold, fractional Kelly). `gse/blend/market_priors.py`: odds enter only as a separate shrunk ensemble member (learned λ per sport/market), never inside the model being bet against. `gse/cv/purged_rolling.py` with K-day gaps.
- TRANSFER: Distributional-margin core transfers to NFL/NHL/MLB with family swaps, to soccer with a count-family swap. EV gate, de-vigging, no-bet discipline, one-distribution-many-markets: 1:1 to every sport. NBA-specific feature weights: zero transfer.
- VERDICT: ADAPT. NGBoost-margin → multi-market derivation + EV gate is the closest thing here to the GSE core loop. Not ADOPT: the shipped betting logic is circular. Not REJECT: the method is correct and scarcer than it looks.

## ruppdj/nba-playoff-predictor
- METHOD: Playoff series winner + series-length pipeline, 1984-2025, strict walk-forward CV across 13 test seasons. Features are era-normalized via within-season z-scores; stacking ensemble (LR + XGBoost + LightGBM → logistic meta → isotonic) predicts series outcome; LightGBM/ordinal head predicts length; 10k Monte Carlo sims fill the bracket. Result: 72.8% — exactly tying the always-pick-higher-seed baseline, with ~0% upset recall.
- MATH: Within-season z-score zᵢⱼ = (xᵢⱼ − μ_j(Season))/σ_j(Season) kills cross-era drift (pace, 3PT revolution, rule changes). Stacking: OOF base probabilities p_m → meta P(y=1) = σ(β₀ + Σβ_m p_m) → isotonic (PAV). mord.LogisticIT: shared linear η(x) with ordered thresholds c₁<…<c₃, P(y=k) = σ(η−c_k) − σ(η−c_{k+1}), distance-aware immediate-threshold loss. Bracket MC: Bernoulli draws per series with calibrated p, length sampled from calibrated classes. Walk-forward: train ≤ N−1 (min 10 seasons), test N, strictly chronological.
- DATA: 1984-2025 team-season playoff data: season stats, L10/L20 momentum, minutes-weighted VORP/BPM, availability-weighted VORP, matchup deltas, H2H. MLflow, MD5 checksums, seeded.
- LEAKAGE: Cleanest structure of the four — walk-forward by season, no random splits. Residual audit: (1) if isotonic is fit on pooled OOF from the same 13 test seasons it's evaluated on, mild optimism — need nested/forward calibration; (2) injury features must be availability *as of playoff start*; if any term reflects playoff availability itself, that's target leakage — undocumented; (3) within-season z-scores legitimately use the test season's own league mean/sd (league-wide stats known pre-playoffs, no outcome info) — fine, but state it. Not leakage but the real verdict: log-loss 0.563 is meaningless without the naive baseline's log-loss; 0% upset recall means **zero winner-selection edge beyond seed** — the only possible skill is sharper probabilities, and that was never demonstrated against the baseline.
- GSE APPLICATION: Three reusable subsystems: (1) within-season normalization as GSE's universal feature layer for all sports/eras; (2) walk-forward CV as the GSE backtest standard (week-level folds for in-season sports); (3) bracket MC + ordinal length head for futures (outrights, series-length markets). Plus the governance rule: no GSE model ships unless it beats the naive baseline (higher seed / home / market favorite) on the same walk-forward.
- IMPLEMENTATION SPEC: `gse/norm/era_zscore.py` (+ trailing within-season windows for as-of-date features); `gse/cv/walk_forward.py` with min-train and purge gap; `gse/ensemble/stack.py` with time-ordered OOF; `gse/ordinal/length_head.py` (or derive length bands from P2's margin distribution — redundant but robust); `gse/sims/bracket_mc.py` upgraded with correlated sampling via shared latent strength prior updated by series state (this is P4's fix #7); `gse/governance/naive_gate.py`. Adopt MLflow + checksums as GSE MLOps standard.
- TRANSFER: All five sports. Best-of-7 → NHL/MLB direct; NFL single-elim collapses the length head; soccer two-legged ties map to aggregate-margin bands. Era z-scores mandatory everywhere (NFL rule eras, MLB ball eras, NHL dead-puck, soccer xG regimes). The upset-recall≈0 lesson transfers too: GBDT on era-normalized team stats will not find upsets; upset edge must come from regime/asymmetry features or mispricing detection, not more boosting.
- VERDICT: ADAPT. The fitted NBA model is REJECT-quality (zero edge beyond seed) — never ship it. The methodology stack — normalization, walk-forward, calibration plumbing, ordinal head, MC bracket, baseline honesty — is the best infrastructure in this set; rebuild multi-sport with nested calibration and correlated sims.

 ## ferhat00/nba-playoffs
- **METHOD:** Hierarchical pipeline: Bayesian player ratings → team-state aggregation → ML matchup head → Monte Carlo bracket simulation. The genuinely interesting move is treating team strength as *emergent from player posteriors* rather than team-level aggregates, then propagating uncertainty through a bracket rather than predicting it point-estimate style.
- **MATH:** PyMC MCMC (presumably NUTS) for per-player rating posteriors — rating ~ Normal(μ_player, σ_player) with game-level likelihood; uncertainty is carried, not collapsed. TeamAggregator produces a 21-dim state vector; LSTM momentum head in PyTorch consumes recent game sequences (trained with BCE, almost certainly without walk-forward discipline — check). XGBoostMatchupEngine: `objective=binary:logistic`, sigmoid output. Bracket sim: per-game Bernoulli draws, best-of-7 series sampled as repeated Bernoulli (implicitly binomial with game-level dependence ignored), 1k–100k trials; bracket path distributions from Plotly.
- **DATA:** nba_api endpoints (player box scores + team logs), cached; Kaggle GPU notebook. Single-season playoff scope.
- **LEAKAGE:** Almost certainly yes, three ways. (1) If player posteriors are fit on the *full* season including the playoff window you're predicting, that's direct in-sample leakage. (2) The LSTM momentum features are the classic trap — unless sequences are strictly t−1 backward-looking and re-fit per prediction date, the LSTM has seen the test games. (3) The XGBoost is trained on what labels and what feature vintage? Nothing in the repo structure suggests walk-forward validation. Also: ignoring game-level correlation in series simulation *understates* underdog variance (favorites' game win probs are correlated via hot streaks), which biases bracket probabilities toward chalk.
- **GSE APPLICATION:** Two components worth wiring in. (a) The player-posterior → team-state aggregation layer generalizes directly to NHL (skater/goalie ratings), soccer (player xG+/xaG contributions), and matters most in NBA where injury news moves lines. (b) The bracket simulator is the *only* correct output format for tournament sports: GSE should emit path distributions (championship probability, round-by-round matchup probabilities), not single winners — this repo is a working template for NFL playoffs and March Madness too.
- **IMPLEMENTATION SPEC:** Add a `TimeTraveler` data access layer that snapshots player state as-of prediction date (the repo's caching is date-naive). Replace the monolithic MCMC with per-player rolling Bayesian updates (conjugate Normal updates or a lightweight particle filter) — 500 draws per player won't scale to 15+ sports. Swap the LSTM for a temporal CNN or simple EMA-of-embeddings to cut training leakage surface. Add series-level correlation: draw a per-series team-form factor and condition each game's Bernoulli on it. Emit bracket paths as a `SimResult` dataclass consumed by GSE's aggregation layer.
- **TRANSFER:** Architecture transfers well — player-hierarchy to NHL/soccer, bracket sim to NHL/NFL playoffs, MLB postseason. Coefficients and the 21-dim vector don't. Momentum embeddings transfer only after retraining per sport.
- **VERDICT: ADAPT.** The architecture (posterior→aggregate→simulate) is top-tier GSE material, but the training discipline is unproven and likely leaky. What would change my mind to ADOPT: evidence of walk-forward fitting throughout (as-of dates on every fit) and a backtest showing calibration against playoff outcomes over 5+ seasons.

## ashaypatla/nba-outcome-prediction
- **METHOD:** Deconstruct the market: use sportsbook-generated signals (spread, total, season phase) as features to predict win/loss with calibrated probabilities, then benchmark against the book's own implied probabilities. It is essentially an experiment asking "can I recover the book's number from the book's number?"
- **MATH:** Almost certainly logistic regression or gradient boosting on 3-4 features: log-odds = β₀ + β₁·spread + β₂·total + Σβ_phase·1[phase=k], trained with binary cross-entropy. Implied prob from spread ≈ Φ(spread/σ_total) or from moneyline de-vig (removing overround: p_implied = p_raw/(p_raw + q_raw)). "61.80%" from spread −3.5, total 221.5 is exactly what the market already prices — the model is a smooth approximation of the vig-free line. Calibration likely isotonic or Platt via sklearn.
- **DATA:** Historical games with pre-game spread/total, one-hot season phase. Single sport, single league, handful of features.
- **LEAKAGE:** Probably clean on the surface (spread/total are genuinely pre-game), but the *conclusion* is the tell: "predictions closely mirror book implied probs." Of course they do — the model is fit to outcomes the line already predicts, and closing spread is the most information-dense feature in all of sports. The reported "modest consistent improvement" over books is the classic overfitting-to-noise artifact unless validated on closing lines *after* fit and with log-loss, not accuracy. Season phase one-hots are borderline — if phases are defined by the game's own date relative to the schedule, fine; if derived from the outcome, leakage.
- **GSE APPLICATION:** Not a predictor — a *market-blend calibration harness*. GSE should absolutely maintain a market-implied baseline per sport (spread→prob, total→pace expectations, de-vigged moneylines) and blend model output with it via log-odds averaging or a stacking layer. This repo is a minimal proof that the harness idea works, but GSE needs nothing from the model itself.
- **IMPLEMENTATION SPEC:** Extract the benchmarking pattern into a GSE `MarketBaseline` module: (1) de-vig function per book (multiplicative, power, or Shin methods — Shin is the right call for NBA), (2) log-loss/Brier comparison harness that every GSE model must beat before shipping, (3) a blend weight optimizer on walk-forward log-loss. Delete the model. Add back-to-back, rest, and injury features *only* as delta-features against the market line, not absolutes.
- **TRANSFER:** The methodology (beat-the-line benchmarking, de-vig, log-loss calibration) transfers trivially to all five sports. The coefficients and the 3-feature model transfer to nothing.
- **VERDICT: REJECT** as a predictor — it rediscovers the closing line with three features and calls the residual an edge. What would change my mind: a genuine log-loss improvement over de-vigged closing lines on a strict walk-forward set with bet-level profit after vig, sustained over 3+ seasons.

Keep the Flask API pattern but serve via GSE's model registry instead. Pin Vegas line vintage explicitly (open vs. close) as a config flag.
- **TRANSFER:** High. Method, feature architecture, anti-leakage contract, and the Vegas-delta edge concept all transfer. Coefficients don't — Four Factors weights are NBA-specific.
- **VERDICT: ADOPT.** This is what the other five should look like: point-in-time discipline as an explicit design principle, a sane baseline model, market features properly framed. To keep ADOPT status it must demonstrate (not just claim) off-by-one correctness in rolling windows and train-only scaler fitting — I'd gate adoption on passing the shuffle-future-rows unit test.

## kyleskom/NBA-Machine-Learning-Sports-Betting
- **METHOD:** The most production-complete betting system here: daily ingestion of NBA team stats and sportsbook odds into SQLite, merge with days-rest, train moneyline and totals models (XGBoost + NN), then a live daily predictor computing win probabilities, totals, EV, and optional Kelly-stake sizing — served via Flask.
- **MATH:** XGBoost (`binary:logistic`) + a neural net (MLP, BCE loss) per target (moneyline, over/under). EV = p_model·(decimal_odds − 1) − (1 − p_model); bet when EV > 0. Kelly: f* = (b·p − q)/b where b = decimal_odds − 1, p = model prob, q = 1 − p. Totals handled as a second binary target (over/under at the book's line — note this target *moves with the line*, a subtle design issue).
- **DATA:** NBA endpoints (team season stats) + SBR odds/scores 2007–present, merged per game with rest days. Two decades, live daily pipeline.
- **LEAKAGE:** Real risks, specific ones: (1) Get_Data pulls *current* team stats — if training features include full-season averages and the training set spans 2007–today, every game's "season stats" include that game and the rest of the season. This is the classic full-season-aggregate leak and I'd bet money it's present. (2) The totals model trained against a book line that moved between line-posting and game time is training on information partially post-hoc. (3) Merging odds at the wrong join timestamp means the model may see closing odds but predict against opening odds in production. ## FIND 1 — Tired of Misattribution (Schedule Fatigue Decomposition, arXiv 2112.14649)
- **METHOD:** Two-stage regression that first strips team quality (game net rating ~ net-rating differential) and then regresses the *residuals* on schedule/travel covariates. This is the correct order of operations — most fatigue studies contaminate schedule effects with strength effects. Stage 1 alone: adj R² = 0.2262, intercept −2.81 (visitor disadvantage), β₁ = 0.968 per point of net-rating edge.
- **MATH:** 3-games-in-4-days: −1.290 pts (p = 1.69e-07; adj R² contribution 4.0e-03 — tiny variance share but the largest schedule term); rest differential: +0.431 pts/day (p = 4.13e-07); B2B dummy: −0.384 (p = 0.164 — **subsumed** once rest-diff is in the model); 3-zone westward travel: −1.740 (p = 0.108, marginal); flight distance: NS. Converted to win probability (margin σ ≈ 12): 3-in-4 ≈ −4.3pp, one day of rest edge ≈ ±1.4pp, B2B label alone ≈ −1.3pp and not distinguishable from zero.
- **GSE APPLICATION:** Feature `L2_rest_diff_continuous` = (team rest days − opponent rest days), fires every game, entering as points-equivalent at +0.431/day (recalibrated per rolling 3-season window) and passed through the engine's margin→prob function. Feature `L2_schedule_density_3in4`, binary, fires on 3rd-game-in-4-days, −1.29 pts equivalent. **Explicitly delete the B2B dummy as a standalone term** — it's collinear residue and including it double-counts. This is the backbone of the schedule block.
- **VERDICT: ADAPT.** The parameterization (continuous rest-diff + density, drop the label) is adopted as-is; coefficients are not, because the sample predates the load-management era where B2B frequency, rotation patterns, and star-rest behavior all shifted. Promote to ADOPT if recalibrated 2019–24 coefficients land within ~20% of published values.
- **X-SPORT:** NFL — rest-diff analog is Thursday/short-week and post-bye asymmetry (weaker; weekly cadence); NHL — B2B is native and strong, same parameterization transfers directly; MLB — remap to pitcher rest/bullpen availability, not team rest; soccer — fixture congestion real but rotation policy confounds it (model first-XI minutes-rest, not club rest).

## FIND 2 — rest_fatigue.py / "Running on Empty" Multiplicative Prop Factors
- **METHOD:** Operationalizes a peer-reviewed B2B performance study (20,000+ games): tired teams shoot worse eFG%, grab fewer OREB, play slower, and *opponents shoot better against them*. Effects wired as multiplicative factors on prop projections, split by channel.
- **MATH:** Total effect band 0.5–2%. Key decomposition: FGM weight 1.0 (fatigue is a **conversion** channel: FGM = FGA × eFG%, and fatigue hits eFG%, not opportunity), FGA weight 0.3 (structural/volume only). 3-in-4 at half the B2B effect; B2B takes precedence — no stacking (correct: a B2B is usually also a 3-in-4; naive stacking double-counts one fatigue state twice). One caveat: verify the primary citation before hanging coefficients on it; the stated effect sizes are consistent with the academic B2B literature, so direction is safe.
- **GSE APPLICATION:** Feature `L2_conversion_fatigue_multiplier` — fires when a player's team rest_days == 0 (3-in-4 gets the 0.5× variant), applied to the efficiency channel of the projection, not volume. Two upgrades required: (a) make it **opponent-symmetric** — fatigue degrades defense too, so the multiplier must also fire on the tired team's *defensive* efficiency (boosting opposing shooter props); (b) re-estimate weights per season. Note prop books shade B2B harder than game lines, so the residual edge is thinner here than anywhere else in the stack.
- **VERDICT: ADAPT.** The FGM/FGA weight split (1.0 vs 0.3) is the single best engineering insight in the find set — it cleanly separates conversion effects from opportunity effects and transfers to any efficiency×volume decomposition. But 0.5–2% effects against sharply shaded prop lines may be exactly zero residual value. ADOPT if a backtest shows flagged props beat the closing line at >52.4% equivalent; REJECT if the edge dies after controlling for the close.
- **X-SPORT:** NHL — the true analog is **goalie** fatigue, not skater shooting: apply the multiplier to the opposing goalie's save% on his B2B start (boosts opponent shot-value props); MLB — pitcher short-rest channel (velocity/whiff-rate decay); NFL — negligible; soccer — xG conversion and press intensity (PPDA) decay on second game of the week.

## FIND 3 — Entine & Small: HCA–Rest Decomposition (JQAS)
- **METHOD:** Game-level regression with team dummies, home dummy, and rest dummies on 2,415 games to decompose home-court advantage into a structural component (crowd/referee/familiarity) and a schedule-driven rest-asymmetry component.
- **MATH:** Second of B2B: −1.77 pts (p = 0.03), win odds ratio 0.75 (≈42.9% in a 50/50 spot, ≈ −5.9pp win prob). Only **zero-days-rest** is significant — 1–2 days rest: NS. Road teams play B2B in 33% of games vs 15% for home teams; **0.3 of the 3.24-pt HCA is rest asymmetry alone**. Second game of a road swing: −1.04 pts (p = 0.07).
- **GSE APPLICATION:** Feature `L2_HCA_rest_interaction_offset`. Split Layer-1's flat HCA into structural + schedule-implied (e.g., +2.8 → +2.5 structural + 0.3 schedule), then replace the 0.3 with the point-in-time Find-1 continuous rest-diff term. This is the **anti-double-count rule** for the whole stack: never apply full HCA *and* a B2B/rest penalty on top — the rest term partially lives inside historical HCA already. Magnitude ranges 0 (symmetric rest) to ~0.6+ pts (home on B2B vs rested visitor).
- **VERDICT: ADOPT.** The structural insight — HCA is not a constant, it's schedule-conditional — is durable across eras and replicated throughout the fatigue literature, and the double-count it prevents is the most common error in exactly this kind of engine. Re-derive the split on modern data (HCA has compressed to ~2.5, B2B frequency shifted), but the feature spec survives.
- **X-SPORT:** NFL — HCA decomposes partially into body-clock/travel (West Coast teams in 1pm ET road slots — documented edge); NHL — same HCA×B2B interaction shape; MLB — small; soccer — HCA is large and worth the same decomposition (crowd/travel vs congestion).

## FIND 4 — Ashman, Bowman & Lambrinos: Fatigue Mispricing in Wagering Markets (JSE 2010)
- **METHOD:** 19-season study asking the only question that matters for Layer 2: does the **market** price fatigue? Tests ATS performance in the "home disadvantage cell" — home team on second night of B2B vs visitor with 1–2 days rest.
- **MATH:** Home teams in that cell underperform the spread against the efficient-market null; effect magnified when the home team traveled 1–2 zones **eastward** between the B2B legs. (Consistent with circadian science: eastward = phase advance, physiologically harder — note the tension with Find 1's westward −1.740 at p=0.108; the interaction direction here is the more physiologically plausible one.) Treat as a directional market test, not a calibrated coefficient set — no usable effect size is exported.
- **GSE APPLICATION:** Feature `L2_fatigue_asymmetry_edge_flag` — graded flag: home ∧ rest_days == 0 ∧ visitor rest ≥ 1 ∧ (eastward tz-delta 1–2 between B2B legs). Fires → shade toward visitor beyond the Layer-1 line by ~0.5–1.5 pts equivalent, sized by the recency re-test. This is the archetype of Layer 2's job: books shade the **B2B label** (headline stat) but under-shade the **interaction cell** (home-B2B × rested-visitor × travel direction) because it's rare and composite.
- **VERDICT: ADAPT.** Highest potential edge in the set because it's measured against the market, not box scores — but the sample ends ~2008 and markets learn the cells they lose to. Mandatory gate: re-run on 2015–24 closing lines. Promote to ADOPT if the cell still beats the close by >1σ; REJECT if evaporated — which would itself be a valuable result, confirming market learning and pointing the hunt at rarer cells.
- **X-SPORT:** The **method** transfers everywhere — cell-level ATS persistence testing against closing lines: NFL short-week road divisional spots; NHL B2B×travel-direction; MLB getaway-day and body-clock cells; soccer Thursday-Europa→Sunday turns.

## FIND 5 — Revenge / Look-Ahead Spots (StatFox, Boyd's, Harvard)
- **METHOD:** Team-level ATS backtests of narrative spots plus a player-level natural experiment on revenge performance (GameScore vs season average for players facing former teams).
- **MATH:** Revenge teams 46.6% ATS (StatFox 2005-08) — **worse than random**; the narrative anti-predicts. Boyd's (since 1995): generic revenge 50.3% ATS (coin flip, −EV after vig); revenge within 6 days of last meeting: 53.8% ATS (643-553-18, +1.4pp over the 52.38% breakeven, ~+2.6% ROI — thin). Look-ahead: winning-record teams 43.3% ATS before big matchups; 35.6% ATS on the road before an opponent's home-revenge game (almost certainly small N — treat as a mirage until N-adjusted). Harvard: 486 players, GameScore delta 55 categories up / 53 down = p ≈ 0.46, pure noise; LeBron 2010 (+55.3%) is a tail anecdote, not signal.
- **GSE APPLICATION:** Kill revenge-as-boost entirely. Salvage: `L2_lookahead_fade_flag` — fires on a winning-record team immediately preceding a defined marquee matchup, shading *against* them; `L2_recency_of_meeting` — days since last H2H, entering as a small positive term only via exp(−t/6) truncated at 6 days. The Harvard result should be loaded as the **default prior for all player-level emotion proxies: expected effect = zero.**
- **VERDICT: ADAPT, narrowly.** Only the fade direction and the 6-day decay window survive. The constructive reading is that 46.6% ATS means **revenge lines are systematically inflated by the market** — the tradeable signal is fading the narrative, not riding it. Would flip to REJECT: if the look-ahead fade fails N-adjusted significance on 2010–24 data.
- **X-SPORT:** NFL — look-ahead/letdown fades are the best-documented analog; soccer — pre-UCL domestic fades; NHL — weak; revenge — dead in every sport (soccer derby lines are fully priced, often inflated).

## FIND 7 — Contextual Adjustment Pipeline (NBA-02)
- **METHOD:** Sequential contextual modulators applied after a base true_probability: pace → defense → rest → clamp. This is already a functioning Layer-2 architecture in miniature — the only find that ships a pipeline rather than a paper.
- **MATH:** prob′ = clamp( (prob_base × clip(pace/100, 0.5, 1.5)) × clip(115/DRtg_opp, 0.7, 1.3) − 0.03·[rest==0], 0.01, 0.99 ). Five defects: (i) **dimensional error** — pace is a volume channel; multiplying a probability by pace conflates expected count with likelihood of over. Correct form: exp_count′ = exp_count × pace_ratio, then p(over) = P(exp_count′ > line) via the count distribution; (ii) flat −0.03 rest penalty ignores sign and magnitude of rest-*differential* (Find 1: ±0.43 pts/day both directions) and models only one team's rest; (iii) DRtg is pace-inflated and position-blind — opponent-**position** defensive allowance beats team DRtg for player props; (iv) order-dependent chain, no interaction terms; (v) missing the largest prop driver of all: projected minutes/role (injury news, blowout risk).
- **GSE APPLICATION:** Keep as the reference skeleton for `L2_modulator_stack`: ordered, bounded, one channel per modulator, full audit log per adjustment. Fixes on ingest: rest modulator → signed continuous rest-diff via the margin model; pace/defense → move to the count/expectation layer with position-level opponent stats; insert a **minutes/role modulator that fires before all efficiency channels**; retain the clamp as the terminal safety. 
- **VERDICT: ADAPT.** Adopt the pattern — it's the right architecture and the natural container for Finds 1–5 — but fix the dimensional error and the rest term before trusting any output. ADOPT once a backtest shows the corrected stack beats base-only on out-of-sample closing-line residuals.
- **X-SPORT:** The pattern generalizes directly; the defense modulator changes identity per sport — NHL: opposing goalie (starter/backup, rest state); MLB: opposing starting pitcher; NFL: DVP-by-position + weather; soccer: opponent xGA + press intensity.

### B. New candidates the first pass missed (5)

**R1 — FiveThirtyEight RAPTOR (player ratings, 1976–2023). VERDICT: ADOPT.**
The lane inventoried 538 Elo (team-level) but completely missed RAPTOR — 538's flagship *player-level* metric with box-score + on/off components and a tracking-era version from 2014. Source: https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/blob/master/nba-raptor/README.md (historical_RAPTOR_by_player.csv / _by_team.csv, possessions, pace_impact, predator columns included). GSE application: minutes-weighted RAPTOR sum is the availability-aware team-strength prior the lane keeps hand-rolling from VORP/BPM (ruppdj) or Bayesian posteriors (ferhat00). It is a better input than both and it's free. Caveat: static since the 2023 shutdown — use as a training prior, not a live feed; never let season-end RAPTOR leak into in-season features (season_type column makes this auditable). This is the biggest dataset miss in the lane.

**R2 — DARKO (Andrew Patton, daily time-decayed player ratings). VERDICT: ADAPT (promote to ADOPT if the update cadence is verified live).**
The lane's ferhat00 evaluation praises "Bayesian player posteriors → team aggregation" as top-tier architecture — DARKO ships that daily, for free, with aging curves and time-decay, at https://craftednba.com/ (meta-metrics dashboard incl. DARKO/DRIP/LEBRON/RAPTOR) and via Patton's own tutorials at https://github.com/mcbarlowe/basic-nba-tutorials (SPM/team-rating/win-projection walkthroughs that double as the build spec if GSE ever needs to re-derive it). Why it matters: DARKO is the production answer to the lane's "player posterior" daydream and the natural strength prior for the injury/news-adjusted team rating. Dunks & Threes EPM (https://dunksandthrees.com/epm) is the corroborating second source. Missed entirely by wave 1.

**R3 — Altitude venue term (Denver/Utah). VERDICT: ADOPT.**
Documented, quantified, replicated, point-in-time trivial: Lopez/Matthews/Baumer (2017) found Denver the largest home advantage in North American sports — ~66.1% home win vs ~62% league baseline against equal opposition; Nuggets all-time .652 home / .350 road, the largest split of any active franchise. Coverage: https://red.msudenver.edu/2018/advantage-altitude-exploring-the-largest-home-advantage-in-sports/ and https://www.espn.ph/nba/story/_slug_=nba-finals-2023-how-denver-altitude-gives-nuggets-edge&id=37762170. The original report's venue feature is a flat `is_home` plus a generic HCA split — altitude never appears once. GSE application: `L2_altitude_venue` term (DEN 5280ft, UTA ~4300ft) inside the Find-3 HCA decomposition, with a 4th-quarter fatigue interaction (visitors fade late — pace/second-half splits). X-sport: NFL Mexico City/Denver games, MLB Coors (already priced), soccer La Paz/Quito extremes.

**R4 — Referee crew tendencies (foul-rate / totals adjustment). VERDICT: ADAPT.**
Published and computable same-day: crew assignments are released the morning of the game; foul-rate differentials per official are large and persistent (recent arXiv referee-impact-metric work on 2021–25 ESPN win-probability data; practitioner tracking at https://www.rotowire.com/basketball/article/nba-referee-kryptonite-worst-teams-96968 and https://www.nbastuffer.com/referee_stats via the betting literature). The original report has zero referee content — yet totals are the market where a ±2 FTA/crew swing is directly convertible to points. GSE application: crew-level expected-fouls adjustment on totals only (sides are noisier), shrunk hard toward league mean (official effects are small-N and mean-revert), gated on beating the closing total, never the side. This is the totals-modeling coverage the lane otherwise lacks.

**R5 — Load-management / tanking regime detection. VERDICT: ADAPT (Layer-2 regime flag + prop-lane usage-spike model).**
The situational layer covered fatigue and rest but never the NBA's signature late-season pathology: healthy scratches, post-elimination shutdowns, and pre-tip injury-report chaos. Academic evidence exists (2021 study: teams rest more healthy players after playoff elimination); 2025-26 set records for 20-point blowouts in April (~1/3 of games) with multiple tanking fines (Utah $500k, Dallas $750k). Practitioner angle (thinly sourced, treat as hypothesis): late-scratch usage redistribution is the most attackable prop inefficiency in the sport — 30 vacated shots flow to a bench player whose line barely moves. Coverage: https://www.pulse.bot/sports-business/news/what-the-data-says-about-nba-tanking-and-how-to-fix-it-26748275-6ca0-47f1-84bb-264ba56e182e/. GSE application: `L2_elimination_rest_flag` (team eliminated × star DNP-probability from rest-pattern history) depressing team strength and inflating variance; `L2_usage_redistribution` for the prop lane firing on late scratches (minutes/hurdle model from franciscomrato is the carrier). Gate: elimination status is point-in-time clean; rest *intent* is not — model the DNP probability, never assert the motive.

Honorable mention (not a full candidate): **pbpstats** (https://pypi.org/project/pbpstats/1.0.8/ and https://www.pbpstats.com/) — free lineup/net-rating/on-off/WOWY data and a Python client. The lane's ruppdj evaluation hand-builds minutes-weighted VORP; pbpstats is the substrate. Also **inpredictable.com** (win-probability/tempo-variance research) — relevant to lane 15's overlap and totals variance modeling, unmentioned.
