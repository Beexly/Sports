## The Five Most Important Follows

1. **@csv_enjoyer (Ray Carpenter).** The open-data well. His research is reproducible by design — EPA/play, success rate, rush-gap splits and Bayesian ranking work you can actually audit and rebuild from. For any engine project, he is the single best source of public methods to benchmark against.
2. **@benbbaldwin (Ben Baldwin).** He builds the infrastructure everyone else stands on: RBSDM and the nflfastR/nflverse ecosystem. Follow him to understand how the numbers are computed before you argue about what they mean — and his fourth-down tool is the standard reference for decision-science arguments.
3. **@ASchatzNFL (Aaron Schatz).** The DVOA standard-bearer. Opponent-adjusted team ratings with the longest track record in public analytics, and his unit-level breakdowns remain the best quick read on why a team is efficient or not.
4. **@KeeganAbdoo (Keegan Abdoo).** Tracking-data research from inside Next Gen Stats itself. His pressure probability and completion-probability work shows what the sensor data can genuinely support — essential for separating real signal from tracking-data theater.
5. **@MikeClayNFL (Mike Clay).** The actionable-projections follow and the strongest reply-friendly account in the dossier (he explicitly invites questions and error reports via his handle). His projection set is the most useful public baseline for comparing any forecast engine against — and he will occasionally engage with good methodological questions.
