# [0492] Analysing Skill Predominance in Generalized Fantasy Cricket (arXiv:2512.18467v1)

**Citation:** Supratim Das, Sarthak Sarkar, Subhamoy Maitra, Tridib Mukherjee (2026). *Analysing Skill Predominance in Generalized Fantasy Cricket*. arXiv:2512.18467v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18467v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 2442 lines).
**Verdict:** REJECT — a cricket fantasy contest-design paper (limited-selection "Expert of Experts" format) with no transfer path to NFL win/spread/total modeling; its one relevant insight (shared-prize-pool crowding dilutes picking-the-best-team) is a DFS-contest observation, not a model.

## 1. Research question
In a limited-selection fantasy cricket contest where users pick among 2–4 expert-designed teams (instead of building their own) under a shared prize pool, does measurable skill emerge, and how do contest-structure parameters (number of experts, inter-team correlation, an "Impact Player" substitution feature) shape the skill–chance balance — both in simulation and with real IPL 2024 data?

## 2. Dataset / schema
Simulated: expert-team scores (P_1,…,P_n)∼LogNormal(μ,Σ) on n=4 teams, N=1000 players, τ=0.2 analytical fraction, Rs. 25 entry, 20% platform cut (prize pool Rs. 20,000), equicorrelated log-scores ρ=0.4, estimation error bound β=0.04 at 95% confidence; π_i estimated from 100,000 draws; 10,000 match iterations per configuration. Real: IPL 2024 — 71 completed matches (scorecards via Cricbuzz Rapid API), 263 registered players with career stats (ESPN Cricinfo); analysis on the final 10 completed matches, 10,000 runs per configuration, 1,000 skill users per strategy, 16,000 random users, stochastic perturbation p=0.05. Code: https://github.com/Supratim2004/Expert-of-Experts (stated).

## 3. Method / model
(a) Score model: (P_1,…,P_n)∼LogNormal(μ,Σ), i.e. (log P_1,…,log P_n)∼N(μ,Σ), equicorrelated; win probabilities π_i=P(P_i≥P_j ∀j≠i) estimated by Monte Carlo. (b) Player behavior: analytical players sample beliefs p∼Dirichlet(απ) with concentration α calibrated so p is within β of π with probability ≥1−δ, via Chebyshev + union bound: α≥n/(4δβ²)−1 (Appendix A); then team choice ∼Multinomial(1,p). Random players choose ∼Multinomial(1,1_n/n). (c) Payoff: shared prize pool — if team i wins, its N_i selectors split the pool (e.g., 20,000/N_i). (d) Metrics: Selection Ratio = (analytical share on team i)/(random share on team i); Mean Winnings including zeros. (e) Extensions: number of experts ∈{2,3,4}; correlation ρ∈{0.1,…,0.9}; Impact Player: 4 candidate impact players (I_1,…,I_4)∼LogNormal(μ_I,Σ_I), ρ_I=0.3, independent of P; lowest-contributor share S_i∼DirichletMin(α) (minimum coordinate of Dirichlet(α), α=10·1 over 11 players); boosted score B_ij=P_i(1−S_i)+max(S_i·P_i, I_j); contest over team–impact-player combos. (f) Real-data protocol: expert teams built by strategy bots (Career Points LP with MEC constraints + n∈{5,…,9} common players; MA5 form; Tournament Points; MeanVarOptim = Form(last 3)−0.5·SD(last 3)); captain/vice-captain = top two; user strategies OneThirdForm=(1/3)Form+(2/3)Career, TwoThirdForm=(2/3)Form+(1/3)Career, StrictForm=Form. Metrics: Average Gain over Random (matchwise and tournament). (g) Regressions: G_i=β0+β1E+β2C+β3V_μ+β4Σ̄²+ε_i (E=#experts, C=common players, V_μ=between-team variance, Σ̄²=within-team variance), OLS, Basic and Impact-Player × Strict/OneThird specs; quadratic extension with C².

## 4. Equations & assumptions
(P_1,…,P_n)∼LogNormal(μ,Σ); (log P_1,…,log P_n)∼N(μ,Σ). π_i=P(P_i≥P_j ∀j≠i). p∼Dirichlet(απ), E[p_i]=π_i, Var(p_i)=π_i(1−π_i)/(α+1); calibration α≥n/(4δβ²)−1 from P(|p_i−π_i|≥β)≤1/(4(α+1)β²)≤δ/n. Team choice ∼Multinomial(1,p) / ∼Multinomial(1,1_n/n). Prize pool=25×1000×(100−20)/100=Rs. 20000; W_k=20000/N_i if k selected team i, else 0. B_ij=P_i(1−S_i)+max(S_i·P_i,I_j), π_ij=P(B_ij≥B_kl ∀(k,l)≠(i,j)). S=min_{1≤k≤11} X_k, X∼Dirichlet(α), "Dirichlet–Minimum" S∼DirichletMin(α). MeanVar=Form(last 3)−λ·SD(points last 3), λ=0.5. G_i=β0+β1E+β2C+β3V_μ+β4Σ̄²+ε_i; quadratic: +β2C². Assumptions: lognormal scores with equicorrelation; analytical players' beliefs are noisy Dirichlet around true win probs; impact-player scores independent of team scores; lowest-contributor share IID DirichletMin across teams; no equations for the real-data bots beyond the stated rules.

## 5. Features / target
Inputs: expert-team score distributions (means, SDs, correlations), number of experts, common-player counts, strategy definitions (Career/Form/Tournament/MeanVar statistics). Targets: per-team win probabilities π_i / π_ij; user-level mean winnings; strategy-level Average Gain over Random (matchwise, tournament). Prediction horizon: single contest / single match; tournament aggregation over the final 10 IPL matches.

## 6. Validation design
Simulation: 100,000 draws for π_i estimation, 10,000 contest iterations per configuration; three team-strength configurations (Equi-mean_Equivariance [500×4, sd 60]; Unequal-mean_Equivariance means [440,473,517,550] sd 60; Unequal-mean_Unequal-std sd [60,30,60,30]); sweeps τ∈{0.1,0.2,0.3,0.4} at β=0.04 and β∈{0.02,…,0.05} at τ=0.2; correlation sweeps; impact-player configurations (IID [45×4,sd 10]; Different_mean [35,40,45,50]; Different_mean_and_std sd [5,10,15,20]). Real data: final 10 IPL 2024 matches, expert teams from bots, 10,000 runs per (k∈{2,3,4} experts × n∈{5,…,9} common players) configuration vs 16,000 random users. Baselines: random (uniform) selection throughout; IID impact-player config as variance baseline (F-statistic denominator). No holdout prediction of match outcomes; the "validation" is of the contest-design hypotheses, not of a predictive model.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
- Baseline sims: analytical players concentrate on the best (4th) team (high selection ratio) in unequal-mean configs; in symmetric configs they behave like random players. Picking the best team yields LOWER mean winnings — crowding dilutes the prize (more selectors split the pool). Fewer analytical players (τ=0.1) → strongest skill advantage; higher τ dilutes payouts; smaller β (better estimates) → higher mean winnings, though the β effect is mild.
- Experts/correlation: 4 experts → higher mean winnings for the best team than 2–3; skill advantage generally falls as ρ rises, EXCEPT in unequal-mean cases where very high correlation makes teams clearly differentiable (paper's Rohit Sharma vs Rinku Singh example).
- Impact player: F-statistic (total variance vs IID) = 28.18735 (Different_mean) and 22.63239 (Different_mean_and_std) — both ≫1, i.e., skill-asymmetric impact players greatly increase outcome dispersion.
- Real IPL 2024: skill presence "limited" — many configurations show NEGATIVE average gain over random for skill strategies. Gain increases with experts, inverted-U in common players (rises then falls). With impact player, gain distributions shift positive and skill strategies clearly beat random; the common-player inverted-U flips to U-shaped (paper attributes to averaging over wide-margin wins).
- Regressions: #experts coefficient +17 to +22, p<0.001 in every spec (StrictOnForm: 20.737, p=0.000; IP StrictOnForm: 21.727/19.109, p=0.000; OneThirdForm: 17.839/15.558, p=0.000; IP OneThirdForm: 17.056/19.001, p=0.000). Between-team variance V_μ positive when significant (0.030/0.028, p=0.000 for StrictOnForm; 0.038, p=0.000 for IP). Within-team variance mostly insignificant. Quadratic (IP OneThirdForm): Common Players 12.375 (p=0.075), C² −0.448 (p=0.053) — inverted-U confirmed. R² ≈ 0.15–0.355. Paper's interpretation: skill measurable, structurally mediated; platform design (expert count, overlap, impact player) controls skill visibility.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- Simulation "results" are artifacts of assumed distributions (lognormal scores, Dirichlet beliefs calibrated by construction) — the finding "analytical players outperform" is nearly tautological given the belief model is centered on the true win probabilities; the paper's real contribution is the crowding-dilution observation and the inverted-U, which are structural.
- The real-data exercise is heavily synthetic: bots build expert teams, bots play the users, final-10-match window, 1,000-vs-16,000 user counts, p=0.05 perturbation — it is a simulation wearing real data's jersey, not an empirical measurement of human skill.
- Regression R² 0.15–0.355 (paper's own "moderate"); many covariates borderline significant; the "aberration" of the U-shaped common-player effect under impact players is hand-waved.
- Prize math assumes fixed 20% platform cut and Rs. 25 entry; results are denominated in this specific payoff structure.
- NFL external validity: none for win/spread/total modeling. The one transferable mechanism — shared-prize-pool crowding making best-team selection less profitable — is directly relevant to DFS tournament theory (Garrett's corpus gap #10: "academic contest-theory / ownership-game equilibrium papers are absent"), but this paper does not model ownership equilibrium or NFL DFS.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
REJECT for implementation. The paper's claims are (a) simulation artifacts of its own assumptions, (b) built on synthetic bot-vs-bot IPL exercises, and (c) in a domain (fantasy cricket contest design) with no NFL transfer path. If the DFS lane ever wants an ownership-equilibrium literature review, revisit with the Getty et al. (2018) and Haugh & Singal (2019) references it cites — not this paper.
