# [0239] Multivariate Conway-Maxwell-Poisson Distribution: Sarmanov Method and Doubly-Intractable Bayesian Inference (arXiv:2107.07561v1)

**Citation:** Piancastelli, L.S.C., Friel, N., Barreto-Souza, W., & Ombao, H. (2021). *Multivariate Conway-Maxwell-Poisson Distribution: Sarmanov Method and Doubly-Intractable Bayesian Inference*. arXiv:2107.07561v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07561
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 10421 lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — the modified-Sarmanov multivariate COM-Poisson construction is a genuinely portable tool for joint (home score, away score) count modeling with flexible positive/negative correlation and per-margin dispersion; GSE should adapt the distributional construction, not the paper's heavy GIMH/noisy-exchange Bayesian machinery (overkill for GSE's purposes — reimplement via two-step likelihood/moment matching instead).

## 1. Research question
How to construct a genuinely multivariate count distribution with Conway-Maxwell-Poisson (COM-Poisson) marginals that (a) permits different dispersion parameters per margin (unlike Sellers et al. 2016, which forces one shared dispersion and non-negative correlation, and whose marginals are not even COM-Poisson), (b) supports a flexible covariance structure with both negative and positive off-diagonal entries, and (c) scales beyond bivariate; and how to do Bayesian inference for it given the likelihood depends on intractable normalizing constants (a doubly-intractable posterior). Applied to quantify the COVID-19 crowdless-matches effect on Premier League home advantage 2018–2021.

## 2. Dataset / schema
- **Premier League match scores:** 2018–2019, 2019–2020, 2020–2021 seasons, 380 matches/season → 1140 observations total: 668 pre-pandemic matches with crowds, 472 matches during the pandemic with no crowds (league resumed June 17, 2020 after break from March 9, 2020). Key columns per match: home goals (X₁), away goals (X₂), Home indicator (≡1 for home team), Pandemic indicator (1 if played during crowdless period). Access: not stated in paper (no URL given; described as match outcomes data, effectively proprietary scrape).
- **Simulation datasets:** two bivariate synthetic sets (n=200 each) and one trivariate synthetic set (n=500), generated via the paper's Algorithm 1.
- **Shunters accident dataset** (Arbous & Kerrich 1951): analyzed only in the Supplementary Material ("available from the authors upon request"), not in the main extract.

## 3. Method / model
- **Modified Sarmanov construction:** proposes a d-dimensional joint density f(x₁,…,x_d) = {∏ f_i(x_i)} · {1 + C(d,2)⁻¹ Σ_{j<k} δ_{jk} φ_j(x_j) φ_k(x_k)}, a parsimonious version of Lee (1996) that drops all ≥3-way interaction terms (δ_{j₁…j_l}=0 for l≥3) so the non-negativity constraint reduces to the well-known bivariate Sarmanov constraints per pair. Exponential kernel φ_i(x) = e^{−ωx} − Ψ_i with Ψ_i = E(e^{−ωX_i}) (Laplace transform), ω>0.
- **MultCOMP distribution (Definition 3.1):** plug COM-Poisson(λ_i, ν_i) marginals into the modified Sarmanov construction; θ = (λ, ν, δ, ω)ᵀ. Since A_{jk}>0 in the correlation formula, sign(δ_{jk}) sets sign of correlation; δ_{jk}=0 gives independence.
- **Ratio-of-normalizing-constants estimation:** r_j = Z(e^{−ω}λ_j, ν_j)/Z(λ_j, ν_j) estimated by (a) importance sampling — simple IS won (smallest MC standard deviation across n_total 10K–200K, 200 replications; IS even beats TINT-trapezium at high ω=3), and (b) thermodynamic integration (TINT-prior with uniform prior on τ, TINT-trapezium with power-fraction schedule τ_i = ω(i/n_rungs)^5, n_rungs = ⌈ω/0.1⌉ or ⌈ω/0.05⌉).
- **Sampler (Algorithm 1):** draw X₁ from its COM-Poisson marginal via Benson & Friel (2020) fast-rejection sampler, then sample sequentially from conditionals p(x_j | x_{j−1},…,x₁) using plugged-in r̂_j and a truncation grid K (minimum K increased until successive-probability difference < tolerance); recommended n_draws > 130K for IS ratio estimates.
- **Bayesian inference (two competing MCMC strategies):**
  - **GIMH (pseudo-marginal):** unbiased likelihood estimator via two independent auxiliary-draw sets (N_z draws to estimate z_j^{−1} via envelope acceptance ratio M̂^{(N)}/B; N_r draws to estimate r_j via IS). Single-site updates: log-normal proposals for λ_j, ν_j, ω; truncated-normal proposals for δ_{jk} in interval (10). Validity check that proposed λ′ keeps every δ inside (10). Adaptation phase tunes N_z targeting log-likelihood estimator SD of 1.2 (per Doucet et al. 2015); N_r fixed at 10K.
  - **Noisy exchange algorithm:** Murray et al. (2006) exchange adapted with auxiliary draws simulated via Algorithm 1 (inexact — no exact sampler exists) and q̂(X|θ, r̂) in place of q(X|θ); recycles r̂ estimates across iterations. Avoids computing ẑ^{−1} but is asymptotically inexact.
- **Premier League application:** bivariate MultCOMP regression: log λ_{1i} = γ₀ + γ₁·Home_i + γ₂·Pandemic_i; log λ_{2i} = γ₀ (common intercept à la Karlis & Ntzoufras; Home_i ≡ 1 always, so γ₁ = home main effect, γ₂ = pandemic effect on home advantage). GIMH, 5 parallel chains, 100K combined posterior draws, N_z ≈ 170K average after adaptation. Model comparison via PSIS-LOO (Vehtari et al. 2017) against a full model (pandemic effect on away goals + correlation), a no-pandemic model, and a bivariate Poisson special case (ν₁=ν₂=1). Posterior predictive checks with 100K replicated datasets each for average home goals and home surplus pre/during pandemic.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Core mathematics, quoted faithfully:
- COM-Poisson pmf: p(x|λ,ν) = λ^x / ((x!)^ν Z(λ,ν)), x ∈ ℕ₀; Z(λ,ν) = Σ_{x=0}^∞ λ^x/(x!)^ν (eqs. 1–2). Overdispersed ν<1, underdispersed ν>1, ν=1 → Poisson; ν=0, λ<1 → geometric; ν→∞ limit → Bernoulli(λ/(λ+1)).
- Sarmanov bivariate: f(x₁,x₂) = f₁(x₁)f₂(x₂){1 + δφ₁(x₁)φ₂(x₂)} (eq. 3) with ∫ f_i φ_i dμ_i = 0 and 1+δφ₁φ₂ ≥ 0 ∀(x₁,x₂).
- δ validity range (exponential kernel): −1/max{(1−Ψ₁)(1−Ψ₂), Ψ₁Ψ₂} < δ < 1/max{Ψ₁(1−Ψ₂), Ψ₂(1−Ψ₁)} (eq. 10).
- Modified multivariate Sarmanov (eq. 8): f(x₁,…,x_d) = {∏ f_i(x_i)}{1 + C(d,2)⁻¹ Σ_{j<k} δ_{jk} φ_j(x_j) φ_k(x_k)}, with the identity C(d,2) + Σ Σ δ_{jk}φ_jφ_k = Σ Σ {1 + δ_{jk}φ_jφ_k} (eq. 9) guaranteeing validity from per-pair constraints.
- MultCOMP joint pmf (eq. 12): f(x|θ) = {∏_{i=1}^d λ_i^{x_i}/((x_i!)^{ν_i} Z(λ_i,ν_i))} · {1 + C(d,2)⁻¹ Σ_{j<k} δ_{jk} (e^{−ωx_j} − Z(e^{−ω}λ_j,ν_j)/Z(λ_j,ν_j))(e^{−ωx_k} − Z(e^{−ω}λ_k,ν_k)/Z(λ_k,ν_k))}.
- Pairwise correlation (eq. 13): corr(X_j,X_k) = δ_{jk}·C(d,2)⁻¹·A_{jk}/√(ζ′(λ_j,ν_j)ζ′(λ_k,ν_k)), with A_{jk}>0 defined via Z′ (series Σ_{x≥1} xλ^{x−1}/(x!)^ν), ζ = ∂logZ/∂λ (mean), ζ′ (variance).
- IS ratio estimator (eq. 14): r̂_IS = (1/N)Σ_{i=1}^N q(x_i|e^{−ω}λ,ν)/q(x_i|λ,ν), unbiased (proof shown).
- TINT log-ratio: log r = ∫₀^ω E_τ[U(x,τ)] dτ, U(x,τ) = ω^{−1}(log q_ω(x) − log q₀(x)); trapezoid estimator log r̂ = Σ_{i=1}^{n_rungs}(τ_i−τ_{i−1})(E_{τ_{i−1}}[U] + E_{τ_i}[U])/2.
- GIMH envelope estimator of 1/Z(λ,ν): M̂^{(N)}/B, B = normalizing constant of Poisson envelope (ν≥1) or geometric envelope (ν<1).
- Regression structure (eq. 16): log λ_{1i} = γ₀ + γ₁·Home_i + γ₂·Pandemic_i; log λ_{2i} = γ₀.
- Exchange acceptance (Algorithm 4): π = min{1, p(θ′)q(X|θ′)q(X′|θ^{(t)})h(θ^{(t)}|θ′)/[p(θ^{(t)})q(X|θ^{(t)})q(X′|θ′)h(θ′|θ^{(t)})]} with Z-terms cancelling.
**Assumptions:** matches independent (iid across matches, conditional on covariates); common intercept γ₀ for both teams (home/away symmetric baseline); Ψ_i (Laplace transforms) computable — handled by truncation/IS in practice; ω>0 shared across margins; for the noisy exchange, approximate sampler error tolerated (asymptotically inexact); PSIS-LOO assumed reliable despite importance weights; truncated-K normalization approximates full support.

## 5. Features / target
Not an ML prediction paper: no feature matrix. Inputs are covariates of a Bayesian regression: Home_i indicator (≡1) and Pandemic_i indicator (1 = crowdless match). Target: (X_{1i}, X_{2i}) = (home goals, away goals) per match i=1,…,1140, modeled jointly as MultCOMP(λ_i, ν, δ, ω). In the model-comparison arm, alternative structures: component-specific pandemic effect on away goals (log λ_{2i} = γ₀ + γ₃·Pandemic_i) and pandemic effect on dependence (log γ = α₀ + α₁·Pandemic_i, α₁ concentrated at zero → no correlation shift).

## 6. Validation design
- **Simulation validation:** Config 1 (n=200; λ=(1,1.5), ν=(0.4,0.8), ω=2, δ=3; empirical means (1.745,1.745), variances (2.915,1.990), corr 0.340) and Config 2 (n=200; λ=(1,2), ν=(0.8,1.5), ω=1.5, δ=−2; corr −0.258, means (1.070,1.420), variances (1.141,0.968)). Five parallel chains of GIMH and noisy exchange, 30K iterations each, first 10K burn-in, 100K combined draws; Brooks-Gelman-Rubin R̂≈1 required. Priors: λ_j ~ Gamma(2,2), ν_j ~ Gamma(1.5,2), ω ~ Gamma(2,0.8), δ ~ Truncated-Normal(0,5). N_z adaptation targeted likelihood SD 1.2 → N_z≈18K (Config 1), ≈15K (Config 2); N_r=10K. Trivariate experiment: n=500, λ=(1.5,1,0.5), ν=(1,0.5,0.8), ω=3, δ₁₂=3.5, δ₁₃=−2.5, δ₂₃=−3; empirical correlations (0.167, −0.165, −0.191); N_z≈15K.
- **Real data:** no train/test split (full Bayesian fit on all 1140 matches; no holdout). Model selection via PSIS-LOO (in-sample penalized predictive criterion, not out-of-time backtest). Posterior predictive checks replicate 100K datasets per model.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- **No train/test separation anywhere:** the Premier League analysis is a full-sample Bayesian fit; all model comparison rests on PSIS-LOO (in-sample approximation) and posterior predictive checks on the same data. Nothing demonstrates out-of-sample goal forecasting skill.
- **Team strength ignored:** 1140 matches pooled across 20 teams with only Home/Pandemic covariates — no team-specific attack/defence strengths (contrast Dixon & Coles, Karlis & Ntzoufras with team effects). The "home advantage" estimate is marginal over team quality; any pandemic-period team-quality compositional shift confounds γ₂. Matches assumed independent — no time structure, no relegation/promotion, no schedule-strength.
- **COVID period assignment is coarse:** "during-pandemic" is a single indicator over Jun 2020–May 2021; the 2019–20 resumption and full 2020–21 empty-stadium season are pooled.
- **Adversarial notes on the method:** the correlation range supported is still limited (ω widens it non-linearly but plateaus — heavy-tailed ω posterior shows weak identification); the parsimonious Sarmanov form (no ≥3-way terms) is a convenience restriction, not a modeling choice; noisy exchange is asymptotically inexact with unquantified bias; GIMH requires N_z≈170K auxiliary draws per update in the real application — computationally heavy for production use.
- **External validity to NFL:** goals are low-count Poisson-like outcomes; NFL team scores are sums over ~60 plays with mean ≈ 24 and roughly symmetric distributions — the COM-Poisson machinery is built for low-count over/underdispersed counts and is a poor fit for NFL point totals. The transfer value is the joint-construction technique, not the fitted model.

- **Duplicate-adjacent:** the repo already inventories Dixon-Coles, Skellam, Poisson, Harville (existing-research-map §1, "Metrics inventoried") — bivariate score modeling for soccer is a known commodity, and this paper itself cites Dixon & Coles (1997) and Karlis & Ntzoufras (2000, 2003). The existing map's GAP LIST does not list multivariate count-model construction; iWinRNFL (1704.00197) covers in-game WP, not joint score distributions.
- **Verdict on overlap:** EXTENSION, not duplicate — no in-repo work models a joint distribution over correlated count outcomes with per-margin dispersion control and a proper Bayesian treatment. The closest analogues are Dixon-Coles-style bivariate Poisson (soccer) and Skellam (score differentials). The transferable piece is the Sarmanov-with-exponential-kernel construction itself, which is sport-agnostic.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Do NOT port the MCMC. Adapt the construction as a fast two-step estimator for joint low-count outcomes:
1. **Target use cases (soccer-adjacent only — NFL scores are too high-count):** (a) exact-score and correlated-total surfaces for soccer content/leagues GSE covers in DFS-adjacent products; (b) joint modeling of (home goals, away goals) for totals + moneyline correlation, e.g., P(total>2.5 AND home win) which independent-Poisson and Dixon-Coles approximate; (c) more relevant to NFL: a joint model over (team A explosive plays, team B explosive plays) or red-zone trip counts — low-count events where negative in-game dependence exists (possessions are a zero-sum clock resource) and COM-Poisson's overdispersion handling beats Poisson.
2. **Estimator:** per-margin COM-Poisson MLE (λ_i, ν_i) via truncated-Z Newton (standard, cheap), then δ/ω by moment-matching on the empirical pairwise correlation using eq. (13) (linear in δ_{jk} given margins), constrained to interval (10). Grid over ω ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2} maximizing held-out joint log-likelihood. This is minutes of CPU vs the paper's 100K-draw GIMH runs with 170K auxiliary draws.
3. **Data:** nflverse play-by-play for event counts (explosive plays, red-zone trips, turnovers); historical odds APIs for market comparison; season-slice refits (rolling 5-week windows).
4. **Serving:** precompute joint probability tables P(X₁=k₁, X₂=k₂) per matchup on a K=15×15 grid at publish time; downstream derived markets (totals, spreads, exact scores) are table lookups.
5. **Effort:** ~2–3 days for the two-step estimator + evaluation harness; ~1 day to add the GSE publishing adapter. Skip the Bayesian machinery entirely unless uncertainty quantification on δ is needed.

## 12. Reproducible test
- **Dataset:** nflverse play-by-play 2021–2024 regular seasons; per-team-game counts of explosive plays (rush ≥10 yds + pass ≥15 yds) for both teams → paired count observations (≈ 2,144 team-game pairs... state exactly: each game yields one paired observation (home explosive plays, away explosive plays)).
- **Baseline:** independent Poisson with per-team λ estimated from rolling 5-week means.
- **Test:** MultCOMP two-step fit (COM-Poisson margins + δ/ω moment-match) vs independent-Poisson on mean joint log-likelihood of the held-out paired counts; evaluation window: 2024 season weeks 1–18, refit weekly with data through week w−1.
- **Metric:** mean per-observation joint log-likelihood difference (MultCOMP minus baseline), plus calibration of derived P(home out-gains away) vs realized frequency in decile bins.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT the adapted estimator if, on the 2024 holdout window, MultCOMP beats independent Poisson by ≥ 0.01 nats/observation in mean joint log-likelihood AND the derived P(home out-gains away) calibration slope in decile bins is within [0.9, 1.1]; REJECT if either fails, or if the fitted ν̂'s are ≈1 everywhere (then it reduces to Poisson and adds nothing) or the empirical home–away count correlation is within ±0.02 of zero (no dependence worth modeling).

## 14. Improvement experiment
Go beyond the paper with a **covariate-dependent δ**: let the dependence parameter vary with game state proxies (pregame spread, total, pace) via δ_{jk}(z) = clip(δ₀ + βᵀz) inside the per-pair validity interval (10) — the paper only lets ω widen correlation and treats δ as constant. Rationale: home–away event-count dependence should intensify in slow-paced, low-total games (fewer total events → stronger zero-sum competition for them) and weaken in shootouts. Fit by profile held-out likelihood on the 2024 window; compare against constant-δ MultCOMP with the same §13 gate.
