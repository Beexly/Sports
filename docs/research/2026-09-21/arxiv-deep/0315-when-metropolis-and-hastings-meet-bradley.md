# [0315] When Metropolis and Hastings Meet Bradley and Terry: Exact MCMC From Preference Voting (arXiv:2609.00905v1)

**Citation:** Ariel Smogorghevski, Nir Rosenfeld, Yaniv Romano (2026). *When Metropolis and Hastings Meet Bradley and Terry: Exact MCMC From Preference Voting*. arXiv:2609.00905v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00905v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 2817 lines, main paper + appendices A–E with full proofs).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — the exact N-vote preference sampler is a genuine new capability (GSE inventories Bradley-Terry but has no exact preference-only MCMC); port it as a diverse-portfolio/lineup sampler driven by analyst pairwise preferences, not as a probability estimator, with the BT-model and judge-independence assumptions treated as the binding risks.

## 1. Research question
The paper asks: can you run an exact Metropolis–Hastings sampler targeting π(x) ∝ p0(x)·pJ(M|x) — a base generative distribution tilted toward a property M as judged by a judge J — when the judge only returns binary pairwise preferences (x vs. x′) and never a pointwise score? It proves an impossibility result (no fixed-budget method can reproduce the oracle BT-MH acceptance for every unknown preference probability), then constructs the N-vote rule that is exact for any N ≥ 1 and Peskun–Tierney optimal among exact fixed-budget rules.

## 2. Dataset / schema
Five experimental settings, no single dataset: (a) synthetic — finite state space X = {0,…,240}, non-uniform base p0 (Gaussian-like, centered 68, scale 56) with a 3-mode preference tilt, 150,000 MH steps; (b) multi-condition text — 50 scenario prompts, opening paragraphs ≤192 tokens, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as base/judge/evaluator, 300 steps per chain, 50 burn-in, final 50 retained; (c) image generation — SDXL-Turbo latents (512×512), Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct judge, 3,000 steps, Gemma-3-12B-IT + Aesthetic Predictor V2.5 as held-out evaluators; (d) molecular design — MARS fragment editing (1,000 ChEMBL fragments, ≤10 heavy atoms, ≤40 heavy atoms/molecule), 200 parallel chains × 1,000 steps × 5 seeds, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507 judge, MolSkill as held-out evaluator; (e) machine translation — WMT23 de→en (549 sentences) and en→de (557), TowerInstruct-7B-v0.2 base, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct judge, CometKiwi-XL QE signal, 128 steps per chain. Access: all models/datasets are public or standard research artifacts (licenses listed in Appx. C.6).

## 3. Method / model
Core construction: for a proposed move x→x′, query the judge N times on the pair (x,x′); let K ~ Binomial(N, pJ(x≺x′)) be votes favoring the proposal; accept with the exact N-vote rule α_N(x,x′;K) = min{1, r0(x,x′)·K/(N−K+1)}, where r0(x,x′) = p0(x′)q(x|x′)/[p0(x)q(x′|x)]. Multi-judge extension (Algorithm 2): query each of m judges N times independently and multiply the vote factors, α = min{1, r0·∏ᵢ Kᵢ/(N−Kᵢ+1)}, targeting the composite πm(x) ∝ p0(x)∏ᵢ pJi(Mi|x). Proposals used: synthetic (0.88 global uniform + 0.12 local nearest-neighbor); text (0.10 independent refresh + 0.90 suffix regeneration); image (0.6 local pCN β=0.08 + 0.3 wide pCN β=0.25 + 0.1 refresh, all prior-reversible so r0=1); molecules (MARS fragment add/delete); MT (suffix regeneration with r0 = n/n′). Compute: MT ~15h per direction on 6×H200; image ~6h; text ~10–12h single GPU; molecules ~48h — judge queries dominate.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Paper's equations (copied faithfully): Bradley-Terry odds `pJ(x≺x′)/(1−pJ(x≺x′)) = exp(s(x′)−s(x))`; judge model `pJ(x≺x′) = σ(s(x′)−s(x))`; exact N-vote acceptance `α_N(x,x′;K) = min{1, r0(x,x′)·K/(N−K+1)}`; multi-judge `α = min{1, r0(x,x′)·∏ᵢ₌₁ᵐ Kᵢ/(N−Kᵢ+1)}`; plug-in (shown invalid) `α_plug-in = min{1, r0·K/(N−K)}`; target `π(x) ∝ p0(x)·pJ(M|x)` with `pJ(M|x) ∝ exp(s(x))`; composite `πm(x) ∝ p0(x)∏ᵢ pJi(Mi|x)`. Theorem 1: no fixed-budget procedure using only N binary outcomes can have marginal acceptance equal to min{1, c·p/(1−p)} for every p∈(0,1) — proof: the marginal acceptance AN(p) is a polynomial of degree ≤N, but matching c·p/(1−p) on (0,1/(1+c)) forces the polynomial B(p)=(1−p)AN(p)−cp to be identically zero, contradicting B(1)=−c≠0. Theorem 2: the N-vote rule satisfies detailed balance w.r.t. π for every N≥1 (proved via the binomial-coefficient identity C(N,ji−1)=C(N,ji)·ji/(N−ji+1) and the min{1,A}=A·min{1,A⁻¹} symmetry). Theorem 3/Corollary 1-2: Peskun–Tierney optimality among exact fixed-budget rules; TV convergence from every initialization under proposal regularity (Assumption 1: π-irreducibility, compatible support, q(·|x)≪π). Stated assumptions: (a) judges follow the BT model with finite latent scores (so pJ∈(0,1)); (b) judges are conditionally independent given x (multi-judge factorization); (c) the proposal kernel satisfies Assumption 1. Unstated-but-load-bearing: the judge's pairwise preferences are transitive/consistent enough for the BT model to be well-specified — real LLM/analyst judges routinely violate this.

## 5. Features / target
Not a feature/target paper in the ML sense. The "target" is the tilted distribution π(x) ∝ p0(x)·exp(s(x)) (single judge) or πm(x) ∝ p0(x)·exp(Σᵢ sᵢ(x)) (multi-judge). The observable inputs are binary votes K ~ Binomial(N, pJ(x≺x′)) per proposal. The sampler's output is a Markov chain whose stationary distribution is exactly π (for any fixed N ≥ 1), achieved without ever estimating s(x) pointwise.

## 6. Validation design
Synthetic: known target, TV distance of cumulative empirical distribution to truth every 500 steps over 150,000 steps, N∈{1,2,4}; baselines: oracle MH (exact scores) and the invalid plug-in rule (same budget). Text: 50 prompts × 300 steps; held-out pointwise (1–10) and pairwise BT-coefficient evaluation; baselines: base LLM, pointwise-MH (sep./joint). Image: 3,000-step trajectories; held-out Gemma-3-12B-IT three-constraint satisfaction + Aesthetic Predictor V2.5; baselines: base, pointwise-MH. Molecules: 200 chains × 1,000 steps × 5 seeds; metrics QED, SA (rescaled), Tanimoto diversity, held-out MolSkill; baselines: MARS, pointwise-MH. MT: WMT23 both directions, chain-mean protocol (32 burn-in, 16 retained states), sacrebleu BLEU/chrF/TER + XCOMET-XL, paired bootstrap (10,000 resamples) at 95%; baselines: QUEST at 6 temperatures, pointwise-MH. Splits are experimental-design splits, not time series; no leakage concern of the usual kind.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
(a) The BT assumption is the load-bearing idealization: real judges (LLMs, human analysts) show intransitivity, position bias, and prompt sensitivity that break pJ(x≺x′)=σ(s(x′)−s(x)); exactness holds w.r.t. the assumed model, not w.r.t. reality. (b) Judge independence across the m judges is assumed for the product target — correlated judges double-count evidence. (c) Cost: every MH step costs N judge queries (N=3–9 in experiments); the MT experiment took ~15h per direction on 6 H200s — expensive sampling for production use. (d) The impossibility theorem cuts both ways: you cannot do better than the N-vote rule within the exact fixed-budget class, but inexact methods (e.g., learned reward models) may mix faster in practice. (e) External validity to NFL: the paper never touches sports; the transfer is by analogy (preference-guided generation), and the sampler produces samples from a tilted distribution — it does not estimate probabilities, so it cannot calibrate or validate the GSE engine. (f) Multi-judge experiments use N=3–9 votes per judge per step; with analyst judges (humans), per-step query budgets are infeasible — the realistic judge is an LLM-as-judge, with all its biases.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Narrow, well-scoped port — preference-guided diverse lineup portfolios: (a) base distribution p0 = GSE's existing DFS optimizer output distribution over lineups (or the engine's projected-points distribution); (b) judges = 2–4 LLM-as-judge prompts encoding analyst preferences the optimizer can't see (e.g., "prefer lineups with correlated stacks," "prefer contrarian low-ownership constructions," "avoid highly duplicated cores") — pairwise comparisons of proposed vs. current lineup; (c) proposal kernel = single-player swaps / stack swaps from the current lineup (symmetric or with computable Hastings ratio); (d) run Pref-MH with N=3–5 votes per judge per step for a few hundred steps per slate, keep the chain's distinct high-quality states as the portfolio; (e) the BT/plurality assumptions are monitored, not trusted: track judge self-consistency (repeat-pair agreement rate) and abort if <70%; (f) serving — offline, weekly, before slate lock; outputs feed the existing optimizer pool; (g) estimated effort: 2–3 weeks (paper's code is public; the work is in the proposal kernel + judge prompts + consistency monitoring). Explicitly NOT for: win probabilities, edge estimation, or anything downstream of the money.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: one full NFL DFS slate (use a completed 2026 season slate with known outcomes and known winning-lineup ownership data). Metric: portfolio-level — fraction of chain-sampled lineups that are "diverse and viable" (pairwise lineup overlap ≤ 6/9 players AND projected points within 5% of the optimizer's best), plus realized best-lineup percentile vs. the field. Baseline to beat: GSE's current optimizer pool generation (no preference sampling). Window: 4 consecutive completed slates. The test measures diversification quality, not predictive accuracy.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
Adopt Pref-MH portfolio sampling only if, on 4 completed slates: (a) ≥80% of retained chain states meet the diverse-and-viable bar (overlap ≤6/9, within 5% of best projection); AND (b) judge self-consistency on repeated pairs ≥70% (else the BT assumption is rejected and the sampler is unsound); AND (c) the portfolio's realized best lineup beats the optimizer-pool baseline's best by ≥10 percentile points on at least 3 of 4 slates. If judge consistency fails, reject regardless of portfolio outcomes — an unsound sampler must not drive lineup decisions.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Beyond the paper: make the judge budget adaptive — spend N=1 vote per step during burn-in/exploration and escalate to N=5–9 only when the chain is near high-quality regions (detected via running projected-points quantile), then test whether adaptive budgeting reaches the same portfolio quality with ≤50% of judge queries. Rationale: the paper fixes N for all steps, but Theorem 2 guarantees exactness for any N ≥ 1 at every step, so a state-dependent N schedule preserves exactness while attacking the paper's main practical cost (judge queries dominate runtime). If it works, it makes analyst-in-the-loop (human-judge) variants feasible for small N.
