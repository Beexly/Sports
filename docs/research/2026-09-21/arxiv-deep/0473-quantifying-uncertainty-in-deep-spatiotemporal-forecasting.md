# [0473] Quantifying Uncertainty in Deep Spatiotemporal Forecasting (arXiv:2105.11982v2)

**Citation:** Dongxia Wu, Liyao Gao, Xinyue Xiong, Matteo Chinazzi, Alessandro Vespignani, Yi-An Ma, Rose Yu (2021). *Quantifying Uncertainty in Deep Spatiotemporal Forecasting*. KDD '21, DOI 10.1145/3447548.3467325. arXiv:2105.11982v2. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.11982v2
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 4,363 lines; main text + Appendix A proofs + Appendix B experiment details read sequentially).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — adopt the MIS (Mean Interval Score) metric as GSE's standard proper score for interval forecasts (props, totals, win-total bands), and the paper's frequentist-vs-Bayesian recipe as the selection rule for GSE's next uncertainty engine. MC dropout and naive bootstrap are ruled out for GSE by this paper's results.

## 1. Research question
How should uncertainty be quantified for deep spatiotemporal forecasting? The paper gives the first systematic benchmark of frequentist (Bootstrap, Quantile, Spline-Quantile, MIS regression) vs Bayesian (MC dropout, SG-MCMC) UQ methods on grid-based (ConvLSTM) and graph-based (DCRNN) multi-step forecasting, unified through statistical decision theory, with a practitioner's recipe.

## 2. Dataset / schema
Three real datasets: (i) **Air quality PM2.5** (grid, KDD Cup 2018 Beijing): hourly weather (21×31×5 tensor: temp, pressure, humidity, wind dir/speed) + 35 station PM2.5 mapped to grid by inverse-distance weighting (eq. 12); (ii) **METR-LA traffic** (graph, 207 loop sensors, 4 months speeds; spatial correlation A_ij=exp(−d(v_i,v_j)/σ²)); (iii) **COVID-19 incident deaths** (graph, 50 US states, May 24–Sep 12 2020; reported JHU deaths vs GLEAM mechanistic-model predictions; spatial A = daily air-passenger traffic OAG/IATA). No download URLs for the assembled splits. Models: ConvLSTM (3×3 filter, 256 hidden, 2 layers) for grid; DCRNN for graph.

## 3. Method / model
Six UQ adaptations: (1) **Bootstrap**: drop 50% of training data per round, retrain, 25 resamples; quantiles from order statistics. (2) **MIS regression**: multi-head net outputs u(x), l(x), f(x); loss L_MIS = (u−l) + (2/ρ)(y−u)1{y>u} + (2/ρ)(l−y)1{y<l} + |y−f| (eq. 7); directly minimizes the 95% interval score. (3) **Quantile regression**: pinball loss L_Quantile = (y−f)(ρ−1{y<f}) at ρ∈{0.025,0.5,0.975} (eq. 8); warned of quantile crossing with limited data/capacity. (4) **Spline Quantile (SQ)**: monotone piecewise-linear quantile function (11 params: intercept, 5 slopes, 5 knot positions), CRPS loss (Gasthaus et al. 2019) — fixes crossing. (5) **MC dropout**: test-time dropout 5%, 50 forward passes, 10 trials averaged (Zhu & Laptev 2017 style). (6) **SG-MCMC**: SGNHT updates θ_{k+1}=θ_k+p_k h; p_{k+1}=p_k−∇ℒ̃(θ)h−ξ_k p_k h+N(0,2Ah); ξ_{k+1}=ξ_k+(p_kᵀp_k/d−1)h; learning rate h=5e−4, prior θ∼N(0,4.0), init θ₀∼N(0,0.2), early stop epoch 50, 25 parallel posterior samples. Unified decision-theoretic framework: frequentist minimizes E[‖f(θ̂)−f(θ)‖²|θ] (eq. 5), Bayesian minimizes E_θ[‖f(θ̂)−f(θ)‖²|{𝒳}] ⇒ posterior mean f (eq. 6).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
MIS_N(u,l;ρ) = N⁻¹Σ_i{(u−l) + (2/ρ)(z_i−u)1{z_i>u} + (2/ρ)(l−z_i)1{z_i<l}}; large-sample limit MIS_∞(u,l,ρ) = (u−l) + (2/ρ)(E[Z−u|Z>u] + E[l−Z|Z<l]). Proposition 1: argmin MIS_∞ gives the (1−ρ) confidence interval; Proposition 2: argmin MIS_N gives the (1−ρ) quantile of the empirical sample. ConvLSTM (2): h_{t+1}=σ(W^h∗h_t+W^x∗X_t); graph ConvRNN (3): h_{t+1}=σ(W∗_g h_t+W∗_g X_t), W∗_g X_t = W·(D⁻¹A)·X_t (4). Assumptions: data (𝒳,𝒴) from p(𝒴|𝒳;θ), θ∼p(θ); MIS consistency needs a density; minibatches treated i.i.d. (authors flag this as a limitation for long graph time series); COVID deep model trains on residual (reported − GLEAM) because raw counts had too few samples.

## 5. Features / target
Features: lagged spatiotemporal tensors/graph signals + exogenous drivers (weather for PM2.5, GLEAM baseline for COVID). Target: H-step-ahead continuous outcomes (PM2.5 μg/m³, traffic speed mph, incident deaths). For NFL transfer: features = team/game state, target = points, margin, total — continuous; intervals = the natural UQ object for props/totals.

## 6. Validation design
Benchmark across all three tasks at multiple horizons (PM2.5 12/24/48h; traffic 15/30/60min; COVID 1–4 weeks). Metrics: MAE (point), MIS at 95%, interval width. Implementation detail: V100 GPUs; computation budget normalized to parallel cores (Table 1: Bootstrap 25, Quantile 1, SQ 1, MIS 1, MC Dropout 1, SG-MCMC 25 for one confidence level; quantile/MIS cost multiplies by n confidence levels). No statistical significance tests reported.

Paper's own caption: SG-MCMC means are much closer to ground truth but often come with narrower credible intervals (a coverage trade-off consistent with the MIS results). Headline findings: Bayesian SG-MCMC best/robust mean predictions (posterior mean), frequentist quantile/MIS-reg best coverage (MIS) — "posterior sampling excels at mean prediction whereas quantile and mean interval score regression obtain confidence levels that cover data variations better"; MIS decreases with samples for both bootstrap and SG-MCMC but faster for SG-MCMC ⇒ smaller sample complexity for posterior sampling (attributed to posterior contraction, Wilson & Izmailov 2020). DeepGLEAM hybrid (Table 3 RMSE): 1W 66.03 vs GLEAM 73.59 vs Deep 239.94; 2W 57.67/213.31/65.46; 3W 70.12/189.49/73.75; 4W 70.75/161.88/70.16 — paper claims 6.6% improvement at 1W and 17% average over GLEAM; pure Deep model catastrophically fails under distribution shift. All numbers are the paper's claims.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Adversarial notes: (a) continuous-outcome UQ only — no binary/event forecasting (win prob, cover prob), so CRPS/MIS machinery needs adaptation to GSE's probability outputs; (b) no significance testing on any table — many MAE gaps (e.g., SG-MCMC vs Point) could be noise; (c) COVID training on residual vs GLEAM makes the "deep learning fails" claim partly a straw man (deep model had ~16 weeks of data); (d) MC dropout with 5% rate/50 iters is a weak configuration — its failure may be tuning, not method; (e) minibatches treated i.i.d. despite acknowledged spatiotemporal dependence; (f) interval widths suggest the models are systematically overconfident (authors' own conclusion §6.3), which cuts against using their intervals unadjusted; (g) computation table assumes one confidence level — quantile/MIS costs multiply by n levels.

## 10. GSE overlap
Extension. The existing map's calibration stack covers isotonic/PAV, ECE-by-slice, CQR, grouping loss, LRD, and proper scores (Brier, log-loss, CRPS in the metric inventory). What is NEW: (i) MIS as the *standard interval-forecast score* with consistency propositions — the map has interval-ish evaluation via CQR but no MIS-based selection of interval methods; (ii) the empirical frequentist-vs-Bayesian recipe (Table 1 + §4.4) for choosing a UQ engine — directly answers GSE's open question of how to put calibrated intervals on point forecasts (totals, props, win-total projections); (iii) DeepGLEAM residual-learning result: training the ML model on the residual vs a mechanistic baseline beats both — GSE analog: train residual models on (GSE engine − market), exactly the "engine-minus-market residual" idea already discussed in 2026-09-17 dossiers, here with quantified 6.6–17% gains; (iv) quantile-crossing warning and the SQ/CRPS fix, relevant if GSE ever ships multi-quantile prop bands. Cite: calibration lane dossiers (2026-09-17/18), CQR entries, market-microstructure (de-vigged consensus).

## 11. GSE implementation spec
1. **MIS as the interval metric**: implement MIS_N per the paper's formula (ρ=0.05) for GSE's total-line and prop interval forecasts; backtest 2021–2025 NFL totals: report MIS, coverage, width by week/season. Effort: hours. 2. **UQ recipe adoption**: per Table 1, GSE's data regime (thousands of games, limited compute) maps to "large data, limited computation" ⇒ recommend MIS regression / quantile regression heads on the existing engine for interval outputs, and rule out MC dropout (worst MIS in all three tasks) and naive bootstrap (high sample complexity, 25 retrains). 3. **DeepGLEAM analog**: train a residual model on (GSE engine projection − closing market line) for totals, using quantile heads; validate the paper's claimed residual-learning gain on 2023–2025 holdout. 4. Quantile crossing: if shipping multi-quantile bands, use the SQ monotone-spline head (11 params) rather than independent pinball heads.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: nflverse 2021–2025 game totals + closing totals from the odds archive; GSE engine's point total projection. Methods to compare: (a) current practice (point forecast ± fixed historical residual band); (b) quantile-regression head (pinball, 0.025/0.5/0.975); (c) MIS-regression head (eq. 7); (d) SQ head (CRPS loss, 11 params); (e) SG-MCMC on a small Bayesian last-layer (if compute allows); (f) MC dropout (expected to fail per paper). Metric: MIS at 95%, empirical coverage, interval width, MAE of the median — Table 2 protocol, reported per season. Train on 2021–2023, validate 2024, test 2025.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
Adopt MIS as the standard interval score and the quantile/MIS-regression head as GSE's interval engine if: on the 2025 test season, the MIS-regression (or quantile) interval beats the current fixed-band practice by ≥10% in MIS with coverage in [0.93, 0.97]. Reject if: coverage or MIS fails to beat the fixed-band baseline — i.e., the deep UQ machinery adds no value over residual bands, consistent with the paper's own finding that the models are systematically overconfident and need more data variation than NFL seasons provide.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Go beyond the paper: (i) the paper's UQ methods treat minibatches i.i.d. and ignore spatiotemporal structure — GSE's analog is ignoring team-strength graph structure; extend the DCRNN-style graph convolution to an NFL team graph (nodes = teams, edges = games played, weights = recency) so the interval head conditions on opponent-adjusted residuals; (ii) combine the paper's two winners — use SG-MCMC's posterior-mean point forecast with the MIS-regression interval head (the paper's §7 future direction: "best combine Bayesian credible intervals with frequentist confidence intervals"), testing whether the combination beats each alone on 2025 NFL totals; (iii) adaptive ρ: calibrate the nominal confidence level per market so realized coverage matches nominal, addressing the paper's §6.3 overconfidence finding.
