# [0403] Bigger data, better questions, and a return to fourth down behavior: an introduction to a special issue on tracking data in the National Football League (arXiv:1909.10631v3)

**Citation:** Michael J. Lopez (2019). *Bigger data, better questions, and a return to fourth down behavior: an introduction to a special issue on tracking data in the National Football League*. Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports (special issue intro). arXiv:1909.10631v3. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10631v3
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 360 lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — an editorial/synthesis paper with no portable model, but two concrete takeaways to implement: (1) the precise-distance-to-gain confounder (~40% of the "go for it" effect is selection bias) must be built into GSE's 4th-down decision estimates, and (2) the NGS tracking-data engineering spec (coordinate system, ±12-inch accuracy, play-flipping, no z-coordinate) belongs in GSE's NGS lane reference docs.

## 1. Research question
What does NFL player-tracking data change about football analytics? Lopez uses the league's oldest analytics finding — that teams should be more aggressive on fourth down (Carter & Machol 1978; Romer 2006; Burke et al. 2013; Yam & Lopez 2019) — as a microcosm: he argues that without the *precise* distance needed for a first down (play-by-play records only integer buckets), past estimates of the aggressiveness benefit were overstated due to unmeasured confounding. The paper then synthesizes the six papers of the JQAS special issue on NFL tracking data and closes with best practices/caveats for working with the data.

## 2. Dataset / schema
- **Fourth-down analysis**: all fourth-down plays from the **2017–2019 NFL regular seasons**. Precise distance to the line to gain computed two ways: (a) football RFID location on 4th down vs. line to gain derived from the football's location on 1st down (goal line on goal-to-go); (b) football RFID chip vs. sideline-chain RFID chip — "differences in the numbers provided were negligible." Restricted to the "go for it" range of the 4th-down-bot (Burke et al. 2013).
- **NGS tracking-data spec** (the paper's reference section): x/y coordinates for all 22 players + football at ~10 fps from RFID chips in shoulder pads and inside the football; 250,000–350,000 rows per game; **no z-coordinate**; location accurate to **±12 inches**, reliable on **99.999%** of players/games over the prior three seasons; football coordinates slightly less reliable than player coordinates; year-to-year speed-measurement differences after RFID tag updates; field coordinates fixed per stadium (x: 0–120, y: 0–160/3); ~half of offensive plays must be flipped (x→120−x, y→160/3−y) so offenses move the same direction; max speed sometimes recorded during/after a tackle (caution).
- Code for the replication: https://github.com/statsbylopez/nfl-fourth-down/tree/master/Code.

## 3. Method / model
1. **Confounder demonstration**: density plots of precise yards-to-go split by decision (go/punt/FG) within integer buckets (4th-and-1, 4th-and-2); two **generalized additive models** (GAMs) — P(go for it | precise distance) and P(convert | precise distance) — showing smooth nonlinear relationships.
2. **Effect replication**: re-runs the Yam & Lopez (2019) propensity-score/causal analysis on 2017–2019 data twice — once with the integer play-by-play distance, once with precise tracking distance — estimating wins-added per team-year and per-play win-probability added for teams that did not go for it had they gone for it.
3. **"Squeezing the balloon"** (Brooks & Ohsfeldt 2013): Figure 4 plots precise 4th-and-1 distance vs. play-by-play-estimated go-for-it probability (Yam & Lopez's 17-variable model: yardline, time, score, timeouts, pre-play WP, etc.), with separate smooths for go vs. no-go teams — showing that conditioning on observed covariates *widened* the imbalance in the unobserved precise distance (a Simpson's-paradox-like pattern).
4. **Synthesis**: summaries of the six special-issue papers (route clustering — Chu et al., Kinney; coverage-scheme clustering — Ventura & Dutta; NGS-from-images + completion% — Mallepalle et al.; EHCP — Evans & Deshpande; LSTM ball-carrier yardage — Yurko et al.), each framed as enabling "apples-to-apples" comparisons.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
No equations are stated — this is an editorial/empirical-demonstration paper, not a methods paper. Stated assumptions and caveats: (i) the GAM curves are descriptive, not causal; (ii) the replication holds the Yam & Lopez (2019) modeling choices fixed and varies only the distance input; (iii) "there could be other reasons why the benefit of aggressive approach remains underestimated (Romer 2006)" — precise distance is one confounder among potentially several; (iv) tracking data is "not a panacea" — play call, audibles, counterfactual defensive alignments, and whether a receiver ran the correct route remain unobserved even in tracking data; (v) the paper's broader causal claim rests on the standard observational-data assumption that conditioning on all relevant variables would identify the effect — which the paper itself argues is rarely achievable in football.

## 5. Features / target
- GAM inputs: precise distance needed for a first down (yards, continuous, from RFID). Targets: binary go-for-it decision; binary conversion (first down gained) conditional on going for it.
- Replication inputs: the 17-variable Yam & Lopez (2019) covariate set (yardline, time, score, timeouts remaining, pre-play win probability, etc.) plus either integer or precise distance. Targets: per-play win-probability added of going for it; wins added per team-year.
- No prediction horizon beyond the play (conversion) and season (wins-added aggregation).

## 6. Validation design
No train/test splits (retrospective causal-demonstration study). Validation is by replication and robustness: (a) two independent distance-measurement strategies (ball-vs-1st-down-line; ball-chip-vs-chain-chip) give "negligible" differences; (b) the GAMs' near-linearity is noted as matching what integer data would have predicted — a consistency check; (c) the squeezing-the-balloon figure conditions on the full 17-variable play-by-play model to show the confounder survives rich observed controls; (d) replication code published. No baselines beaten — the "baseline" is the prior literature's integer-distance estimate.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Paper's claims:
- 4th-and-1: teams that went for it were a median **0.70 yards** from the line to gain vs. **0.98 yards** for teams that did not (difference 0.28 yards). 4th-and-2: **1.98 vs. 2.06 yards**.
- GAM estimates: 4th-and-inches teams went for it **~70%** of the time vs. **~30%** for long 4th-and-1; conversion **~79%** on 4th-and-inches vs. **~55%** on long 4th-and-1.
- Replication (2017–2019, go-for-it range only): with **integer distance**, aggressive 4th-down strategy worth **+0.35 wins/team/year**; per-play WP-added for non-go teams had they gone: **3.8% (95% CI 2.6%–4.9%)**. With **precise distance**: **+0.22 wins/team/year**; per-play **2.2% (95% CI 0.6%–3.4%)**. **Roughly 40% of the benefit is negated** by accounting for precise distance.
- Squeezing the balloon: across nearly all go-for-it probabilities, the precise-distance gap between go and no-go teams *exceeds* the aggregate 0.28-yard gap — e.g., among low-probability teams, go-teams were **more than half a yard closer** than no-go teams.
- Context numbers: play-level data ≈ 160 plays/game → tracking ≈ **300,000 observations/game**; Big Data Bowl drew **1,800+ participants**; tracking covers 2016 season onward; Yurko et al. example: Patterson's expected gain 15 yards on 2nd-and-short near midfield vs. ~5% historical success rate for such plays — the "apples-to-apples" payoff.

## 8. Code / data availability
Replication code: **https://github.com/statsbylopez/nfl-fourth-down/tree/master/Code**. Tracking-data spec references https://nextgenstats.nfl.com/ and the 2019 Big Data Bowl page. Raw tracking data: NFL-proprietary (Big Data Bowl releases are public but limited to 2017 weeks 1–6).

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- **Single-confounder story**: precise distance is shown to explain ~40% of the effect, but the remaining 60% still rests on the same unconfoundedness assumption the paper critiques — other unmeasured confounders (QB sneak ability, OL strength, defensive front) could cut either way, and the paper admits the benefit could also be *under*estimated.
- **Selection on the analyzed range**: restricted to the 4th-down-bot "go for it" range — estimates don't generalize to all 4th downs.
- **No uncertainty on the 40% figure itself**: the CIs (2.6–4.9% vs. 0.6–3.4%) overlap substantially; the attenuation is directionally clear but its magnitude is noisy.
- **Editorial, not peer-reviewed-method**: no new estimator, no falsifiable model — the contribution is a measurement correction plus synthesis. The six summarized papers are not evaluated, only described.
- **Staleness**: 2017–2019 data; fourth-down aggressiveness has risen since (Stuart 2017 trend noted in-paper), so the +0.22 wins figure is a historical estimate, not a 2026 number.
- **External validity**: the precise-distance lesson generalizes to *any* GSE causal claim from play-by-play data (the real warning), while the +0.22 number itself is era-specific.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Two concrete ports, both small:
1. **Precise-distance 4th-down correction**: wherever GSE estimates or cites 4th-down go-for-it value, replace integer distance-to-go with fractional distance computed from tracking data (ball x-coordinate at 4th down vs. line-to-gain from 1st-down ball position — exactly the paper's wrangling strategy, reproducible from Big Data Bowl/nflverse tracking). Apply the paper's ~40% attenuation as a documented discount to any historical aggressiveness-value claim until GSE recomputes it on 2023–2025 data. Effort: ~3 days (data wrangling + recompute on modern seasons).
2. **NGS data-handling reference**: add the paper's §4 best-practices list to the NGS lane docs (coordinate conventions, play-flipping procedure, ±12-inch accuracy bound, no-z limitation, football-vs-player reliability difference, year-to-year tag-update caution) as the canonical data-QA checklist for every tracking-data pipeline GSE builds. Effort: ~1 day of documentation.
3. **Causal-hygiene rule**: adopt the "squeezing the balloon" check as a standing diagnostic — whenever GSE matches or conditions on play-by-play covariates for a causal claim, test whether the adjustment *increases* imbalance on any tracking-derived covariate; if it does, flag the estimate as suspect. Effort: ongoing, ~1 day to write the diagnostic.

## 12. Reproducible test
Recompute the paper's headline number on modern data: take 2023–2024 NFL regular-season 4th downs in the go-for-it range (nflverse play-by-play + Big Data Bowl-style tracking for fractional distance; where tracking is unavailable, use the play-by-play yardline fractional component as a proxy and document the gap), fit the two GAMs (go-for-it rate and conversion rate vs. precise distance), and re-run the Yam & Lopez-style wins-added estimate with integer vs. precise distance. Success criterion for the paper's claim: the precise-distance estimate is 25–55% lower than the integer-distance estimate (replicating the ~40% attenuation directionally). Time window: 2023–2024 seasons. Runnable with nflverse + public tracking; documents whether the confounder persists in the modern aggressive era.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
**Adopt** the precise-distance correction into GSE's 4th-down content and models if the reproducible test shows attenuation of **≥25%** (integer vs. precise) on 2023–2024 data — the confounder is live in the modern era and every 4th-down value claim gets the discount. **Reject** (keep as historical reference only) if attenuation is **<10%** on modern data — meaning the 2017–2019 finding was era-specific (coaches now go for it from longer distances, compressing the selection gap) — in which case GSE cites the paper as historical context but does not discount current estimates. The NGS data-handling reference (§11.2) is adopted unconditionally — it is era-independent engineering fact. Gate evaluated on 2023–2024 data before any model change.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Go beyond the paper by testing the *next* unmeasured confounder it gestures at but doesn't measure: **defensive front / box count at the snap**. Using modern tracking data (which the 2017–2019 paper lacked for this purpose), compute the number of defenders in the box and their average distance to the line on 4th-and-short, and test whether adding box-count to the precise-distance model further attenuates the go-for-it effect. Hypothesis: part of the remaining 60% is coaches reading light boxes — i.e., the decision contains information about defensive vulnerability that no distance measure captures. Metric: change in the wins-added estimate when box-count is added (2023–2024, same protocol as §12). If the estimate drops another ≥15%, GSE's 4th-down model permanently includes pre-snap box-count as a covariate — turning the paper's one-confounder correction into a standing confounder-hunting procedure.
