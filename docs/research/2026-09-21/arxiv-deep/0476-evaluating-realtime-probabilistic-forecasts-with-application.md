## 1. Research question
How do you evaluate *continuously updated* probabilistic forecasts (win-probability curves p̂_i(t), t∈[0,1]) for calibration and for skill relative to competing methods? Developed for ESPN's real-time NBA home-win forecasts: new tools — calibration surface plots and their summaries, pointwise skill-difference confidence intervals, and a novel functional test for skill aggregated across game time — validated by Monte Carlo simulation and applied to two NBA seasons.

## 2. Dataset / schema
ESPN real-time home-win probability forecasts + play-by-play scores from espn.com/nba, NBA regular seasons 2017–2018 (train: 1,137 games, 354,749 processed events) and 2018–2019 (test: N=1,213 games, 396,991 events). Forecasts linearly interpolated between events to curves p̂_i^ESPN(t), t∈[0,1] (game fraction); overtime removed (<10% of games); multi-events at one timestamp averaged. Benchmarks: CF (p̂=0.5), HomeWP (p̂=0.593, 2008–2017 home win rate), and pointwise GLMs (logit/probit, R glm, IWLS): PgRS (g(p)=β₀(t)+β₁(t)RS_i), LS (leading status −1/0/1), ScDnoInt (g(p)=β₁(t)ScD_i(t)), ScD, PgRSLS, PgRSScD (pregame strength + score difference) — fit on 2017–18, evaluated rolling on 2018–19. RS_i = ESPN pregame home-win prob. Pseudo-R²/importance diagnostics: team strength dominates early, score difference dominates late.

## 3. Method / model
**(a) Calibration surfaces:** at each t, adaptive rank-based bins (M=10; bin reference p̃_j(t) = median forecast in bin) — chosen because in-play forecasts cluster near 0/1 late, breaking fixed decile bins; Wilson intervals (Bonferroni over M bins) for bin event frequencies; linear interpolation of interval bounds over (t, p̃) gives upper/lower calibration surfaces U_{1−α}(t,p), L_{1−α}(t,p); calibrated iff reference plane f(t,p)=p lies between them. Summary: U^min_{1−α}(t)=min_j[U(t,p̃_j)−p̃_j] ≥ 0 and L^max_{1−α}(t)=max_j[L(t,p̃_j)−p̃_j] ≤ 0; smoothed with 5%-of-game moving average. Extremes (|p̂−{0,1}|<0.005) analyzed separately. **(b) Pointwise skill CIs:** loss difference Δ̂_N(t)=N⁻¹Σ_i[L(Y_i,p̂_i^A(t))−L(Y_i,p̂_i^B(t))] (Brier; generalizes to any loss with linear equivalent: KL, log-score); Lai et al. (2011) CLT (Δ̂_N−Δ_N)/s_N → N(0,1) with s_N²(t)=N⁻¹Σ_i δ_i²(t)p_i(t)(1−p_i(t)); conservative CI Δ̂_N(t) ± z_{1−α/2}s_N(t)/√N using p(1−p)≤1/4. **(c) Functional skill test:** H₀: ‖Δ_N‖²=0 (L² norm over t); ‖Z_N‖²=N‖Δ̂_N‖² →ᴰ Σ_{i≥1}λ_iχ²_i(1) (Theorem 1, proved in Appendix A); λ_i conservatively estimated by eigenvalues of Ĉ_cons(t,s)=N⁻¹Σ_i[p̂_i^A(t)−p̂_i^B(t)][p̂_i^A(s)−p̂_i^B(s)]; p-value via Q_D=Σ_{i=1}^D λ̂_iχ²_i(1), D=10, Monte Carlo or Imhof (1961). Caveat: invalid for nested models (degenerate kernel; cf. Clark & McCracken 2015).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Adaptive bins: p̂_{(i)}(t) in bin j iff ⌊N/M⌋(j−1)+1 ≤ i < ⌊N/M⌋j; Ȳ_j(t)=mean of Y_i in bin. Wilson interval with κ=z_{α/(2M)}: [n_jȲ_j+κ²/2]/[n_j+κ²] ± κ√n_j/[n_j+κ²]·(Ȳ_j(1−Ȳ_j)+κ²/(4n_j))^{1/2}. Calibration summaries U^min, L^max as above. Δ̂_N(t) (eq. 3), Δ_N(t) (eq. 4), s_N²(t) (eq. 5), CI (eq. 6), eigenproblem λ̂_iφ̂_i(t)=∫Ĉ_cons(t,s)φ̂_i(s)ds (eq. 7). Simulation DGP: ScD_i(t)=t·RS_i+W_i(t) (Brownian), Y_i=1{ScD_i(1)>0}, oracle p_i(t)=Φ((ScD_i(t)+RS_i(1−t))/√(1−t)). Assumptions: martingale-difference forecast errors (mild for genuine forecasts), non-degenerate s_N² (non-nested, non-identical models), p(1−p)≤1/4 conservatism. **Appendix A (Theorem 1 proof):** p_i(t)=E[Y_i|ℱ_{i−1,t}] (filtrations ℱ_{i−1,t}⊂ℱ_i⊂ℱ_{i,t}); Z_N(t)=√N[Δ̂_N(t)−Δ_N(t)]=N^{−1/2}Σ_i⟨(p̂^A_i−p̂^B_i)(p_i−Y_i),v⟩ = N^{−1/2}Σ_i X_{i,v}, X_{i,v} a martingale difference w.r.t. ℱ_i (tower property + A.1). Covariance kernel (eq. 8): C_N(t,s)=(4/N)Σ_i(E[(p̂^A_i(s)−p̂^B_i(s))(p̂^A_i(t)−p̂^B_i(t))p_i(s)(1−p_i(s))|ℱ_{i−1}]1_{s>t} + [·p_i(t)(1−p_i(t))·]1_{t>s}), symmetric, positive semi-definite. A.1: p̂^A_i(t),p̂^B_i(t) ℱ_{i−1,t}-measurable. A.2: C_N→C in L² with ∫₀¹∫₀¹[C_N−C]²dtds=o_P(1) (stationarity/ergodicity between games; analog of Seillier-Moiseiwitsch & Dawid 1993 cond. (a)); Mercer → eigenvalues λ₁≥λ₂≥⋯, eigenfunctions φ_i, operator 𝒞(f)(t)=∫C(t,s)f(s)ds. A.3: Z_N uniformly tight in L²[0,1] (sufficient: sup_NΣλ_{i,N}<∞). Under H₀:‖Δ_N‖²=0 and A.1–A.3, ‖Z_N‖²→ᴰΣ_iλ_iχ²_i(1): ⟨Z_N,v⟩/2→ᴰN(0,σ_v²) with σ_v²=⟨𝒞(v),v⟩ via the martingale CLT (Brown 1971; Lindeberg holds by uniform boundedness of X_{i,v}; degenerate case σ_v²=0 handled by Cauchy-Schwarz + uniform integrability), then weak convergence of Z_N to a Gaussian process (Prohorov, Bosq 2000), Karhunen–Loève + continuous mapping. Conservative eigenvalue estimate: Ĉ_cons(t,s)=N⁻¹Σ_i[p̂^A_i(t)−p̂^B_i(t)][p̂^A_i(s)−p̂^B_i(s)], whose eigenvalues uniformly dominate those of C_N because p(1−p) is replaced by its 1/4 upper bound.

## 5. Features / target
Features: in-game score difference ScD_i(t), pregame relative strength RS_i (ESPN pregame prob). Target: binary home-win indicator Y_i; forecasts are the full probability curve p̂_i(t) over normalized game time.

## 6. Validation design
Monte Carlo: 1,000 replications × N∈{100,250,500} games × two seasons (train/test); null models OraBM1/OraBM2, OraOU1/OraOU2 (oracle + independent noise, equal skill); alternative models = GLMs incl. correctly specified PgRSScD (probit). Metrics: empirical rejection rates at 10%/5%/1% for H₀.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Simulation (Table 3): under H₀, rejection rates close to but slightly below nominal (e.g., OraBM1 vs OraBM2 at N=500: 0.089/0.030/0.003 at 10/5/1%) — test is slightly conservative; oracle vs noisy oracle: power ≈1.000 at all levels; PgRSScD vs PgRS: power 1.000 even at N=100; vs ScD: 0.510/0.377/0.176 at N=100 → 0.995/0.982/0.907 at N=500; vs LS: 0.795/0.704/0.415 → 1.000; vs PgRSLS: 0.820/0.648/0.253 → 1.000/1.000/1.000. Lesson: needs N≥500 to separate competitive models. ESPN application (N=1213, 2018–19): ESPN well-calibrated at all t (Fig. 4c); extremes calibrated — 555 games with p̂>0.995 at some t: home won 553 (0.9964); 343 games with p̂<0.005: home won 1 (0.0029). ESPN significantly beats PgRS, ScD, LS, PgRSLS in aggregate (Fig. 7). ESPN vs simple logit PgRSScD (Fig. 8): Brier-skill point estimates slightly favor the simple model except in the final moments, but the difference is NOT significant at 5% at any t nor in aggregate — the proprietary model's extra information adds no demonstrable skill. All numbers are the paper's claims.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Adversarial notes: (a) the conservative p(1−p)≤1/4 bound is very loose when forecasts are near 0/1 (late game) — CIs there are wide and the "no significant difference" for ESPN vs PgRSScD may partly reflect low power, not equivalence; authors tried auxiliary variance models with little change; (b) the functional test is invalid for nested models (degenerate covariance) — GSE model-iteration comparisons are often nested, so this test cannot be used for engine-version A/B; (c) N≥500 needed for competitive-model separation — one NFL season has 272 games, so single-season in-play comparisons will be underpowered; pool across seasons; (d) linear interpolation between events creates artificial curve smoothness; (e) overtime removal (<10% games) slightly biases the late-t calibration sample; (f) RS_i = ESPN's own pregame prob leaks the benchmark's target into the covariate — fine for calibration benchmarking, not for a standalone model.

## 10. GSE overlap
Extension. The existing map covers pregame calibration (ECE by slice, isotonic/PAV, reliability diagrams, Brier decomposition 0471) but has NO in-play/live evaluation methodology — no calibration surfaces, no time-indexed skill comparison, no functional skill test. What is NEW: (i) the calibration-surface + U^min/L^max summary protocol for GSE's live win-probability curves (directly applicable to GSE in-play products and to evaluating any live model vs books); (ii) the Lai-style pointwise Brier skill CI for model-vs-model live comparison with a formal test; (iii) Theorem 1's functional L² test for aggregate skill; (iv) the adaptive rank-binning fix for late-game forecast clustering (GSE's win probs will have the same 0/1 clustering); (v) the substantive benchmark result — a 2-covariate logit matches a proprietary in-play model — sets the bar GSE's live model must clear and suggests the "score + pregame strength" logit as GSE's own in-play baseline. Cite: 2026-09-17/18 calibration dossiers, 0471 (Brier decomposition — complementary: 0471 decomposes pregame Brier, this evaluates in-play curves), market-microstructure entries.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
1. **In-play calibration surfaces**: for GSE's live win-probability feed (and any book's live odds as comparison), build U/L calibration surfaces over normalized game time with rank-based M=10 bins, Wilson–Bonferroni intervals, and the U^min/L^max summary plots; run on 2022–2025 NFL games (need play-level win prob series; nflverse + GSE engine logs). Effort: ~3 days. 2. **Live skill comparison**: implement Δ̂_N(t) Brier skill curves with conservative 95% CIs comparing GSE live vs (a) pregame-strength+score logit baseline (the paper's PgRSScD — fit once on 2022–2023), (b) live book consensus; use Theorem 1's functional test (D=10 eigenvalues, Imhof) for the aggregate verdict. Pool ≥2 seasons (N≥500) per the power analysis. 3. **In-play baseline**: fit the paper's PgRSScD-equivalent logit for NFL (pregame GSE win prob + live score differential, pointwise per game-fraction) as the permanent "simple model" GSE's live engine must beat. 4. Extremes audit: replicate Table 2 for p̂>0.995/<0.005.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: 2023–2025 NFL regular-season games with GSE live win-probability series (per-play or per-minute) and final outcomes; live closing book odds for comparison. Baseline: NFL PgRSScD logit (pregame GSE prob + score diff, pointwise in game-fraction t). Metrics: (a) calibration surface containment (U^min≥0, L^max≤0 across t); (b) pointwise Δ̂_N(t) Brier skill CI curves, GSE vs baseline and GSE vs book; (c) functional-test p-value for aggregate skill. Test protocol: fit baseline on 2023, evaluate 2024–2025 pooled (N≈540, satisfying the N≥500 power requirement).

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
Adopt as GSE's standard in-play evaluation if: on 2024–2025 pooled games, the pipeline runs end-to-end and the functional test has demonstrated power — i.e., it significantly separates GSE live from the coin-flip/HomeWP baselines (p<0.01), proving the test is not vacuous on NFL data. Reject if: the surfaces/CIs are uninformative on NFL data (e.g., persistent degeneracy from nested-model comparisons, or the Wilson intervals are too wide to ever exclude the reference plane) — then fall back to pregame-only evaluation.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Go beyond the paper: (i) fix the nested-model gap the authors flag — implement the Clark & McCracken (2015)-style adjustment for nested comparisons so GSE can formally test engine version N+1 vs N on live curves (the paper's test cannot); (ii) replace the paper's conservative p(1−p)≤1/4 variance bound with a calibrated auxiliary model for p_i(t)(1−p_i(t)) that stays stable late-game (the authors' attempts failed — try a beta-regression on (pregame prob, |score diff|, t)); (iii) extend the calibration surface to a joint (win × cover) surface for GSE's live win+spread products.
