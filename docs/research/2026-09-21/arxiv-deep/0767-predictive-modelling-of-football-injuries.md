**Verdict:** ADAPT — three real-data injury-prediction investigations on Premier League data (Tottenham Hotspur first team; Wolverhampton Wanderers): (a) UEFA injury-record recovery-time modelling with Poisson/negative-binomial/ordinal regression plus ML comparison and feature selection; (b) Gaussian-process regression with a dynamic-time-warping kernel for days-to-first-injury from variable-length exposure records; (c) supervised PCA for weekly intrinsic-injury classification from 69 in-training GPS variables. ## 1. Research question
Can predictive modelling forecast (i) the recovery time (days lost) after a football injury from UEFA-standard injury recordings, (ii) the time until a player's first injury of the season from training/match exposure (minutes-per-day) records, and (iii) whether an intrinsic (non-contact) injury will occur in a given week from in-training GPS data? Three investigations, in collaboration with Tottenham Hotspur FC (THFC) and Wolverhampton Wanderers (WW), plus the PGA European Tour mention in the abstract.

## 2. Dataset / schema
- **Investigation 1 (recovery time):** three UEFA injury-recording datasets — THFC (152 injuries), WW, and an integrated merged dataset. UEFA recording = standard fields: recurrence, activity, position when injured, phase of play/mechanism, body part injured, type, injury (specific diagnosis), side, stage of season, age, footwear, strapping, surface condition, referee's decision, date of injury. Target: recovery time in days (response: THFC mean 15.5 days, variance 1298.8 — extremely skewed; WW mean 10.8, variance 341.3; integrated mean 13.8, variance 975.8). Not public.
- **Investigation 2 (exposure records):** daily training + match exposure (minutes) for 35 THFC first-team players from 2012–13 pre-season (reference day 8 July 2012; days off recorded as 0 exposure). After cleaning (removed 3 goalkeepers, 2 players injured within first 3 days, 1 collision injury, 5 transient injuries replaced by next injury; 9 right-censored cases): 29 players, 21 injured, response = days to first injury (range 14–312, mean 91.73, median 49.5). Mean training exposure 35.83 ± 42.75 min/day. Not public.
- **Investigation 3 (GPS):** STATSports Viper10 training-session GPS for 29 THFC first-team players, July–Dec 2014: 1,310 session rows × 68 GPS variables + session duration (69 total). Variables split into 6 speed zones (Z1 <35% max speed … Z6 >75%): distance, high-speed running, speed intensity, accelerations/decelerations (>0.5 m/s², >0.5 s), metabolic power variables (EMD, HML distance, AMP), impacts, dynamic stress load, steps, total loading. Weekly player-level aggregation (mean over the week) → Approach A: 206 player-weeks × 69 features, 11 injury-weeks (injured players only, intrinsic injuries); Approach B: 426 player-weeks, 70 variables incl. class (all players). Not public.

## 3. Method / model
- **Inv. 1:** Statistical arm — Poisson, negative binomial, and ordinal regression (ordinal: "severity" discretization of days lost), with White (1980) robust SEs for the Poisson model since mean ≠ variance everywhere (e.g., THFC 15.5 vs 1298.8). ML arm — k-NN, SVM (polynomial kernels degree 2–3), Gaussian process (polynomial kernel), MLP (45 hidden, 2500 epochs), random forest; evaluated by 10 rounds of 10-fold CV reporting mean ± std of RMSE, Pearson correlation, concordance correlation coefficient (ccc); classification variant reporting accuracy + Cohen's kappa. Feature selection: random-forest node-impurity ranking (500 trees, RSS impurity) and correlation-based feature-subset selection (CFS) via genetic algorithm (population 50, 1000 generations, crossover 0.7, mutation 0.05, roulette selection), assessed with 10-fold CV.
- **Inv. 2:** Gaussian-process regression on log(days-to-first-injury) with a composite DTW-based covariance kernel (see §4). Test protocol: leave-one-out CV with the test subject's record truncated at T, T−1, …, T−12 days before injury (T−12 chosen because minimum response was 14 days) — i.e., a forecast-horizon/landmark evaluation measuring how skill decays as prediction is made further ahead. Metrics: concordance correlation coefficient and MAE across 1000 γ values ∈ [0.00002, 0.2] and noise values ∈ [0.0001, 0.01], discarding parameter sets with negative predictive variance.
- **Inv. 3:** Weekly binary classification (injury week vs not) with: supervised PCA (Bair et al. 2006; α ∈ {0, 0.01, …, 0.5}, components ∈ {2..50}), ridge logistic regression, random forest, naïve Bayes, SVM/GP (RBF/poly kernels), MLP, k-NN. 10 rounds of 10-fold CV; primary metric Cohen's kappa (classes wildly imbalanced). SPCA components also interpreted: correlations with raw variables (Appendix Table 10.12), e.g., Approach-A PC1 = "absence of high-speed running" (dominated by DistanceZone5, r=−0.764), PC2 = "medium-speed sprinting".

## 4. Equations & assumptions
(1) Poisson log-link with robust SEs (White 1980) because E[y] ≠ Var(y) in every dataset. (2) Negative binomial and cumulative-logit ordinal models; ordinal model abandoned after kappa 0.31–0.40 showed the medically-motivated severity discretization was worse than plain regression. (3) DTW–RBF kernel: k(xᵢ, xⱼ) = exp(−γ · DTW(xᵢ, xⱼ)), γ > 0 constrained so predictive variances stay positive; composite kernel over the two exposure series K(xᵢ,xⱼ) = ½[k_train(xᵢ,xⱼ) + k_match(xᵢ,xⱼ)]. (4) DTW distance DTW(X,Y) = min over warping paths of Σ d(xᵢ, yⱼ) — admits comparison of different-length exposure histories; the thesis acknowledges DTW is not necessarily positive semi-definite and leans on the GP closed-form solution (a solution always exists unless K + σ²I is non-invertible) plus empirical variance-positivity checks. (5) ccc = 2ρσₓσᵧ/(σₓ² + σᵧ² + (μₓ−μᵧ)²); Cohen's kappa for imbalanced classification. (6) Supervised PCA: screen features by univariate regression |xⱼᵀy|, PCA on survivors with α-thresholded selection (α here = correlation screening threshold), logistic/GP-style classification on the retained components.
Assumptions: injuries ≤3 days into pre-season attributed to pre-existing conditions, not workload (removed); "intrinsic" = non-contact, confirmed with club medics; days off = 0-exposure imputation; log(days) approximately normal for the GP; ordinal severity categories presumed to be ordered correctly; the DTW-kernel GP variance-positivity check is empirical, not guaranteed.

## 5. Features / target
- Inv. 1: UEFA categorical fields (recurrence, phase of play/mechanism, body part injured, injury diagnosis, side, stage of season, etc.). Target: days lost (regression) or UEFA severity class (classification).
- Inv. 2: two daily time series per player — training exposure minutes, match exposure minutes — from pre-season start to (truncated) first injury. Target: days to first intrinsic injury (log-transformed).
- Inv. 3: 69 weekly-aggregated GPS variables per player-week. Target: binary injury-in-week indicator.

## 6. Validation design
- Inv. 1: 10 rounds of 10-fold CV for ML models; classical omnibus likelihood-ratio tests + factor-level LR tests for the statistical models. Baselines: majority-class ("Transient") for classification (66.4% accuracy baseline on THFC), intercept-only model for regressions.
- Inv. 2: leave-one-out CV combined with the T−0…T−12 truncation scheme (12 nested landmark horizons); best-of-grid per horizon selected on ccc/MAE.
- Inv. 3: 10 rounds of 10-fold CV with per-model parameter grids (Table 7.2); Cohen's kappa as the decision metric because accuracy is dominated by the majority class (Approach B supervised PCA: 97.07% accuracy but kappa only 0.14).

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
- Inv. 1 statistical fits (in-sample fit quality): Poisson — THFC RMSE 11.4/corr 0.94/ccc 0.94; WW 4.9/0.96/0.96; integrated 19.0/0.79/0.75. Negative binomial worse everywhere (THFC RMSE 43.45/corr 0.78/ccc 0.65; WW 9.38/0.85/0.85; integrated 26.22/0.55/0.51), with one 256-day meniscus-lesion outlier (Cook's D 0.006, 96th of 152 — low influence). Ordinal regression: accuracy 71.0–75.0%, kappa 0.31 (THFC) / 0.40 (WW) / 0.37 (integrated) — rejected by the author as worse than regression. Significant covariates (>50% of tests at 5%): recurrence 6/6, phase of play 6/6, body part injured 5/6, injury diagnosis 5/6, stage of season 3/4; position when injured 2/2.
- Inv. 1 ML regression (10×10-fold CV, THFC): random forest best overall — RMSE 30.1, corr 0.471±0.341, ccc 0.406; SVM poly deg3 — RMSE 28.8, corr 0.511, ccc 0.269; all correlations <0.5 with very large variance (k-NN corr 0.225±0.387). Classification: every classifier ≈ baseline accuracy (THFC baseline 66.4%); kappa best = RF 0.256 (THFC), SVM 0.225 (WW). After CFS feature selection (keeping e.g. THFC: phase of play, injury, body part, recurrence, age): SVM THFC corr improved 0.511→0.584, ccc 0.269→0.455; RF robust to noisy features throughout.
- Inv. 2: GP–DTW, LOOCV: ccc 0.95–0.98 from T down to T−12; best MAE ≈ 11 days (response mean 91.7, median 49.5). Skill deteriorates with truncation (as expected); MAE minimized at T−3. Caveat noted by author: predictions reliable to 12 days ahead on this data, but no longer horizons could be tested.
- Inv. 3: supervised PCA best by kappa in both approaches — Approach A: kappa 0.21±0.04 (acc 88.8%, precision 0.55, recall 0.33; α=0.01, 41 components); ridge logistic second (kappa 0.16±0.33); RF/SVM/GP/MLP/k-NN all ≈ 0 (RF kappa 0.06, k-NN 0.0). Approach B (all players): supervised PCA kappa 0.14±0.09 (α=0.09, 40 components); GP second (0.12). Author notes RF's failure on all-numeric high-dimensional data and k-NN's curse-of-dimensionality collapse.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
(i) Inv. 1 Poisson/neg-binomial fits are in-sample (fit vs actual), so the corr-0.94 numbers are fit quality, not CV-validated — the honest numbers are the ML CV results (corr ≤ 0.5); (ii) Inv. 2's ccc 0.95–0.98 under LOOCV with a small (n=29), single-club, first-injury-only dataset — author flags single-club generalizability explicitly and never tests beyond T−12; training subjects contribute full records while the test subject is truncated, so the model has population-level "future" schedule information; (iii) Inv. 3 Approach A discards all uninjured players, biasing the sample toward the injured; kappa 0.21/0.14 is "feasible" not "good"; the 6-zone variable expansion creates 68 highly collinear variables from ~20 underlying quantities; (iv) thesis-scale (2016): no deep learning, no calibration of injury probabilities, no conformal coverage — predicted probabilities (where used) are not calibrated or intervalized; (v) intrinsic-injury definition is medic-judged, not data-verified.

## 10. GSE overlap
Extension of the causal/injury lane. Complements (not duplicates) the existing research map: (a) GP with DTW kernel for variable-length workload histories — the map has ACWR-style workload features but no kernelized schedule-similarity method; (b) the T−0…T−12 landmark evaluation measuring skill decay vs forecast horizon — no horizon-decay protocol exists in the map; (c) supervised PCA for high-dim wearable/tracking features — the map's GPS/tracking lane lacks a supervised dimensionality-reduction step correlated to the injury target; (d) return-to-play regression evidence: recurrence + phase of play + body part + diagnosis drive recovery-time prediction, kappa-over-accuracy discipline for imbalanced injury classification, and the "regression beats severity-discretization" lesson. Direct NFL translation: days-to-first-injury ↔ games-missed-after-return; weekly player GPS ↔ weekly practice-load reports; UEFA fields ↔ official injury report designations.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
- **GP–DTW workload-risk model**: build daily workload series per NFL player (practice participation % × session length, snap counts, travel distance) and fit GP regression on log(days-to-next-missed-game) with k(xᵢ,xⱼ)=exp(−γ·DTW(xᵢ,xⱼ)) per series, averaged across series types. Grid γ ∈ [2e-5, 0.2], noise ∈ [1e-4, 1e-2], discard negative-variance parameter sets. Use for rest-management flags on questionable/tagged players.
- **Landmark horizon evaluation**: evaluate every GSE availability model at horizons h ∈ {0, 3, 7, 10, 14} days before the game (predict-then-truncate protocol); report ccc/MAE vs h. Ship only horizons where skill exceeds the no-information baseline — this directly answers "how far ahead can we trust the injury call."
- **Supervised-PCA injury components**: on weekly NFL tracking/workload features (p≫n per position group), run Bair-style supervised PCA screened on the injury-in-week label, keep components with α<0.05, and feed 3–4 injury-correlated components (interpretable: "absence of high-speed running", "medium-speed sprinting") into the availability model instead of raw GPS. Report kappa, never accuracy alone, on imbalanced injury weeks.
- **Return-to-play regression**: for the questionable→out/doubtful transition, model expected games missed with negative-binomial-style count regression on injury type + body part + recurrence + mechanism, and prefer regression over discretized severity classes per the thesis's ordinal-regression failure.
- Effort: ~3 days for the GP–DTW + landmark harness on GSE's participation data; ~2 days for supervised-PCA components in the availability pipeline.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: GSE 2022–2024 weekly player practice-participation + snap-count series with official injury-report outcomes (questionable/doubtful/out, games missed). (a) Fit GP–DTW on log(days to next missed game) with LOOCV at horizons T−0…T−14; success = ccc ≥ 0.60 at h=7 (calibrated to the thesis's 0.95–0.98 but discounted for a noisier cross-club NFL setting and honest CV). (b) Supervised PCA (α grid, 2–50 components) vs ridge logistic vs RF on injury-in-week classification, 10×10-fold CV; success = kappa ≥ 0.10 with precision ≥ 0.30. (c) Negative-binomial games-missed regression with recurrence/phase/body-part features; success = Spearman ≥ 0.40 between predicted and actual games missed.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADAPT the landmark-horizon protocol and kappa-discipline immediately (cheap, assumption-light). ADOPT the GP–DTW model only if the reproducible test (a) clears ccc ≥ 0.60 at 7-day horizon; otherwise REJECT the kernel in favor of a simpler DTW–kNN baseline. ADOPT supervised-PCA components only if test (b) clears kappa ≥ 0.10; otherwise REJECT — raw workload features suffice. REJECT the ordinal severity approach entirely (regression dominates, thesis's own conclusion).

## 14. Improvement experiment
Fuse the thesis's three investigations into one pipeline: use the GP–DTW exposure forecast (investigation 2) as a *feature* inside the supervised-PCA injury classifier (investigation 3) — i.e., predicted days-to-injury from workload history becomes an input to the GPS-based weekly injury model. If the fused model beats either alone on the kappa gate, exposure dynamics and acute GPS state are complementary rather than redundant signals.
