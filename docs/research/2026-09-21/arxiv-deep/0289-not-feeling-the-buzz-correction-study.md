# [0289] Not feeling the buzz: Correction study of mispricing and inefficiency in online sportsbooks (arXiv:2306.01740v4)

**Citation:** Clegg, L., & Cartlidge, J. (2023). *Not feeling the buzz: Correction study of mispricing and inefficiency in online sportsbooks*. arXiv:2306.01740v4. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01740v4
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 2648 lines).
**Verdict:** ADOPT — a cautionary replication study whose core lessons (Kelly-sized returns collapse on one bad odds row; cleaned WTA "competitive match" buzz strategy kept +12.44% ROI to Feb 2020, died after) are directly actionable for GSE's data-QA and odds-pipeline discipline.

## 1. Research question
The paper replicates and corrects Ramirez, Reade & Singleton (2023, "Betting on a buzz," IJF) — which claimed a Wikipedia-based "WikiBuzz" crowd-interest factor predicts bookmaker mispricing in WTA tennis and funds a profitable Kelly-staked betting strategy (ROIs up to 29.38%). Clegg & Cartlidge ask: do RRS's results survive independent replication, and are the reported profits robust to data-quality errors? They confirm the mispricing-model replication, then show most Bet365 profits came from a single bet (Polona Hercog vs Misaki Doi, 22 Mar 2019) placed on an erroneous Bet365 quote of 5.50 (true market max 2.10), and extend the test to Aug 2023 to check whether the residual edge persists.

## 2. Dataset / schema
- RRS original dataset (from https://github.com/philiprami/betting_on_a_buzz): WTA matches from tennis-data.co.uk, Jul 2015 – Feb 2020, with pre-match odds (Bet365, market average, best odds from 40–60 bookmakers via oddsportal.com), WTA rankings, and Wikipedia page-view metrics (columns: wiki_yesterday_w, wiki_yesterday_l, wiki_med365_w, wiki_med365_l) from the Wikimedia Pageview API. In-sample 2016–2018: 15,854 player-matches for the mispricing regression; out-of-sample Jan 2019 – Feb 2020.
- Extended/cleaned dataset built by the authors: raw 37,700 rows (18,850 matches, 688 players, Jul 2015 – Aug 2023); after cleaning (require ≥1-year-old Wikipedia profiles; drop Bet365 odds > best market odds; drop missing odds; drop best-odds-vs-average-odds gaps > 4 SD of the 0.014 mean implied-probability difference), 35,274 rows, 17,751 matches, 584 players, 03 Jul 2015 – 31 Aug 2023. Open-sourced at https://github.com/Faxulous/notFeelingTheBuzz.
- Not NFL data: WTA tennis only.

## 3. Method / model
Mincer–Zarnowitz (1969) forecast-evaluation framework: OLS regression of bookmaker forecast errors e_ij (distance of odds-implied probability from 0 on losses / 1 on wins) on implied probability z_ij, rank distance, and WikiBuzz, with year/season fixed effects, standard errors clustered by tournament-match (Python `linearmodels` library, written to mirror RRS's Stata code). For inefficiency testing, a linear outcome model (PM, Eq. 5; PM w/o rank distance, Eq. 6) produces win-probability forecasts fed into a Kelly staking rule: f*_ij = max(p̃_ij − (1−p̃_ij)/b_ij, 0), with bankroll reset to 1 before each bet (per Hvattum & Arntzen 2010; Boshnakov et al. 2017; da Costa et al. 2022; Holmes et al. 2023 convention). Two odds sources tested: "Best Odds" (oddsportal best) and "Bet365 only." Competitiveness filters (market-implied p ∈ [0.2,0.8], [0.4,0.6], etc.) subset matches by how close the market sees the contest. Random-strategy significance framework from Wunderlich & Memmert (2020): p_bs = probability a random strategy of the same bet count achieves equal/higher return, via 100,000 simulation trials.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Eq. 1 (mispricing regression): e_ij = α + β₁ z_ij + β₂ RankDist_ij + β₃ WikiBuzz_ij + ψ_{S(j)} + φ_{T(j)} + ε_ij.
Eq. 2: z_ij = 1/odds_ij (raw decimal odds; overround kept, so z_ij + z_ji = 1 + K > 1). Eq. 3: RankDist_ij = −(1/rank_i − 1/rank_j), unranked → inverse rank 0. Eq. 4: WikiBuzz_ij = ln(w_i/w̃_i) − ln(w_j/w̃_j), w = yesterday's page views, w̃ = median daily views over prior year. Eq. 5 (PM outcome model): p̃_ij = α̂ + (1+β̂₁) z_ij + β̂₂ RankDist_ij + β̂₃ WikiBuzz_ij. Eq. 6 (PM w/o RD): same minus RankDist. Eq. 7 (Kelly): f*_ij = max(p̃_ij − (1−p̃_ij)/b_ij, 0), b_ij = decimal odds − 1. Eq. 8 (profit): π_ij = (f*×odds) − f* if win; −f* if loss. Eq. 9: ROI = Σπ_ij / Σf*_ij. Eq. 10: absolute return = B_T/B_0. Worked example (Appendix A): Danielle Collins vs Jelena Ostapenko, Miami Open semi-final 30 Mar 2018: 6.93 = ln(39955/26.5) − ln(39/27). Assumptions: raw implied probabilities (no Shin/Štrumbelj de-vigging); clustering by tournament-match; linear conditional-mean effects; bankroll reset to 1 per bet; best-odds strategy admitted as "not normally realistic."

## 5. Features / target
Features: odds-implied probability (z_ij, average bookmaker odds), WTA rank distance (inverse-rank difference), WikiBuzz (relative excess Wikipedia page views). Target for mispricing: forecast error e_ij (odds-implied probability minus realized outcome, signed). Target for inefficiency test: binary match outcome y_ij ∈ {0,1}, prediction horizon = one match (pre-match). Bets selected by Kelly rule on the model's probability edge over Bet365 or best-odds quotes.

## 6. Validation design
In-sample fit 2016–2018 (15,854 player-matches); out-of-sample backtest Jan 2019 – Feb 2020 (PM: 312 bets; PM w/o RD: 276 bets on 5,156 player-matches; mean overround 6.46%). Extended test: re-fit on 2015–2018, test Jan 2019 – Aug 2023 on the cleaned dataset; mispricing re-estimated on Feb 2020 – Aug 2023 (13,913 player-matches). Baselines: random-betting bootstrap (Wunderlich–Memmert p_bs, 100k trials); cross-odds-source comparison (best odds vs Bet365); competitiveness subsamples. No time-ordered walk-forward — single fixed train/test split (typical of the literature, and a limitation).

## 7. Numerical results / baselines
Strict replication of RRS exact (only correction: RRS's Table 4 PM ROI 17.26% is a typo; Stata code shows 17.29%). Mincer–Zarnowitz estimates (Table 1, PM): β̂₁ = 0.025 (0.029), β̂₂ = 0.055 (0.029), β̂₃ = 0.009 (0.004), α̂ = −0.043 (0.015) — WikiBuzz and RankDist significantly non-zero, confirming RRS's mispricing claim. The Hercog bet: 39.3% of bankroll at 5.50 (market max 2.10; Hercog rank 93 favoured over Doi rank 112; 14th-longest winner odds in 2019 among 2,453 rows). Removing it: PM ROI 17.29% → −7.36%, absolute return 1.24 → −0.50; PM w/o RD ROI 28.82% → −6.31%, absolute return 1.44 → −0.29. Competitive-match strategy (PM w/o RD, p ∈ [0.4,0.6]): ROI 29.38% → 12.44% after removal (263 bets, overround 7.01%); p_bs = 0.002 (0.2% of 100k random 262-bet strategies matched it) — the one surviving significant strategy. Broader filter p ∈ [0.2,0.8]: ROI 20.11% → −3.36% after removal. Non-competitive p ∈ (0,0.4)∪(0.6,1): ROI 0.81%, p_bs = 0.22 (not significant, 87 bets). Best-odds strategy (PM): ROI 3.05% (2,350 bets, −0.24% overround), unaffected by Hercog (best odds correctly 2.09). Extended/cleaned data: the competitive strategy earns zero further profit after Feb 2020 (Fig. 2b); mispricing re-estimation Feb 2020–Aug 2023: WikiBuzz β̂₃ = 0.001 (0.004), p = 0.837; RankDist β̂₂ = 0.021 (0.030), p = 0.471 — both insignificant. Cleaning removed: 2,020 new-player rows, 32 Bet365>best-odds rows, 174 missing-odds rows, 200 four-sigma outlier rows.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
Adversarial notes: (a) The headline lesson of the paper — one bad odds row × full-Kelly sizing (39.3% bankroll) generated >150% cumulative-return spike; Kelly is a fragility amplifier, not a virtue. (b) Bet365-odds-only strategies are only as good as the odds feed; tennis-data.co.uk's known inconsistency (Bet365 5.50 vs market max 2.10) is the kind of row-level corruption that survives aggregate checks — the authors' "Bet365 > best odds" filter catches it, but subtler errors (e.g., stale lines) wouldn't be. (c) No walk-forward: a single train/test split; the competitive-strategy survival may be selection luck across four competitiveness bands (multiple-testing across Table 5 columns, only partly addressed by p_bs). (d) p_bs tests significance of *returns*, not of the *model* — per Wunderlich & Memmert, betting returns are not a valid accuracy measure. (e) Wikipedia view data quality unverified (bot traffic, page moves); authors flag this. (f) Edge died post-2020 — could be efficiency gains or regime shift (COVID scheduling, player churn); not identified. (g) Tennis-only; no transfer path asserted to NFL — different market, different data exhaust.

## 10. GSE overlap
Extension, not duplicate. Market microstructure is a documented gap in the existing-research map (gap #3: only 1211.4000 + PLOS ONE 2023; "steam-move predictability, limit-order-book analogues" thin). No replication/correction studies of betting strategies exist in the corpus; the map's "odds market" lanes list closing-line value, de-vigged consensus, beat-the-close, and line movement — this paper adds concrete operational cautions (odds-feed row validation before backtest; Kelly leverage × data error interaction; random-betting significance framing for strategy P&L). Garrett's NFL focus means WikiBuzz itself (WTA Wikipedia views) has no direct analog, though the broader concept (crowd-attention proxies for mispricing) overlaps loosely with the ML brief's area-13 text/news features (no papers read yet).

## 11. GSE implementation spec
Not a model to port — a QA and evaluation protocol to install: (1) Add an odds-sanity filter to GSE's market-capture pipeline: drop/flag any row where a book's quote exceeds the cross-book best by more than 3 SD of the best-minus-mean spread (the paper's 4-SD variant), and any row where a single book's line is an extreme outlier vs the consensus (z-score on implied probability) — implement in the daily capture job before backtest data is written. (2) Adopt the Wunderlich–Memmert random-betting bootstrap (100k trials) as the mandatory significance screen for any published GSE strategy P&L: report p_bs alongside ROI for all posted picks/strategies; never post a strategy with p_bs > 0.05. (3) Cap per-bet Kelly fractions (fractional Kelly ≤ 0.25× full Kelly) in the GSE bet-sizing module to bound single-row data-error damage; log any bet sized >10% bankroll for manual review. Effort: ~1 engineer-day for the filters + bootstrap (Python, plugs into existing backtest harness); Kelly cap is a one-line change with config.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: GSE's NFL odds history (The Odds API captures / historical lines) for 2024–2025 seasons, cross-book. Metric: (a) fraction of odds rows flagged by the paper's 4-SD best-vs-average filter and by a "book quote > cross-book best" check — baseline expectation >0 given tennis precedent; (b) re-run GSE's most profitable 2024 NFL strategy with and without the flagged rows, reporting ΔROI and p_bs via 100k random-betting trials. Baseline to beat: flagged-row removal changes published backtest ROI by less than ±0.5 pp AND p_bs stays <0.01 — i.e., current results are robust. Window: 2024 season train, 2025 season test, all markets GSE publishes.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT the QA protocol (no further test needed — the filters are cheap insurance). For the reproducible test: if dropping flagged odds rows changes the 2025 backtest ROI of any published GSE strategy by more than ±2 pp, or flips any strategy from p_bs < 0.05 to > 0.05, treat the published P&L as data-error-contaminated and re-issue corrected figures before posting any new picks; otherwise close the issue with a one-line log entry.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Extend the paper: run the same Mincer–Zarnowitz mispricing regression on NFL moneylines using a crowd-attention proxy GSE can actually build — beat-writer/news article volume and X engagement about each team in the 48h before kickoff (per-team log-ratio of attention vs season median, mirroring WikiBuzz's Eq. 4 construction), plus a rank/Elo-distance term. If the attention coefficient is significant and a Kelly-on-mispricing strategy beats p_bs < 0.05 out-of-sample, GSE has an NFL analog of the buzz factor — directly filling ML-brief area 13 ("text/news as features beyond the price").
