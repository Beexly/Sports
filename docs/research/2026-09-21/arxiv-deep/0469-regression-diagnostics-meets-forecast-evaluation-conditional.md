# [0469] Regression Diagnostics meets Forecast Evaluation: Conditional Calibration, Reliability Diagrams, and Coefficient of Determination (arXiv:2108.03210v3)

**Citation:** Tilmann Gneiting and Johannes Resin (2022). *Regression Diagnostics meets Forecast Evaluation: Conditional Calibration, Reliability Diagrams, and Coefficient of Determination*. arXiv:2108.03210v3. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03210v3
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 4532 lines; read sequentially including Appendices A–C).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — the CORP isotonic-recalibration diagnostics and universal coefficient of determination R* are directly usable for GSE model diagnostics; port them alongside (not replacing) the existing conformal calibration stack.

## 1. Research question
How should calibration be defined and diagnosed for real-valued outcomes — bridging the two-sided divide between regression diagnostics (in-sample fit) and forecast evaluation (out-of-sample)? The paper builds a measure-theoretic framework of hierarchies of calibration (auto-calibration → threshold/quantile/CEP/probabilistic/marginal), defines conditional T-calibration for statistical functionals, and gives practical nonparametric tools (T-reliability diagrams and score decompositions estimated via isotonic regression / PAV), culminating in a universal coefficient of determination R* that nests classical R² (least squares) and R1 (quantile regression).

## 2. Dataset / schema
Pure theory + three demonstration datasets: (a) Tredennick et al. (2021) butterfly population forecasts — point forecasts of log-transformed butterfly population size from a null model vs ridge regression; (b) synthetic forecasts (perfect, unfocused, lopsided predictive distributions from Examples 2.1–2.2) with samples of size n=400 for empirical demos; (c) Bank of England CPI inflation forecasts Q3 2005–Q1 2020 (n=59 quarters), two-piece normal fanchart distributions, horizons 0–6 quarters, threshold at the 2% policy target, 75%-quantile forecasts (App. C.2, Figs. 8, 12–17). No public download URLs for these datasets in the paper (Bank of England data is public via the Bank's site, but not stated).

## 3. Method / model
Two-part contribution. **Theory:** prediction-space setting with random triple (F, Y, U) where F is a posited CDF, Y the outcome, U a standard uniform for PIT randomization. Defines auto-calibration F(y) = Q(Y ≤ y | F), CEP calibration (eq. 4), threshold calibration (eq. 5), and conditional T-calibration T(L(Y | T(F))) = T(F) for identifiable functionals (means, quantiles, expectiles, moments, Huber). Proves (Thm 2.11) auto-calibration implies conditional T-calibration for functionals defined via an identification function. **Method:** the T-PAV algorithm (Alg. 1) — general pool-adjacent-violators isotonic regression that, given sorted (x_i, y_i), outputs increasing recalibrated values x̂_i = T(δ_{k:l}) that are conditionally T-calibrated w.r.t. the empirical measure and optimal under any consistent scoring function of mixture form (Thm 3.1, Jordan et al. 2022). CORP (Consistent, Optimal, Reproducible, PAV-based) empirical T-reliability diagrams connect (x_i, x̂_i); score decomposition S̄ = MCB − DSC + UNC estimated empirically (Thm 3.3 guarantees nonnegativity — fails for non-PAV recalibration). Extended decomposition S̄ = MCBu + MCBc − DSC + UNC splits miscalibration into unconditional (translation-recalibration) and conditional parts (Thm 2.27/3.4). Universal coefficient of determination R* = (DSC − MCB)/UNC (eq. 31), in [0,1] under in-sample loss-minimization conditions (Thm 3.6). Consistency/resampling bands for reliability diagrams (Alg. 2–3 in Appendix B; residual-resampling for point forecasts).

## 4. Equations & assumptions
PIT: Z_F = F(Y−) + U(F(Y) − F(Y−))  (1). CEP calibration: Q(Z_F ≤ α | qα−(F)) = α a.s., ∀α ∈ (0,1)  (4). Threshold calibration: Q(Y ≤ t | F(t)) = F(t) a.s., ∀t ∈ R  (5). Strong threshold calibration: Q(Z_F ≤ α | F(t)) = α a.s. (6). Identifiable functional: T(F) = [T−(F), T+(F)] with T−(F) = sup{x : ∫V(x,y)dF(y) < 0}, T+(F) = inf{x : ∫V(x,y)dF(y) > 0} (7). Conditional T-calibration: T(L(Y | T(F))) = T(F) a.s. (Def. 2.7). Recalibrated version: X_rc = T(L(Y | X)) (11). Score decomposition: S̄ = MCB_S − DSC_S + UNC_S (14), MCB_S ≥ 0, DSC_S ≥ 0. Elementary loss: S_η(x,y) = (1{η ≤ x} − 1{η ≤ y}) V(η,y) (15); canonical loss: S(x,y) = a ∫ S_η(x,y) dλ(η) + b(y) (17). Canonical losses (Table 3): mean → (x − y)²; α-expectile → 2|1{x≥y} − α|(x − y)²; α-quantile → 2(1{x≥y} − α)(x − y). For squared error: MCB = E[(X − X_rc)²], DSC = E[(X_rc − µ_Y)²], UNC = Var(Y). Empirical decomposition: S̄̂ = MĈB − D̂SC + ÛNC (27); extended: S̄̂ = MĈB_u + MĈB_c − D̂SC + ÛNC (29). Universal R²: R* = (DSC − MCB)/UNC (31). Empirical skill score: S_skill = 1 − S̄̂/Ŝ_mg = (D̂SC − MĈB)/ÛNC (30). Translation optimality (Thm 2.26): X unconditionally T-calibrated iff E[S(X + c, Y)] ≥ E[S(X, Y)] ∀c ∈ R. Assumptions: Assumption 2.6 (F ∈ F, T(F) measurable, L(Y|T(F)) ∈ F); 2.8 (identification function of prediction error form or x − T(δ_y)); 2.15 (continuous strictly-increasing CDFs on shared support, countable G); 2.19 (T single-valued / lower-upper version, X_rc well-defined finite); 2.25 (canonical loss, integrability of shifted loss); 3.5 (in-sample loss minimization with intercept, Ŝ_mg > 0).

## 5. Features / target
Not an ML prediction paper — features/target are theoretical: inputs are posited predictive distributions F (or stand-alone point forecasts x_i = T(F_i)); target is the real-valued outcome y_i. In demos: features = model forecasts (null model, ridge, Bank of England fancharts); target = realized butterfly population size / realized CPI inflation.

## 6. Validation design
No train/test split (theory paper). Empirical illustrations: n=400 simulated samples from the perfect/unfocused/lopsided forecast examples with residual-based 90% consistency bands; butterfly population forecasts assessed out-of-sample (from Tredennick et al. 2021); Bank of England CPI inflation forecasts 2004+ at lead times 1–6 quarters — prequential-style evaluation (forecast-generating mechanism not considered, per the prequential principle of Dawid 1984). Baselines in examples: null model vs ridge regression; marginal/unconditional reference forecast x0 = T(L(Y)) in skill scores; 90% consistency bands as null hypothesis for calibrated forecasts.

## 7. Numerical results / baselines

Quantile (α=0.10): perfect QS 0.351, MCBu 0.000, MCBc 0.031, DSC 0.180, UNC 0.500; unfocused QS 0.453, MCBc 0.067, DSC 0.115; lopsided QS 0.440, MCBu 0.050, MCBc 0.043, DSC 0.153. Table 4 (MSE under mean forecasts, UNC = 2): perfect forecast — MSE 1, MCB 0, DSC 1; unconditional — MSE 2, MCB 0, DSC 0; unfocused — MSE 1 + η0²/4, MCB (1/4 − I(η0))η0², DSC 1 − I(η0)η0²; lopsided — MSE 1 + 2δ0²/π, MCB (1/4 − I(√(8/π)δ0))(8/π)δ0², DSC 1 − I(√(8/π)δ0)(8/π)δ0², where I(·) is a numerically-evaluated integral. ## 9. Leakage & limitations
Theory paper — no empirical ML fitting, so no lookahead/survivorship risk in its own results. Limitations: CORP consistency of T-reliability diagrams requires the isotonicity assumption on the population recalibration function (non-monotone miscalibration gets flattened into horizontal segments — a feature for stability but a distortion risk if the true recalibration is genuinely non-monotone); consistency bands for point forecasts rely on a residual-resampling scheme that assumes independence between forecasts and residuals (violated in most NFL time-series contexts with serially correlated errors); App. B.1 discusses issues when using auto-calibration as null for T-calibration bands. R* ∈ [0,1] only under in-sample loss-minimization-with-intercept conditions (Assumption 3.5) — does not hold out-of-sample, where the paper says to use the (possibly negative) skill score instead. Identifiability restriction: the auto-calibration ⇒ T-calibration implication holds only for functionals defined via an identification function (counterexample in paper: variance/standard-deviation functionals fail). External validity to NFL: machinery is generic (works on any (forecast, outcome) pairs), but consistency bands assume exchangeability — NFL game outcomes with team-strength persistence violate this; seasonal retraining needed. This paper was not checked against my own computation — all claims above are the paper's.

## 11. GSE implementation spec
1. **In-sample model diagnostics for the GSE engine**: after each training run (XGBoost / tabular models on nflverse features), compute CORP T-reliability diagrams for mean forecasts (margin/spread-total) under squared error and for quantile forecasts (win-probability tails, α=0.1/0.5/0.9) under pinball loss, using the PAV algorithm (scikit-learn has `IsotonicRegression`; for quantiles implement T-PAV via `statsmodels` or the R `reliabilitydiag` generalized per Alg. 1 — effort: ~1-2 days to implement T-PAV for quantile/expectile in Python). Report MCB/DSC/UNC + R* per model version in the run log — this becomes the standard "variance-explained" metric replacing ad-hoc R² for quantile heads.
2. **Out-of-sample forecast evaluation**: on rolling backtests (2020–2026 nflverse games, prequential, time-ordered), apply the empirical decomposition (eq. 27/29) to GSE's spread and total forecasts; plot MCB vs DSC scatter (per paper's suggestion as a Taylor-diagram alternative) across model variants. Skill score vs marginal reference (team-averaged margin) tells whether the model beats the unconditional baseline.
3. **Serving**: no serving change — this is a diagnostics/evaluation library, not an inference component. Estimated effort: ~2-3 days for a Python CORP diagnostics module + integration into the existing backtest harness; 90% consistency bands via residual resampling (note the independence caveat — use block resampling by week to respect game-time dependence).

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: nflverse play-by-play derived game-level table, 2020–2025 regular seasons (~1,600 games): GSE engine's closing spread forecast (mean functional) + actual margin. Baseline: current engine diagnostics (plain MSE / in-sample R²). Metric: CORP decomposition under squared error — compute MCB, DSC, UNC, R* via T-PAV isotonic recalibration (in-sample on train window, evaluated out-of-sample on 2025 season). Test: (a) verify MCB̂ ≈ 0 and R* stable across the two windows; (b) compare two engine versions on the 2025 out-of-sample window using the skill score S_skill = 1 − S̄̂/Ŝ_mg and MCB̂ (paper's recommended separation: pick the version with lower MCB̂ when DSĈ are comparable).

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
Adopt (ADAPT) if: on the 2025 out-of-sample window, CORP diagnostics detect miscalibration in the current engine (MĈB ≥ 0.15 × UNC, i.e., ≥15% of score variance is recalibratable bias) OR the engine's current diagnostics cannot separate calibration from discrimination — either outcome justifies adopting the decomposition as the standard diagnostics suite. Reject if: MĈB < 0.05 × UNC on both train and 2025 windows (engine already conditionally mean-calibrated; plain MSE suffices) AND quantile diagnostics add nothing (MĈB under pinball loss < 0.05 × ÛNC at all three α levels).

## 14. Improvement experiment
Go beyond the paper: extend the MCB-vs-DSC scatter-plot comparison across *ensembles* — for each game, compute the PAV-recalibrated value of the GSE ensemble mean vs each constituent model's forecast, and test whether cross-fitted (out-of-fold) isotonic recalibration of the ensemble mean reduces out-of-sample pinball/MSE loss vs the raw ensemble mean. The paper only evaluates single forecasts; NFL-specific twist: stratify the CORP diagrams by market regime (closing-line favorite vs underdog) and by week-block to test whether miscalibration concentrates in specific regimes (e.g., large favorites) — something the paper's isotonicity assumption would flatten globally but block-stratified PAV would recover. This directly attacks the independence caveat in §9.
