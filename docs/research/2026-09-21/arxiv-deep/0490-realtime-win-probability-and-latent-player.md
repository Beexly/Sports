# [0490] Real-time Win Probability and Latent Player Ability via STATS X in Team Sports (arXiv:2602.19513v1)

**Citation:** Yasutaka Shimizu, Atsushi Yamanobe (2026). *Real-time Win Probability and Latent Player Ability via STATS X in Team Sports*. arXiv:2602.19513v1. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19513v1
**Ledger completed:** 2026-09-21. **Read:** full text (PDF text extract, 7116 lines).
**Verdict:** ADAPT — adapt the closed-form Brownian-motion win-probability derivation and the "Interval on Fire" (IoF) flow-contribution index design to NFL drive-level data; the basketball linear model itself does not transfer.

## 1. Research question
Can discrete game outcomes (win/loss) and scores be mapped to a continuous "dominance" measure (T-score) whose time-evolving stochastic process (T-process) yields analytically tractable, real-time win probabilities — and can intervals of rising dominance ("Intervals on Fire") be used to quantify players' latent, flow-based contributions (STATS X) that static metrics like EFF and PER miss?

## 2. Dataset / schema
Japanese professional basketball B.League, 2022–2023 season: regular season n=60 games per team analysis, official data via B.League website and minute-by-minute play-by-play + box-score data from Data Stadium Inc. (contract-restricted, not public). Eight representative STATS per game: Points (PTs), Field Goal Made (FGM), Three Points FGM (3FGM), Defence Rebound (DR), Offence Rebound (OR), Assists (AS), Turn Over (TO), Foul Drawn (FD). Test applications: two 2022–2023 playoff games of the Chiba Jets (lost 73–88 to Ryukyu Golden Kings on 2023/5/28; won 94–66 vs Alvark Tokyo on 2023/4/30). The minute-by-minute granularity and the B.League data are proprietary to the competition.

## 3. Method / model
(a) T-score function T(a,b): [0,∞)²→ℝ mapping scores to continuous dominance, with draw benchmark c satisfying T(x,x)=c and sign(T(a,b)−c)=sign(a−b). Paper proposes five variants: simple difference a−b (c=0); simple ratio a/b (c=1, asymmetric); symmetrized ratio T=(2−b/a)1{a≥b}+(a/b)1{b>a} (c=1, |T−1|=|a−b|/max(a,b), antisymmetric); log-ratio T=log((a+κ)/(b+κ)) (c=0, antisymmetric, scale-invariant when κ=0); relative difference (a−b)/(a+b+κ) (c=0, bounded in (−1,1), antisymmetric but not scale-invariant); normalized type (a−b)/√(a+b+κ), motivated as asymptotically standardized score difference under Poisson scores (Slutsky + CLT). Basketball analysis uses the symmetrized ratio (2.1). (b) Team model: T^k = α0 + F_α(S^k) + ε^k, IID noise σ², where α0 ≡ TFS (Team Fundamental Score = pre-game baseline strength) and F_α(S^k) ≡ TSS^k (Team Statistical Score = game-specific performance). (c) Player model: T^k = α0 + Σ_j f_α(S^{k,j}) + ε^k with a COMMON player function f_α (same STATS evaluated the same way across players); PSS^{k,j}=f_α(S^{k,j}); PCS^{k,j} = α0/J + α0·(PSS^{k,j}−TSS^k/J)/D^k ·1{D^k>0}, D^k=Σ_j|PSS^{k,j}−TSS^k/J| — equal-share baseline plus deviation-weighted reallocation of TFS (Σ_j PCS = α0). (d) Dynamic T-process: diffusion standardization S_i(t)=(S̃_i(t)−m_i t)/v_i (note: denominator is v_i, not v_i√t, deliberately matching Brownian variance structure Var∝t); under high-frequency Poisson-type accumulation the standardized STATS converge weakly to Brownian motion (functional CLT). T(t)=α0+F_α(S_1(t),…,S_d(t))+σε(t) with ε a standard Brownian motion; modified T-process _mT(t)=T(α0,β0)+F_α(S(t))+σε(t) incorporating opponent TFS β0. Predicted T-process _pT(u|t)=T_*(t,a,b)+(_mT(u)−_mT(t)) with ad hoc anchor T_*(t,a,b)=(1−t)T(α0,β0)+t·T(a,b) (weights pre-game reputation early, realized score late). (e) Win probability: PW_t = P(_pT(1|t)>c | F_t); under Brownian STATS + linear F_α, closed form PW_t = 1−Φ((c−T_*(t,a(t),b(t)))/√((1−t)(τ²+σ²))), τ²=Σ_i α_i². Sensitivity: ∂PW_t/∂S_i = φ((c−T_*)/√((1−t)(τ²+σ²)))·α_i/√((1−t)(τ²+σ²)). (f) STATS X: δ-Interval on Fire IoF_δ^k = union of [t_r,t_{r+1}) where Δ^{(r)}_mT^k/Δ>δ (δ chosen per game as the fourth-largest rate of change in the empirical application); X-index X_δ^{k,j}=∫_{I^{k,j}} 1_{IoF_δ^k}(t)dt = player's time on court during IoF (I^{k,j}=player j's court time); STATS X = h(X_δ^{k,j}), paper uses h(x)=δx (so δX bounds the _mT increment over IoF); PTS^j=(1/n)Σ_k[PCS^{k,j}+h(X^{k,j})]. Estimation: least squares (7.1) for α_{0:8}, AIC stepwise selection; δ per game.

## 4. Equations & assumptions
Key equations, faithfully: (2.1) T(a,b)=(2−b/a)1{a≥b}+(a/b)1{b>a}, c=1. (3.1) F_α(0,…,0)=0. (3.2) T^k=α0+F_α(S_1^k,…,S_d^k)+ε^k. (3.3) TFS:=α0. (3.4) TSS^k:=F_α(S_1^k,…,S_d^k). (3.5) T^k=α0+Σ_{j=1}^{J} f_α(S_1^{k,j},…,S_d^{k,j})+ε^k. (3.6) PSS^{k,j}=f_α(…). (3.7) PCS^{k,j}=α0/J+α0·(PSS^{k,j}−TSS^k/J)/D^k·1{D^k>0}, D^k=Σ_j|PSS^{k,j}−TSS^k/J|. (4.1) S_i(t)=(S̃_i(t)−m_i t)/v_i. (4.2) T(t)=α0+F_α(S_1(t),…,S_d(t))+σε(t). (4.3) _mT(t)=T(α0,β0)+F_α(S(t))+σε(t). (4.4)/(4.5) _pT(u|t)=T_*(t,a,b)+{_mT(u)−_mT(t)}, T_*(t,a,b)=(1−t)T(α0,β0)+t·T(a,b). (5.1) PW_t:=P(_pT(1|t)>c|F_t). (5.2) PW_t=1−Φ((c−T_*(t,a(t),b(t)))/√((1−t)(τ²+σ²))), τ²=Σα_i². (5.3) ∂PW_t/∂S_i as above. (6.1) PTS^j=(1/n)Σ_k[PCS^{k,j}+h(X^{k,j})]. (7.1) LS estimator α̂_{0:8}=argmin Σ_k|Y^k−(α0+Σα_iS_i^k)|². Assumptions: IID mean-0 noise; common f_α across players; diffusion-standardized cumulative STATS ≈ d-dimensional Brownian motion (high-frequency/Poisson regime); ε independent of each S_j; linear F_α for the closed form; opponent effect enters only through T(α0,β0); IoF threshold δ arbitrary (chosen as fourth-largest rate of change).

## 5. Features / target
Inputs: cumulative minute-by-minute STATS (PTs, FGM, 3FGM, OR, DR, AS, TO, FD) + cumulative scores a(t), b(t) + pre-game team strengths (α0, β0). Targets: end-of-game T-score T(a,b); in-game: _mT(t) path, PW_t; player-level: PCS, X-index, PTS. Prediction horizon: continuous t∈[0,1] through game end; win probability updated sequentially.

## 6. Validation design
No out-of-sample predictive validation. Empirical analysis: (1) team-level TFS estimated on 60 regular-season games, correlation with win rate; (2) coefficient estimation + AIC stepwise for Chiba Jets; (3) two held-out playoff games (model fit on regular season, applied to playoffs) — visual/path analysis of _mT vs PW_t, strategic interpretation; (4) player PCS/STATS X/PTS on those two games compared against EFF and PER rankings. No reported WP accuracy (Brier, log-loss) vs any baseline; no calibration; splits are time-ordered only in the sense that playoff games follow the regular-season fit.

## 9. Leakage & limitations
- The TFS "correlation" ρ=0.989 is circular: α0 is estimated from the same 60-game regular-season outcomes whose win rates it is then correlated with — it's a fitted intercept regressed on final scores, not an out-of-sample strength estimate. Presented as validation; it is in-sample fit.
- Playoff "predictions" are path illustrations on 2 hand-picked games of the league's best team; no accuracy metric for PW_t anywhere (no Brier score, no calibration curve, no baseline comparison). The PW_t formula's assumptions (Brownian STATS, linear F_α, ad hoc T_* anchor) are never empirically tested.
- T_*(t,a,b)=(1−t)T(α0,β0)+t·T(a,b) is explicitly ad hoc (Remark 4.3) — the PW_t is "a definition, not a theorem" (Remark 5.1). The closed form depends on this unmotivated linear time-blend.
- n=60 games, 9 parameters, heavy multicollinearity (PTs vs FGM vs 3FGM); the paper itself notes OR's negative sign is likely a conditioning artifact (Remark 7.1/7.2) and warns that reflecting raw coefficient signs into PCS produces domain-inconsistent deductions — a candid but fundamental limitation of the additive linear PCS.
- IoF threshold δ is chosen per game as "the fourth-largest rate of change" — arbitrary; IoF length and all X-index comparisons are threshold-dependent.
- Being on court during IoF does not identify who generated the momentum (paper concedes); X-index is a participation measure, and the "seasonal averaging washes out coincidence" claim is asserted, not tested (analysis is 2 games).
- NFL external validity: Brownian high-frequency approximation is far more strained for NFL (17 games, low event counts, discrete play structure); the "lightweight" appeal is real but the inference guarantees break down.

## 10. GSE overlap
Partial overlap, but the mechanism is new. Garrett's corpus has extensive in-game win-probability machinery: iWinRNFL (1704.00197) already read; nfl4th fourth-down WP models; conformal WP (2208.08598); and general state-space team strength (1701.05976, nested AR(1)). The ρ=0.989 TFS-vs-win-rate display is a weaker version of existing strength models (no new capability there). What is NEW and not in the corpus: (a) the closed-form Brownian-motion WP derivation under diffusion standardization — an analytic WP family that could complement nfl4th's empirical grid; (b) the IoF/X-index "flow interval" player evaluation — nothing in the repo quantifies player involvement in team momentum intervals (the closest is samhoppen WPA waterfalls, which is accounting, not stochastic process); (c) the antisymmetric T-score normalizations (log-ratio with κ, normalized (a−b)/√(a+b+κ)) as target transforms — GSE's margin/score models use raw margins; these variance-stabilizing transforms are unused. So: extension of the WP lane (new analytic form) + new capability (flow-interval player evaluation) + new capability (score-target normalization theory).

## 11. GSE implementation spec
NFL adaptation plan: (1) Replace minute-by-minute STATS with drive-level cumulative statistics from nflverse play-by-play (drives as time steps, t = fraction of game drives elapsed): cumulative team EPA, success rate, turnovers, third-down conversions, field position. (2) Use the normalized T-score T(a,b)=(a−b)/√(a+b+κ) on final margins (κ as a stabilizer, fit by CV) as the regression target instead of raw margin — variance-stabilized, approximately Gaussian under the paper's Poisson argument. (3) Estimate team TFS per season via the linear model on drive-aggregated STATS; compute _mT paths for in-game flow and derive IoF intervals per game (δ tuned per game-week as a quantile of |Δ_mT|). (4) Player X-index: NFL "on the field during IoF" from snap participation (nflverse roster/participation) — aggregates to season-level flow-involvement ratings for WRs/CBs/QBs, complementary to existing coverage-matchup tables. (5) Closed-form PW_t with τ² from fitted coefficients as an analytic alternative to the nfl4th grid for live WP — calibrate on 2020–2024, compare Brier/log-loss. Effort: ~1–2 weeks for a statistician-engineer (data are all public nflverse; no proprietary dependency). Main risk: Brownian assumptions in a 17-game, low-event league — validate calibration before any use.

## 12. Reproducible test
Dataset: nflverse play-by-play 2020–2024, drive-aggregated. Baseline: nfl4th WP grid (or logistic WP on score differential + time remaining) on the same games. Metric: Brier score and log-loss of in-game WP at each drive boundary, plus calibration-by-decile. Window: fit TFS/coefficients on 2020–2022, validate closed-form PW_t on 2023–2024. Runnable entirely on public data.

## 13. Acceptance / rejection gate
ADOPT the Brownian closed-form WP as a live-model component if, on the 2023–2024 holdout, its Brier score is ≤ the nfl4th-grid Brier score (within 0.0005) AND it is well-calibrated in all deciles (Hosmer-Lemeshow-style max decile deviation <0.03); ADOPT the IoF/X-index player metric if season X-index has test-retest stability ≥0.5 across season halves (i.e., it measures a persistent trait, not noise). REJECT both otherwise — the paper provides no accuracy evidence, so the burden is on our replication.

## 14. Improvement experiment
Replace the ad hoc linear anchor T_*(t,a,b)=(1−t)T(α0,β0)+t·T(a,b) with an empirically estimated weight w(t) fit by minimizing out-of-sample WP log-loss — i.e., learn how fast pre-game strength should decay as game information accumulates, as a monotone function of t. Hypothesis: the optimal w(t) decays faster than (1−t) early (NFL games have fewer, higher-leverage early events than basketball), which would beat the paper's fixed anchor on calibration; this turns the weakest assumption into a fitted, testable component.
