## 1. Research question

Can reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) train a *calibrated aleatoric forecaster* — where the output itself is a probability and the label is one stochastic Bernoulli outcome, not a correct/incorrect answer (this is the regime RLVR calibration work had not studied; prior work covered epistemic confidence only)?

Two failure mechanisms are isolated:

1. **Label-noise variance:** rewarding the realized per-play outcome `y ∈ {0,1}` gives a noisy target — one draw from the rate being estimated — whose policy gradient "pulls the policy toward whichever result occurred" and decalibrates the forecaster.
2. **Gradient corruption of reasoning:** when the model writes a chain of thought before the probability, optimizing the final probability rewrites the reasoning into incoherent pseudo-quantitative arguments for ever more extreme numbers.

The paper's claim: replace the realized-outcome reward with a **verifiable, label-free, state-conditioned empirical win rate** `p̂(x)` estimated from past outcomes (low variance), and **keep the policy gradient off the reasoning** (direct prediction, or a gradient mask confined to the answer span). Then a 7B model with no human labels and no supervised fine-tuning reaches the betting market's calibration.

---

## 2. Dataset/schema

- **Source:** public nflfastR play-by-play data, NFL regular seasons 2015–2024.
- **Example:** one play = one training example.
- **State features (the forecaster's input):** score margin of the team in possession, quarter and time remaining, down and distance, field position, team in possession, and the **public pregame point spread** (the only market signal in the prompt; fixed before kickoff).
- **Output:** a single probability that the team in possession wins the game.
- **Label for training the reward:** the realized outcome of whether the possession team won (used only to build the empirical-rate table, never as the reward directly).
- **Evaluation reference:** the live in-game betting market win probability, converted from odds to probability per Štrumbelj [32]; **withheld from the model and the reward**, entering only at evaluation.
- **Splits (disjoint by season):** 40,246 training states (2015–2022), 5,241 selection states (2023), 5,185 test states (2024). "No game appears in more than one split."

---

## 3. Method/model

**Base model:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, a dense non-thinking model, with **no supervised fine-tuning**; the only training is RL against the rate reward. Compared against the untrained model (direct and CoT prompts), a zero-shot frontier model (DeepSeek-V4 via API), the empirical-rate teacher `p̂`, the nflverse WP model, a gradient-boosted model on the standard feature set, and the betting market.

**Reward (the paper's contribution):** score each forecast against a state-conditioned empirical win rate instead of the realized outcome:
- Bin training plays by **score margin** (14 bins, edges at ±1, ±4, ±7, ±10, ±14, ±21 points) × **time remaining** (7 bins, edges at 2, 5, 10, 15, 30, 45 minutes) × **pregame point spread** (9 bins, edges at ±0.5, ±3, ±7, ±10).
- Each bucket's raw value = fraction of its training plays the possession team won.
- **Sparse buckets shrunk toward coarser parents** by a hierarchical empirical-Bayes rule with pseudocount M = 25.
- The paper "calls `p̂` the teacher, because a model trained on eq. (1) can be no better calibrated than `p̂` itself."
- Reward uses the **coarse** table only: adding field position and down under the same backoff did not improve game-level calibration (2023: coarse 0.1532 Brier / 0.0117 ECE vs fine 0.1534 / 0.0099, market 0.1495 / 0.0193).

**Optimizer:** group-relative policy optimization (GRPO), TRL implementation, vLLM rollouts colocated in the training process, **single NVIDIA L40S (48 GB) GPU**. LoRA (rank 16, α = 32, dropout 0.05) on attention and feed-forward projections, bfloat16. 8 completions per state at temperature 0.9, token-level loss, single on-policy update per step. TRL's vLLM importance-sampling correction disabled (it "masks the loss under the small per-token mismatch between vLLM and training log-probabilities"). 250 steps, 20-step warmup, checkpoint every 50; **checkpoint selected by Brier on the full 2023 split**, reported on 2024.

**Two gradient-decoupling variants:**
- **Direct model:** drops the chain of thought entirely, emits the probability in ≤ 48 tokens. Learning rate 2×10⁻⁵, KL coefficient 0.01, reward scaling on, gradient accumulation over 16 micro-batches.
- **Masked model:** keeps up to 640 tokens of reasoning but narrows the completion mask (the tokens receiving advantage) to the final "Probability: NN%" span — the reasoning is sampled and conditions the answer but receives no gradient. KL coefficient **β = 0** ("Masking the gradient needs the KL penalty turned off, because on the few tokens of the answer span the gradient is concentrated enough to make the penalty's estimator overflow and the loss diverge"). Learning rate 3×10⁻⁵.

**Evaluation:** Brier score, ECE and MCE over ten equal-width bins, accuracy vs the realized winner, Murphy decomposition (reliability / resolution / uncertainty); reliability diagrams; 95% CIs from nonparametric bootstraps over plays with 10⁴ resamples; paired bootstrap of per-play Brier differences to compare forecasters on the same plays. Checkpoint selection during training uses a fixed random sample of 128 held-out 2023 states.

## 4. Equations & assumptions (copied from the text; not invented)

Ideal target (never observed): `η(x) = Pr(win | x)`, "the conditional win rate."

Naive per-outcome reward:
`r = 1 − (p − y)²`, where `y ∈ {0,1}`.

Proposed rate reward (eq. 1):
`r = 1 − (p − p̂(x))²`

Brier score: "the mean squared error (p − y)² between a forecast p and an outcome y ∈ {0,1}" — "strictly proper: among all functions of the state, its expectation is minimized uniquely by η(x)."

Hierarchical empirical-Bayes shrinkage for a bucket with count n, wins w, parent estimate p̂_parent, pseudocount M = 25, applied from the global rate downward:
bucket value = `(w + M·p̂_parent) / (n + M)`

Ablation target in Table 1: "Blend ½ y + ½ p̂."

**Key assumptions stated in the text:**
- The Brier score's expectation is minimized uniquely by the true conditional rate, so optimizing it "should produce forecasts whose probabilities match observed frequencies."
- Calibration error "also tests whether training found η rather than memorizing the training outcomes, since a model that memorized them would be miscalibrated on held-out games."
- The betting market is taken "as the ceiling": the gap between a forecaster and the market "measures the live in-game information that the public state lacks."

## 5. Features/target

- **Features (input):** score margin, quarter/time remaining, down and distance, field position, team in possession, public pregame point spread. (Finer features — field position, down — were tested in the fine table and found not to add game-level signal; "the reward uses the coarse score-by-time-by-spread table.")
- **Model output:** single win probability for the possession team, emitted as a final "Probability: NN%" span (or ≤48 tokens directly).
- **Training target:** the empirical rate `p̂(x)` from the reward table — itself estimated from realized outcomes plus the public pregame line. "The target uses no human labels. It comes from the same outcomes a binary reward would use, averaged over states to yield a low-variance estimate of η(x)."
- **Evaluation targets:** realized outcomes (for Brier/ECE/accuracy) and the withheld market probability (as the ceiling reference). On held-out 2024, the teacher itself scores a Brier of 0.143 against the market's 0.136.

---

## 6. Validation design

- **Season-disjoint splits:** train 2015–2022, select 2023, test 2024; no game in more than one split. The empirical-rate target for evaluation plays is read from the **training-season table alone**, "so no test outcome enters its own target."
- **Reward-target ablation (Table 1)** under identical training (direct prediction, in-training held-out n=128): realized outcome vs ½/½ blend vs pure rate.
- **Gradient-decoupling experiment (Fig. 2):** the full-completion run and the masked run "share the same reward, prompt, and base model, and differ only in the gradient mask" — the cleanest identification in the paper.
- **Paired bootstrap over plays** (n=5185, 10⁴ resamples) for every forecaster comparison (Table 2).
- **Blinded-judge protocol (Appendix C):** 250 held-out 2023 plays stratified across game states; a separate model, blind to source, judges whether the chain of thought misreads any of five game facts (possession, score, spread, clock, pregame favorite) and whether the stated probability follows from the reasoning, with a quote supporting each judgment. The authors "audited the judge by reading every flagged case."
- **Data-integrity audit (Appendix D):** scanned all 40,246 training prompts and the 2023/2024 evaluation prompts for the market win probability — "found no occurrence"; verified the live market probability appears in none of the training prompts; "every reported number is recomputed from the per-play predictions by a single deterministic script."
- **Hyperparameter sweeps (Appendix B):** learning-rate, batch-size, and KL sweep reported separately for both models; selection metric is the in-training greedy Brier on a fixed n=128 held-out-2023 sample.
- In-training checkpointing uses a fixed random sample of 128 held-out 2023 states scored greedily — a noisy selection signal relative to the final full-split numbers (worth noting in §9).

## 7. Numerical results/baselines (quoted EXACTLY as the paper states them)

**Reward-target ablation (Table 1, in-training held-out, n = 128):**

| Reward target | Brier | ECE |
|---|---|---|
| Realized outcome y ∈ {0, 1} | 0.166 | 0.10 |
| Blend ½ y + ½ p̂ | 0.181 | 0.121 |
| Empirical rate p̂ (ours) | 0.154 | 0.050 |

"Rewarding the realized outcome reaches a Brier of 0.166, but its calibration error drifts to 0.10 as the policy fits individual outcomes. An equal blend of outcome and rate is worse on both, 0.181 and 0.121, because it reintroduces the outcome's noise."

**Full-completion CoT training (rate reward, §4.2):** "Applied to the full completion, the reward of eq. (1) drives the held-out Brier score from the base model's 0.25 to 0.34 and the calibration error from 0.19 to 0.30 (fig. 2)."

**Paired comparisons (Table 2, ΔBrier with 95% CI):** Direct − Masked-CoT: **−0.0079*** [−0.0101, −0.0057]; Direct − Market: **+0.0088*** [0.0068, 0.0107]; Masked-CoT − Market: **+0.0167*** [0.0140, 0.0194]; Direct − DeepSeek-V4: +0.0005 [−0.0019, 0.0028]; DeepSeek-V4 − Market: **+0.0083*** [0.0064, 0.0102]. ("*" = significant.)

**Headline calibration claims:** "the model matches the market's calibration on held-out games, at an expected calibration error (ECE) of 0.029 against the market's 0.027"; "Its maximum calibration error, 0.060, is below the market's 0.082"; "the direct model is better calibrated than the frontier model, 0.029 against 0.043."

**Resolution decomposition:** the 0.008 Brier gap to the market is "resolution, not reliability: the model is as well calibrated as the market but less sharp, 0.106 against 0.115."

**Static-feature forecasters (Table 4, held-out 2024):** Coarse p̂: Brier 0.1432, ECE 0.0437; nflverse WP model: 0.1562, ECE 0.0188; GBM, all features: 0.1584, ECE 0.0260; Direct RLVR: 0.1443, ECE 0.0292; Betting market: 0.1355, ECE 0.0273. "Two models with more features than the coarse buckets are worse on Brier."

**Reasoning-consistency (Fig. 3 / Table 8, blinded judge, 95% CI):** Base CoT 22.4% [17.6, 27.6] inconsistent → Masked-CoT RLVR 4.4% [2.0, 7.2]. Controls: Naive CoT-RLVR (p̂ reward) 18.8%, naive CoT-RLVR (outcome reward) 14.0%, base with masked prompt (no training) 6.8%. "Full-completion reinforcement learning leaves the base rate unchanged."

**Teacher granularity (Table 5, 2023):** Coarse 0.1532 / 0.0117; Fine (+field position + down) 0.1534 / 0.0099; Market 0.1495 / 0.0193.

**Masked-model hyperparameter notes:** with β=0.01 the loss diverges ("the k3 KL estimator overflow"); learning rate inverted-U peaking at 3×10⁻⁵, collapsing ("over-sharpens", MCE past 0.5) at 4×10⁻⁵.

## 9. Leakage & limitations (adversarial read)

1. **The "RL" is largely distilling a lookup table.** The reward teacher `p̂` is a hand-binned empirical rate from the same training outcomes; the policy can at best match it, and the authors admit "a model trained on eq. (1) can be no better calibrated than `p̂` itself." The headline "reaches the calibration of the betting market" is really "reaches the calibration of the table" — and notably, on the 2024 test the trained model is *better* calibrated than its own teacher (ECE 0.029 vs 0.044), which the paper attributes to the policy "smoothing the bucketed target." A smoother could do that without RL: fit an isotonic or spline calibration on the bucketed rates and skip the GPU entirely. The paper never compares to that one-line baseline.

2. **Noisy in-training selection.** Checkpoint selection and the Table 1 ablation use a fixed n=128 held-out sample scored greedily. Per-play Brier variance is high; the 128-sample standard error is large enough that checkpoint picks among "every 50 steps" are substantially noise-driven. The reported test numbers use the full splits, but the *selection* that produced them was noisy.

3. **The GRPO normalization reversal is thin.** The paper's swipe at Bereket & Leskovec [7] ("with a denoised conditional-rate target we find instead that the normalization is necessary") rests on a single lever in the Appendix B ablation (reward scaling off → Brier 0.178, ECE 0.064) in the noisy n=128 selection regime. That's a reward-scaling lever, not the group-std-normalization of the advantage — the two are conflated.

4. **KL=0 + concentrated-gradient overflow is an engineering hack, not a result.** Removing the KL anchor is justified only by "the k3 estimator overflows"; the inverted-U learning-rate curve ending in "over-sharpens" at 4×10⁻⁵ with MCE past 0.5 shows the masked variant is finicky. Any GSE reuse of gradient masking should expect the same instability.

5. **The blinded-judge evidence is weak.** The judge is an unnamed "separate model"; the inconsistency CIs for the masked prompt format alone (6.8% [4.0, 9.6]) and masked training (4.4% [2.0, 7.2]) overlap at n=250 — the paper concedes "At 250 plays the format and training intervals overlap" and leans on a "separate control on the reported checkpoint" for significance. The 22.4%→4.4% headline number conflates format change with training effect; the isolated training effect is roughly 6.8%→4.4%, much smaller.

6. **"Convergence of three estimators ⇒ information ceiling" is an interpretation, not a proof.** Direct model, DeepSeek-V4, and the tabular rate all landing near 0.143 could equally reflect a *shared bias* (all consume the same public game state) rather than the state's information limit. The market's 0.008 edge is attributed to "live in-game information" — but the decomposition says the gap is *resolution*, which is exactly what a better static feature set would also close. The GBM-with-more-features failure is one feature set, not a bound; Brill et al. [26] is cited for an analytic bound, but the paper's own empirics don't separate "no static model can close it" from "we didn't find the features."

7. **The regime is unusually kind to the method.** Outcomes are dense (40k plays), resolve in hours, and the state space is low-dimensional. The conclusion itself concedes: "sparse or long-horizon events would weaken both the reward and the comparison against a market." GSE's bread-and-butter — pregame spread/total/moneyline picks with ~16 games/week and props with heterogeneous contexts — is precisely the sparser regime.

8. **ECE on ten equal-width bins is a forgiving metric.** The MCE story is mixed: direct model MCE 0.0596 vs market 0.0824 looks good, but the teacher's MCE is 0.0993 — the smoothing that helps ECE may just be hiding tail miscalibration in low-density bins. Reliability diagrams "with area proportional to plays in the bin" confirm most mass sits on the diagonal, but sparse-bin behavior is unreported.

9. **Authorship provenance.** "Cascade Research" is not an established institution I'm aware of; the paper is dated June 2026 and this is v1. The code release is a genuine verifiability plus, and Appendix D's audit is unusually thorough — but treat headline numbers as pending independent replication.

10. **Credibility of "no human labels."** True in the narrow sense, but the empirical rate is estimated from realized outcomes with human-chosen bin edges (score ±1/±4/±7/±10/±14/±21; time 2/5/10/15/30/45; spread ±0.5/±3/±7/±10) — substantial human structure is injected through the binning. The "coarse table is enough" finding depends on those specific edges.

Relevant known engine modules (from the task; I have not read their file contents — overlap assessed by function name and stated purpose only):

## 11. GSE implementation spec (concrete, implementation-complete)

Build a **denoised-rate calibration auditor** as a new module `packages/prediction-engine/src/calibration-rate-audit.db.ts` (new file; composes with `calibration-ladder` and `pick-clv`, does not modify them):

**Step 1 — Build empirical-rate tables (offline job, weekly).**
- Context key: for game picks, `bin(edgeBucket, situationalBucket, marketBucket)` where `edgeBucket` = engine's own predicted probability in 10 equal-width bins, `situationalBucket` = coarse game context (home/away × rest-differential bucket × divisional-game flag), `marketBucket` = closing-spread bucket with edges at ±0.5, ±3, ±7, ±10 (mirror the paper).
- For props (dense, nightly-resolving — the best fit): `bin(playerTierBucket, lineBucket, matchupBucket)` over the trailing 2 seasons of resolved props.
- Bucket value = hit rate of engine picks in that bucket; shrink sparse buckets toward the coarser parent via `(w + M·p̂_parent)/(n + M)` with **M = 25** (paper's value), applied global → situational → full key. Minimum bucket n for unshrunk reporting: 30.
- Store one table per market type (spread, total, moneyline, props) in the engine DB.

**Step 2 — Score every posted pick against the rate (not the outcome).**
- For each pick with engine probability `p` and realized outcome `y`: compute `r_rate = 1 − (p − p̂(key))²` and `r_outcome = 1 − (p − y)²`.
- Output row: `{ pick_id, market_type, model_p, empirical_rate_p_hat, bin_key, n_bucket, reward_rate, reward_outcome, ece_contribution, flagged }`.
- `flagged = true` when `|model_p − p̂(key)| > 0.05` AND `n_bucket ≥ 30` (a materially miscalibrated bucket with enough support).

**Step 3 — Weekly report (the usable product).**
- Per-bucket: mean `r_rate` vs mean `r_outcome`, bucket ECE, n.
- Variance comparison: bootstrap the variance of per-pick `r_rate` vs `r_outcome`; expect the paper's qualitative finding (rate reward far less noisy). Report the ratio.
- The convergence test: maintain three independent probability sources per pick — engine `p`, empirical table `p̂`, market-implied `p_mkt`. When all three agree within a Brier tolerance on a rolling 4-week window (|pairwise ΔBrier| < 0.005, cf. the paper's non-significant 0.0005 direct-vs-frontier gap), emit a `CEILING_REACHED` signal for that market type: stop tuning model capacity there, redirect effort to new information (features, markets).

**Inputs:** engine's historical picks with probabilities (already in the picks table — 3,411 picks), realized outcomes, closing lines (for market-implied probs), trailing-2-season resolved props.

**Method:** exactly the paper's Appendix A recipe (coarse bins, M=25 hierarchical backoff, train-table-only targets, season-disjoint evaluation) minus the LLM.

**Output shape:** one DB table `rate_audit` (rows per §Step 2) + one weekly markdown report with bucket table, variance ratio, and CEILING_REACHED flags.

## 12. Reproducible test

**Test:** "Rate reward is lower-variance and finds miscalibration the outcome metric misses."

- **Dataset:** GSE's historical NFL game picks (or, if picks lack probabilities, the engine's backtested game-win probabilities) for 2022–2024, with closing lines for market-implied probabilities. Build the rate table on 2022–2023 (train), evaluate on 2024 (test). If probabilities are unavailable, substitute the paper's own released per-play predictions + outcomes from the Zenodo archive as the fixture (code/data released, §8).
- **Procedure:**
  1. For each 2024 pick, compute `r_rate = 1 − (p − p̂)²` and `r_outcome = 1 − (p − y)²`.
  2. Bootstrap (10⁴ resamples over picks) the variance of the mean reward for both; assert `Var(mean r_rate) ≤ 0.5 × Var(mean r_outcome)`.
  3. Compute bucket ECE from realized outcomes on 2024; identify the worst-calibrated bucket with n ≥ 30. Assert the rate audit flags it (`|p − p̂| > 0.05`) **before** it is visible in cumulative outcome-ECE — i.e., the flag fires on the 2024 data while rolling outcome-ECE stays below 0.03.
- **Pass criteria:** (a) variance ratio ≤ 0.5; (b) at least one flagged bucket with ≥5pp deviation and n ≥ 30 in a period where outcome-ECE < 0.03. Both must hold.
- **Failure mode to watch:** if no bucket ever flags, the test is inconclusive, not a pass — report "no miscalibration found at n≥30," do not claim the method works.

## 13. Acceptance/rejection gate (numeric)

**ADOPT the rate-audit module (§11) if and only if ALL of the following hold on the held-out 2024 evaluation:**

1. `Var(mean r_rate) ≤ 0.5 × Var(mean r_outcome)` under the 10⁴-resample bootstrap (variance reduction of ≥50%).
2. `|ΔBrier| ≤ 0.005` between scoring picks with `r_rate` vs the engine's standard calibration metric — i.e., the denoised reward does not systematically move the level, only the noise (mirrors the paper's significant-but-tiny deltas).
3. The audit flags **≥1 bucket** with `|model_p − p̂| > 0.05` at `n_bucket ≥ 30` during a window where realized-outcome ECE stays **< 0.03** — proving it detects miscalibration the standard metric misses.
4. The three-source convergence check (engine, `p̂`, market-implied) reproduces the paper's qualitative pattern on at least one market type: pairwise |ΔBrier| < 0.01 with all three trailing the market — validating the CEILING_REACHED signal logic.

**Otherwise REJECT the module** (keep the bin-and-shrink recipe as a documented pattern only). In particular: if the engine's picks lack reliable per-pick probabilities, the module is unusable — mark BLOCKED-on-data, not ADOPT.

## 14. Improvement experiment (what would make this better / GSE-specific extension)

1. **Learned rate model as teacher (the paper's own suggested extension).** Replace the coarse lookup table with a gradient-boosted `p̂(x)` trained on trailing seasons — including market odds and situational features the paper excluded. The paper's coarse table is deliberately weak ("a model trained on eq. (1) can be no better calibrated than `p̂` itself"); a stronger teacher raises the ceiling the auditor enforces. A/B: audit flags from table-`p̂` vs GBM-`p̂`; adopt whichever flags earlier on walk-forward 2024.
2. **Props-first deployment.** The paper's regime works because outcomes are "dense and resolved quickly." GSE player props resolve nightly with thousands of samples — a far better fit than 16 games/week. Build the first rate tables for props, where bucket n accumulates in weeks, not seasons.
3. **Masked-gradient consistency for GSE's pick explanations.** If GSE ever fine-tunes an LLM to write pick rationale, apply the paper's answer-span mask: gradient only on the probability/edge tokens, never on the reasoning. Test with the paper's blinded-judge protocol adapted to pick writeups (does the stated edge follow from the writeup?). Expected effect per the paper: inconsistency 22.4% → ~4%.
4. **Close the resolution gap, don't admire it.** The paper stops at "the market's 0.008 edge is live information." For GSE the actionable version: decompose every pick's Brier vs market into reliability/resolution/uncertainty weekly; any persistent *reliability* gap is a calibration bug to fix this week, any *resolution* gap is a feature-hunt ticket. Wire this into the calibration-ladder as the standing weekly diagnostic.
5. **Adversarial use — synthetic fade trigger.** Invert the reward: where `|p_engine − p̂|` is large and persistent across weeks at n ≥ 30, that's a fade signal on the engine's number (or evidence the bucket is stale). Test whether fading those picks improves realized CLV over a season; if yes, the auditor doubles as a position-sizer input.
