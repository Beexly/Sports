## 1. Research question

Survey question, not an experimental one: given the explosion of large, sparse comparison datasets (sports, social choice, psychometrics, LLM preference alignment), what are the recent statistical and computational advances in the Bradley–Terry (BT) model — specifically (a) asymptotic theory for estimation/inference in the regime where both the number of objects n and the number of comparisons grow to infinity; (b) estimation algorithms (fixed-point iteration, gradient methods, spectral methods, Bayesian EM); (c) generalizations — general pairwise models with alternative link functions, Plackett–Luce for multiple comparisons, mixture models, covariate-assisted models; and (d) where the models apply (sports analytics is treated as a core application, with NFL listed as a dense-regime dataset).

The paper's stated contribution is "a systematic synthesis of recent advances... from both theoretical and computational perspectives" — it proposes no new estimator.

## 2. Dataset/schema

No novel dataset collected; the paper is a review. The schema formalized for comparison data (Section 3):

- **Objects:** n indexed by [n] = {1,…,n} (e.g., 32 NFL teams).
- **Comparison graph/hypergraph:** Hn(E) = ([n], E), E a multiset of edges; each e ∈ E specifies objects compared on one occasion; pairwise data is |e| = 2 ∀e. Outcomes {Ye}e∈E assumed independent conditional on Hn(E).
- **Pairwise outcome:** Yij ∈ {i ≻ j, j ≻ i} (or {−1, 1} in the log-likelihood form); for PL, Ye ∈ {iπ(1) ≻ ··· ≻ iπ(k) : π ∈ Sk} (full ranking) or partial rankings (choice-s).
- **Covariates:** xe,i ∈ R^d for object i on edge e; static (xe,i = xi, e.g., physical attributes) vs dynamic (varies per edge, e.g., home-field advantage).
- **Wins/totals:** wij = # times i defeats j; nij = wij + wji.

Table 2 reference datasets (sports rows, quoted verbatim): ATP |e|=2, |E|=130K, n=1.8K; Club Football 2/230K/1.2K; NBA 2/72.6K/30; **NFL 2/5.3K/32** ("National Football League matches"); Horse Racing 10–14/6.3K/2.8K; Pokemon 2/50K/800; Starcraft II 2/374K/10.8K; Chess 2/65K/8.6K.

Supplementary empirical comparison (S.3) datasets: synthetic ER graph (n=1000, |E|=16,481, utilities ∼ Unif[−1,1] centered, 100 replications) + ATP, Vervet Monkeys, Algebra I 2005–2006 (all pruned for strong connectivity with dmin > 30).

## 3. Method/model

Survey of the BT model and its extensions (no single new method):

- **Core BT model:** P(i beats j) = γi/(γi+γj) = σ(ui−uj), γi>0 strength, ui=log γi utility. Equivalent to logistic regression with covariate xij = ei−ej. Identifiable only up to additive constant (constraints: 1ᵀu=0, recommended by Wu et al. 2022 for its variance-minimization property; or ui=0 reference).
- **General pairwise comparisons (Han et al. 2023 framework):** outcome Yij takes values in symmetric set A (e.g., {−1,1}; {−1,0,1} with ties; R for cardinal), pmf f(Yij; ui−uj) with valid symmetry f(y;u)=f(−y;−u). Covers Thurstone–Mosteller, **Rao–Kupper (1967) and Davidson (1970) tie models**, ordinal models (Tutz/Agresti), paired cardinal model (Shah et al. 2016).
- **Plackett–Luce (PL) for multiple comparisons:** sequential choice probabilities; Gumbel latent-variable interpretation convenient for Bayesian inference.
- **BT mixture models:** P(Yij=i≻j) = Σh ωh σ(ui^(h)−uj^(h)) — heterogeneous populations/rater groups.
- **Covariate-assisted (PlusDC, Dong et al. 2025):** utility = ui + x{e,i}ᵀv, strictly generalizes BT with home-field advantage as a special case; time-varying ui(t) variants (Fahrmeir–Tutz, Cattelan et al. 2013).
- **Estimation reviewed:** MLE (BT-MLE, with Ford (1957) strong-connectivity existence condition), ridge-regularized MLE, spectral methods (RankCentrality, LSR/I-LSR, ASR), Bayesian (Gaussian priors, gamma-prior EM, Power EP, Gibbs, HMC), rank-breaking + quasi-MLE for partial rankings.
- **Algorithms reviewed:** Zermelo's MM fixed-point iteration; **Newman's (2023) faster fixed-point iteration** + Han et al. (2026) convergence theory; projected gradient descent (Elo update = online SGD step on BT log-likelihood per Király & Qian 2017 / Olesker-Taylor & Zanetti 2024); nonlinear power iteration for I-LSR.

## 4. Equations & assumptions (copy faithfully from the text; never invent — write "No equations stated" if none)

(BT-S): P(Yij = i ≻ j) = γi/(γi + γj)

(BT-U): P(Yij = i ≻ j) = exp(ui)/(exp(ui) + exp(uj)) = σ(ui − uj), σ(x) = (1 + exp(−x))^(−1)

(BT-MLE): û = arg max_{u ∈ R^n, 1ᵀu=0} Σ_{{i,j}∈E} log σ(Yij(ui − uj))

(BT-Bayes): p(u | {Ye}e∈E) ∝ g(u) × Π_{{i,j}∈E} σ(Yij(ui − uj))

(BTMixture): P(Yij = i ≻ j) = Σ_{h∈[H]} ωh σ(ui^(h) − uj^(h)), ωh > 0, Σh ωh = 1

(PlusDC): P(Yij = i ≻ j) = σ(ui − uj + (x{i,j},i − x{i,j},j)ᵀv)

(BT-RM): P(Yij = i ≻ j) = σ(r(xe,i) − r(xe,j))

(PL): P(Ye = iπ(1) ≻ ··· ≻ iπ(k)) = Π_{j=1}^{k−1} exp(uiπ(j)) / (Σ_{ℓ=j}^{k} exp(uiπ(ℓ)))

(Zermelo): γi = Σ_{j∈[n]} wij / Σ_{j∈[n]} nij/(γi + γj), with normalization Σi γi = 1 per iteration

(Newman): γi = [Σ_{j∈[n]} wij γj/(γi + γj)] / [Σ_{j∈[n]} wji/(γi + γj)]

(EM-MAP): γi = (a − 1 + Σ_{j∈[n]} wij) / (b + Σ_{j∈[n]} {nij/(γi + γj)}) — contains Zermelo as special case a=1, b=0

(GD): u(t+1) = Π_{1⊥}(u(t) + ηt ∇l(u(t))) — projected gradient descent with sum-to-zero projection

SST (strong stochastic transitivity, core assumption of all valid-f models): min{pij, pjk} ≥ 1/2 ⟹ pik ≥ max{pij, pjk}

Assumptions stated: (1) outcomes {Ye} independent conditional on the comparison graph; (2) comparison-graph choice independent of utilities (paper flags this as routinely violated); (3) identifiability requires connected graph + linear constraint (1ᵀu=0); (4) MLE existence requires Ford (1957) strong connectivity — a team that won every game gets ûi → ∞; (5) asymptotic theory leans on Erdős–Rényi sampling assumptions (or SBM/general topologies); (6) uniform-consistency optimal sparsity pn ≳ (log n)/n for ER graphs.

## 5. Features/target

- **Target:** pairwise win probabilities pij = σ(ui − uj); derived team ratings γi or utilities ui; rankings; confidence intervals for utilities; ranking-position confidence sets.
- **Features used by the models:** none for vanilla BT (only the (i,j) identity via xij = ei − ej). Extensions add: dynamic edge-level covariates (home-field indicator → PlusDC; the survey explicitly cites "the BT model with home-field advantage in sports analytics (Agresti, 1990)" as a special case of PlusDC), static object attributes, time index (ui(t)), mixture-component membership, margin/ordinal outcome values (Rao–Kupper/Davidson ties, ordinal MOV).
- **Sports-specific framing given:** "Sports outcomes are often influenced by both internal strength and the external environment; in such cases, it may be interesting, and sometimes necessary, to incorporate temporal dynamics and covariate information." NFL/NBA = dense regime (O(n²) comparisons, fixed pools, highly connected graphs); ATP/Chess = sparse regime.

## 9. Leakage & limitations (be adversarial — attack the methodology)

- **It's a survey: zero new empirical evidence.** Every "result" is a citation or a theorem. Nothing in this paper validates BT for sports prediction against a bookmaker baseline — the NFL row in Table 2 is decorative. There is no CLV, no Brier score, no ROI anywhere.
- **SST (strong stochastic transitivity) is assumed by the whole model class, and it's false in football.** The paper itself admits SST fails in Rock–Paper–Scissors settings. NFL is rife with style-matchup cycles (team A beats B's man coverage, B's pass rush beats C's weak line, C's ground game beats A's light front). BT forces these into a transitive ladder and eats the misspecification silently. The paper's remedies (matrix completion without SST, Borda counts) are mentioned but not evaluated for sports.
- **The independence assumption {Ye}|graph is violated by design in the NFL.** Schedules are not Erdős–Rényi draws: strength of schedule is engineered (division rotation, prior-year finish). The paper flags that graph/outcome coupling exists but its headline theorems (optimal pn ≳ (log n)/n consistency) all assume it away. NFL is n=32 dense — the entire asymptotic regime (n→∞) is irrelevant theater for a 32-team league; consistency at n→∞ tells you nothing about week-3 rating error.
- **Ford's strong-connectivity existence condition is a real failure mode in-season.** An undefeated team (or a team that hasn't played a connected schedule early season) sends ûi→∞. The paper's fixes are regularization (ridge / gamma-prior EM-MAP) — which is exactly what we should take from it, but note the vanilla MLE the paper centers is unusable for early-season NFL without it.
- **Newman's FPI speed claim comes with a landmine.** Han et al. (2026) show the acceleration "critically depends on asynchronous updates" and the synchronous version "may diverge when the comparison graph is near-bipartite." The Algebra dataset in their own benchmark IS bipartite. An implementer copying the equation without the async discipline gets divergence.
- **Mixture models are theoretically half-open.** BT mixtures are only known generically identifiable for H=2 on complete graphs; the EM algorithms have "mostly empirical" analysis and no convergence guarantees (paper's own words). Promising for home/road splits, but the theory isn't there to trust it blindly.
- **PlusDC identifiability demands "generic position" covariates** — fine for home-field indicators, shaky for static covariates (paper: static covariates "can be absorbed into the object utilities").
- **No ties treatment for American football is wrong-headed if extended to spreads:** Davidson/Rao–Kupper ties handle exact draws; NFL ties are ~0.3% of games — negligible. The real ordinal signal is margin of victory, which the paper only touches via ordinal models in passing.
- **LLM-alignment material (BT-RM, DPO) is irrelevant to GSE** — correctly ignored for the verdict.

## 11. GSE implementation spec (concrete, implementation-complete: what exactly to build, inputs, method, output shape)

Build **`bt-team-ratings`** — a standalone TypeScript module (new file; do not touch existing engine files until the gate in §13 passes):

**Inputs:**
- `games: Array<{home: TeamId, away: TeamId, homeScore: number, awayScore: number, date: string, season: number}>` — NFL/CFB game results, any season window. Outcome: home win → Y=+1 for home; ties → drop or split (Davidson tie extension optional later).
- `lambda: number` — exponential time-decay half-life in days (default 365; tune in §14).
- `homeAdv: boolean` — include PlusDC home-field covariate (default true).
- `priorA, priorB: number` — gamma-prior pseudo-counts for EM-MAP (default a=1+small, b tuned; start b equivalent to ~2 pseudo-games vs league-average per team).

**Method (exact):**
1. Build win counts wij with time-decay weights: each game contributes weight w = 0.5^(daysAgo/halfLife) to the winner's tally. (Time-varying strengths per Fahrmeir–Tutz/Cattelan are the literature alternative; decayed weights are the cheap equivalent.)
2. **Fit with Newman's FPI, asynchronous updates** (mandatory — synchronous may diverge per Han et al. 2026): iterate γi ← [Σj wij γj/(γi+γj)] / [Σj wji/(γi+γj)] in random coordinate order until max|Δγi| < 1e-10, renormalizing Σi γi = 1 each sweep. Fallback: EM-MAP update γi = (a−1+Σj wij)/(b+Σj nij/(γi+γj)) — which doubles as the regularized early-season estimator when some team is undefeated (Ford condition fails).
3. **PlusDC home advantage:** after fitting base utilities, fit scalar home effect h by 1-D MLE on σ(ui − uj + h·I(home=i)); i.e., win prob for home team = σ(ui − uj + h). (Full joint PlusDC fit is the extension; start with the separable version.)
4. Convert to fair moneyline probabilities: p_home = σ(ui − uj + h); fair odds = 1/p.

**Output shape:**
```ts
interface BTTeamRatings {
  asOf: string;                 // ISO date
  teams: Record<TeamId, { utility: number; strength: number; gamesWeighted: number }>;
  homeAdvantage: number;        // h in log-odds (PlusDC scalar)
  fittedWith: "newman-async" | "em-map";
  iterations: number;
  converged: boolean;
}
interface BTFairPrice { homeTeam: TeamId; awayTeam: TeamId; pHome: number; fairHomeML: number; fairAwayML: number; }
```
Function: `fitBTRatings(games, opts): BTTeamRatings`; `fairPrice(ratings, home, away): BTFairPrice`.

**Where it plugs in (pending §13 gate):** as an additional signal in the multi-market ensemble (UNVERIFIED interface) and as a fair-price input to the CLV module (UNVERIFIED interface) — integration code only after the gate passes.

## 12. Reproducible test (what the test asserts, pass criteria)

1. **FPI correctness:** on a synthetic round-robin (8 teams, true utilities −1.5..1.5, 2000 simulated games from BT), Newman async FPI recovers utilities within ±0.05 of the `scipy`-free reference MLE computed by brute-force projected gradient descent to 1e-12. **Pass:** max|û_newman − û_gd| < 1e-6 and max|û − u_true| < 0.15.

## 13. Acceptance/rejection gate (numeric)

**ADAPT if ALL of the following hold** (else REJECT the integration, keep the module as research-only):

1. **Calibration gate:** On NFL regular seasons 2022–2024 (out-of-sample, walk-forward: ratings fit only on games before week w, predicting week w), the BT+home-advantage model's moneyline Brier score is **≥ 2% lower (better)** than the current engine's moneyline Brier on the same weeks. (Baseline: engine v5.2.7 predictions — pull from the Neon `picks` table per the 2026-09-13 access note; if unavailable, baseline = closing-line-implied probabilities, gate = Brier ≥ 1% better than closing line.)
2. **CLV gate:** Fair prices from `fairPrice` vs. Pinnacle closing moneylines, 2024 season: mean |fair prob − closing prob| ≤ 0.03 on games where the engine also has a pick (i.e., it tracks the efficient price rather than living in fantasy).
3. **Stability gate:** Week-over-week utility changes for a fixed team: median |Δu| ≤ 0.08 across a season (no thrash); and no team hits the Ford-divergence path under EM-MAP in any week 1–4 window.

**REJECT integration if:** Brier is not ≥2% better than the engine baseline (BT is then redundant with existing ratings), or mean deviation from Pinnacle exceeds 0.05 (miscalibrated vs. the market), or async Newman fails to converge on any 4-week early-season window (implementation risk realized).

## 14. Improvement experiment (what would make this better / GSE-specific extension)

1. **Ordinal margin-of-victory BT (highest value):** Replace binary Yij with ordinal buckets (e.g., loss by 10+, loss by 1–9, win by 1–9, win by 10+) under the Rao–Kupper/Davidson ordinal generalization the paper cites (Tutz 1986; Agresti 1992). Win probability stays σ(ui−uj); margin distribution adds spread-relevant signal. This turns the rating into a **spread-model feeder** — directly serving the engine's SPREAD market (the paper's binary BT only serves moneyline). Test: does ordinal-BT spread prediction beat binary-BT + constant home edge on ATS Brier, 2022–2024 walk-forward?
2. **Two-component BT mixture for game script:** H=2 mixture (Zhang et al. 2022 says generically identifiable on complete graphs) with components ≈ "neutral-script" vs "blowout-script" — captures the SST violation where a pass-funnel defense beats pocket passers but loses to mobile QBs. EM-LSR (Nguyen & Zhang 2023) for fitting. Test: mixture log-likelihood vs single BT on held-out games; then Brier on moneyline.
3. **Time-varying utilities with learned decay:** Replace fixed half-life with a learned decay (grid-search half-life 60–730 days on walk-forward Brier) — the paper's ui(t) literature (Cattelan et al. 2013 dynamic BT for sports tournaments) is the principled version; learned decay is the cheap version. Particularly relevant for CFB (roster turnover) vs NFL.
4. **Uncertainty → calibration ladder:** Use the Gao et al. (2023) asymptotic-normality variance estimates (Fisher information = weighted graph Laplacian, per Han & Xu 2025) to put standard errors on each team's utility, feeding the calibration ladder's uncertainty quantification (UNVERIFIED interface) — lets the engine down-weight picks driven by high-SE ratings.
5. **RankCentrality as a cold-start:** For CFB week 1 (sparse cross-conference graph, Ford condition fails broadly), initialize with the RC spectral estimator (cheap, always exists) then refine with EM-MAP — the paper's own result that RC is consistent under optimal sparsity justifies this.
