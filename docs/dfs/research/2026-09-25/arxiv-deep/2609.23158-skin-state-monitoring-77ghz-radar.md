## 1. Research question

Can contactless 77 GHz FMCW radar detect exercise-induced physiological changes in the superficial skin layer (stratum corneum / upper epidermis) — specifically, can pre-activity vs post-exercise skin states be classified from single-chirp (102.4 µs) radar snapshots of a seated subject's chest, using Wavelet Scattering Transform features and lightweight ML classifiers, in a subject-independent (leave-one-subject-out) setting? The authors deliberately frame this as *classification of skin states* rather than exact hydration estimation, arguing (their words) that mmWave dehydration claims "require careful interpretation due to the shallow penetration depth of mmWave frequencies" and that lightweight, interpretable classification is "a physically and theoretically sound approach."

## 2. Dataset/schema

- **Subjects:** 15 university-level ultimate frisbee athletes, IRB KAUST Protocol 24IBEC032, written informed consent.
- **Protocol (controlled pre/post design):**
  - Pre-activity reference: 15 min rest in a hot outdoor environment, radar recording 1–2 min.
  - Post-exercise: 2 hours of intense sports activity with **water intake restriction**; radar recorded within 10 minutes after cessation, 1–2 min session.
- **Hardware:** INRAS 77 GHz FMCW MIMO radarbook, 2 GHz bandwidth, 2 Tx / 16 Rx antennas; configured on a **single Tx–Rx channel**, standoff distance 1 m from subject's chest. Chirp duration T = 102.4 µs, 256 fast-time samples per chirp, ~7.5 cm range resolution.
- **Schema:** 125 single-chirp samples selected per subject per condition → **1875 samples/class, 3750 total**. Each sample = one 1×256 fast-time complex vector from the dominant chest reflection. No multi-chirp averaging.
- **Feature schema after WST:** scattering tensor 25 (paths) × 8 (temporal windows) × 3750 (samples), reshaped to a **3750 × 200** feature matrix (scattering-path × temporal-window concatenation). Labels: binary pre-activity / post-exercise skin state.

## 3. Method/model

Pipeline: (1) FMCW range profiling via FFT across fast time; (2) chest-response selection — Hamming window (sidelobe reduction), range FFT, second-order-section (SOS) Butterworth band-pass filter retaining the dominant chest-reflection range interval, suppressing clutter/multipath; (3) **Wavelet Scattering Transform** feature extraction with analytic Morlet (Gabor) wavelets, invariance scale 128 samples (half the signal), quality factors tested Q = [2,1], [4,1], [8,1]; (4) classification with five lightweight models: Naive Bayes (NB), SVM, KNN, Kernel-based method, and a feedforward NN.

Design rationale: single-chirp snapshots of 102.4 µs are orders of magnitude shorter than cardiopulmonary cycles (respiration 0.1–0.5 Hz, cardiac 0.8–2.5 Hz), so slow-time vital-sign signatures are explicitly suppressed and the classifier sees instantaneous amplitude/phase/spectral variation — intended for edge deployment (ARM Cortex-M class) with local inference and no body-worn devices.

## 4. Equations & assumptions (copied faithfully)

Transmit chirp (complex form):
`s(t) = e^{j2π(fc + Bt/(2T))t}`, 0 < t < T, where T = chirp duration, fc = carrier frequency, B = bandwidth.

Received signal (L targets):
`r(t) = Σ_{l=1}^{L} α̃_l e^{j2π(fc + Bt/(2T))(t−τ_l)} + n̄(t)`  (1), with `τ_l = 2R_l / c`, `n̄(t) ~ N(0, σ²)`.

Demodulated (multiply by transmitted chirp, low-pass filter):
`x(t) = Σ_{l=1}^{L} α̃_l e^{j2πfcτ_l} e^{j2π(Bτ_l/(2T))t} + n(t)`  (2), where `n(t) = n̄*(t) e^{j2π(fc + Bt/(2T))t}`.

Substituting τ_l:
`x(t) = Σ_{l=1}^{L} α_l e^{j2π(2BR_l/(cT))t} + n(t)`, 0 ≤ t ≤ T  (3), with `α_l = α̃_l e^{j4πfcR_l/c}`. Beat frequencies `f_bl = 2BR_l/(cT)` estimated by FFT across fast time (range FFT).

Hamming windowing (pre-processing):
`xw(t) = x(t) · w(t)`  (4).

Normal-incidence reflection model for the skin–air interface:
`Γ ≈ (1 − √ε*) / (1 + √ε*)`, where ε* is the local complex permittivity.

**Assumptions (stated or implicit):** normal incidence; L point targets; additive white Gaussian noise; the chirp is quasi-static (target stationary over 102.4 µs); range resolution 7.5 cm suffices to isolate the chest; single Tx–Rx channel and dominant-chest-reflection selection are representative; low-pass WST energy is driven by genuine skin-state dielectric change rather than motion artifacts.

## 5. Features/target

- **Features:** WST coefficients (analytic Morlet wavelets, invariance scale 128, Q = [4,1] chosen as best tradeoff between compactness and discriminative resolution; the Q41 reshaping preserving joint scattering-path × temporal-window structure beats the alternative WST0 Q41 unfolding and beats the raw-signal baseline — Fig. 2). Interpretability via two-sample |t|-statistics across the 200 coefficients: **zeroth-order S0 ≈ 67.9%** of total mean absolute t-statistic, **second-order ≈ 24.4%**, **first-order ≈ 7.6%** — i.e., low-pass signal energy/amplitude stability dominates, consistent (per the authors) with quasi-static surface-permittivity shifts.
- **Target:** binary class label — pre-activity vs post-exercise superficial skin state.

## 6. Validation design

- **Primary:** Leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) cross-validation — train on 14 subjects, test on the 15th, repeated over all subjects; explicitly adopted "to evaluate generalization across inter-subject variability" and "to avoid subject-level data leakage."
- **Secondary reference only:** random 5-fold cross-validation repeated over 10 runs (the authors note random-CV results are higher, as expected).
- LOSO reported as mean ± SD across subjects with a 95% confidence interval.

## 9. Leakage & limitations (adversarial)

1. **n = 15 subjects is tiny, and the headline number is fragile.** 3750 samples = 250 highly correlated chirps per subject. LOSO SD is ±14.39 with CI [84.27, 98.83] — a 14.6-point-wide interval. Per-subject LOSO accuracies are not reported; with that SD, several held-out subjects are plausibly near chance. The 91.55% mean masks likely heavy-tailed subject-level failure.
2. **The core physical claim is unverified — no ground truth.** The authors admit future work must collect "concurrent ground-truth measurements of skin temperature, corneometry, sweat electrolyte concentration, and body-mass loss." As it stands, nothing links the radar amplitude shift to skin dielectric change vs. confounds.
3. **Amplitude is the confound channel.** The discriminative features are S0 low-pass energy / amplitude stability (67.9%). But post-exercise subjects, recorded within 10 minutes of 2 hours of intense activity, are panting, sweating, and shifting at a 1 m standoff — chest expansion, body sway, and orientation changes all modulate return amplitude. The "single chirp suppresses cardiopulmonary motion" argument suppresses *periodic* signatures, not bulk amplitude modulation from a heaving, moving athlete. The classifier may be detecting "exhausted moving person vs calm resting person," not skin permittivity.
4. **Environmental drift uncontrolled.** Pre-state: 15 min rest in a hot outdoor environment. Post-state: 2 h later, outdoors, after intense activity — ambient temperature/humidity drift over 2 hours is unreported and uncorrected; the hot-outdoor baseline itself is thermally loaded.
5. **Sample selection is a black box.** 125 chirps "selected" from 1–2 minute sessions (which contain thousands of chirps at ~10 ms cadence) via "the same selection procedure applied uniformly" — the procedure is never specified. Selection bias risk is real and unaddressed.
6. **The interpretability analysis violates its own assumptions.** The authors state the t-statistic ranking is "used only as an exploratory feature-ranking tool, since multiple chirps from the same subject are not fully independent" — then use it as the central interpretability evidence (Fig. 3). Non-independent samples inflate |t|; the 67.9/24.4/7.6 split is directionally suggestive at best.
7. **Random-CV vs LOSO gap shows subject-level memorization.** NN: 99.28% CV → 91.55% LOSO; NB: 89.84% → 76.35%. The random-CV numbers reflect within-subject leakage (train and test chirps from the same subject), which is exactly the leakage LOSO was introduced to fix — and LOSO still leaves a wide CI.
8. **Easiest possible task, narrow population.** Binary pre/post classification (not graded hydration/fatigue states), 15 university ultimate-frisbee athletes, single sport, demographics unspecified. No evidence this generalizes to graded states, other sports, or field conditions.
9. **No code, no data, no independent verification path.**

## 11. GSE implementation spec

**No implementation warranted — nothing to build.** There is no GSE data stream this method can consume and no GSE task it maps to (see §10). For the record, what a faithful implementation would require, and why it stops:

- **Required input:** a high-rate sequential sensor stream with multi-scale transient structure plus a binary state label — e.g., continuous athlete biometric telemetry. GSE does not have this and has no acquisition path for it.
- **If such a stream ever existed**, the build would be: (1) windowed snapshots of the stream; (2) WST featurization (analytic Morlet wavelets, invariance scale ~½ window, Q sweep [2,1]/[4,1]/[8,1]); (3) lightweight classifier (feedforward NN per the paper's winner); (4) LOSO-style group-out validation over subjects/units; (5) |t|-statistic order-contribution analysis. Output shape: binary state probability + per-order contribution vector.
- **Recommendation:** do not build. The honest engineering conclusion is that WST-on-odds-streams would be speculative R&D with no demonstrated edge, and Garrett's standard is real implementable value, not method tourism.

## 14. Improvement experiment (GSE-specific extension)

The only honest extension is conditional on a data stream GSE does not have: **if** GSE ever acquired athlete workload/fatigue telemetry (e.g., for player-prop markets — practice participation, travel, workload proxies), the paper's template worth re-implementing would not be the radar but the *validation and interpretability discipline*: group-out (leave-one-player-out) validation instead of random CV, plus order-contribution analysis to check whether the model is learning physiology or a confound (the paper's S0 finding is the cautionary tale — always test whether your features are just amplitude). A sharper version of their experiment would add the missing ground truth (corneometry, skin temperature, sweat electrolytes, body-mass loss) and a graded-state design (rest / mild / severe exertion) instead of the trivial binary pre/post split — plus per-subject LOSO accuracy reporting, environmental controls, and a specified chirp-selection procedure. None of this changes the verdict: without the hardware and the feed, there is no GSE experiment to run.
