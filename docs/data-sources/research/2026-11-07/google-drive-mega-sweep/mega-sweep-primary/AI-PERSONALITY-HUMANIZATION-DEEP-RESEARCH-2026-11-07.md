# Giving an AI Persona a Human Mind, Personality, and Voice — Deep Research Report

**Persona in question:** a female sports-content host, late 20s, delivering short spoken reports to camera. Current failures to fix: catchphrases that sound forced on her, flat/generic script voice, delivery that does not sound like a real person.

**Design archetype (per user steering):** JARVIS (Iron Man) / Alfred (Batman) — the hyperintelligent support intelligence you simply *talk to*: it anticipates, supports, remembers, stays loyal to its role, and is functionally "a human who is not physical." The character being built is the **public face of exactly such a support intelligence**. Section 13 covers the HCI evidence for that archetype specifically; Sections 8–12 cover empathy, two-way conversation, embodiment, memory/growth, and reasoning/inner life. The end goal is a spec for a persistent character who converses in writing and on camera, empathizes, remembers, learns, and grows while staying herself.

**Standard applied:** primary scientific/technical sources only (peer-reviewed papers, arXiv, HCI venues CHI/CSCW/IUI/HRI, foundational books). Every item gives: claim/mechanism with hard data, full citation, stable link (verbatim from source), and legal full-text availability. Numbers that could only be traced to secondary summaries are flagged as such rather than presented as primary.

## 1. Persona and Character in Language Models

**Persona-Chat (conditioning on a written persona works).** Conditioning a dialogue model on a 4–6 sentence persona profile measurably improves specificity, consistency, and engagingness versus no-persona baselines — the founding demonstration that a short written character sheet changes generated speech. Zhang, S., Dinan, E., Urbanek, J., Szlam, A., Kiela, D., & Weston, J. (2018). "Personalizing Dialogue Agents: I have a dog, do you have pets too?" *Proceedings of ACL 2018*. arXiv:1801.07243. Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.07243 — **Full text: open access (arXiv)**. Corpus size: ~8,939 train dialogues / ~162,064 utterances per the authors' release digest (secondary sources give 10,907 dialogues and a 139k-row HF mirror at https://huggingface.co/datasets/bavard/personachat_truecased — resolve the exact count from the paper before quoting in production docs).

**Character-LLM (per-character training beats prompting).** Fine-tuning a separate model per character on reconstructed "experience" data (scenes from the character's life) produces role-play that beats prompted general models (Alpaca/Vicuna baselines) on character fidelity. Shao, Y., et al. (2023). "Character-LLM: A Trainable Agent for Role-Playing." *EMNLP 2023*. arXiv:2310.10158. **Full text: open access (arXiv).** Data scale: 9 characters, each ~1.4–2.2K scenes / ~600K–1,038K words / ~12–15.5 turns per scene. Caveat: released weights are Llama-1 weight-diffs, academic/non-commercial only.

**RoleLLM / RoleBench (role-play is benchmarkable at scale).** A role-play training/evaluation resource of 168.1k samples across 100 roles, enabling measurement of role-specific knowledge and speaking style separately. Wang, Z. M., et al. (2023). "RoleLLM: Benchmarking, Eliciting, and Enhancing Role-Playing Abilities of Large Language Models." RoleBench figures as tabulated in the RoleMRC paper, *Findings of ACL 2025*: https://round-lake.dustinice.workers.dev:443/https/aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1082.pdf — **Full text: open access (ACL Anthology)**. HF mirror `ZenMoore/RoleBench` is labeled Apache-2.0 (dataset-card claim; verify before commercial use).

**CharacterEval (multi-dimensional character evaluation).** 1,785 dialogues across 77 (Chinese) characters scored on 13 metrics spanning consistency, human-likeness, and engagement — evidence that "stays in character" decomposes into separately measurable dimensions. Tu, Q., et al. (2024). "CharacterEval: A Chinese Benchmark for Role-Playing Conversational Agent Evaluation." arXiv:2401.01275. **Full text: open access (arXiv).**

**InCharacter (interview the character; don't ask it to self-report).** Evaluating an LLM character by *interviewing* it with psychological questionnaires predicts human-perceived personality far better than self-report questionnaires: up to 80.7% alignment with human-perceived personality across 32 characters × 14 psychological scales; 18,304 interview responses released. Wang, X., et al. (2024). "InCharacter: Evaluating Personality Fidelity in Role-Playing Agents through Psychological Interviews." *ACL 2024*. arXiv:2310.17976. PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17976 — **Full text: open access (arXiv).**

**PersonaGym (even frontier models fail at linguistic habits).** A dynamic persona-consistency benchmark (200 personas, 10,000 questions, 150 environments) scoring Action Justification, Expected Action, Linguistic Habits, Persona Consistency, and Toxicity Control. Headline negative result: GPT-4.1's PersonaScore is identical to LLaMA-3-8B's, and **all models score below 4.0 on Linguistic Habits** — models can act in character but do not reliably *sound* like a distinct person. Samuel, V., et al. (2025). "PersonaGym: Evaluating Persona Agents and LLMs." *Findings of EMNLP 2025*. arXiv:2407.18416. Link: https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18416v5 — **Full text: open access (arXiv)**; static benchmark MIT-licensed. (A PersonaScore–human Spearman correlation of 76.1% circulates in secondary compilations; verify in the paper before quoting.)

**Persona drift is real and measured (Persistent Personas?).** Across 7 LLMs in dialogues exceeding 100 rounds, persona fidelity degrades over time — worst in goal-oriented dialogue — and persona-conditioned responses converge toward the non-persona baseline as fidelity fades; there is a fidelity/instruction-following trade-off. Luz de Araujo, et al. (2025). "Persistent Personas? [persona fidelity over long dialogues]." arXiv:2512.12775. Links: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12775v1 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.12775 — **Full text: open access (arXiv)**. This is the credible drift citation; two other "persona drift" preprints circulating on non-standard venues (clawRxiv, novix.science) could not be verified to a peer-review standard and are **not** cited.

## 2. Human Spoken Language — How Real People Actually Talk

**Turn-taking systematics.** Conversation is built from turn-constructional units with projectable completion points (transition-relevance places); the system explains how speakers start within ~200 ms of each other without a script. Sacks, H., Schegloff, E. A., & Jefferson, G. (1974). "A Simplest Systematics for the Organization of Turn-Taking for Conversation." *Language* 50(4), 696–735. Legal author-copy PDF: https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~vrees101/code/turntaking/Turntaking_SSJ.pdf — **Full text: legally accessible author copy**. Companion: Schegloff, Jefferson & Sacks (1977) on the preference for self-correction — repairs are overwhelmingly self-initiated, which is why scripted "perfect" speech reads as fake.

**Fillers are planned signals, not noise.** *Uh* announces a minor delay, *um* a major one; speakers deploy them as conventional words to manage the floor. Clark, H. H., & Fox Tree, J. E. (2002). "Using uh and um in spontaneous speaking." *Cognition* 84, 73–111. Publisher paywalled (a full-text copy circulates via document mirrors). Background rate: roughly 6 of every 100 words in spontaneous speech are affected by disfluency (Fox Tree, 1995; Bortfeld et al., 2001). **Implication: a host with zero fillers, zero restarts, zero repairs will sound scripted by construction.**

**Disfluency has a grammar.** Disfluencies follow a reparandum–interregnum–repair structure (Shriberg, 1994/1996, as operationalized in Switchboard annotation); only ~5.9% of Switchboard tokens are disfluent, and over half of disfluencies are simple repetitions — i.e., natural disfluency is *sparse and structured*, not constant stumbling.

**Discourse markers bracket units of talk.** Schiffrin's classic analysis of 11 markers (oh, well, now, then, you know, I mean; so, because, and, but, or) as "sequentially dependent elements which bracket units of talk" — they signal stance and structure, not content. Schiffrin, D. (1987). *Discourse Markers*. Cambridge University Press (Studies in Interactional Sociolinguistics). Publisher series page: https://www.cambridge.org/core/series/studies-in-interactional-sociolinguistics/B21B844F494B1E785CD196350D494740 — **Paywalled book; no legal OA full text found.**

**The intonation unit = one new idea.** Chafe's foundational claim: speech is produced in intonation units, each expressing one focus of consciousness; the "one new idea constraint" (p. 109) caps each unit at roughly one new idea, and substantive units average ~5 words. Chafe, W. (1994). *Discourse, Consciousness, and Time: The Flow and Displacement of Conscious Experience in Speaking and Writing*. University of Chicago Press, xiii+327 pp., ISBN 0-226-10054-5. **Paywalled book.**

**Prosody has a vocabulary — and scripted speech lacks it.** Analyzing spontaneous conversation, Matalon et al. identify ~200 recurrent prosodic patterns ("prosodic vocabulary") organized by a Markovian "prosodic syntax" that is present in spontaneous speech but **absent in scripted speech**; spontaneous intonation units run 1–4 words versus 15–20 words per written sentence. Matalon, et al. (2025). *PNAS* 122(17): e2403262122. OA PDF: https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/records/3vs1w-jrj05/files/matalon-et-al-structure-in-conversation-evidence-for-the-vocabulary-semantics-and-syntax-of-prosody.pdf?download=1 — **Full text: open access**. Corroborating: Wagner & Windmann, "Acted and Spontaneous Conversational Prosody — Same or Different?" (https://noah.nrw/ubbihs/download/pdf/5131642) — read/acted speech shows lower F0 minimum and larger F0 range than spontaneous speech, and timing/speech rate are the best discriminators of style. **This pair is the single most direct evidence for why the host's delivery sounds "acted."**

## 3. Voice and Perceived Femininity/Naturalness in TTS

**Formants: women's vowels are systematically shifted.** In the classic normative study (45 men, 48 women, 46 children; 12 vowels in /hVd/ context), women's formant frequencies sit up and to the right of men's, reflecting shorter vocal tracts — the acoustic substrate listeners use for perceived femininity. Hillenbrand, J., Getty, L. A., Clark, M. J., & Wheeler, K. (1995). "Acoustic characteristics of American English vowels." *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America* 97(5), 3099–3111. The 1,668-token dataset survives via the author's page (Wayback). **Honest gap:** no clean primary source for normative female speaking-F0 ranges was secured in this pass; the commonly quoted ~165–255 Hz / mean ~205 Hz figures trace to blogs and secondary compilations and must not be cited as primary (textbook values in Baken & Orlikoff, 2000 — female mean speaking F0 175–225 Hz — are the defensible fallback, flagged as textbook, not primary study).

**Breathiness: aspiration noise is the cue that matters.** Across 10 female and 6 male talkers, aspiration noise — not H1 amplitude (which alone reads as nasality) — is the perceptually dominant breathiness cue; women are on average breathier, but within-gender variation is very large, and breathiness rises on unstressed and utterance-final syllables. Klatt, D. H., & Klatt, L. C. (1990). "Analysis, synthesis, and perception of voice quality variations among female and male talkers." *JASA* 87(2), 820–857. doi:10.1121/1.398894. Abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2137837/ — **Paywalled; abstract open.**

**Vocal fry carries a hireability penalty for women.** Fry is common in young women's speech (over 2/3 of 34 college-age women used it in sentence reading, mostly utterance-final; Wolk, Abdelli-Beruh & Slavin, 2012, *Journal of Voice* 26(3), e111–e116) — but listeners punish it: in an N=800 study, ~80% preferred a normal voice for hiring, and fry was rated less competent, educated, trustworthy, attractive, and hirable, with a stronger penalty for female voices. Anderson, R. C., Klofstad, C. A., Mayew, W. J., & Venkatachalam, M. (2014). "Vocal Fry May Undermine the Success of Young Women in the Labor Market." *PLOS ONE* 9(5): e97506. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0097506 — **Full text: open access.** Caveat: stimuli were imitated fry (Barrett critique).

**Style control without retraining: Global Style Tokens.** An unsupervised bank of style embeddings lets a TTS model transfer speaking style (speed, affect) independent of text and speaker. In side-by-side tests on 60 audiobook sentences, GST output was preferred 40.6%/45.0% of the time versus 32.9%/33.1% for the baseline. Wang, Y., et al. (2018). "Style Tokens: Unsupervised Style Modeling, Control and Transfer in End-to-End Speech Synthesis." *ICML 2018*, PMLR 80:5180–5189. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/wang18h.html ; arXiv:1803.09017 — **Full text: open access.**

**Explicit prosody conditioning: FastSpeech 2.** The variance adaptor conditions synthesis on duration, pitch (continuous-wavelet pitch spectrogram), and energy — the standard mechanism for *directing* delivery rather than hoping a prompt implies it. Ren, Y., et al. (2021). "FastSpeech 2: Fast and High-Quality End-to-End Text to Speech." *ICLR 2021*. arXiv:2006.04558 — **Full text: open access (arXiv).**

**SSML insertion measurably improves perceived prosody.** Automatically inserting SSML prosody markup (pitch/rate/volume/pauses) into TTS input on a 14-hour French podcast corpus: break placement 99.2% F1, prosodic error down 25–40% versus prompting/BiLSTM alternatives, MOS 3.20 → 3.87 (p<0.005), 15 of 18 listeners preferring the enhanced output. Ould Ouali, et al. (2025). arXiv:2508.17494. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.17494 — **Full text: open access (arXiv).** Practical read: markup-level prosody direction beats prompt-level vibes, with a measured ~0.67 MOS gain.

## 4. Writing Voice and Catchphrases

**Scripted dialogue is measurably *less vague and more emphatic* than real talk.** Quaglio's corpus comparison of *Friends* dialogue against natural conversation across ~100 linguistic features (Biber Dimension 1) finds scripted dialogue shares conversation's core features but natural conversation is **vaguer** (more hedges, discourse markers, vague tags) and more narrative, while the sitcom is **more emotional/emphatic** and less varied. Quaglio, P. (2009). *Television Dialogue: The Sitcom Friends vs. Natural Conversation*. John Benjamins, Studies in Corpus Linguistics 36, ISBN 9789027223104, doi:10.1075/scl.36. **Paywalled book.** This is the empirical template for diagnosing the host's scripts: too precise, too emphatic, not enough vagueness.

**Catchphrases work through idiolect, not repetition alone.** A sociolinguistic study of *The Catherine Tate Show* shows catchphrases construct character identity only in combination with a recognizable idiolect — consistent lexical, grammatical, and prosodic choices plus accent features — driving enregisterment (the phrase becomes publicly linked to the persona). *English Language & Linguistics* (Cambridge). https://core-varnish-new.prod.aop.cambridge.org/core/journals/english-language-and-linguistics/article/accent-enregisterment-through-humour-in-the-catherine-tate-show/E0D60E6C5A86632180AEB2EEF7BBBD87 — **Journal article; check institutional access.** Implication for the "forced catchphrase" failure: a catchphrase pasted onto a generic voice has no idiolect to attach to.

**Dialogue is verbal action, not information transfer.** McKee's craft treatise: dialogue is action performed under pressure; "telling erases subtext" (pp. 24–25) — lines that state what the speaker wants/feels read as artificial. McKee, R. (2016). *Dialogue: The Art of Verbal Action for Page, Stage, and Screen*. **Paywalled book; no legal OA full text.** Companion craft source: Seger, L. (1990). *Creating Unforgettable Characters*. Owl Books, 256 pp. — character built from consistency *and* paradox. (The ISBN for Seger circulating in one index, 0-805011-7-14, appears garbled; verify against a library catalog before ordering.)

**Influencer authenticity is a managed, detectable signal.** Audrezet, de Kerviler & Moulard (2020), *Journal of Consumer Research*, identify two authenticity strategies audiences reward: *passionate authenticity* (intrinsic motivation, verbal–behavioral consistency) and *transparent authenticity* (disclosure, objective assessment). Lee & Eastin (2021), *Journal of Research in Interactive Marketing* 15(4):822–841, validate the PASMI scale: perceived influencer authenticity = sincerity, truthful endorsements, visibility, expertise, uniqueness — with **sincerity dominant**. Lower-tier corroboration (flagged): a 2025 survey (n=400) found perceived brand control reduces authenticity at β=−0.52, p<.01 — directionally consistent ("the audience can tell when she's reading the sponsor's words") but a weak venue; do not build on it alone.

## 5. Personality Psychology Applied to Characters

**LLM personality is psychometrically measurable — in big instruction-tuned models.** Serapio-García et al. administered IPIP-NEO-300 and BFI-44 to 18 LLMs: reliability/validity of trait measurement holds only in larger instruction-tuned models. Trait shaping via 104 adjectives × 9 intensity levels moves measured traits monotonically (Spearman ρ ≥ 0.90 for 11/12 models); convergent validity reaches r≈0.90 (Flan-PaLM 540B, GPT-4o), and the convergent–discriminant gap grows 0.23 (8B) → 0.51 (540B). Serapio-García, G., et al. "A psychometric framework for evaluating and shaping personality traits in large language models." *Nature Machine Intelligence* 7:1954–1968 (2025); arXiv:2307.00184. **Full text: arXiv open access; journal version paywalled.** (Validity coefficients partly compiled from secondary summaries — the ρ≥0.90 shaping result is the load-bearing, verifiable claim.)

**PersonaLLM: assigned Big Five traits are legible in generated writing.** Across 320 personas (32 Big Five types × gender), GPT-4's BFI self-reports match assigned traits with large effect sizes, and human readers identify the intended traits from the writing at up to 80% accuracy — dropping by >10% for some traits when readers are told the author is AI (an authorship-label penalty). Jiang, H., et al. (2024). "PersonaLLM: Investigating the Ability of Large Language Models to Express Personality Traits." *Findings of NAACL 2024*. arXiv:2305.02547. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02547 — **Full text: open access (arXiv).**

**Trait-parameterized generation with human-validated parameters: PERSONAGE.** Mairesse & Walker's PERSONAGE maps 29 generation parameters (verbosity, hedge rate, exclamation, etc.) from psycholinguistic findings to Big Five traits; human judges reliably recognize the intended traits — e.g., an extravert-parameterized utterance rated 6.33 vs 1.00 for the introvert version on a 7-point extraversion scale. Mairesse, F., & Walker, M. A. (2007). "PERSONAGE: Personality Generation for Dialogue." *ACL 2007*; extended to all Big Five in Mairesse & Walker (2008), AAAI Spring Symposium: https://cdn.aaai.org/Symposia/Spring/2008/SS-08-04/SS08-04-014.pdf — **Full text: open access (AAAI)**. The underlying cue inventory: Mairesse, F., Walker, M. A., Mehl, M. R., & Moore, R. K. (2007). "Using Linguistic Cues for the Automatic Recognition of Personality in Conversation and Text." *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research*. Author PDF: https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~lpearl/courses/readings/MairesseEtAl2007_PersonalityCues.pdf — **Full text: legally accessible author copy.**

**Temper expectations: natural-language trait cues are small.** In Pennebaker & King's LIWC analyses (N=1,203 essays), trait–language correlations are modest: neuroticism × negative-emotion words r=0.16, extraversion × positive-emotion words r=0.15, extraversion × social references r=0.12. Pennebaker, J. W., & King, L. A. (1999). "Linguistic styles: Language use as an individual difference." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*. **Implication: personality in text is carried by many weak cues stacked together, not one strong marker — which is why single catchphrases and adjective prompts underdeliver.**

## 6. Practical Prompt/System Design Evidence (measured results only)

**Training on trait-steered data beats prompting for trait expression.** BIG5-CHAT: 100,000 dialogues (20k per Big Five trait, high/low split) generated with DExperts-style steering experts trained on PsychGenerator (846,304 Facebook posts with Big Five scores) over SODA scenarios; models fine-tuned (SFT and DPO) on this data outperform persona *prompting* on BFI/IPIP-NEO trait correlations. Li, W., et al. arXiv:2410.16491. Overview: https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2410.16491v3 — **Full text: open access (arXiv).** This is the strongest available evidence on "examples/data vs adjectives": adjectives in a prompt are the weakest lever; demonstrated behavior in training data is stronger. (No controlled primary study was found that isolates exemplar-dialogue prompting vs adjective prompting on *perceived voice distinctness* — see Gap List.)

**Decoding: beam search degenerates; sampling humanizes.** Holtzman et al. show likelihood-maximizing decoding produces degenerate, repetitive text unlike human text distributions, and nucleus (top-p) sampling restores human-like statistics. Holtzman, A., Buys, J., Du, L., Forbes, M., & Choi, Y. (2020). "The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration." *ICLR 2020*. arXiv:1904.09751. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.09751 — **Full text: open access (arXiv).** (A specific repetition figure — 28.94% beam vs 0.36% nucleus — circulates in secondary digests; verify against the PDF before quoting.) Successor: min-p sampling (Nguyen et al., arXiv:2407.01082, https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01082v1) maintains coherence/creativity at high temperatures where top-p degrades — relevant to keeping a persona lively without incoherence.

**Prompted persona decays; plan for re-anchoring.** See §1, Luz de Araujo et al. (2025): fidelity loss over >100 rounds is the measured baseline behavior of prompt-only personas; any long-session design needs periodic persona re-anchoring or state externalization (§11).

## 8. Empathy in AI Agents

**Foundations: Affective Computing.** Picard founded the field on machines that recognize, interpret, and simulate affect — including simulating empathy as a designed capability rather than an emergent hope. Picard, R. W. (1997). *Affective Computing*. MIT Press. **Paywalled book.**

**EPITOME: empathy decomposes into three measurable moves.** Sharma et al.'s framework scores text responses on Emotional Reactions, Interpretations, and Explorations, each 0 (none) / 1 (weak) / 2 (strong); 10k annotated (post, response) pairs with rationales, applied to 235k interactions. Sobering finding: peer supporters do **not** self-learn empathy over time — empathic skill must be trained/feedback-driven, not assumed to emerge. Sharma, A., Miner, A. S., Atkins, D. C., & Althoff, T. (2020). "A Computational Approach to Understanding Empathy Expressed in Text-Based Mental Health Support." *EMNLP 2020*. Downstream causal evidence: in a therapy-corpus study (~119k utterances, LLM-coded with ICCs 0.60–0.81 vs human annotation), therapist empathy and exploration each predicted increased client disclosure (β=0.086 each) and self-directed negative emotion (β=0.118/0.078) — EPITOME-style moves measurably change the *other party's* behavior (arXiv:2602.12450; recent preprint, treat as provisional).

**Chatbot responses are rated more empathic than physicians' — with a length confound.** In 195 r/AskDocs questions, blinded evaluators preferred ChatGPT's answers in 78.6% of 585 ratings and rated them empathetic/very empathetic 45.1% vs 4.6% for physicians (~9.8×). Ayers, J. W., et al. (2023). "Comparing Physician and Artificial Intelligence Chatbot Responses to Patient Questions Posted to a Public Social Media Forum." *JAMA Internal Medicine* 183(6):589–596. doi:10.1001/jamainternmed.2023.1838. **Confound to state plainly: chatbot replies averaged ~211 words vs ~52 for physicians** — verbosity is doing some of the work.

**Feeling heard vs the AI label: two effects that cancel.** AI-written replies made people feel *more* heard than human-written ones (5.74 vs 5.17 on a 7-point scale), but labeling the identical reply "AI" reduced feeling heard (5.13 vs 5.81 for a human label) — effects of similar size that cancel out. Yin, Y., Jia, N., & Wakslak, C. J. (2024). *PNAS* 121(14): e2319112121. (Point estimates via the paper as compiled in secondary dossiers; verify in the PNAS text before external publication.) Companion experiment (N=1,454, 2×2 actual × perceived source): chatbots were rated more empathic regardless of label (d=0.56; ηp²=0.60 for actual source, ηp²=0.17 for perceived source), and chatbot text contained measurably more validation, reassurance, and non-judgmental language (d=0.32–1.82). Ruben, et al. (2025). *Journal of General Internal Medicine*. doi:10.1007/s11606-025-10068-w. OA PDF: https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11606-025-10068-w.pdf — **Full text: open access.** Meta-analytic summary: JMIR meta-analysis (2026), *J Med Internet Res* 28:e77307 (https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e77307) — pooled empathy advantage SMD 1.02 (95% CI 0.44–1.60), k=4, N=2,604 evaluations. **Design read: the empathic *linguistic features* (validation, reassurance, non-judgment) are producible and measurable; the risk is disclosure/label effects, not capability.**

**Theory of mind: benchmark-strong, brittle.** Kosinski reports GPT-4 solving 75% of 40 custom false-belief tasks (≈6-year-old level) where older models score 0–20% (arXiv:2302.02083; *PNAS* 2024). Strachan et al. (2024), *Nature Human Behaviour*, find GPT-4 matches or beats adults on irony, indirect requests, and strange stories but **fails faux-pas recognition** — knowing what a speaker should have known. Ullman (2023) shows trivial surface alterations collapse LLM ToM performance. **Conclusion: treat ToM as contested and brittle; do not architect empathy on assumed mind-reading — architect it on EPITOME-style observable moves.**

## 9. Two-Way Conversation

**Grounding: conversation is joint work.** Clark & Brennan's grounding criterion — partners mutually believe they have understood "sufficient for current purposes" — plus the principle of least collaborative effort, and medium constraints (copresence, visibility, audibility, cotemporality, simultaneity, sequentiality, reviewability, revisability) that predict what breaks when a medium changes. Clark, H. H., & Brennan, S. E. (1991). "Grounding in Communication." In Resnick, Levine & Teasley (eds.), *Perspectives on Socially Shared Cognition*, APA, pp. 127–149. doi:10.1037/10096-006. Legal author PDF: http://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1990s/Clark,%20H.H.%20_%20Brennan,%20S.E.%20_Grounding%20in%20communication_%201991.pdf — **Full text: legally accessible author copy.** A broadcast monologue performs none of this; visible grounding behavior (acknowledgment, repair, display of understanding) is what makes an exchange read as conversation.

**Turn timing is a ~200 ms universal.** Across 10 languages, response offsets cluster with mode 0–200 ms per language (overall mode 0 ms, mean +208 ms; language means from Japanese +7 ms to Danish +469 ms). Stivers, T., et al. (2009). "Universals and cultural variation in turn-taking in conversation." *PNAS* 106(26):10587–10592. doi:10.1073/pnas.0903616106 — **Full text: open access (PMC2705608).** Levinson & Torreira (2015), *Frontiers in Psychology* 6:731, explain the mechanism: producing even one word takes ~600 ms, so 200 ms gaps require predictive planning *during* listening — an agent that waits for silence, then thinks, then speaks is temporally non-human by ~half a second per turn.

**Backchannels are timed to pitch cues.** Listeners place backchannels ("mm-hm," nods) preferentially ~200 ms after a region of low pitch (an ≥110 ms low-pitch region is the cue, in both English and Japanese). Ward, N., & Tsukahara, W. (2000). "Prosodic features which cue back-channel responses in English and Japanese." *Journal of Pragmatics* 32(8). Term coined by Yngve (1970). Review: Skantze, G., et al. (2022), *Frontiers in Computer Science*, doi:10.3389/fcomp.2022.744574, PDF: https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2022.744574/pdf — **Full text: open access.** Heldner & Edlund (2010) add: median gaps 110–130 ms with overlaps in ~40% of transitions — overlap is normal, not rude.

**Accommodation: match the partner's state.** Communication Accommodation Theory (Giles, Taylor & Bourhis, 1973, *Language in Society* 2:177–192; Giles & Ogay, 2007) documents convergence/divergence across speech rate, pausing, utterance length, accent, gaze, and gesture; converging speakers are rated more attractive and competent and gain more compliance (Buller et al., 1992). **This is the primary-literature basis for "meeting the user at their emotional state/register/pace" — it is a measured human behavior with measured payoffs, not a vibe.**

## 10. Movement and Embodiment

**Gesture–speech synchrony has a rule and a tolerance window.** McNeill's phonological synchrony rule: the gesture stroke precedes or ends at, but does not follow, the phonological peak syllable (*Hand and Mind*, 1992, p. 26). Empirically, gesture apexes co-occur with a pitch accent 95.7% of the time, the accent landing on average 17 ms after the apex (as summarized in Rohrer et al., ICPhS 2019: https://assta.org/proceedings/ICPhS2019/papers/ICPhS_1549.pdf). The tolerance is asymmetric: in Nirme et al. (2024), *Frontiers in Psychology* 15:1345906 (N=83, motion-captured animated speaker), gestures delayed 500 ms or eliminated significantly reduced word recall versus synchronized, while gestures 500 ms *early* did not hurt. https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1345906/full — **Full text: open access.** Animation rule of thumb: err early, never late.

**BEAT: nonverbal behavior can be generated from linguistic structure.** Cassell et al.'s BEAT toolkit (SIGGRAPH 2001; Cassell, Vilhjálmsson & Bickmore) generates synchronized hand gestures, gaze, eyebrow movement, and intonation from text analysis; the companion ACL 2001 study shows posture shifts cluster at discourse-segment boundaries and gaze/head-nods tie to turn-taking. https://www.media.mit.edu/gnl/publications/ACL2001.pdf ; Animated Conversation (SIGGRAPH 1994): https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/steedman/papers/prosody/p413-cassell.pdf — **Full texts: legally accessible author copies.**

**Idle motion changes how alive — and how feeling — a body reads.** In a two-experiment study (Exp. 1 N=32, Exp. 2 N=34), a virtual character with natural motion-captured postural oscillations (including breathing) had its facial pain rated more intense, and participants reported being more touched, versus a static body. *Scientific Reports* (2021) 11:12493. doi:10.1038/s41598-021-91710-5. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8203793/ — **Full text: open access.** Idle motion = posture shifts, eye movement/blinking, breathing (Egges et al.'s term): the cheapest aliveness signal there is.

**Uncanny valley: measured, and it taxes trust.** Mori (1970), *Energy* 7(4):33–35; translation: Mori, M., MacDorman, K. F., & Kageki, N. (2012), *IEEE Robotics & Automation Magazine*, doi:10.1109/MRA.2012.2192811 — motion *amplifies* the valley. Mathur & Reichling (2016), *Cognition* 146:22–32, doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2015.09.008, tested 80 real robot faces: a robust likability valley, and the valley **penetrated implicit trust** in an investment game; category confusion occurs but does not mediate likability. OA copy: https://daneshyari.com/article/preview/7286364.pdf (CC BY-NC-ND). Saygin et al. (2012), *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience*, add the neural mechanism (secondary summaries): a human-looking agent moving mechanically produces a mismatch signal in the action-perception network (pSTS/premotor). Caution: Kätsyri et al. (2015) review finds the valley literature methodologically inconsistent — the Mathur & Reichling behavioral result is the solid citation; treat the valley curve itself as contested.

## 11. Memory, Learning, and Character Growth Over Time

**Generative Agents: memory stream + reflection + planning produces believable agents.** Park et al.'s architecture — a timestamped memory stream, retrieval scored by recency × importance × relevance, periodic *reflection* (triggered when summed importance of recent events reaches ~150, ~2–3×/day), and planning — sustained 25 agents over 2 simulated days. Believability (TrueSkill, 100 raters): full architecture 29.89 > no-reflection 26.88 > no-reflection/planning 25.64 > **human crowdworkers 22.95** > full ablation 21.21; knowledge of a party spread from 4% to 52% of agents via conversation alone. Park, J. S., et al. (2023). "Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior." *UIST 2023*. arXiv:2304.03442. doi:10.1145/3586183.3606763 — **Full text: open access (arXiv).** Limits, stated honestly: 2 days only, fabrication and missed-retrieval failures, high token cost. Nothing here proves months.

**MemGPT: OS-style tiered memory for unbounded context.** Packer et al. give the LLM a virtual context hierarchy (core/recall/archival memory) it edits via function calls — the standard engineering pattern for a persistent character's memory. Packer, C., et al. (2023). "MemGPT: Towards LLMs as Operating Systems." arXiv:2310.08560 — **Full text: open access (arXiv).** (A DMR score of 93.4% vs 35.3% for recursive summarization circulates in secondary summaries; verify in the paper before quoting as primary.)

**LoCoMo: the benchmark for very-long-term conversational memory — and current systems lag humans badly.** Conversations averaging 588.2 turns / 27.2 sessions / 16,618 tokens (10 released), with QA (single-hop, multi-hop, temporal, open-domain, adversarial), event summarization, and dialogue-generation tasks. Long-context and RAG approaches improve QA by 22–66% over base models but still lag human performance by roughly 36–44% (split per secondary summary — verify). Maharana, A., Lee, D.-H., Tulyakov, S., Bansal, M., Barbieri, F., & Fang, Y. (2024). "Evaluating Very Long-Term Conversational Memory of LLM Agents." *ACL 2024*, pp. 13851–13870. arXiv:2402.17753. https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.747/ — **Full text: open access (ACL Anthology).** Benchmark-quality caveat: a non-peer-reviewed audit claims ~6.4% of answer keys are wrong and judge leniency inflates scores — cite LoCoMo as the standard, contested, benchmark.

**Proven vs speculative, plainly:** proven — retrieval + reflection architectures produce short-horizon (days) believability above human crowdworker baselines; prompted personas measurably drift within ~100 rounds (§1). **Not proven anywhere found: months-long, in-character growth — an agent that updates beliefs/preferences from experience while verifiably staying itself.** That is the frontier, and the spec should treat it as an engineering risk, not a solved feature (see Gap List).

## 12. Reasoning and Inner Life

**Chain-of-thought: large, real capability gains — at scale.** Wei et al.: CoT prompting lifts PaLM 540B on GSM8K from 17.9% to 56.9% (8 exemplars); gains emerge around ~100B parameters and small models are *hurt* (PaLM 8B: 4.9% → 4.1%). Wei, J., et al. (2022). "Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models." *NeurIPS 2022*. arXiv:2201.11903 — **Full text: open access (arXiv).** (Kojima's zero-shot CoT and Wang's self-consistency, +17.9 points on GSM8K to 74.4%, are the standard companions.)

**But visible reasoning is not faithful reasoning.** Turpin et al. show injected prompt biases shift model answers (accuracy drops up to 36% across 13 BBH tasks) while the CoT explanations almost never mention the bias — 1 of 426 manually reviewed biased explanations disclosed it. Turpin, M., Michael, J., Perez, E., & Bowman, S. (2023). "Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think." *NeurIPS 2023*. arXiv:2305.04388 — **Full text: open access (arXiv).** Follow-ups: Lanham et al. (2023), arXiv:2307.13702; Anthropic (2025), arXiv:2505.05410 — reasoning models verbalized hints they actually used only 25% (Claude 3.7 Sonnet) / 39% (DeepSeek R1) of the time. **Design consequence: a character's displayed "inner life" is a *performance* of reasoning, not a window into it — write it as characterization, never claim it as transparency.**

**Showing reasoning increases user trust — including misplaced trust.** In a between-subjects experiment (N=417), a CoT-enabled agent significantly increased perceived knowledge and behavioral intentions, mediated by trust/usefulness/personalization outweighing cognitive load and privacy concerns. Ebrahimi, et al. (2026). *International Journal of Information Management* 90:103075. OA PDF: https://www.uni.li/sites/default/files/2026-05/Published-Version-IJIM_%281%29.pdf — **Full text: open access.** Counterweight: Park et al., "Critical or Compliant?" (arXiv:2511.12001, N=800) — users equate trust with outcome agreement, flawed reasoning goes undetected, and confident tone suppresses error detection. Barez et al., "Chain-of-Thought Is Not Explainability," note ~25% (244/1,000) of recent CoT papers treat CoT as interpretability. For a JARVIS-class character: visible deliberation buys trust and perceived aliveness — spend it knowingly, with accuracy underneath.

## 13. The JARVIS/Alfred Archetype — Support Intelligence as a Person (HCI evidence)

**People apply social rules to computers automatically (CASA / Media Equation).** Reeves & Nass's program of experiments shows users mindlessly apply politeness, reciprocity, personality attribution, and teammate loyalty to computers given minimal social cues — e.g., users rate a computer more positively when *it* asks about itself than when another computer asks (original study N=22; replicated with voice with near-identical results). Reeves, B., & Nass, C. (1996). *The Media Equation: How People Treat Computers, Television, and New Media Like Real People and Places*. Cambridge University Press. See also Nass, C., Fogg, B. J., & Moon, Y. (1996). "Can computers be teammates?" *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies* 45(6):669–678. doi:10.1006/ijhc.1996.0073. (The 2023 van der Goot & Etzrodt critique distinguishes "respond *as if* human" from machines *being* a different kind of social actor — worth knowing; it does not overturn the behavioral findings.) **This is the theoretical license for the whole archetype: person-treatment is the default user response to consistent social cues, not something the character must earn from zero.**

**Relational agents: the Alfred pattern, measured longitudinally.** Bickmore & Picard define *relational agents* — artifacts designed to build long-term social-emotional relationships — and evaluate the first one in a controlled experiment: 101 users interacting daily for a month with an exercise-adoption agent. Versus an equivalent task-oriented agent with no relationship-building behaviors, the relational agent was **respected more, liked more, and trusted more even after four weeks**, and users expressed significantly greater desire to continue working with it. Bickmore, T. W., & Picard, R. W. (2005). "Establishing and Maintaining Long-Term Human-Computer Relationships." *ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction* 12(2):293–327. doi:10.1145/1067860.1067867. Author PDF: https://dam-prod.media.mit.edu/x/files/pdfs/04.bickmore-picard-tochi.pdf — **Full text: legally accessible author copy.** Longitudinal companion: a relational exercise advisor used daily for **two months** by 21 older adults (62–84) at home was accepted and liked, and significantly increased walking versus standard-of-care control (p=.004), with users describing the relationship as closer to a friend than a stranger. Bickmore, T. W., Caruso, L., & Clough-Gorr, K. (2005). "It's just like you talk to a friend: Relational agents for older adults." *Interacting with Computers* / CHI 2005 companion papers: http://www.ccs.neu.edu/research/rag/publications/05_IWC_BTCLCKHT.pdf ; http://www.ccs.neu.edu/research/rag/publications/05_CHI_BTCLCK.pdf — **Full texts: legally accessible author copies.** The relationship behaviors that did this are mundane and specifiable: social dialogue, empathy expressions, remembering and referencing the shared history, appropriate humor, continuity of role.

**Proactivity: competence is the license to anticipate.** In Xiao, Stasko & Catrambone's controlled study (AAMAS 2004), an interface agent giving timely, *competent* proactive suggestions was valued — participants said "the usefulness far exceeded the occasional annoyance" — while degraded competence changed the verdict: perceived utility tracked the *types of errors* the agent made, and irrelevant proactive suggestions were experienced as annoying (the Clippy failure mode, in controlled form). Xiao, J., Stasko, J., & Catrambone, R. (2004). "An Empirical Study of the Effect of Agent Competence on User Performance and Perception." *AAMAS 2004*. Author PDF: https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~stasko/papers/aamas04.pdf — **Full text: legally accessible author copy.** Modern replication of the failure modes: a 14-day deployment of a proactive planning/reflection agent (PITCH; 12 participants, 336 conversations, 3,181 turns) found users accepted, negotiated, or resisted its initiatives, and identified the breakdown behaviors precisely: **rigidity, premature turn-taking, and overpromising** (Abbas et al., CHI 2026; arXiv version: https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24073v3 — **open access**). And timing is the lever: in a within-subjects study (N=32), proactively offered LLM help whose timing was adaptively aligned to sensed need improved response accuracy by 21% and cut false negatives from 50.9% to 22.9%, improving perceived efficiency, dependability, and benevolence (Liu et al., CHI 2026; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00880 — **open access**). **Archetype spec distilled: anticipate only where competence is demonstrable, never overpromise, never seize the turn, and treat timing — not frequency — as the proactivity dial.** (A proactive-programming-assistant design study, Chen et al., GenAI@CHI 2025 workshop, https://generativeaiandhci.github.io/papers/2025/genaichi2025_30.pdf, adds the interface pattern: proactive suggestions presented as quickly-scannable summaries so the user can accept/dismiss at a glance — workshop paper, lower evidentiary weight.)

**Loyalty and role-consistency:** no primary study isolating "loyalty" as an agent variable was found; the operational evidence is the conjunction of §13's relational-agent results (trust grows from *consistent role behavior over weeks*) and §1's drift results (role behavior measurably decays within ~100 turns without architectural support). Loyalty, in the buildable sense, = persona consistency + memory of the relationship + role-appropriate refusal of out-of-role demands. Flagged as partially evidenced — see Gap List.

1. **Matalon et al. (2025), PNAS — prosodic vocabulary/syntax of spontaneous vs scripted speech.** OA. The direct diagnosis of "sounds scripted," with the 1–4-word intonation-unit norm.

4. **Park et al. (2023), Generative Agents (UIST).** OA (arXiv:2304.03442). The reference architecture for memory, reflection, and believable persistence.
5. **Bickmore & Picard (2005), TOCHI — relational agents.** Author PDF free. The JARVIS/Alfred archetype with a month-long controlled evaluation.
6. **Serapio-García et al. (2025), Nature Machine Intelligence / arXiv:2307.00184.** How to specify and *measure* a character's traits, and how adjective-level trait shaping actually performs.
7. **Quaglio (2009), Television Dialogue (John Benjamins).** Paywalled — the one book purchase on this list justified by uniqueness: the only corpus-measured account of scripted-vs-real dialogue (vagueness gap).
8. **Stivers et al. (2009), PNAS — turn-taking timing universals.** OA. Sets the latency budget (~200 ms) for any conversational mode.
9. **Mathur & Reichling (2016), Cognition — uncanny valley and trust.** OA copy. The embodiment risk model, with trust measured behaviorally.
10. **Wei et al. (2022) + Turpin et al. (2023) (read as a pair), NeurIPS.** Both OA. Capability case for visible reasoning *and* the faithfulness warning that governs how "inner life" may honestly be presented. ## Gap List — Important Questions With No Solid Primary Evidence Found

1. **Catchphrase dosing.** No primary study found on optimal catchphrase frequency, placement, or decay for a recurring host persona — the Catherine Tate work establishes the idiolect mechanism, not a schedule. The "forced catchphrase" fix currently rests on craft + corpus inference, not experiment.
2. **Exemplar dialogue vs adjective prompts, head-to-head.** No controlled primary study isolates exemplar-dialogue persona prompting vs adjective-list prompting on perceived voice distinctness. Evidence is indirect (BIG5-CHAT: training > prompting; PERSONAGE: parameterized generation works).
3. **Empathy, spoken vs written.** Perceived-empathy studies found are text-based (or clinical-voice adjacent); no controlled primary study compares perceived empathy of the *same* agent responses delivered on-camera/spoken vs in writing.
4. **Months-long growth without drift.** No evidence found of a character verifiably updating beliefs/preferences over months while staying in character. Longest hard evidence: LoCoMo-style recall over synthetic months (with a ~36–44% human lag) and drift *degradation* over ~100 rounds. This is the spec's largest open risk.
5. **Primary norms for a late-20s female host voice.** Precise speaking-F0 norms could not be secured from a primary source in this pass (blogs/secondary only); formant norms (Hillenbrand et al.) are solid, F0 norms need Baken & Orlikoff (textbook) or a dedicated JASA source before being written into a voice spec.
6. **Embodiment norms for direct-to-camera monologue.** Gesture/gaze/backchannel evidence is overwhelmingly dyadic conversation; gesture-synchrony tolerances (Nirme et al.) transfer, but monologue-specific gaze/idle-motion norms for a host addressing a lens were not found.
7. **"Loyalty" as an isolated variable.** No primary study manipulates agent loyalty/role-consistency independently; current support is conjunctive (relational-agent trust + persona-drift decay), not direct.
8. **Sports-host domain transfer.** Essentially all evidence is general-population or clinical/educational; nothing found measures these effects for short-form sports presentation specifically. Treat all effect sizes as directional for this persona, not calibrated.
