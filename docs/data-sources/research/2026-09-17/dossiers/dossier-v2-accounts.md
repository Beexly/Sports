# Method Deep-Dives: 10 Engagement Targets

## 1. Ray Carpenter — @csv_enjoyer

**Computational method.** Carpenter's contribution is data-engineering infrastructure, not a predictive model: he builds reproducible NFL analytics pipelines with a modern stack — Docker, dbt, Airflow, Kubernetes, DuckDB — over raw nflfastR CSVs. His public repo `football_data_engineering` implements a bronze → silver → gold medallion architecture: bronze = raw CSV from nflfastR; silver = cleaned team abbreviations (dbt views/tables); gold = aggregated stats; plus dbt data-quality tests and Airflow DAGs for orchestration. Quick start: `python download.py 2025`, then `dbt run`, `dbt test`.
**Inputs/rules.** Raw nflfastR play-by-play CSVs; dbt models for staging/cleaning; k8s manifests; Airflow DAGs. No betting rules — the "model" is the pipeline itself.
**Dated threads/papers/posts.** GitHub repo `array-carpenter/football_data_engineering` (active; updated ~104 days before Sept 2026 crawl). Authorship line in the repo README: "Ray Carpenter — @csv_enjoyer | thespade.substack.coom". Substack: The Spade (thespade.substack.com).
**Data sources.** nflfastR (nflverse) raw CSVs; DuckDB as the compute layer.
**Differentiator.** He treats NFL analytics as a *data-engineering* problem first — most of the ecosystem argues about models while running ad-hoc notebooks; Carpenter's edge is production-grade pipelines (dbt tests, Airflow scheduling, Kubernetes) that make analysis repeatable. Engagement angle: pipeline reliability, dbt modeling patterns, nflverse data-quality issues.
**Evidence:** https://github.com/array-carpenter/football_data_engineering

## 2. Ben Baldwin — @benbbaldwin

**Computational method.** Baldwin is the co-founder of the RBSDM.com database and the **nflfastR** R package (efficient access to NFL play-by-play data with EPA/WP columns baked in), and author/maintainer of **nfl4th** — an R package (CRAN, v1.0.4, MIT) of functions to calculate *optimal fourth-down decisions*. nfl4th imports xgboost, mgcv, nflfastR, and nflreadr, and powers the **@ben_bot_baldwin** 4th-down decision bot, which grades every coach's go-for-it/kick/punt call in near real time against the model's recommendation.
**Inputs/rules.** nflfastR play-by-play; expected-points and win-probability models underneath; the bot's decision rule is "go when the model's win-probability-maximizing recommendation says go." Public track record: using his code and data, theScore found teams went for it in toss-up situations 16.8% (2019) → 18.1% (2020) → 26.5% (early 2021) — the league moved *toward* the model.
**Dated threads/papers/posts.** jetsxfactor.com (Feb 20, 2023) quotes his canonical line: "No team should ever punt on 4th & 3 in a game they are losing." theScore (Sept 2021) on the bot's league-wide influence. CRAN nfl4th docs (published 2023-08-21).
**Data sources.** nflfastR (co-created), nflverse play-by-play, NFL.com/ESPN.com fourth-down play feeds.
**Differentiator.** Baldwin didn't just publish a paper — he shipped the *database the entire field now runs on* (nflfastR → nflverse) and a live bot that publicly grades 32 coaching staffs weekly. His current ESPN role (chief data scientist) means his public threads now carry institutional weight.
**Evidence:** https://cran.crest.fr/web/packages/nfl4th/nfl4th.pdf · https://www.thescore.com/nfl/news/2198395/amp

## 3. Aaron Schatz — @ASchatzNFL

**Computational method.** Creator of **DVOA (Defense-adjusted Value Over Average)**, now housed at FTN Fantasy. Method: every NFL play is compared to a league-average baseline *for that exact situation* — down, distance, field position. Five yards on 3rd-and-4 is worth far more than five yards on 3rd-and-12. Opponent adjustments are applied (passing also adjusted by target position group: RB/TE/WR), fourth-quarter blowout performance is downweighted, all fumbles count equally, and each season is normalized so league average = 0%. Weighted DVOA decays early-season games. The preseason **projection system** (2026 edition, Sept 2026): offense/defense/special-teams DVOA projected separately via regressions trained on 2012–2024 data; inputs = three-year DVOA + separate QB projection + regression-to-mean adjustments (personnel changes, coaching experience, draft history, OL age/tenure, returning-from-injury players); QBs new to a system are penalized unless elite. The 32 team projections run through **50,000 season simulations** with a dynamic adjustment (+1.5% DVOA to winners, −1.5% to losers within a sim) to mimic in-season learning.
**Inputs/rules.** NFL play-by-play (straight from the league — Schatz has explicitly corrected the record that DVOA is *not* charting-based); preseason priors; 50k Monte Carlo sims.
**Dated threads/papers/posts.** *FTN Football Almanac 2026* (21st annual edition, July 2025 lineage); "Aaron Schatz's 2026 DVOA Projections" (ftnfantasy.com, Sept 2026); FTN DVOA rankings/method page (opponent adjustments begin after Week 4; Forest Index for Estimated Wins).
**Data sources.** NFL official play-by-play; FTN charting for adjacent products.
**Differentiator.** DVOA is the longest-running opponent- and situation-adjusted efficiency system in football (roots in *The Hidden Game of Football*), and Schatz's projection write-ups are unusually honest about their own weird outputs ("A few of them will look strange to you. A few of them look strange to *me*.").
**Evidence:** https://ftnfantasy.com/nfl/aaron-schatzs-2026-dvoa-projections · http://ftnfantasy.com/stats/nfl/team-total-dvoa

## 6. Seth Walder — @SethWalder

**Computational method.** ESPN analytics writer (since 2017) and ESPN Bet Live regular. His analytical home base is ESPN's **Football Power Index (FPI)**: a Bayesian predictive rating measuring points better/worse than an average NFL team on a neutral field. EPA is the foundation — each play gets an adjusted EPA from historical data, decomposed into offensive, defensive, and special-teams components. Preseason priors sit on unit EPA rates derived from preseason expectations; the *only* single position that moves FPI is quarterback (predictive QBR added, with aging curves, injury probability, and replacement-level rookies). Game-level projections add altitude, travel distance (~0.5 pts in extremes), rest differentials, and seasonal offensive-EPA decay; each team's season is then simulated **10,000 times** for playoff/Super Bowl/draft-slot odds. Walder's own framing (quoted 2023): FPI rankings are "based substantially on win totals from Caesars Sportsbook and strength of schedule, along with factors such as past team performance and returning starters" — i.e., the model is deliberately market-aware.
**Inputs/rules.** EPA per play by unit; QB efficiency/injury probability; travel/rest/altitude; market win totals as a prior input; 10k Monte Carlo season sims.
**Dated threads/papers/posts.** "Biggest NFL Football Power Index swings of the season" (ESPN, Nov 13, 2021 — byline confirms analytics-writer role and @SethWalder handle); Wharton Moneyball clip on real-time probability tools; ESPN's "Guide to NFL FPI."
**Data sources.** NFL play-by-play; ESPN Stats & Info; Caesars win totals; QBR components.
**Differentiator.** Walder is the public explainer-in-chief for the most-cited black-box-adjacent model in mainstream football media — and he's unusually candid that FPI *leans on the betting market* as an input, which is exactly the market-vs-model tension GSE should engage him on.
**Evidence:** https://www.espn.ph/nfl/insider/story/_/id/32608631/biggest-nfl-football-power-index-swings-season-which-teams-better-worse-preseason-expectations · https://www.espn.ph/blog/statsinfo/post/_/id/123048/a-guide-to-nfl-fpi

## 7. Kevin Cole — @KevinCole___

**Computational method.** Creator of **Unexpected Points** (ex-PFF). Three signature methods: (1) **Adjusted scores** — weekly "advanced reviews" that quantify team play quality by emphasizing stable metrics (success rate, adjusted EPA) and downweighting high-variance events (turnovers, special teams, penalties, fumble luck); tested against actual scores for slight predictive lift. (2) **Bayesian Quarterback Rankings** — go-forward projections of adjusted QB efficiency, blending multi-season adjusted EPA/play priors (250-dropback minimums) with current-season evidence. (3) **Improvement Index** — projects a team's upcoming season from the prior year's roster, then adjusts for offseason changes with an NBA-style plus-minus: player value measured by EPA-based on/off-field impact, smoothed through clustering of similar players, weighted by participation (snap counts), with injury-risk, coordinator-change, and scheme-tendency adjustments. A +43 index means ~43 points of point-differential improvement vs. keeping the roster intact (~1.3 wins).
**Inputs/rules.** Play-by-play EPA and success rate; snap counts; roster/coordinator changes; DraftKings Showdown slate sims (methodology + most-similar-historical-matchup comps per slate).
**Dated threads/papers/posts.** Weekly "Advanced Reviews" and "Bayesian Quarterback Rankings" (Unexpected Points, 2024–2025 seasons); "The QB GOAT Series" (methodology post using ~1M QB dropbacks over nearly a century); Wharton Moneyball appearances (Apr 2025, Sept 2026); Improvement Index breakdown (ivy.fm recap, 2025).
**Data sources.** nflverse-style play-by-play; PFF grades (as a cross-check against EPA, not an input — Cole notes where they align/diverge); historical rosters.
**Differentiator.** Cole is the rare analyst whose weekly product is *explicitly* built on the stable-vs-noisy decomposition — he publishes the adjusted score alongside the real one every week, so his process is auditable in public.
