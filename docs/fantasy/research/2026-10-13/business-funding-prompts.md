# Business Funding AI Prompts — Playbook
Source: "Claude Just Changed Business Funding Forever" — Irvin Peña (162K subs), Apr 22, 2026
Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hk-OWK--n0g

## The 4 prompts (paste into Claude, fill the brackets)

**1. Business description**
> Write a professional company description for my LLC [insert name]. Explain what we do, who we serve, and why we're positioned for growth. Make it funding-ready for a bank, credit union or SBA loan.

**2. Market analysis**
> Generate a market analysis for my business in the [insert your industry]. Highlight industry growth, competitor gaps, and why my company is a low-risk choice for funding.

**3. Financial projections**
> Create a 3-year financial projection for my LLC assuming [X in year 1 revenue], [Y% growth each year], and [Z% operating expenses]. Present in a table with revenue, expenses, and profit so it's bank-ready.

**4. Compliance & fundability**
> Write a section for my business plan that highlights compliance factors banks look for: real business address, correct NAICS code, EIN, business bank account, and tradelines.

## How to use them (his method)
- Tailor every output with REAL metrics: revenue, client count, growth %. Don't sound like everyone else.
- Use ONE uniform business description everywhere: bank account application, D&B profile, credit card applications, vendor accounts. Banks lend to businesses they understand.
- Reframe "funding" as "partnership" / "low-risk choice" — mirror underwriter language.
- Keep projections conservative. Inflated numbers raise flags. Present projections alongside tax returns, financials, bank statements.
- Reuse the materials as a pitch deck for customers, teammates, contracts, bids — not just lenders.

## Fundability checklist (what banks check)
- Real business address (legit virtual office OK, not cheap garbage ones)
- Correct NAICS code (bank-level classification) + SIC code (4-digit, what credit bureaus use)
- EIN, business bank account, existing tradelines
- D&B profile set up

## Funding targets he names
Banks, credit unions, SBA loans (business purchase, real estate, equipment), private lenders, 0% business credit cards, business lines of credit ($100K–$500K/line), vendor accounts/tradelines.
