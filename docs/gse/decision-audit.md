# Decision Audit Offer

## Offer name
GSE Sports Decision Process Audit (Trust-First Diagnostic)

## Target buyer
Founder-operators, analysts, and serious sports bettors who want an accountable forecasting process without fabricated outcomes or deceptive promises.

## Pain
- Overloaded by noisy sports signals and conflicting "edge" narratives.
- No reliable audit trail for why a recommendation should be trusted.
- Previous vendor claims are opaque on methodology, assumptions, and sample limits.
- Need a documented process that separates education from performance claims.

## Promise
A transparent, evidence-first audit of each user’s sports-decision process, including data provenance, assumption checks, and playbook-quality recommendations for safer decision discipline.

What this offer is:
- A process audit (not guaranteed outcomes).
- A practical framework for how to evaluate picks, risk, and confidence.
- A founder-reviewed report that documents what is known and what is uncertain.

## What it is not
- It is not a guarantee of win-rate, ROI, edge, or profitability.
- It is not an auto-pick bot.
- It is not live odds timing or guaranteed money-management advice.
- It is not a public performance report.

## Deliverables
- Discovery intake + scoring context review
- Backtest and evidence snapshot (honest current status)
- Playbook of decision filters used for this buyer
- Risk and uncertainty map by sport/market
- 30/60/90-day process improvements checklist
- Signed owner-gated closure criteria document

## Emergency price draft
- Founder-intel emergency sprint: **USD $49** (one-time consult-style onboarding lane only; payable via manual invoice).
- Includes only one call and a one-page diagnostic note.

## Standard price draft
- Core decision audit lane: **USD $199** (non-recurring service package).
- Includes intake, findings summary, and documented owner sign-off path.

## Intake questions
1. What decision types are currently most frequent (lines, props, futures, parlays)?
2. What data sources do you currently trust and why?
3. What recurring losses/discomforts are hardest to explain?
4. What is your current process for rule-breaking or exceptions?
5. What budget and timeline are realistic for process changes?
6. Who is accountable for accepting/rejecting recommendations?
