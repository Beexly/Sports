# [GSE] Decision Process Research Brief — Rest and Travel Spots

A complete, no-claim example brief built from `research-brief-template.md`. Draft only;
owner-review required before any external use. No performance claim is attached.

## Topic
- Scenario: how short rest (e.g., a Thursday game after a Sunday) and long travel relate
  to team scoring and result variance.
- Sport / market type: NFL and NBA; game-level totals and margins — analysis only, not a
  recommendation.
- Evidence source: public schedule + final-score data (rights-gated via the clearance
  engine) plus internal notes.

## Claim level
- **Level 1 — Interpretation.** Observes patterns and interprets them with caveats.
  No recommendation, no outcome claim, no predictive-superiority claim.

## Observation
- Teams on short rest tend to show higher result variance than teams on standard rest in
  the samples reviewed. "Tend to" carries weight here: the direction is suggestive, not
  settled.
- Long cross-country travel before an early local kickoff is frequently cited; in the data
  reviewed the effect is small and noisy once injuries and opponent strength are accounted
  for.

## Interpretation (with caveats)
- The most defensible read is "wider uncertainty," not "a known direction." A short-rest
  spot is a reason to widen your own confidence band, not to assume a result.
- Confounds dominate: injury status, roster-rest decisions, and opponent quality can each
  be larger than the rest/travel signal itself.

## Evidence required (before this brief is used)
- Source URL(s) or internal extraction record(s), each with a captured rights snapshot.
- Data recency and the exact window analyzed.
- Sample sizes per spot type, stated plainly (these are usually small).
- Counterexamples and at least one failure mode.

## Uncertainty
- Small per-spot samples; multi-season pooling mixes different rule eras.
- Confounding by injuries / opponent strength is hard to remove cleanly.
- Bands should be wide; a point estimate here would overstate what's known.

## No-bet / decision-hygiene framing
- This is educational decision-process support — not betting advice, and not a promise of
  outcomes.
- **Falsifiable rule (example):** "Over the next 50 qualifying short-rest games, if the
  variance of margins is not materially higher than standard-rest games, retire the
  short-rest caveat." Write the rule before looking; let the data retire it.
