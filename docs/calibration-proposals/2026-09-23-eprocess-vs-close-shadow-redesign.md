# E-process calibration redesign: shadow scaffold

Date: 2026-09-23
Branch: `gse/eprocess-calib-shadow`

## Decision summary

The calibration gate story retires the flat Brier `0.22` floor and the consecutive-green-window streak. It does not delete the legacy diagnostics; they remain available for comparison while the new instrument is evaluated in shadow mode.

The replacement candidate is an anytime-valid forecast-dominance e-process against the de-vigged closing probability. The scaffold is additive and unexported from the package runtime barrel. It cannot publish, alter a pick, change a gate, or change a model version.

The only implementation in this PR is a pure package module with direct unit tests. It reuses the existing `packages/prediction-engine/src/forecast-skill-eprocess.ts` kernel rather than creating a second likelihood-ratio implementation.

## Null and observation contract

For one settled binary selection:

- `p` is the model probability at the lock or would-have-published decision.
- `m` is the de-vigged closing probability for the same event, frozen before the outcome.
- `y` is the realized binary outcome. PUSH and VOID rows are outside this instrument.
- The population is `would-have-published`, including rows marked `published` and `suppressed`. Rows marked `not-would-have-published` are counted and excluded before scoring.
- The settlement sequence is strictly increasing and supplied explicitly.
- At most one scored row per event/game is accepted. Same-game multi-market rows must not be treated as independent.
- The sport and market group universe is supplied before replay. Groups are registered as `sport × market`; a new group is not silently created from observed data.
- The group mixture uses fixed equal weights. Data-dependent reweighting is not part of this scaffold.

The module fails closed on malformed rows, unknown groups, duplicate scored events, and out-of-order settlement. It has no database, environment, clock, randomness, or network dependency.

## Statistical construction

For a binary outcome, the per-pick likelihood ratio is

```text
E_t = p_t / m_t                  when y_t = 1
E_t = (1 - p_t) / (1 - m_t)      when y_t = 0
```

The group wealth is the product of those factors, accumulated in log space by the existing kernel. The running maximum, not the final value, is the statistic tested continuously. Under the null that outcomes are generated by the supplied close distribution, this is a nonnegative martingale; Ville's inequality gives an anytime-valid crossing bound. The existing kernel's shrink and clamp safeguards are retained.

For registered groups `g = 1..K`, the scaffold evaluates a fixed-weight mixture:

```text
M_t = (1 / K) * sum_g M_{g,t}
```

A group with no observations has wealth `1`. The mixture is recomputed after every scored settlement and its running maximum is recorded. This prevents a strong market family from being silently treated as the same distribution as a weak or unrelated family. The group-level values are diagnostics; the shadow verdict is based on the registered mixture.

The construction is a binary specialization of the Henzi–Ziegel forecast-dominance / sequential forecast-comparison direction. Related anytime-valid foundations are Ville (1939) and Ramdas, Grünwald, Vovk and Shafer (2023). The scaffold does not claim a new theorem.

## Shadow thresholds and verdict semantics

| Shadow state | Condition | Action |
|---|---:|---|
| Insufficient | fewer than 30 scored observations | collect only |
| Publishable shadow | mixture running maximum `M >= 20` | internal review only; no publish path |
| Claim-eligible shadow | mixture running maximum `M >= 100` | prepare evidence only; no public claim |

The 30-observation floor is a reporting floor carried from the existing shadow toolkit. It is not a replacement Brier floor and is not wired to eligibility. The module exports `ENABLED = false` and reports `gateAction: "none"` in every result.

A threshold crossing is not a profitability result, a win-rate claim, a `PROVEN` result, or permission to flip a production flag. The acceptance work remains prospective and must be reviewed separately.

## Market-family split

- **Spreads and totals:** CLV is the primary family readout. The e-process is a skill diagnostic against the de-vigged close, not a substitute for line-level CLV accounting.
- **Moneylines:** Brier Skill Score and CORP/PAV diagnostics are the primary calibration readout, with the close-relative e-process as the sequential skill diagnostic. Neither BSS nor CORP is a gate in this PR.
- **Self-honesty:** the existing calibration-sequence and ECE displays remain separate instruments. ECE is diagnostic, not the promotion law.

This split prevents a spread/total price-space result from being presented as a moneyline probability result, or vice versa.

## Acceptance gates before any future activation

1. Freeze a prospective export schema containing sequence, event id, sport, market, lock-time `p`, de-vigged close `m`, settled outcome, and the would-have-published label.
2. Verify the close source is de-vigged and the close timestamp precedes settlement. Raw vig-inclusive probabilities invalidate the intended null.
3. Replay the same ordered rows through the existing single-pick kernel and the group mixture; require byte-stable results across repeated runs.
4. Validate the null property on deterministic and seeded free fixtures, including the one-pick-per-event restriction and continuous running-max monitoring.
5. Report the WHP population, published-only secondary diagnostic, per-group `n`, `logM`, `maxM`, growth, CLV, BSS, and CORP outputs. Never score only the rows that survived the live filter.
6. Require an explicit founder-reviewed calibration proposal before changing eligibility, public claims, pricing, or `MODEL_VERSION`.
7. Keep the current legacy metrics during comparison. Retirement is a later decision, not a side effect of this scaffold.

## CEPT boundary

CEPT Theorem 1 is the admissible future gate/ensemble context: validity is only as broad as the pre-registered reaction menu. This scaffold does not implement performativity robustness or a randomized publication coin.

CEPT Theorem 2 remains prepare-not-flip: it is documentation and a future experiment design only. No public claim, production behavior, or causal claim is enabled here. Section 5 is ornamental for this PR and is not used as a gate.
