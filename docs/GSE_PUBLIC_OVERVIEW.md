# Galaxy Sports Edge — How It Works

*A plain-English overview of what Galaxy Sports Edge does, the method behind it, and how we hold ourselves accountable. Public-safe: no proprietary weights, no internal jargon.*

---

## What Galaxy Sports Edge is

Galaxy Sports Edge reads the sports betting market, scores every available matchup with a consistent model, and only publishes the picks that clear our quality and proof checks. We start from real, licensed market data — never invented numbers — and we show our work: the factors, the freshness of the data, and an honest record over time.

Our promise is simple and bounded: **no outcome is ever promised.** Sports are uncertain. What we offer is a disciplined, transparent read and a track record you can check.

---

## The method: PRICE

Everything runs through one method with five pillars. Their initials spell **PRICE** — fitting for a market product — and each is a real part of the engine.

| | Pillar | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | **Proof** | Can we show, after the fact, that we were right — without asking you to take our word for it? |
| **R** | **Read** | What does the market actually believe once the sportsbook's built-in cut is removed? |
| **I** | **Integrity** | Is the data fresh, broad, and trustworthy enough to act on at all? |
| **C** | **Context** | What real situational signals reinforce or undercut the read? |
| **E** | **Edge** | How much genuine pricing advantage is on the table? |

The pipeline is just three steps: **Read the board → Score the math → Gate the slate.** A thin or low-quality slate can produce zero picks — that is the system working, not failing.

## The GSE Score

Each pick carries one headline number, the **GSE Score (0–100)**. It blends the read, the edge, the context, and the data quality into a single ranking, then adjusts for how provably we can stand behind that specific pick.

**What it is:**
- A single, consistent ranking signal for a pick.
- Shown next to its inputs — our confidence read and the public Edge Index — never on its own.
- Deterministic: the same facts always produce the same score.

**What it is not:**
- It is **not** a win probability. We only show calibrated probabilities once enough settled results exist to prove them honestly.
- It is **not** a promise, and it is **not** a betting instruction. It is information; the decision is yours.

## What the model reads

The factors are public even though the exact weights are not. Among them:

- Market consensus across sportsbooks, and how many books cover the game
- Line movement since the opening number
- Rest and schedule context
- Head-to-head and venue history
- Cross-market agreement (does the spread agree with the moneyline?)
- Data quality — coverage, freshness, and breadth

The exact formula, weights, and constants stay proprietary. The framework above does not.

## How we prove it

A track record only matters if it cannot be quietly edited or cherry-picked. Two mechanisms keep us honest:

- **Tamper-evident receipts.** Before kickoff, each published pick is frozen with a cryptographic fingerprint. Change any detail later and the fingerprint no longer matches.
- **Pre-committed slates.** Before the first game starts, the whole day's set of picks is committed under a single fingerprint with a fixed count. We cannot add a winner or drop a loser afterward without the record showing it.

On top of that we publish:

- **Closing Line Value (CLV)** — did the price we took beat where the market closed? It is the clearest leading indicator of a real edge, and we publish it under the same gate as our win rate.
- **Calibration** — when we say a confidence level, how often does it actually hold up? We show the honest curve.

Performance statistics stay hidden until enough settled picks exist to compute them fairly. Until then, you will see an honest "not yet" rather than a number we cannot back.
