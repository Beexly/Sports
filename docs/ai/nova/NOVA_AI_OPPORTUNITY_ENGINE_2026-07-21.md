---

## 1. Executive decision

GSE does not need another AI-news page, integration guide collection, or agent that summarizes product announcements.

It needs a continuously operating **opportunity intelligence system** that can answer five questions faster and more honestly than a founder can manually:

1. **What materially changed?**
2. **Why does it matter to GSE, GSN, XXX, or another owned project?**
3. **Can it reduce cash burn, increase development capacity, create a product, open distribution, or create revenue?**
4. **What is the smallest reversible test that can prove or falsify the opportunity?**
5. **What evidence is required before a discovered possibility may be represented as available, approved, activated, earned, invoiced, or paid?**

The resulting system is named **NOVA — AI Opportunity Intelligence & Venture Architect**.

NOVA is an internal intelligence and venture engine. It discovers broadly, verifies narrowly, prototypes reversibly, measures actual outcomes, and preserves every decision so the system compounds rather than repeatedly rediscovering the same tools and ideas.

NOVA does **not** receive authority to install arbitrary software, accept terms, create accounts, submit applications, contact partners, activate affiliates, spend money, publish, deploy, merge, alter production data, share data, train models, or change its own production scoring weights.

That distinction is load-bearing:

> Internal autonomy is not external authority.

## 4. Agent organization

NOVA reports to JARVIS and is reviewed by METER, BOBBY, TAL, AUDIT, and GAUGE. It is intentionally not yet added to the canonical cockpit/Prisma agent enum. Canonical registration occurs only after persistence, scheduled execution, and cockpit proof exist.

NOVA coordinates six bounded subagents.

### RADAR — source and change intelligence

RADAR monitors allowlisted release feeds, registries, APIs, and research metadata. It produces normalized observations, source-health records, and material-change alerts.

It cannot install discovered software, invoke discovered tools, retain prohibited source content, or use unregistered sources.

### TERMS — offers, credits, fees, and operative rules

TERMS verifies pricing, eligibility, deadlines, payout mechanics, marketplace fees, credit coverage, affiliate terms, reversals, restrictions, and expiration.

It cannot submit an application, accept terms, claim eligibility without evidence, or treat a maximum program award as an approved asset.

### YIELD — monetization and business design

YIELD translates verified capabilities into buyer jobs, product units, revenue lanes, partnerships, affiliates, distribution, cost savings, and unit economics.

It cannot publish an offer, change pricing, contact a partner, activate an affiliate link, or represent scenario revenue as earned money.

### ATLAS — capability, API, data, and marketplace mapping

ATLAS maps discoveries against the existing GSE capability registry, source-rights system, provider router, product routes, and commercial surfaces. It must identify duplication before proposing another framework or dependency.

It cannot create accounts, grant credentials, install integrations, or bypass RELAY, TAL, source-rights, or owner gates.

### FORGE — isolated prototype engineering

FORGE receives only approved experiment contracts. It builds the smallest proof in a disposable branch, worktree, container, fixture, or dark feature flag. It runs narrow tests first, then applicable typecheck, lint, tests, build, security, rights, and guardrail checks.

FORGE cannot merge, deploy, publish, spend, use production credentials, modify production data, or push to a protected branch.

### PROOF — adversarial verification

PROOF attacks evidence, claims, costs, rights, security, benchmark quality, and revenue state. It verifies the complete experiment, including negative results and rollback behavior.

PROOF cannot approve its own prototype, rewrite evidence, clear owner gates, or change scoring weights.

## 5. The autonomous operating loop

NOVA's intended loop is not “browse, summarize, and recommend.” It is a closed evidence and development cycle.

### Step 1 — Sense

RADAR selects due sources from the allowlist. Security/deprecation sources, model/platform changes, credits, marketplaces, and APIs receive different priority weights. Per-cycle capacity prevents an unbounded polling or cost storm.

Manual terms and credit pages are never put into the autonomous fetch queue.

### Step 2 — Capture metadata safely

The collector:

- uses HTTPS only;
- sends no cookies or credentials;
- rejects cross-origin redirects;
- uses conditional requests through ETag and Last-Modified when available;
- caps response size;
- times out;
- runs sequentially to respect rate limits;
- captures only allowed metadata;
- fingerprints the payload;
- discards raw bodies;
- retains prior observations when a source fails;
- increments failure state and applies backoff.

### Step 3 — Detect material change

Every observation is compared to the prior observation using stable identity and content fingerprints. Changes are classified as:

- `NEW`
- `UPDATED`
- `UNCHANGED`
- `REMOVED`

Materiality is raised for security issues, shutdowns, deprecations, breaking changes, terms changes, pricing changes, deadlines, credits, grants, partnerships, model releases, APIs, registries, and free tiers.

### Step 4 — Convert a change into an opportunity candidate

A source change is not automatically a business opportunity. NOVA creates a candidate only when the change can be tied to at least one GSE project, capability gap, cost center, buyer job, distribution channel, data asset, or risk.

Every candidate states:

- what changed;
- target project;
- opportunity class;
- revenue lanes;
- evidence;
- rights state;
- security posture;
- required cash and owner time;
- time to first evidence;
- hypothesized time to cash;
- risks;
- smallest proposed action;
- expiration;
- whether external action, credentials, data sharing, model training, or jurisdiction review is involved.

### Step 5 — Verify evidence

Evidence has tiers:

1. official primary source;
2. regulator or standards body;
3. official repository release;
4. vendor terms or program rules;
5. independent secondary source;
6. community signal;
7. unverified claim.

Money, credits, pricing, commissions, grants, payouts, and eligibility require primary evidence. Community excitement may trigger research but cannot advance the money state.

Contradictions reduce confidence. Stale evidence reduces confidence. A missing official source prevents strong claims.

### Step 6 — Map the full economic surface

YIELD must evaluate five distinct value outputs:

1. **Direct revenue** — money paid for an offer, API, dataset, model, workflow, service, app, benchmark, report, or sponsorship.
2. **Indirect revenue** — affiliate, referral, partnership, co-sell, reseller, or revenue-share economics.
3. **Non-dilutive leverage** — credits, grants, prizes, training, support, or partner benefits.
4. **Cost leverage** — verified cash or owner-time reduction.
5. **Asset value** — proprietary data, evaluation records, model capability, distribution, or reusable infrastructure that can later support revenue.

These categories are not interchangeable. Savings are not revenue. Credits are not cash. Pipeline is not booked revenue. Approved commissions are not paid commissions.

### Step 7 — Score opportunity and risk

Positive signals are scored from 0–5:

- strategic fit;
- evidence strength;
- revenue potential;
- time to value;
- recurring leverage;
- data flywheel;
- distribution leverage;
- cost reduction;
- defensibility;
- reversibility;
- learning value;
- urgency.

Risks are scored from 0–5:

- cash;
- owner time;
- implementation complexity;
- legal;
- security;
- data rights;
- vendor lock-in;
- volatility.

The evidence engine independently measures evidence strength. The submitter cannot self-award credibility by setting a high `evidenceStrength` signal.

Priority bands are `P0`, `P1`, `P2`, `P3`, or `HELD`. A candidate is held when a hard blocker exists even if its theoretical upside is large.

### Step 8 — Apply hard policy gates

Possible dispositions are:

- `IMPLEMENT_INTERNAL`
- `PROTOTYPE_SANDBOX`
- `RESEARCH_MORE`
- `OWNER_REVIEW`
- `WATCH`
- `QUARANTINE`
- `REJECT`

Hard blockers include:

- blocked rights;
- blocked security posture;
- model training or data sharing without cleared rights;
- proof-bearing money states without operative evidence;
- expired offers;
- no evidence;
- invalid economics;
- claims that paid money is still only a potential range.

Every decision explicitly carries:

- `externalActionsAllowed: false`
- `automaticInstallAllowed: false`
- `automaticSpendAllowed: false`
- `automaticPublishAllowed: false`

### Step 9 — Select a portfolio, not a pile

Unbounded agents create backlog and integration sprawl. NOVA limits concurrent experiments.

Under scarce capacity, it preserves portfolio diversity:

- at least one credible direct-revenue or distribution path when available;
- at least one cost/capacity path when available;
- then the highest-value remaining candidate.

This prevents the system from spending every cycle on interesting research while revenue and burn remain unresolved.

### Step 10 — Run the smallest reversible experiment

Every experiment states:

- hypothesis;
- smallest test;
- success criteria;
- failure criteria;
- evidence to capture;
- rollback plan;
- maximum cash;
- maximum owner hours;
- maximum calendar days;
- maximum premium-model calls;
- whether a sandbox is required.

The default experiment budget is zero cash. A candidate requiring cash moves to owner review.

### Step 11 — Adversarial verification

PROOF verifies:

- exact versions and configuration;
- complete diff;
- commands and test output;
- quality delta;
- latency;
- reliability;
- token and cash cost;
- owner-time cost;
- rights;
- privacy;
- security;
- jurisdiction;
- vendor lock-in;
- rollback;
- actual economic evidence.

A prototype can fail. A negative result is an asset when the reason is preserved.

### Step 12 — Measure outcome and learn

NOVA records:

- success/failure;
- shipped or rolled back;
- actual cash;
- actual owner hours;
- days to first evidence;
- 30-day revenue;
- 30-day savings;
- predicted probability;
- source IDs;
- opportunity class.

It then computes success rate, Brier score, revenue, savings, and rollback rate by source and opportunity class.

The system can recommend that a source or class be upweighted, downweighted, or subjected to stronger evidence gates. It cannot change production weights directly.

## 6. Money-state truth

The following states are sequential and cannot be skipped:

1. `hypothetical`
2. `discovered`
3. `eligibility_unverified`
4. `eligible`
5. `applied`
6. `approved`
7. `activated`
8. `earned`
9. `invoiced`
10. `paid`

`rejected` and `expired` are terminal exits.

Examples:

- A program page advertising “up to $100,000” is **discovered**, not approved.
- A founder matching public requirements is **eligibility_unverified** until operative terms and exclusions are checked.
- An application receipt supports **applied**, not approved.
- An award email supports **approved**, not activated.
- A balance visible and usable in the account supports **activated**.
- A commission dashboard may support **earned**, but only a payout receipt supports **paid**.
- Marketplace pipeline is neither invoiced nor paid.

This state machine is designed to prevent the exact failure mode where attractive maximums become fictional runway.

## 8. Data and model commercialization rules

NOVA supports the idea of earning money or credits from systems trained on GSE information, but only through an explicit asset-rights decision.

Every data asset records:

- source IDs;
- ownership;
- rights state;
- personal data;
- third-party expression;
- commercial display permission;
- derived-use permission;
- model-training permission;
- redistribution permission;
- deletion/revocation process;
- review timestamp;
- evidence URLs.

Uses are evaluated separately:

- internal analysis;
- commercial display;
- derived data product;
- model training;
- evaluation benchmark;
- training-data license;
- partner sharing.

A right to read is not a right to store. A right to store is not a right to train. A right to derive is not a right to redistribute. A right to use commercially is not necessarily a right to license the underlying records to another model developer.

Potential monetizable owned assets include:

- GSE-authored taxonomies;
- GSE-created labels;
- GSE-created synthetic fixtures that do not reproduce protected expression;
- human-reviewed evaluation judgments;
- operational incident/evidence structures;
- source-freshness and provenance schemas;
- calibration and uncertainty evaluation tasks;
- rights-cleared derived sports features;
- benchmark harnesses;
- model-behavior and agent-reliability outcomes.

Before participating in any program that offers credits or payment in exchange for data, evaluations, model feedback, or training:

1. identify exactly what is transferred;
2. identify whether prompts, outputs, source content, code, customer data, or personal data are included;
3. establish ownership and permission;
4. verify retention and training terms;
5. verify deletion and opt-out rights;
6. establish compensation and valuation;
7. prohibit transfer of secrets, customer information, private legal/HR material, or unlicensed third-party content;
8. obtain owner approval;
9. preserve a transfer receipt and asset manifest.

## 9. Self-learning without uncontrolled self-modification

NOVA is designed to learn, but not to rewrite itself based on a handful of anecdotes.

### What it learns

- which sources produce useful downstream opportunities;
- which opportunity classes succeed;
- which risk patterns predict rollback;
- whether its probability estimates are calibrated;
- which experiments reach evidence or cash fastest;
- which monetization lanes create real revenue versus paperwork;
- which integrations duplicate existing capabilities;
- which tools save owner time without increasing defect risk;
- which credits actually displace cash before expiration.

### What it measures

- sample size;
- success rate;
- Brier score;
- rollback rate;
- 30-day revenue;
- 30-day savings;
- time to first evidence;
- owner hours;
- cash spent;
- source yield;
- opportunity-class yield.

### What it cannot do automatically

- change production scoring weights;
- lower evidence thresholds;
- lower rights/security gates;
- expand the source allowlist;
- install a discovered tool;
- increase experiment cash or token budgets;
- promote its own prototype;
- clear a contradiction;
- represent a scenario as an outcome.

### Promotion mechanism

Once sufficient measured outcomes exist, NOVA may propose a champion/challenger scoring policy. The challenger is replayed over historical outcomes and a holdout period. AUDIT and METER review calibration, economics, false-positive cost, and risk. Only then may the owner approve a weight change.

This prevents “self-learning” from becoming unobserved drift.

## 10. Anti-Goodhart and anti-bloat controls

NOVA must not optimize for:

- number of sources;
- number of integrations;
- number of guides;
- number of files;
- commits;
- tools installed;
- maximum credit totals;
- theoretical TAM;
- feature count;
- activity;
- novelty;
- confidence language.

It optimizes for:

- verified material change captured early;
- avoided outage or deprecation failure;
- measured cash saved;
- measured owner time saved;
- real payment evidence;
- qualified buyer evidence;
- rights-cleared proprietary asset growth;
- reusable capability;
- reversible experiments;
- calibrated decisions;
- low rollback and defect rates;
- preserved founder attention.

Every proposed dependency must answer:

1. What existing GSE capability does it replace, extend, or unblock?
2. Why is the existing implementation insufficient?
3. What buyer or cost center benefits?
4. What is the migration and rollback cost?
5. What is the security, rights, privacy, and lock-in impact?
6. What metric decides whether it remains?

## 15. Success criteria

NOVA is successful when it creates a measurable advantage in decision speed and economic outcomes—not when it produces more research.

### Intelligence

- critical changes found before they break GSE;
- low false-positive alert rate;
- every material claim traceable to evidence;
- no stale terms represented as current;
- no repeated rediscovery of rejected or already-integrated tools.

### Development

- experiments smaller and more reversible;
- fewer speculative integrations;
- lower rollback and regression rates;
- measured reduction in premium-model use for routine work;
- faster time from discovery to tested decision.

### Economics

- cash saved;
- owner hours saved;
- credits activated and consumed before expiration;
- revenue tied to receipts;
- partnerships tied to qualified pipeline and contracts;
- affiliates tied to approved conversions and payouts;
- data/model assets tied to rights-cleared commercial use;
- experiments killed quickly when evidence fails.

### Governance

- zero automatic external actions;
- zero automatic spend;
- zero automatic installs;
- zero unapproved data sharing or training;
- zero production self-modification;
- complete decision and evidence history.

## 16. Final architecture principle

The durable moat is not knowing that a new model, API, tool, database, credit program, or marketplace exists.

Everyone can read an announcement.

The moat is a system that can:

1. detect the change;
2. establish what is actually true;
3. map it against everything already built;
4. identify the economic and strategic consequences;
5. design the smallest valid test;
6. build and test in isolation;
7. reject weak ideas quickly;
8. preserve negative and positive evidence;
9. learn which sources and opportunity classes produce value;
10. convert only verified outcomes into product, distribution, savings, or revenue.

That is NOVA.
