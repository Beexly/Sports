# Week 2 Waiver Wire: The Handcuff Economy Is Wide Open

*Stat lines: ESPN Week 1 box scores.*

Week 1 answered a few backfield questions and created about nine new ones. What follows is the six running backs our board would actually pay for this week, ranked by how much we trust the role, plus the receiver and quarterback adds your league is already bidding on. Percentages assume a $100 season budget, because pretending your FAAB is unlimited is how benches get ugly.

## Priority adds, in order

### 1. Kyle Monangai, RB, Chicago Bears

Monangai turned 10 carries into 100 yards and a touchdown, and the score came on a 61-yard run that likely cost somebody their waiver priority. Ben Johnson praised him after the game, which is the closest thing to a public commitment a head coach offers in September. He is the true 1B to D'Andre Swift in an elite run game, and the knee scare from early camp is behind him. Bid 25 to 30 percent. He is the one back here whose role grows even if nobody gets hurt.

### 2. Blake Corum, RB, Los Angeles Rams

The 10 carries for 54 yards will not win you a week on their own, but the near 50/50 split with Kyren Williams is real. Williams still owns third downs and the goal line, though, and those are the snaps that pay in fantasy. That leaves Corum an elite handcuff with fringe flex value in the right matchup rather than a starter. Bid 10 to 15 percent, and push toward 20 if Williams is already on your roster.

### 3. Emmett Johnson, RB, Kansas City Chiefs

The passing-down work is the tell: two catches for 44 yards next to eight carries for 24. Johnson is the clear RB2 behind Kenneth Walker III, and Kansas City shows no interest in turning that into a committee. Premium handcuff stash, zero standalone value while Walker is healthy, which makes him the most annoying kind of player to hold. Bid 8 to 12 percent and accept that you are buying insurance, not upside.

### 4. Kaelon Black, RB, San Francisco 49ers

Black out-carried Christian McCaffrey 14 to 10, the kind of line that gets a backup rostered everywhere by Wednesday. He finished with 65 yards on the ground plus one catch for five, and the explanation is cramps plus planned workload management, a polite way of saying San Francisco would like McCaffrey upright in December. Black is a designed 1B rather than a gadget, and his value is tied entirely to the health of the man in front of him. Bid 10 to 15 percent if you do not roster McCaffrey, and closer to 20 if you do.

### 5. Tyler Allgeier, RB, Arizona Cardinals

A 17 to 11 carry edge over Jeremiyah Love is the entire argument for Allgeier, who turned it into 61 yards plus two catches for nine. The head coach says the split is game-flow based and that it is sticking. In practice that means the unglamorous carries that keep drives alive, a volume floor with a ceiling roughly the height of a coffee table. Bid 6 to 10 percent if you need a body who will not post a zero, and skip him if you need one who might post 20.

### 6. Emari Demercado, RB, Dallas Cowboys

Two carries for seven yards and five snaps is not a breakout, it is a footnote. Demercado is the primary handcuff to Javonte Williams by attrition, and Dallas just signed another back to compete for the same job. Deep bench only. Bid 1 to 3 percent in a 14-team league, or let a leaguemate spend the money for you.

## Consensus adds, rapid fire

- **WR Jalen Coker, Carolina Panthers:** 9 targets, 8 catches, 138 yards, 2 touchdowns. The target volume is the reason to bid, not the touchdowns.
- **WR Christian Watson, Green Bay Packers:** 8 targets, 6 catches, 147 yards, 2 touchdowns. Health is the only thing that has ever held him back.
- **TE Isaiah Likely, New York Giants:** 8 targets, 8 catches, 78 yards, 2 touchdowns. Eight for eight is the kind of efficiency that tends to repeat.
- **WR Devaughn Vele, New Orleans Saints:** 9 targets, 7 catches, 69 yards, 1 touchdown. Volume without the ceiling, and that plays fine in PPR.
- **QB Tyler Shough, New Orleans Saints:** 35 of 56 for 410 yards, 3 touchdowns, 2 interceptions. The yardage is real and so is the interception risk.
- **QB Jaxson Dart, New York Giants:** 16 of 20 for 230 yards and 3 touchdowns, plus 54 rushing yards. Efficient, mobile, and worth a superflex add.

## The close

Everything above is a price, not a promise, and Week 2 will reprice most of it by Sunday night. Spend on the roles you can explain to a leaguemate, and let somebody else pay for the ones you cannot.
