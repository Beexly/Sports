## 4.1 Target sample design

**30 interviews from 3 source pools:**

| Source pool | Count | Why | Response rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Galaxy Elite subscribers | 10 | Most likely buyers; validates upgrade-path | 70% |
| Twitter followers (unsubscribed) | 15 | Aware of brand; surfaces conversion blocker | 20–25% |
| Sports-betting Discord communities | 5 | Adjacent audience; validates reach | 30–40% |

**Diversification criteria (within total sample):**

- **Frequency tiers:** Daily 8–10 / Weekly 12–15 / Monthly-seasonal 5–8
- **Spend tiers:** <$1k 3–5 / $1–5k 8–10 / $5–25k 10–12 / $25k+ 3–5
- **Tenure tiers:** New (<2y) 5–7 / Mid (2–5y) 12–15 / Vet (5y+) 10–13
- **Sport coverage:** NFL-primary 12–15 / NBA-primary 8–10 / MLB 4–6 / Multi-sport 5–8
- **Existing subscription state:** Currently paid 18–22 / Never paid 8–12

Audit the sample mid-week (after 12–15 booked). Bias subsequent outreach to under-sampled categories.

## 4.2 Target lists — how to build them

- Site interaction ≥ 5 times in last 30 days.
- Has engaged with Loss Room or Pass List at least once.

### Twitter followers (15 targets)

- Public account, not private.
- Bio mentions sports / betting / fantasy / handicapping / NFL / NBA / etc.

Avoid:
- Other tout services (competitors, biased signal).
- Anonymous accounts with no profile info.

Rank by: follower count (5k–100k preferred) + engagement with Galaxy posts + profile clarity.

- Members who post regularly.

## 4.4 The 20-question interview guide

**Duration:** 30 minutes / interview.
**Format:** Video call (Zoom or Google Meet, pick one and stay consistent).
**Recording:** Ask consent first.

### Opening (2 min)

> "Hey [name], thanks for taking 30 minutes. I'm Garrett — founder of Galaxy Sports Edge. The goal of this call is research, not sales. I'm validating a premium subscription tier called Vault before I build it. I want to ask you about how you currently research sports bets, what works and doesn't, and react to a specific pitch.
>
> A few ground rules: there are no wrong answers. If something I describe sounds dumb, tell me. I'm going to record this if that's OK with you, just so I don't have to type while you talk. Ready?"

### Section A — Background (5 min, Q1–Q3)

**Q1.** "How often do you bet on sports?" → Tag: `frequency_tier`
**Q2.** "What's your annual spend on sports betting, roughly? Rough order of magnitude is fine." → Tag: `spend_tier`
**Q3.** "Do you currently subscribe to any sports betting research products? Which?" → Tag: `existing_subscriptions`. If "never paid": flag.

### Section B — Existing-tool experience (5 min, Q4–Q6)

**Q4.** "Tell me about your relationship with [their primary subscription]. What works?" → Tag: `current_research_mode`
**Q5.** "What's the most frustrating thing about your current research stack?" → Tag: `top_frustration` (verbatim quote essential)
**Q6.** "If you canceled tomorrow, why would you?" → Tag: `churn_driver`

### Section C — Galaxy positioning test (10 min, Q7–Q10)

Share screen to galaxysportsedge.com. Walk them through Loss Room, Pass List, Ledger, methodology.

**Q7.** "First reaction — what does this seem to be?" → Tag: `positioning_recognition`
**Q8.** "We publish our losses with autopsies, every game we considered and didn't publish, refresh every 30 minutes. How does that read?" → Tag: `transparency_reaction`
**Q9.** "What's missing? What would make you trust Galaxy more?" → Tag: `trust_gap`
**Q10.** "Compared to [their named subscription], does Galaxy feel more or less trustworthy? Why?" → Tag: `relative_trust`

### Section D — Vault offer test (10 min, Q11–Q17)

Pitch script:

> "OK, here's what I want to react to. I'm thinking about a premium tier above Elite called Vault. $200/year. Capped at 1,000 founding members. Members get:
>
> 1. Weekly internal-rationale digest — I write the reasoning behind a major pick of the week, Wednesdays.
> 2. Monthly group office hours — 60 minutes on Discord, members ask anything about model/picks/losses.
> 3. Quarterly private data review — performance internals not in the public Almanac.
> 4. Early access to the Model Journal weekly draft.
> 5. Vault-only Discord channel.
>
> $200 per year. How do you react?"

**Then stop talking. Silence is fine.**

**Q11.** Initial reaction → Tag: `initial_reaction`
**Q12.** "What would make Vault worth $200/year to you?" → Tag: `lead_benefit`
**Q13.** "What would make Vault feel overpriced?" → Tag: `overprice_risk`
**Q14.** "First 1,000 are founding members. Manufactured scarcity. Does that change anything?" → Tag: `scarcity_reaction`
**Q15.** "Would you join the founding 1,000? Walk me through your decision." → Tag: `intent_to_join` (headline question)
**Q16.** "If no — what's the blocker?" → Tag: `blocker_category`
**Q17.** "If yes — what would you tell a friend about why?" → Tag: `referral_message` (verbatim quote)

### Section E — Wrap (3 min, Q18–Q20)

**Q18.** "What's one thing I should change about how I described Vault?" → Tag: `pitch_feedback`
**Q19.** "Would you be willing to be one of the first 50 founding members if Vault launches in [target month]?" → Tag: `early_commit`
**Q20.** "Anyone in your network I should talk to?" → Tag: `referrals`

### What to listen for beyond the script

- **Energy shifts.** Did they lean forward at autopsies? Lean back at $200?
- **Question type.** "How do I subscribe?" = buyer. "How do you know the model works?" = skeptic worth converting. No questions = soft yes (polite, not real).
- **Vocabulary they introduce.** If 3+ independently use "transparency" or "edge" — these are landing-page-copy seeds.

## 4.5 Interviewer judgment — the politeness filter

Garrett is the founder. Respondents will be polite. Tag `politeness_suspected = TRUE` whenever any of these fire:

- No follow-up questions about the offering.
- Generic reasoning ("sounds cool") without naming a specific Vault benefit.
- Multiple benefits cited in quick succession with shallow per-benefit reasoning.
- Visible body-language tension at $200 reveal but they said yes anyway.
- No engagement on the founding-50 prompt with any specific commitment.

The 20-of-30 threshold is calibrated for *qualified* yes, which excludes politeness-suspected responses.

## 4.7 Day-7 decision matrix

### Compute the qualified yes-count

```
qualified_yes = COUNT(respondents WHERE
    intent_to_join IN ('definitely', 'likely')
    AND politeness_suspected = FALSE
    AND intent_reasoning cites ≥1 specific Vault benefit
)
```

### Match against thresholds

| qualified_yes | Decision | Action |
|---|---|---|
| ≥20 of 30 | GO at $200/year | Plan A — engineering begins Monday |
| 15–19 of 30 | LIKELY GO with retest | Plan B — 10 additional interviews at sharpened script |
| 10–14 of 30 | PIVOT to $150/year retest | Plan C — 10 fresh interviews at $150 |
| 5–9 of 30 | DEEP PIVOT | Plan D — three alternative offerings tested |
| 0–4 of 30 | NO-GO | Plan E — reposition value as Elite-tier perk |

### Secondary gates (all three must pass for any GO)

1. **Reason cluster coherence:** ≥3 of 5 Vault benefits each cited as `lead_benefit` by ≥20% of yes-respondents.
2. **Objection addressability:** <50% of no-respondents cite structural blockers (price-tolerance, never-pay-for-research).
3. **Vocabulary alignment:** ≥3 distinct non-Galaxy-seeded phrases appear in 5+ interviews each.

If yes-count passes but a gate fails → drop to the more conservative plan.

> ## The year, on the record.
>
> It is October of 2026 as I write this, and the picks that will fill the back half of this book have not all happened yet. By the time you hold this book in your hands, they will have. The losses we are about to take will be in the Loss Room. The passes we are about to make will be in the Pass List. The methodology that ran the year will be frozen at whatever weights it lands at on December 31st.
>
> I am writing this opening essay first because the entire book points back to a single argument, and I want to make it before the year's data does it for me.
>
> Most sports analytics disappears. That is the argument.
>
> It disappears in two ways. The first is the obvious way: a model publishes a confidence number Tuesday morning. The game happens Wednesday. By Friday the result is forgotten — or rebranded as a hit, or quietly never mentioned again if it was a miss. The internet has no memory, and most sports content is built to take advantage of that absence. The number sits on a page for forty-eight hours and then dissolves into the feed.
>
> The second way it disappears is more interesting. It happens inside the writer's own head. Even people who genuinely care about being right develop a habit of remembering the calls they got right and forgetting the calls they got wrong. The bias is so reflexive that almost no one notices it. I notice it in myself constantly. The calls I am proud of feel like they had reasons. The calls I am embarrassed by feel like luck. The asymmetry is the problem.
>
> This book is built against both of those disappearances. The first chapter is a year of settled picks — every one, win or loss, with the confidence number we published it at and the result. The second chapter is the Loss Room — every losing call, with the autopsy that lives on galaxysportsedge.com but is now bound to the spine. The third chapter is the Pass List — every game we considered publishing and didn't, archived so that the discipline of not-publishing is as visible as the discipline of publishing. The fourth chapter is the methodology, frozen at year-end. The fifth is the changelog, every model version that shipped in 2026, what changed in each, and what we were trying to fix.
>
> This is not a book of takes. There is no Top 10 Picks of 2026 list, because Top 10 lists are the kind of survivorship-biased content that this book exists to argue against. There is no "lessons learned" chapter in the inspirational sense, because the lessons-learned chapter is the autopsies, and the autopsies are not inspirational. They are the unflattering data about what the model believed and what was wrong with the belief.
>
> I wrote the autopsies. I am the operator who chose the factor weights. I am the one who has to look at the patterns in the Loss Room and decide which of them are signal — the model has a real weakness here that needs a structural fix — and which of them are noise that any honest probability publication will accumulate. That distinction is the hardest work in this entire enterprise, and I do it imperfectly. The book preserves that imperfection rather than smoothing it.
>
> Why bind this into a book at all?
>
> It is a fair question. Every chapter of this book is already on galaxysportsedge.com for free. A subscriber who reads the site daily has already seen most of it. The reason for the binding is not that the data is hidden behind a paywall. The reason is that **a year is a different unit than a week**.
>
> When you read Galaxy's losses on Tuesday morning, you read one loss. When you read the Loss Room as a chapter — every loss from 2026 in a sequence — you see the shape. Patterns become visible that no individual post could ever show. You can see, across forty or sixty losing publications, which factors were the most expensive — the factor we kept underweighting, the assumption we kept making. The book turns the noise back into structure.
>
> The same is true of the Pass List. Skimming Pass List entries one at a time, you might never realize that we passed on certain configurations of games at a much higher rate than others. Reading them in aggregate, you see which patterns we wouldn't touch, and why. The aggregate teaches a different lesson than the individual posts can.
>
> This is the argument for why the book exists at $99 instead of $0. The data is not the product. The shape of the year is the product. I do not believe in pretending that physical objects matter in 2026 — the digital edition of this book ships the same day as the hardcover, at $39 — but I do believe that the binding changes how the reader can interact with the material. A book is read in sections, not in scroll. A book gets re-opened months later, when a similar situation recurs and you remember reading about it. A book sits on a shelf and reminds the operator that the year happened, that the losses were as numerous as the wins, that the calls we did not publish were a discipline worth its own chapter.
>
> I will say one more thing about what this book is not.
>
> It is not a 2027 prediction guide. Galaxy does not predict. Galaxy publishes calibrated probabilities at the time of decision, and then we preserve what happened. The 2026 Almanac does not tell you what is going to work in 2027. It tells you what the model believed in 2026, and what the year's data did to those beliefs. If you read it carefully, you will see places where the model's beliefs survived contact with the data and places where they didn't. The places where they didn't are the places where the methodology is going to evolve in 2027. That evolution will be in next year's Almanac.
>
> What I hope the book becomes, over years, is the publication that other sports analytics work eventually has to compete against — not by being louder or more confident, but by being equally honest about what it doesn't know. The first version of that publication is what you are holding now. It is imperfect in ways I can already see, and probably imperfect in ways I cannot. The second version will be better. The third will be better again. The accumulation of that discipline, year over year, is the work.
>
> Galaxy Sports Edge is one operator's bet that transparent rigor outlasts hype. This Almanac is the first annual record of how that bet went.
>
> Thanks for reading.
>
> — Garrett
> October 2026

# PART 13 — KPI MONTHLY REVIEW (the operator ritual)

The mechanical rules are in `docs/monetization-v3/04-kpi-decision-rules.md`. The ritual layer is in `docs/monetization-v3/audit/kpi-operator-ritual.md`. Summary here for Codex visibility.

## The Last Friday ritual

**When:** Last Friday of every month, 09:00–11:00 Eastern. Recurring calendar block. **Do not move.**

**Slip discipline:** If last Friday is blocked, review moves to following Monday only. Does NOT move to next month.

### 09:00 — Cockpit walk (10 min)

Read the numbers out loud for each track. Active members / customers / partners. Headline KPI. Period change. Performance vs target.

### 09:10 — Felt response (2 min, per track)

Sit with the numbers. Capture one-sentence emotional read per track. Examples:
- "Vault: feels good — members are talking in Discord."
- "Almanac: feels uncertain — pre-orders aren't materializing."
- "Live: feels stalled — no founding partners closed."

### 09:15 — Mechanical read (30 min)

Apply rules from `04-kpi-decision-rules.md`. For each track:
- Kill threshold? → sunset by Monday.
- Doubling-down trigger? → invest within 2 weeks.
- Between thresholds? → apply between-threshold rule.

**Between-threshold rule:**

| Performance vs target | Decision |
|---|---|
| Below 30% | Kill criterion |
| Below 50% for two consecutive periods | Approaching kill; active intervention |
| Below 80% | Wait one more period |
| 80–100% | Maintain |
| 100–150% | Maintain + extend with 20% more capacity |
| 150%+ | Doubling-down trigger |

**Compare felt response to mechanical read.** Mismatches are signal:
- Feels good + mechanically warning → denial. Trust data.
- Feels bad + mechanically green → something invisible going wrong. Investigate.

### 09:45 — Decision sheet (10 min)

For each track: write action + deadline.

### 09:55 — Override protocol (5 min if used)

Override mechanical kill criterion only if:
1. Decision-log entry written (explain why).
2. New kill criterion written.
3. First override per track only.

Second override = automatic sunset.

### 10:00 — Monthly memo (30 min)

Fill `templates/monthly-kpi-review.md`. Archive as `docs/monetization-v3/reviews/YYYY-MM.md`.

Memo includes:
- Felt response (per track, one sentence)
- Mechanical read (per track)
- Decisions this period
- Override invoked? (link to decision log)
- Notes worth carrying forward
- What I'd do differently this month

### 10:30 — Soft close (30 min)

- Reply to Vault Discord posts.
- Read member feedback.
- Look at one random subscriber's journey.
- Re-read last month's memo.
- One sentence in decision log capturing the most important thing the month taught you.

### 11:00 — Close cockpit
